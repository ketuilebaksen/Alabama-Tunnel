First, let’s set the stage, because you have to understand how much pressure was on this game.

Five days before kickoff, the College Football Playoff committee released its first rankings of the season. Miami was number one. Georgia was two. Utah was three. Indiana was four. And then, at number five, Alabama, with two losses. The committee put the Crimson Tide ahead of a seven-and-one Kentucky team, ahead of an undefeated UCLA team at number eight, and ahead of a seven-and-one BYU team at number nine. And the reaction was loud. A lot of people asked how a two-loss team could sit ahead of teams with cleaner records. Some national analysts said Alabama hadn’t done anything to deserve it.

Alabama didn’t argue back in the press. It didn’t need to. Because the committee had basically handed the Tide a challenge: prove it. And the place to prove it was Tiger Stadium, against a ranked LSU team, on the road, with the whole country watching.

On the other sideline was Lane Kiffin, in his first season as LSU’s head coach. And Kiffin has a history in this series. Across his head coaching career, he had never beaten Alabama. LSU brought him in with huge expectations, a talented roster and a quarterback in Sam Leavitt who was supposed to be one of the best in the country. This was the kind of game LSU hired him to win.

And then there was Russell. Let’s talk about who he is, because his story is remarkable.

Keelon Russell was born on April twenty-fourth, 2007. He’s nineteen years old. He came out of Duncanville High School in Texas as a five-star recruit and the consensus number two quarterback in the 2025 class. He enrolled early at Alabama in January of 2025 and spent his freshman season learning behind Ty Simpson. And then this summer, he battled senior Austin Mack for the starting job. On August twenty-second, reports came out that Russell had won it.

So think about that. When Alabama walked into Death Valley on November seventh, Russell had been the starting quarterback for less than three months. He had never started a full college season. He had already been through a lot this year. A wild lightning-delayed shootout against Florida State. A foot injury in the Texas A&M game. Tough road days at Tennessee. And now, the biggest stage of his young career.

And Tiger Stadium is a different kind of place. More than one hundred two thousand fans. The noise gets trapped and bounces right back onto the field. Visiting quarterbacks have trouble hearing their own snap counts. It’s one of the hardest places in the country for a young quarterback to play.

The first half was tight. Alabama’s defense set the tone early, stopping LSU on third and goal from the five on the opening drive and holding the Tigers to a field goal. Conor Talty answered with a forty-three-yarder. Then Ryan Coleman-Williams, in his first game back from injury, scored on a one-yard trick play run. Luke Metz picked off Leavitt before halftime. Alabama went into the locker room up ten to three. But it still felt like anybody’s game.

And then came the third quarter. LSU kicked a field goal. And then Russell went down. He headed to the medical tent on the sideline with an ankle injury. Every Alabama fan watching held their breath. And Russell said later that when he went into that tent, “a lot of heads went down on the team.” With two minutes and forty-four seconds left in the third quarter, LSU running back Dilin Jones scored from five yards out, and LSU took a thirteen-to-ten lead. Tiger Stadium exploded.

Picture that moment. You’re nineteen years old. Your ankle is taped up. More than a hundred thousand people are screaming against you. The scoreboard says you’re losing. Your team’s playoff hopes are on the line. A lot of quarterbacks, even older ones, would have stayed in that tent. Nobody would have blamed him.

But Russell came back out. And DeBoer made it clear there was never any doubt. He said, “He was not getting pulled off the football field.”

And on Alabama’s very next drive, Russell found Rico Scott for a fifty-seven-yard catch-and-run down to the LSU eighteen. LSU’s lead lasted two minutes and eight seconds. Four plays later, Russell threw a seven-yard touchdown pass to Josh Ford with thirty-six seconds left in the third quarter. Seventeen to thirteen, Alabama. DeBoer said that Rico play “sparked us, and it was instantaneous. So, no lull there.”

And people who watched closely noticed something about Russell during that stretch. He looked calm. Not rattled. Not nervous. He looked like a quarterback who had been there a hundred times. That’s rare for any player, let alone a teenager in his first season as a starter.

Then the fourth quarter. Alabama’s defense forced a three-and-out. Ryan Coleman-Williams returned the punt thirty-two yards down to the LSU twenty-five. And on the very next play, Russell hit freshman Cederian Morgan for a twenty-five-yard touchdown. Twenty-four to thirteen. The defense made another fourth-down stop. And Russell finished it with a four-yard touchdown pass to Kaleb Edwards. Thirty-one to thirteen.

Three touchdown passes. Three different receivers. All three in the second half. All three after he came out of the medical tent. And Alabama scored twenty-one unanswered points to end the game.

Russell finished seventeen of twenty-four for two hundred six yards, three touchdowns and zero interceptions. He also ran for twenty-five yards. And against an LSU defense that came into the game ranked second in the country in sacks, he was sacked just twice. He didn’t turn the ball over once. It was the first time all season Alabama played a turnover-free game on the road.

After the game, Russell talked about the ankle. He said, “And through the grace of God, it wasn’t a big injury.” He said, “I was able to step back on the field and just able to give it my all.” And he explained the game plan in simple terms: “We can’t take sacks, we can’t have negative plays, and we can’t turn the ball over going into a hostile environment.” That’s a nineteen-year-old who understood exactly what his team needed from him, and then went out and did it.

And let’s give credit to the guys around him, because Russell would be the first to say he didn’t do it alone. The offensive line, with Racin Delgatty back at center, held up against one of the best pass rushes in the country. Russell had time to get through his reads, and when nothing was there, he threw the ball away instead of forcing it. Rico Scott made the play that flipped the game, turning a catch-and-run into fifty-seven yards. Josh Ford found the soft spot in the end zone for the go-ahead score. Cederian Morgan, a freshman who has only caught two passes all season, made both of them count for touchdowns, including the twenty-five-yarder that broke LSU’s back. And Kaleb Edwards caught three passes for fifty yards and the final touchdown. Three different receivers caught touchdowns, and that tells you Russell was going through his progressions and taking what the defense gave him. That’s what a mature quarterback does. And Ryan Coleman-Williams’ punt return set up a short field at exactly the right time. As DeBoer said, “That’s team football.” A young quarterback is only as good as the people around him, and on Saturday, everybody around Russell showed up for him, just like he showed up for them.

Now let’s get to the new stuff, because on Monday, Kalen DeBoer and his coordinators met with the media to kick off Vanderbilt week, and there was a lot to talk about.

First, the injury update. DeBoer said Russell is “day to day.” He said, “He’s day to day and will continue.” And he pointed to how Russell finished the LSU game as a positive sign. He said, “He finished out the game. That’s a good sign right there.” DeBoer added, “He’s got to go out there and be able to play at the level to help us win.” And then he said, “I’d expect him to do everything he can to get out on the football field.” Knowing Russell, I don’t think anybody doubts that.

Offensive coordinator Ryan Grubb added some important context. He said the forty-eight to seventy-two hours after a game are “the most critical part” of the recovery, and the staff will check on Russell’s condition on Tuesday and Wednesday, with Alabama’s athletic training staff managing his practice workload. And Grubb sounded optimistic. He said, “They seem pretty confident Keelon is going to be alright.” He also pointed out that Russell “responded really well from a foot injury in the Texas A&M game.” So this kid has already shown he can bounce back from being banged up.

And if Alabama does need to turn to the backup, DeBoer said the team has “a lot of confidence” in Austin Mack. Remember, Mack was in a real competition for the starting job this summer. He knows the offense. But let’s all hope Russell is good to go on Saturday. Get well soon, Keelon.

Then Grubb said something that every Alabama fan should hear. He said, “His best football’s ahead of him. He’s one of, if not the best, quarterbacks in the country.” And he described Russell’s performance at LSU as “one of the guttier performances I’ve ever been around for a kid that age.”

Think about who’s saying that. Ryan Grubb has coached a lot of great quarterbacks. He coached Michael Penix Jr. at Washington, when the Huskies made the national championship game. So when Grubb says this was one of the guttiest performances he’s ever seen from a player that age, that means something.

DeBoer also reflected on the LSU win as a whole. He said, “Thought it was one of the more, if not the most complete game, we played.” And he pointed right to the turnover battle: “No turnovers.” He added, “I thought the guys just played really hard.” And then he said something that connects to the bigger story of this season: “Second-half success, the fourth quarter in particular, continues to show up.” That’s been the identity of this team all year. Alabama has now come back from second-half deficits to win four times this season, against Kentucky, Florida State, Texas A&M and LSU. According to one national broadcast, that’s the most of any Power Four team.

Grubb called it “a hard fought game against a really good defense,” and said, “Great win. Guys were incredibly excited.” And defensive coordinator Kane Wommack talked about his unit’s performance. He said, “I thought our guys played really physical. We hit really hard.” On the four fourth-down stops, he gave all the credit to the players. He said, “They brought those calls to life and executed them.” That’s a coach who trusts his guys.

Wommack also praised LSU, calling it “a really great battle last week down in Baton Rouge against a very good opponent.” And that’s important to remember. LSU is a good team. Leavitt threw for three hundred eighty yards. The Tigers outgained Alabama by more than one hundred seventy yards. Lane Kiffin’s team made Alabama earn every bit of this win. Kiffin said afterward, “I credit them. They did a really good job.” Credit to LSU, too. That’s a talented team with a tough schedule ahead, with Texas at home this week and then a trip to Tennessee.

Now, here’s a detail from the LSU locker room that I absolutely love, and it tells you everything about the kind of program DeBoer is building. Minutes after beating LSU, before the team even left Baton Rouge, DeBoer was already talking about Vanderbilt. He told the Crimson Tide Sports Network, “We talked about it already in the locker room.” He explained why: “A lot of these guys don’t have the memory from a couple years ago. Big win, then going up to Vanderbilt.”

He’s talking about 2024. In DeBoer’s first season, Alabama beat Georgia in an instant classic, then went to Nashville the next week and lost to Vanderbilt forty to thirty-five. It was one of the biggest upsets of that whole season. And DeBoer is making sure this team doesn’t repeat that mistake. He said, “Every game is its own game, and this week means nothing when stepping on the football field next Saturday.”

That’s championship-level leadership. Celebrate the win, and then flip the page immediately.

And it’s not just DeBoer. Wommack said the 2024 Vanderbilt loss “had a major impact on me.” He said there are “a lot of challenges in a number of different ways” with this Vanderbilt team, and he said of Clark Lea’s staff, “This staff, I have tremendous respect for.” DeBoer said this week is about “reset and do it again,” and that Lea and his staff “do such a good job.” He called it “a great challenge there.”

So nobody in that building is overlooking the Commodores. Vanderbilt is four and five after a twenty-five-to-twenty-three road win at Mississippi State last weekend. The game is Saturday in Nashville at eleven a.m. Central time. Early kickoff. On the road. Right after the biggest win of the season. That’s the definition of a trap game. But this staff has been through it, and it’s clearly using that 2024 memory as fuel. And honestly, I love that DeBoer brought it up right away. Some coaches would avoid talking about a painful loss like that. DeBoer is using it as a teaching moment for a roster full of players who weren’t there to feel it. That’s how you build a program that doesn’t have letdowns.

DeBoer was also asked about the future of the College Football Playoff and its expansion. Russell’s numbers weren’t huge. Two hundred six passing yards is a modest day. Alabama’s offense only had two hundred eighty-five total yards. The run game is still a work in progress, with Trae’shawn Brown leading the way with thirty-five rushing yards. It’s about leading your team when everything is going against you. And on all of those measures, Russell was outstanding. Zero turnovers. Three touchdowns. Perfect in the red zone. And he did it on one good ankle. That’s the kind of performance that wins championships.

DeBoer said after the game, “I thought he just did a great job of staying focused and locked in. The things that we’ve talked about with all of us, trying to do too much at times. He didn’t try to be Superman. He let the game come to him, and when the moment arrived, he delivered.

Back in September, after the Florida State game, Russell admitted, “We got too many mental mistakes right now, but we’re going to fix it.” He fixed it. Week by week, he’s gotten better. The quarterback who walked into Tiger Stadium on Saturday was a lot more polished than the one who started the season. And he’s only nineteen.
