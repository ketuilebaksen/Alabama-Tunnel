Let’s start with how the game unfolded, because the story of this one is all about the response.

The first half was a grind. Conor Talty opened the scoring with a forty-three-yard field goal in the first quarter. And that’s a big moment by itself. Remember, Talty missed two of his three field goals against Texas A&M after making fifteen in a row. A lot of people wondered how he’d bounce back. He bounced back on the very first opportunity. LSU answered with a field goal from Scott Starzyk to tie it at three.

Then, in the second quarter, came one of my favorite plays of the season. Alabama put together an eleven-play, sixty-two-yard drive, and at the goal line, the coaches dialed up a trick play. LSU’s defense followed Daniel Hill, and Ryan Coleman-Williams slipped into the end zone untouched on a misdirection run. One yard. Touchdown. In his first game back from knee surgery and a head injury, Coleman-Williams scored Alabama’s first touchdown of the game. Ten to three.

And then the defense came up with a huge play before halftime. Starzyk missed a fifty-six-yard field goal wide left, and later Luke Metz intercepted Sam Leavitt and returned it twenty-eight yards. Alabama went into the locker room up ten to three.

The third quarter was where things got tense. LSU kicked a forty-one-yard field goal to make it ten to six. Then, with two minutes and forty-four seconds left in the quarter, Dilin Jones ran in from five yards out, and LSU took a thirteen-to-ten lead. And here’s the scary part. Keelon Russell had gone to the medical tent with an ankle injury. The crowd was rocking. The momentum was all LSU. That’s the moment where a lot of teams would have folded in Death Valley.

Alabama didn’t. Russell came back out with heavy tape on his left ankle, and almost immediately, he hit Rico Scott for fifty-seven yards down to the LSU eighteen. LSU’s lead lasted just two minutes and eight seconds. A few plays later, with thirty-six seconds left in the third, Russell found Josh Ford for a seven-yard touchdown. Seventeen to thirteen, Alabama.

And then the fourth quarter belonged to the Crimson Tide. Alabama’s defense forced a three-and-out. Ryan Coleman-Williams fielded the punt and returned it thirty-two yards down to the LSU twenty-five. On the very next sequence, Russell threw a twenty-five-yard touchdown pass to freshman Cederian Morgan. Twenty-four to thirteen, with nine minutes and forty-four seconds left. Then the defense stopped LSU on fourth down again, and Russell finished it off with a four-yard touchdown pass to Kaleb Edwards. Thirty-one to thirteen. Ballgame.

Twenty-one unanswered points. A fourth-quarter shutout. In one of the toughest places to play in college football. And think about the parallel DeBoer talked about all week. In 2024, his first season, Alabama lost on the road at Tennessee and then went into Baton Rouge and won big. This season, Alabama lost at Tennessee again, and DeBoer said the goal was “taking those moments and learning from it and being better because of it.” Mission accomplished. Same script, same result. And with this win, DeBoer is now three and oh against LSU, with a combined score of ninety-three to thirty-five.

Now let’s talk about the decisions that made it happen, because this game was won by smart, bold calls from DeBoer and his staff.

Decision number one: trusting Ryan Coleman-Williams right away.

A lot of coaches would have eased a player back slowly after a month off. Coleman-Williams had arthroscopic knee surgery and a head injury, and he hadn’t played since October tenth. But DeBoer and the staff didn’t just put him on the field. They gave him the ball at the goal line on a trick play, and they put him back deep on punt returns. Both decisions paid off with points.

DeBoer talked about this after the game. He said, “I just think that Ryan brings a lot to us, and doesn’t always show up on the stat sheet.” He added, “He brings just an energy to us throughout the course of the week. There’s a presence he has when he’s on the football field.” And he said something really interesting about the effect Coleman-Williams has on the whole offense: “For us, it’s just there’s a little better flow. It just seems like everything’s in sync.” He continued, “Anytime he touches the football, you know, it can be electric, and so the other teams are probably holding their breath anytime he does, and he showed it tonight what he’s capable of.”

And DeBoer made sure to give him credit for the work behind the scenes. He said, “He worked really hard to get back.” He added, “There’s always more behind it than what you actually see when you show up on a Saturday,” and that Coleman-Williams “was very motivated to make sure he could get on the football field tonight.”

Think about that story. A junior. A team captain. A guy who changed his name this year to honor his mother and called this season “an opportunity to have a hard reset.” He misses four weeks with injuries, and in his first game back, he scores a touchdown and sets up another with a big punt return in Death Valley. That’s a legacy moment.

And DeBoer explained why his teammates block so hard for him on returns. He said, “Guys just believe in him and are going to go block their tails off for him whenever we get that chance.” He also said that some of the guys have been around now, “not just the first year but in their second year, that understand what we’re trying to accomplish, and anytime they see one back there, they’re going to go block hard for him.” That’s culture. And remember, Lane Kiffin said earlier in the week, “I bet Ryan will be back.” He was right. And Ryan made LSU pay for it. After the game, Coleman-Williams said the team wanted to take advantage of the punt return opportunity, and he pointed out that players on both sides of the ball play big roles. That’s a captain talking. Not about himself, but about his teammates.

Decision number two: the defensive game plan on third and fourth down.

This might have been the biggest key to the win. LSU went for it on fourth down four times, and Alabama stopped every single one. Zero for four. The first came at LSU’s own twenty-five, when Leavitt was sacked for a seven-yard loss.

DeBoer said that’s what it always comes down to: “getting off the field.” He explained, “The times we’ve been successful, that’s what we’re doing. And the times we’ve fallen short, it’s been a struggle.” And then he gave credit to Kane Wommack and the defensive staff. He said the players were “really locked in on the plan all week long,” and that “you could feel the confidence growing in all phases, but in defense specifically, you could see those guys really feeling like, hey, the third down, fourth down plan was in a good spot.” He added, “We had enough, I think, in the game plan to be able to keep them off balance.” And he said, “Coach Wommack and the staff, I thought, did a nice job.”

And here’s the thing. LSU actually moved the ball. The Tigers outgained Alabama by more than one hundred seventy yards, finishing with around four hundred fifty-seven total yards to Alabama’s two hundred eighty-five. Leavitt completed thirty-five of forty-seven passes for three hundred eighty yards. But he threw zero touchdown passes and one interception. Two LSU drives stalled inside Alabama’s ten-yard line, and on fourth and four from the nine, Leavitt’s pass was batted down. Two other drives ended at the Alabama thirty-six.

That’s bend-but-don’t-break defense at its finest. And it connects directly to what Wommack said before the game. He said, “You’ve got to create enough doubt in their minds and then capitalize.” He said Alabama had “found ways to capitalize and create takeaways off RPOs.” That’s exactly what happened. LSU had chances. Alabama created doubt, and then capitalized, with the Metz interception, the sacks and the fourth-down stops. You can give up yards. What matters is points. And Alabama held an explosive LSU offense, a top-ten offense according to DeBoer, to just thirteen points and only one touchdown. In the fourth quarter, LSU scored zero.

The pass rush came alive too. Alabama sacked Leavitt four times. Devan Thompkins had two sacks. Yhonzae Pierre had one. And Terrance Green, in his first game back from a foot injury, had one too. Remember what Pierre said earlier in the week? “We got TG coming back, Terrence Green.” And then he said, “It’s going to be amazing to see what we do Saturday.” He was right.

And the tackling was relentless. Luke Metz led the team with eleven tackles to go with that interception. Red Morgan and Bray Hubbard had nine each. Caleb Woodson had eight, plus a pass breakup, a quarterback hurry and a forced fumble. And think about Red Morgan’s journey. Two weeks ago, he was ejected for targeting against Texas A&M. On Saturday, he was one of the leading tacklers on the field. After the game, Morgan said the key is “what we do after that,” talking about how the team responds to bad moments. That’s this whole team’s story.

A reporter asked DeBoer about Wommack’s “swarm D” philosophy, because LSU couldn’t break big plays after contact. DeBoer said Leavitt is “a really tough guy to bring down, and so you have to keep running to the ball carrier.” He added, “It’s all about the swarm, you know, and pursuing and not assuming that a guy’s going to be on the ground. You create your breaks, you create opportunities by hustling and giving great effort.” That’s exactly what we saw.

Decision number three: sticking with Keelon Russell through the injury.

When Russell went to the medical tent in the third quarter with an ankle injury, a lot of Alabama fans held their breath. But he came back out with heavy tape and led the comeback. He finished seventeen of twenty-four for two hundred six yards, three touchdowns and no interceptions. He also ran for twenty-five yards on eight carries.

And here’s what I love. After the game, Russell said, in effect, that an ankle injury wasn’t going to stop him from playing the way he plays, and it wasn’t going to be the reason his team played poorly. That’s a leader.

DeBoer said, “The guy’s tough, you know, and it doesn’t surprise me because I’ve seen it before with him. The physical toughness and just his determination to be out there on the football field. It wasn’t just tonight. I’ve seen it throughout the course of the year and different times.”

And remember DeBoer’s message to Russell all week: stay upright, get rid of the football, don’t try to do too much. Well, Russell did exactly that. DeBoer said, “I thought he just did a great job of staying focused and locked in. The things that we’ve talked about with all of us, trying to do too much at times. You know, he threw the ball away, but he obviously got the tools to go make the plays.” He added that there were a couple of plays Russell will want back, and that’s what they’ll focus on more than the great ones. But he said, “I thought he did a great job just running and managing the offense, and making the plays when he had the chance.”

Zero interceptions against one of the best pass defenses in the SEC. Three touchdown passes. On a bad ankle. In Death Valley. That’s a sophomore quarterback growing up right in front of our eyes. And don’t overlook the return of Racin Delgatty at center. Against an LSU defense with thirty-four sacks coming into the game, the Tide’s offensive line gave Russell enough time to make the throws that mattered, in one of the loudest stadiums in the country. Having the starting center back to handle the communication made a difference, even if the run game still has room to grow.

Decision number four: the bye week plan.

A reporter told DeBoer his record after bye weeks is “incredible” and asked what his secret is. DeBoer said it’s partly about “a freshening up of the mind that needs to happen” and a physical piece too. But he gave most of the credit to his staff. He said, “I just really credit our staff, putting a plan together, and then that plan being executed.” He also talked about timing the team’s energy. He said, “There’s a mindset too that’s got to be created and an energy that you got to bring at the right time as you build.” And he explained, “You don’t want that energy to be reaching a climax the first week. You need to have it as we’re getting close to game and kickoff.”

He also described what practice felt like during the week. He said, “You can listen to a practice and the pads are popping and there’s chatter and there’s just good juice.” He added, “It got better and better and better as we put the game plan together for these guys tonight.” And he talked about the locker room right after the A&M game. He said there was “an energy right there in that locker room knowing some of these guys were going to come back.” He said, “When we get our complete team together, and we’re still not there yet, and maybe you never will be, there’s a confidence that grows, especially when they’re your leaders, when they’re your playmakers.”

That’s exactly what we saw. Coleman-Williams, Racin Delgatty and Terrance Green all came back and all made an impact. And the confidence was obvious.

Now let’s talk about the play that turned the game. Rico Scott’s fifty-seven-yard catch.

When LSU took the lead, the stadium was shaking. And on the very next drive, Russell found Rico Scott for fifty-seven yards. DeBoer said, “I thought the response when we were down there in the third quarter and hit the big play to Rico, you know, sparked us, and it was instantaneous. So, you know, no lull there.”

And DeBoer connected it to Rico’s breakout game against A&M. He said, “Rico’s had his game last week, and you know, there’s a confidence in a playmaker that everyone believes in when it comes to him, and himself as well.” Rico finished with three catches for a team-high sixty-seven yards. And that one catch might have been the most important play of Alabama’s season so far.

DeBoer also talked about the message to the offense after that touchdown. He said, “There’s a lot of football left,” and “it’s all about just being in the moment and staying focused on the next play.” He added, “We’ve had a number of situations now in the second half where you just stay the course.” And then he said this: “There’s a physicality that I think our team still has, and a presence that we get in these SEC games, that takes over.” That’s exactly what happened in the fourth quarter. And DeBoer specifically pointed to the defensive line. He said, “I think there was times where our D-line, you see the pass rush continue to put pressure on them.” He added, “You go back to the training you have, the toughness, the resiliency.” That’s a defense that wore LSU down as the game went on, and that’s why LSU didn’t score in the fourth quarter.

And the special teams deserve a shoutout too. DeBoer said, “Special teams coming up with, obviously, Conor doing his thing and doing a good job there, and then also with Ryan and the big return.” He called it “team football,” and said, “That’s what makes me proud as the head coach, is that all the areas understand the value they have, and you’re ready for the moment.”

And the scoring was spread all over the roster. Coleman-Williams on the ground. Josh Ford, Cederian Morgan and Kaleb Edwards through the air. Edwards had three catches for fifty yards and a touchdown. Morgan, a freshman, scored on a twenty-five-yard strike. That’s what a deep, healthy offense looks like. And DeBoer explained the bigger philosophy behind those scores. He said, “When you can get that field position that we got there and then take advantage of it immediately,” that’s “team football.” He added, “Against a team like this, it’s hard to go down the field each and every play and stack those plays, and so you got to have explosive plays generated somehow, whether it’s takeaways, whether it’s special teams.” That’s exactly how Alabama won. The defense and special teams created short fields, and the offense cashed in. Coleman-Williams’ punt return led straight to Morgan’s touchdown. That’s complementary football.

And let’s give some love to Cederian Morgan. A freshman, scoring a twenty-five-yard touchdown in the fourth quarter of a top-fifteen road game in Death Valley. That’s a moment he’ll never forget. And it shows you how much this receiver room has grown while Coleman-Williams was out. DeBoer said earlier in the week that the younger receivers were getting “really quality reps in practice,” so that “when those moments come, they’re even more ready than they have been.” That moment came, and Morgan was ready.

DeBoer summed it up perfectly to open his press conference: “Super proud of the guys. Team win all around.” He said the defense came up with big stops and got the offense field position, and the offense took advantage. And he finished with this: “Good football team we played there. Just got to play four quarters, and I’m proud of our guys. We pride ourselves on that, and that’s what it took tonight.”

Now, let’s be honest about a few things, because that’s what we do here on Alabama Tunnel.

LSU moved the ball. Four hundred fifty-seven yards is a lot to give up, and Leavitt threw for three hundred eighty. Alabama’s offense only had two hundred eighty-five total yards. The run game still wasn’t where the staff wants it. Trae’shawn Brown led the team with thirty-five yards on eleven carries. So there’s still work to do, and DeBoer would be the first to say it. He even said there are plays Russell left on the field that the team will focus on.

But here’s the other side. Great teams find ways to win when things aren’t perfect. Alabama didn’t throw a single interception, and it picked off Leavitt once. It won on fourth down. It won on special teams. It won in the red zone. And it won the fourth quarter fourteen to nothing. That’s how you win big games on the road.

And credit to LSU. Lane Kiffin’s team is talented and well coached. DeBoer called them a “good football team,” and they were. Leavitt is a tough, dangerous quarterback. After the game, Kiffin said, “It got kind of ugly there in the end.” And Leavitt said he thought LSU was “just about to go win the game” before things turned. He called the fourth-down failures “pretty brutal.” We wish them well the rest of the way.

And what about the kickoff time drama? All week, Kiffin complained about the two-thirty start. He said, “It’s interesting that all of a sudden this year, for the first time ever since 2010,” Alabama wasn’t coming to Baton Rouge at night. And DeBoer’s answer was simple: “It is what it is. We just got to show up and play.” Well, Alabama showed up and played. The game kicked off in the afternoon sun and finished under the lights, and Alabama owned the stretch run. Day or night, it didn’t matter.

So what does this win mean for Alabama?

Alabama is now seven and two, five and two in the SEC. It has a top-ten road win at Kentucky, a road win over a ranked LSU team, and a win over a ranked South Carolina team. That’s a résumé the committee will love. Remember, Alabama was ranked fifth in the first College Football Playoff rankings, the highest-ranked two-loss team in the country. A lot of people said that was too high. Paul Finebaum said before the game, “If Alabama loses this game, forget it.” Well, Alabama didn’t lose. It won by eighteen. And to critics like Danny Kanell, who called the committee “the worst, most non-sensical one yet,” this is the best possible answer. We said all week, the best way to end the debate is to win on the field. Alabama did that, and it did it convincingly. The next rankings come out on Tuesday, and I’d expect Alabama to be in a very strong position to host a playoff game in Tuscaloosa.

The SEC Championship picture is still complicated, with Georgia and Kentucky both sitting at five and one in conference play before their games this weekend. But Alabama controls what it can control. Win out, and it’ll be a ten-and-two team with one of the best résumés in the country.
