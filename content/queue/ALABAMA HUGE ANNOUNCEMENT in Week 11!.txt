Winner number one: Austin Mack.

After years of patiently waiting for his opportunity, Mack finally got his first career start. And he made the most of it. After a slower start, he settled into the offense and completed twenty of twenty-five passes for two hundred fifty-four yards and four touchdowns. He also scored on a quarterback sneak. That's five total touchdowns in his first career start. At one point, he completed thirteen straight passes.

What impressed me most was how his confidence grew as the game went on. Early on, you could tell he was settling in. He admitted afterward that he was a little nervous, and then he said, "Eventually settled in, and me and the guys got going." By the second half, he was attacking downfield and delivering accurate passes to his receivers. He hit Rico Scott for a twenty-nine-yard touchdown right before halftime, capping a five-play, eighty-three-yard drive in just fifty-nine seconds. And he led a ninety-two-yard drive to open the second half that ended with a touchdown pass to Noah Rogers.

Sure, Chattanooga isn't an SEC defense. But Mack deserves a lot of credit. He's been one of the most loyal and hardworking players in this program. He followed DeBoer from Washington to Alabama. He prepared like a starter before Vanderbilt and then went back to supporting Russell without a word. DeBoer said then, "I can't give him enough credit." And on Saturday, Mack finally got his moment. So what are the keys for Alabama on Saturday night? Let me give you five.

Key number one: protect the football. Grubb made it clear. If Alabama takes care of the ball, it has a great chance to win. Rivalry games often come down to turnovers, and a sharp, rested Russell gives Alabama the best chance to play clean.

Key number two: contain Byrum Brown. Keep him in the pocket, tackle him in space, and don't let him extend plays with his legs. Alabama's defense has been elite against the run, and that has to continue.

Key number three: win early downs. DeBoer said last week that "early efficiency on early downs" is the key to the offense's rhythm. If Alabama stays ahead of the chains, the play-action game opens up, and Russell can attack downfield.

Key number four: keep running the football. Alabama just ran for two hundred sixty-three yards. Hill and Brown are running with confidence, and Crowell could be back. A strong run game controls the clock and keeps Auburn's offense on the sideline.
