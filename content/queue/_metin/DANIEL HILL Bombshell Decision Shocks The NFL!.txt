Now, let’s talk about Daniel Hill.

To understand why Saturday mattered so much, you have to go back to the beginning. Daniel Hill was born on November twenty-first, 2005. And yes, if you’re doing the math, that means he turns twenty-one this Saturday, the same day Alabama hosts Chattanooga at Bryant-Denny Stadium. Keep that in mind, because we’ll come back to it.

Hill grew up in Meridian, Mississippi, and starred at Meridian High School. And his high school story is one of real resilience. He broke his leg not once, but twice, during his high school career. For a lot of young players, one serious injury like that can change everything. Some never come back the same. Hill came back stronger. As a senior, he ran for more than two thousand yards and scored twenty-three touchdowns. He became a four-star recruit, he played in the All-American Bowl, and he committed to Alabama in the 2024 class. Think about that path. Two broken legs, and he still turned himself into one of the top running backs in his class. That tells you a lot about who this young man is.

His freshman season in 2024 was a learning year, which is normal for a running back at Alabama. He played in nine games, with twenty-one carries for sixty-one yards and a touchdown. He was learning the offense, learning the speed of SEC football, and learning what it takes to earn snaps in a crowded running back room.

In 2025, his role grew. He finished second on the team in rushing with close to three hundred yards. His best game came against Oklahoma, when he ran for sixty yards and two touchdowns and also caught five passes for thirty-seven yards. And he became a real weapon in the passing game, finishing the year with twenty-eight catches for two hundred three yards. Good numbers. Solid contributions. But Daniel Hill knew there was another level to reach.

And that’s where the offseason transformation comes in. Hill decided to reshape his body. He was listed at two hundred forty-four pounds last season, he worked his way down to about two hundred thirty-six, and Alabama’s current roster lists him at two hundred thirty-four. He cut out junk food. He even cut out cookies, which, let’s be honest, is a real sacrifice. And he added more cardio to his routine. This spring, he explained how it felt. He said, “I feel way faster, my body feels better.”

He also went to work on the part of his game he knew needed the most improvement: pass protection. And he was completely honest about it. He said, “I know I gave up a few sacks last year, and as a running back, that just can’t happen.” Tide Nation, that’s a player who holds himself accountable. He didn’t make excuses. He didn’t blame anybody else. He looked at his own film, he saw what needed to be better, and he went and fixed it. That’s exactly the kind of mindset you want in your locker room.

The hard work got noticed, too. Hill was named to Bruce Feldman’s famous “Freaks List” for 2026. If you’re not familiar with that list, it highlights some of the most impressive athletes in college football, the guys who do things in the weight room and on the practice field that make people stop and stare. Being on that list means your physical transformation is real.

And Alabama’s offensive coordinator, Ryan Grubb, saw it every day. Back in preseason camp, he said, “He’s put a lot of work in to being one of our workhorses.” One of our workhorses. That was the plan for Daniel Hill coming into 2026.

And the start of the season showed why. In the SEC opener at Kentucky, Hill ran for one hundred four yards on eighteen carries and scored a touchdown in a big road win. That was the Daniel Hill everybody had been waiting for. Powerful, decisive, hard to bring down.

But then, football got hard. After that Kentucky game, Hill’s production dropped off. According to BamaCentral, he went six straight SEC games without reaching forty rushing yards, and he didn’t get more than nine carries in any of them. Alabama’s run game as a whole struggled during that stretch. Opponents were loading the box. The blocking wasn’t always consistent. And the offense leaned more and more on Keelon Russell’s arm. A lot of people started asking whether Hill could still be the guy when it mattered most.

That’s a tough spot for any player. You do everything right in the offseason, you have a big game early, and then your role starts shrinking week after week. Some players get frustrated. Some players check out. Daniel Hill kept working.

And then came Vanderbilt.

Hill finished with fourteen carries for eighty-nine yards. That’s six point four yards per carry, his best average of the season. It wasn’t a hundred-yard game. But the context is what makes this performance so significant. It wasn’t just the numbers. It was when he got them, and how he got them.

He ripped off a twenty-yard run, one of Alabama’s most explosive plays on the ground all day. On one drive alone, he picked up thirty-seven rushing yards, wearing down a Vanderbilt defense that had been playing with a lot of energy. He converted a third and one with a three-yard run, the kind of short-yardage play this offense has struggled with at times this season. And in the fourth quarter, with Alabama trying to protect its lead and run the clock, Hill faced third and two and broke off a twelve-yard run for a first down. That’s the kind of run that takes the life out of the other team. That’s how you close out games in November.

Those are winning plays. Short-yardage conversions. Clock-killing runs. Hard yards when everybody in the stadium knows what’s coming.

And here’s the context that makes it even bigger. Alabama finished with ninety-two rushing yards as a team. Daniel Hill had eighty-nine of them. Now, in college football, sack yardage counts against your team’s rushing total, and Russell was sacked three times, so that explains part of it. But the bottom line is simple: on a day when Alabama couldn’t get much going on the ground otherwise, Hill carried the load almost by himself.

He ran with purpose. He ran with physicality. He fell forward. And he ran like a guy who knew this was his chance to reclaim his role.

Now let’s be honest about the bigger picture, because that’s what we always do here on Alabama Tunnel. We celebrate the good, and we’re real about what still needs to get better.

Saturday was still the fourth straight game in which Alabama was held under one hundred rushing yards as a team. That’s a real issue, and the coaches know it. DeBoer and Grubb have both talked about improving the run game as a top priority. Earlier in the season, freshman Trae’shawn Brown gave this offense a big spark and scored some important touchdowns. He’s a talented young player with a bright future. But like the rest of the run game, his production has slowed down over the last month. EJ Crowell, who is explosive when he’s healthy, has been battling injuries. So the running back room has been in flux for most of the year, and the staff has been searching for a consistent answer.

That’s exactly why Hill’s performance matters so much. When a running back averages six point four yards per carry, converts on third and short, and helps close out a road win, he earns more carries. It’s that simple. Hill has given the coaches a very good reason to lean on him in the final weeks.

And think about why a reliable running game is so important right now. DeBoer explained it perfectly last week. He said the number of plays Alabama runs has been “limiting who gets touches.” When you don’t stay on the field, you don’t get enough snaps to establish the run. His solution was simple: “Stay on the football field.” When you can convert third and one and third and two, the drives keep going, the run game gets more chances, and the defense gets more rest on the sideline. On Saturday, Hill did exactly that.

There’s also the Keelon Russell factor, and this might be the most important piece of all. Russell is playing through an ankle injury. He was sacked three times against Vanderbilt, and he took plenty of other hits, too. Grubb said last week that he wants Russell to protect himself, to “get the football out of his hand,” and to avoid unnecessary shots. Well, one of the best ways to protect your quarterback is to have a running game that works. If Daniel Hill can carry more of the load, Russell doesn’t have to do it all on every snap. He doesn’t have to drop back thirty-five or forty times. And he has a much better chance of being at full speed in December, when Alabama will need him the most.

And don’t forget the pass protection piece. Remember, Hill made that a priority this offseason after admitting he gave up some sacks last year. A running back who can run hard and also pick up a blitzing linebacker is incredibly valuable, especially when your nineteen-year-old quarterback is banged up. That’s one more reason the coaches trust him, and one more reason he could see the field even more down the stretch.

Now, let’s talk about the rest of the game, because there were some other big storylines in Nashville.

Let’s start with Keelon Russell. Remember the drama leading up to this game. On Friday, ESPN reported that Austin Mack would start. Then on the final availability report, ninety minutes before kickoff, Russell was still listed as a game-time decision. And then he ran out with the first team. And the nineteen-year-old sophomore delivered. He completed twenty-two of thirty-three passes for two hundred seventeen yards and three touchdowns, all while playing on that sore ankle.

Was it perfect? No. He threw an interception on a tipped ball, and he lost a fumble on a strip sack. But when Alabama needed him, he answered. He led three straight touchdown drives that decided this game. He hit tight end Marshall Pritchett for a nine-yard touchdown with just six seconds left in the first half, a huge momentum swing heading into the locker room. Then he found Lotzeir Brooks for a thirty-nine-yard touchdown, and later Brooks again from nine yards out. Brooks finished with five catches for sixty-two yards and two touchdowns, one of his best games in an Alabama uniform. And at the very end, Austin Mack, who prepared all week as if he might start, came in to take the final knee. Credit to Mack, too. Being ready when your number might be called is a big part of being a good teammate.

And then there’s the play that flipped the field: Dijon Lee Jr.’s eighty-three-yard kickoff return. That return set Alabama up with a short field and helped turn a tight game into a two-score lead. Special teams win games in November, and Lee delivered a game-changing play.

Now, let’s talk about Kane Wommack’s defense, which was the best unit on the field on Saturday.

Vanderbilt finished with just two hundred thirty-five total yards. And here’s a stat that’s hard to believe: Vanderbilt ran for just twelve yards. Twelve rushing yards, for the entire game. Again, sacks count against rushing yards in college football, and Alabama got home five times, but that’s still complete control of the line of scrimmage. Vanderbilt went just two for fifteen on third down and one for four on fourth down. That’s three for nineteen on the money downs. And the Commodores didn’t score a single touchdown. They got two field goals and nothing else. Alabama’s defense has now given up just one touchdown in its last eight quarters of football.

Alabama sacked Vanderbilt freshman quarterback Jared Curtis five times. Linebacker QB Reese had a career-high eight tackles and the first sack of his career, a big step for a young player getting more snaps. Wolf linebacker Justin Hill had a sack and one and a half tackles for loss in his most extensive action in weeks after dealing with a nagging injury. Getting him back healthy for this final stretch is a big boost for the pass rush. And Dijon Lee Jr., on top of that huge kickoff return, had seven tackles, his season high. What a day for him.

So think about what all of that means. Alabama didn’t force a single takeaway, and it still held an SEC opponent to six points and twelve rushing yards. That’s not a defense that depends on turnovers or lucky bounces. That’s a defense that wins snap after snap after snap. Vanderbilt’s head coach even called his own offense a mess after the game. And this Alabama defense is playing its best football at exactly the right time of the year.

Of course, not everything was perfect. The offense had some frustrating moments. The two turnovers kept Vanderbilt in the game longer than it should have been. Some fans questioned a few of the late play calls, especially some of the deep shots and quick throws with a two-score lead, when running the ball and draining the clock might have made more sense. And DeBoer himself didn’t pretend everything was great. After the game he said, “We know we got to continue to move on, continue to improve.” That’s the right attitude. Win the game, learn from the film, and get better.

Now let’s talk about what all of this means for the playoff picture.

Alabama was ranked third in the last College Football Playoff rankings, and the new rankings come out Tuesday night. Here’s something worth watching. Alabama’s wins over teams that were ranked in that last CFP release were Kentucky, South Carolina and LSU. And all three of those teams lost on Saturday. Kentucky lost to Florida, and South Carolina and LSU both went down as well. That could take a little shine off Alabama’s résumé in the committee’s eyes. So it’ll be interesting to see where Alabama lands on Tuesday night, and whether it stays in position for a first-round bye.

But here’s the thing. Alabama controls what it can control. Win the next two games, against Chattanooga and Auburn, and the Tide finishes the regular season at ten and two, with a lot of quality wins. That’s a résumé that belongs in the playoff. And in the SEC race, Alabama still has a path to Atlanta, but it has to take care of its own business first.

And that’s exactly where Daniel Hill comes in. You don’t win an Iron Bowl, an SEC Championship Game or a playoff game by relying only on your quarterback’s arm, especially when your quarterback is playing through an ankle injury. You need to be able to run the football when everyone in the stadium knows you’re going to run it. You need to convert third and one. You need to close out games in the fourth quarter. On Saturday, Hill showed he can do all of that.

The big question is whether Vanderbilt was a turning point or a one-game flash. Hill has been here before. He had a great game at Kentucky, and then he went through a long, tough stretch. But this time feels different, because of how he did it. His biggest runs came in the toughest moments of the game, when Alabama needed him most. That’s not empty yardage. That’s winning football.

The next chance to build on it comes this Saturday, November twenty-first, when Alabama hosts Chattanooga at Bryant-Denny Stadium. And as we mentioned earlier, it’s a special day for Hill, because it’s his twenty-first birthday. What better way to celebrate than with a big day at home in front of Tide Nation? Against an FCS opponent, this is the perfect chance for Hill to get more carries, build rhythm and gain even more confidence heading into the Iron Bowl. It’s also a great chance for the offensive line to get its run blocking on track, and for the rest of the running backs to find a rhythm, too.

And with Russell’s ankle still a question, a strong running game could let Alabama lean on the ground and give its quarterback a lighter workload. DeBoer was clear about Russell’s status going forward. He said, “If he can go, we’ll expect him to go.” But even if Russell plays, the smart move is to protect him. And the best protection is a running back who can move the chains.

Then comes the big one. The Iron Bowl, on November twenty-eighth. Rivalry games are often decided in the trenches and on the ground. If Alabama can bring a confident, physical Daniel Hill into that game, it changes how defenses have to play this offense. Safeties have to come down. Linebackers have to respect the run. And that opens up the play-action shots to Brooks and the rest of Russell’s receivers. Everything in this offense gets easier when the run game works.
