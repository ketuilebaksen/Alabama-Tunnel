Alabama went into Neyland Stadium on the Third Saturday in October banged up. No Ryan Coleman-Williams. No Terrance Green. A backup likely starting at center. And it was a tough day. Tennessee won twenty-six to ten. The Vols forced four turnovers, two interceptions and two fumble recoveries, and held Alabama to just two hundred one total yards with no points in the second half. DeSean Bishop carried the ball thirty-two times for one hundred fifty-nine yards and two touchdowns, and George MacIntyre managed the game well in his second career start. There’s no sugarcoating it. It turned the ball over four times, and Tennessee became just the third team in the last eleven seasons to force four turnovers against Alabama. Tennessee’s defensive line, led by Jeremiah Telander and Tyree Weathersby, had multiple sacks and forced fumbles. And without Coleman-Williams, and with a new center in the middle of the line, the offense just never found a rhythm. That’s not a talent problem. Alabama beat Texas A&M thirty-one to twenty. It snapped the skid. It avoided what would have been Alabama’s first three-game losing streak since 2007, Nick Saban’s very first season in Tuscaloosa. It was also the first meeting between Alabama and Texas A&M since 2023, and it didn’t come easy. A&M led twenty to seventeen going into the fourth quarter. But the Alabama defense held the Aggies to just two hundred seventy-eight total yards and one hundred fifteen rushing yards on forty carries. A&M head coach Mike Elko even said afterward that he considered making a quarterback change during the game because of the turnovers. Alabama’s defense made Marcel Reed’s day that uncomfortable. And Alabama’s offense finished with three hundred eighty total yards. When it mattered most, in the fourth quarter, this team was simply better.

Some people wondered if Lee would lose snaps. Instead, he answered on the field. That’s what competition does in a healthy program. It brings out the best in everybody. Djion Lee didn’t complain, didn’t point fingers. He went out and made the play of the game.

Now, let’s talk about the harder story of the week. The dismissal of Dre Kirkpatrick Jr.

Here’s what we know. Kirkpatrick was suspended before the Texas A&M game for what was described at the time as a violation of team rules. Two days after the game, DeBoer said there were still details he was trying to understand. Then on Tuesday, he announced the decision. His statement was short and direct: “After gathering more information on the situation, Dre Kirkpatrick Jr. will no longer be a part of our program.” And he added that Kirkpatrick “did not live up to the standards and expectations that I have set for this team and our players.”

According to reports, the dismissal followed an arrest on misdemeanor charges. It’s important to be clear here: those are allegations, and the legal process will play out on its own. Nobody on this channel is going to act as judge or jury. But whether a player stays on the football team is a separate question, and that decision belongs to the head coach. DeBoer made it.

And there’s history here too. This was Kirkpatrick’s second suspension in two years. In 2025, he played in the first eight games and recorded nine tackles before he was suspended following an arrest, and he didn’t play again that season. He came back this year and appeared in seven games, recording five tackles, mostly as a reserve defensive back. So DeBoer gave him another chance. And when the coaching staff gathered more information this time, it decided the program had to move on.

This one is especially tough for a lot of Alabama fans because of the name. His father, Dre Kirkpatrick Sr., was an All-SEC cornerback who won two national championships at Alabama and played ten seasons in the NFL. That family is part of Alabama football history. So nobody is celebrating this. It’s disappointing for everyone involved.

But here’s why I think DeBoer made the right call, and why it actually says something positive about this program. Standards have to apply to everybody. It doesn’t matter whose son you are, what recruiting ranking you had, or how many games you’ve played. If the head coach sets expectations for the team and those expectations aren’t met, there have to be consequences. That’s how you build a culture. And honestly, that’s how you earn the respect of the other hundred-plus players in that locker room who are doing things the right way every day.

We talked a lot this season about the character question DeBoer raised after the Georgia loss. Character isn’t just about how you respond to a tough loss on the field. It’s also about the standards you hold off the field. On Tuesday, DeBoer showed his team, and the whole country, that those standards are real.

From a football standpoint, Kirkpatrick wasn’t one of Alabama’s main contributors this season. But every reserve defensive back matters in an SEC season, especially with Zabien Brown banged up and Nick Sherman already out. So who steps up in that secondary? Carmelo O’Neal, who Kane Wommack has praised for getting better every single week. Chuck McDonald III, who just got his first career interception. And of course Djion Lee Jr., who’s coming off the best game of his season. Add Bray Hubbard, Keon Sabb and, hopefully, a healthy Zabien Brown after the bye, and this secondary is in good hands.

On November seventh, Alabama goes to Baton Rouge to face LSU. And this year has a brand-new twist: Lane Kiffin is in his first season as LSU’s head coach after leaving Ole Miss. LSU is six and two, three and two in the SEC, and ranked eighteenth. The Tigers opened the season with a fifty-one to ten win over Clemson, beat Texas A&M thirty-five to six, and shut out Mississippi State thirty-one to nothing. Their two losses came on the road at Ole Miss and in overtime at Kentucky. And look closer at those results. LSU lost thirty-two to twenty-four at Ole Miss and thirty-five to thirty-four in overtime at Kentucky. That’s two road losses in close, high-scoring games. And remember, Kentucky is the same team Alabama beat by twenty-eight in Lexington back in week two. LSU also beat Texas A&M thirty-five to six, the same A&M team Alabama just beat by eleven. This is going to come down to execution, turnovers and who makes the big play in the fourth quarter.

And there’s another storyline here that Alabama fans know very well. Lane Kiffin used to be on Alabama’s sideline. Then he became a head coach at Florida Atlantic and Ole Miss, and now he’s at LSU, trying to beat the program where he helped win a title. Kiffin knows Alabama. So what are the keys in Baton Rouge?

Key number one. Win the turnover battle. In the loss to Tennessee, Alabama turned it over four times. In the win over A&M, Alabama forced three turnovers and scored a defensive touchdown. That’s the whole story. Protect the football, take it away, and Alabama wins.

Key number two. Get the run game going. Against A&M, Alabama ran for just sixty-eight yards on twenty-eight carries. That’s not going to be enough on the road at LSU. Ryan Grubb wants EJ Crowell touching the ball at least ten times a game. Trae’shawn Brown and Daniel Hill have shown they can break big runs. And if Delgatty or Coleman-Williams come back healthy after the bye, everything about this offense gets easier. Balance is what makes Russell dangerous.

Key number three. Keep the pressure on Sam Leavitt. LSU’s offense can light up the scoreboard when its quarterback has time. Henry, Pierre, Devan Thompkins and Woodson have to make him uncomfortable. Make LSU one-dimensional. That’s been DeBoer’s message all season. And think about how good this front looked against A&M. Henry with two sacks, Woodson with ten tackles, a defense that held the Aggies to under three yards a carry. If that version of Alabama’s front seven shows up in Baton Rouge, Leavitt is going to have a long afternoon.
