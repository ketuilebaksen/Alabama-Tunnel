The College Football Playoff committee has spoken, and Alabama is still number three in the country. Even after all three of its ranked wins lost on the same Saturday, the Crimson Tide didn’t move. But now Kalen DeBoer faces the question that could shape the rest of this season. Does he play a banged-up Keelon Russell against Chattanooga, or does he protect him for the Iron Bowl? And on top of that, Alabama is doing something it doesn’t normally do, holding Senior Day a week early. Tide Nation, there’s a lot going on in Tuscaloosa this week. Let’s break it all down.

What’s up, everybody, James here, and welcome back to Alabama Tunnel. Alabama is eight and two, six and two in the SEC, and it’s Chattanooga week. On paper, this looks like a simple home game against an FCS opponent before the Iron Bowl. But this week is anything but simple. There’s the latest playoff ranking. There’s the quarterback situation and what DeBoer and offensive coordinator Ryan Grubb said on Monday. There’s the debate over whether Alabama can afford to rest its starter. There’s an emotional Senior Day with twenty players being honored. And there’s the matchup itself, against a Chattanooga team that’s having a good season. We’re covering all of it today. If you’re new here, hit that subscribe button and turn on notifications, because we break down everything Crimson Tide right here on Alabama Tunnel. Let’s go.

Let’s start with the big news from Tuesday night: the new College Football Playoff rankings.

Alabama stayed at number three. And that’s a really big deal, because there was real reason to worry heading into Tuesday. Last Saturday, all three of Alabama’s wins over teams ranked in the previous CFP release lost. Kentucky lost. South Carolina lost. And LSU lost to Texas by a single point. A lot of people expected the committee to knock Alabama down a spot or two because of that. Instead, the committee kept the Crimson Tide right where they were.

Here’s how the top of the rankings looks right now. Georgia is number one at nine and one. Utah is number two at ten and oh. Alabama is number three at eight and two. UCLA is number four at ten and oh. Texas is fifth, Notre Dame is sixth, Miami is seventh, Indiana is eighth, Tennessee is ninth, BYU is tenth, Wake Forest is eleventh and Texas Tech is twelfth.

So what does that tell us? It tells us the committee respects Alabama’s schedule and its overall résumé. Yes, Alabama has two losses, to Georgia and at Tennessee. But it has beaten a lot of good teams, it went into Baton Rouge and won by eighteen, and it handled its business at Vanderbilt. Right now, Alabama is in the top four, and that matters, because the top four teams get a first-round bye in the playoff.

And think about the teams around Alabama in the rankings. Utah and UCLA are both undefeated at ten and oh, and the committee still has Alabama ahead of UCLA. Texas, also eight and two, is just two spots behind at number five, and the Longhorns just beat LSU. Tennessee, a team that beat Alabama, is all the way down at number nine. That tells you how much weight the committee is putting on Alabama’s full body of work. It’s not about one loss or one win. It’s about the whole season.

It also tells you there’s very little margin for error. With Texas right behind at number five and so many strong teams packed between six and twelve, one bad result could change everything. Staying in the top four means staying focused, week after week, and that starts on Saturday.

And here’s the big picture. If Alabama wins its last two regular-season games, against Chattanooga and Auburn, it finishes ten and two with one of the best résumés in the country. That’s why this week matters, even against an FCS opponent. Nothing can go wrong. Alabama needs to take care of business, stay healthy, and get ready for the Iron Bowl.

And let’s remember what a first-round bye actually means. In this twelve-team format, the top four teams skip the first round entirely. That’s one fewer playoff game to win, an extra week of rest, and more time to get healthy. For a team that DeBoer says needs health and rhythm more than anything, that extra week could be priceless. Teams ranked five through twelve have to play an extra game just to reach the quarterfinals. So holding on to a top-four spot isn’t just about bragging rights. It’s a real advantage on the road to a national title.

Which brings us to the biggest question of the week: Keelon Russell.

Let’s quickly recap how we got here. Russell hurt his left ankle in the second half of the win at LSU. He had to go to the medical tent, but he came back and finished the game. After that, he was considered day to day. Then came a chaotic week before Vanderbilt. Russell had very limited practice reps. Austin Mack took a lot of the work with the first team. On Friday, ESPN reported that Mack was expected to start. Ninety minutes before kickoff, Russell was still a game-time decision. And then, Russell ran out there and started. DeBoer said after the game that the staff “truly didn’t really know the last twenty-four hours” which direction they would go, and that Russell showed “a lot of improvement” in the last twenty-four to forty-eight hours before the game.

Russell completed twenty-two of thirty-three passes for two hundred seventeen yards and three touchdowns in the twenty-one to six win. He also had a tipped interception and a strip-sack fumble, and he was sacked three times. Not perfect, but on a bad ankle, with almost no practice, he led three straight touchdown drives and got the win.

So what’s the latest? On Monday, DeBoer was asked about Russell again, and he was honest. He said, “The injury wasn’t gone completely, so it’s just matter of managing it, and we’ll see as the week goes on where we’re at.” He also said Russell “got through relatively clean” at Vanderbilt, even though he took a couple of hits. And DeBoer pointed out that there’s always some injury risk any time a player steps on the field, no matter the opponent.

Meanwhile, Ryan Grubb sounded optimistic. Asked about Russell, he said, “Evaluating him after the game obviously, but yeah, he’s doing really good right now.” And then he added two words: “I think status quo.” In other words, the plan right now is for Russell to be Alabama’s quarterback against Chattanooga.

And remember what DeBoer said right after the Vanderbilt game: “If he can go, I’d expect him to go.” So if you’re keeping score at home, the head coach and the offensive coordinator are on the same page. If Russell is healthy enough, he plays.

Now, here’s where it gets interesting. Normally, a game against an FCS opponent the week before your rivalry game is the perfect time to rest a banged-up starter. And DeBoer has a history of handling these games a certain way. In 2024, he started Jalen Milroe against Mercer and got him out by halftime. In 2025, he did something similar with Ty Simpson against Eastern Illinois. Start your guy, build a big lead, and get him on the sideline in a baseball cap before the second half.

But one analyst at Bama Hammer made an interesting argument this week. He said Alabama’s offense hasn’t earned that luxury this year. And the numbers he pointed to are real. During a five-game stretch earlier this season, Alabama averaged about forty-nine and a half points per game. Over its last five SEC games, that number has dropped to twenty-two points per game. Russell has been sacked twenty-seven times this season. Alabama has converted about forty-one percent of its third downs. And the run game has been held under one hundred yards for four straight games.

So the argument goes like this. If Alabama sits Russell completely and starts Austin Mack, there’s no guarantee the offense builds the kind of early lead that lets the starters rest. And if Russell sits, he misses another week of real game reps. Remember, he barely practiced before Vanderbilt, and DeBoer said it took him until the middle of the second quarter to find his groove. If he misses meaningful snaps again this week, he could go into the Iron Bowl rusty.

On the other side, there’s the obvious concern. Every snap Russell takes on that ankle carries some risk. And the Iron Bowl is the game that matters most right now. So it’s a balancing act.

Here’s what I think happens, and it’s the same thing that analyst expects. Russell starts. Alabama tries to score early and build a comfortable lead. And if everything goes to plan, Russell is on the sideline by halftime, and Austin Mack gets meaningful snaps in the second half. That gives Russell rhythm and live reps, and it gives Mack real game experience, which could be really valuable if Alabama ever needs him in a big moment.

And let’s talk about Austin Mack for a second, because he deserves it. Last week, he prepared like a starter. He took most of the practice reps. There were reports he would start. And then he went back to the sideline and supported his teammate. Grubb said Mack handled the situation “like a champ.” And DeBoer said after the Vanderbilt game, “I can’t give him enough credit.” Mack hasn’t had a lot of chances to play in his career, but he’s been a great teammate the whole way. If he gets a chance to play some meaningful snaps on Saturday in front of the home crowd, Tide Nation, let’s give him a big ovation.

Grubb also gave some honest insight into the slow start at Vanderbilt. Alabama went nearly twenty-nine minutes of game time without scoring in the first half. Grubb said his group “didn’t have its fastball” early, and he said the team’s “competitive stamina” was “super low” at the start of that game. That’s an offensive coordinator holding his unit accountable. And Chattanooga week is a perfect opportunity to fix that, to come out sharp from the very first snap and set the tone early.

Defensive coordinator Kane Wommack had a message, too. He said Alabama has to “stay relentless in the details” heading into the postseason. And his defense has been doing exactly that. It has allowed just one touchdown in its last eight quarters. Against Vanderbilt, it held the Commodores to six points, two hundred thirty-five total yards and twelve rushing yards. If the offense can match that defense’s consistency, this team is dangerous.

Now let’s get to the emotional part of the week: Senior Day.

On Saturday, Alabama will honor twenty players before the game against Chattanooga. And here’s the interesting part. Alabama is holding the ceremony a week earlier than usual. Normally, Senior Day happens at the last home game of the season. But DeBoer explained that the team chose this date earlier in the year when setting up the schedule. And he made it clear that Chattanooga “is not our last home game.” That’s right, Tide Nation. The Iron Bowl, on November twenty-eighth, will be played at Bryant-Denny Stadium. So the seniors will get one more home game after their big day.

Here are the twenty players being honored. Linebacker Jah-Marien Latham. Defensive lineman Kedrick Bingley-Jones. Wide receiver MJ Chirgwin. Tight end Peyton Fox. Defensive lineman Fatutoa Henry. Offensive lineman Baker Hickman. Defensive back Bray Hubbard. Linebacker Jake Ivie. Tight end Danny Lewis Jr. Offensive lineman Davis Peterson. Offensive lineman Billy Roby. Long snapper Alex Rozier. Defensive back Keon Sabb. Defensive back Walter Sansing the Third. Offensive lineman Mac Smith. Long snapper Ethan Stangle. Defensive lineman Devan Thompkins. Punter Adam Watford. Linebacker Lane Whisenhunt. And linebacker Caleb Woodson.

That’s a lot of guys who have meant so much to this program, both the stars and the players who’ve worked behind the scenes for years.

Two names on that list really stand out: Bray Hubbard and Caleb Woodson. Both of them are being honored, but both of them will actually have eligibility remaining for 2027 under the NCAA’s new five-in-five eligibility rules. So this isn’t necessarily goodbye. But Alabama is recognizing what they’ve meant to this program.

Bray Hubbard has been a cornerstone of the Alabama secondary for years. He’s a veteran leader, a smart player and a big reason this defense has been so good in the back end.

And Caleb Woodson has been a perfect fit since transferring in from Virginia Tech. He’s Alabama’s leading tackler this season. And remember his performance in the SEC opener at Kentucky? Twelve tackles and two sacks, one of the reasons Alabama came back and won that game on the road. He’s been the quarterback of this defense all season.

And there are other big names on that list, too. Fatutoa Henry and Devan Thompkins have been key pieces of the defensive line. Thompkins also had two sacks in that win at Kentucky. Keon Sabb has been an important part of the secondary. And Danny Lewis Jr. is getting his big opportunity right now at tight end, with Josh Ford out for the rest of the season. What a moment it would be for Danny Lewis to make a big play on his Senior Day.

There’s also an interesting detail about who is not on the list. Several fourth-year players who could have been honored are not part of the ceremony, including Yhonzae Pierre, Terrance Green, Racin Delgatty and Jayvin James. And for Alabama fans, that’s a good sign, because under the new eligibility rules, those players could be part of this program again next season. Pierre has been one of the most disruptive pass rushers on this team, and Green, Delgatty and James are all important pieces up front. Keeping that kind of experience around would be a big boost for 2027.

And think about what this senior class has been through. Many of these players arrived during a time of big change in Tuscaloosa. They’ve played for new coaches, they’ve adjusted to a new playoff format, and they’ve helped keep Alabama in the national title conversation. Some of them have been starters for years. Others have been special teams contributors or scout team players who pushed the starters every day in practice. Every one of them has a role in what this team has become. That’s what Senior Day is all about.

So, Tide Nation, if you’re going to Bryant-Denny on Saturday, get there early. These seniors deserve a huge ovation. They’ve given everything to this program.

Now, let’s talk about the opponent, because Chattanooga deserves respect.

The Mocs come into this game at seven and four, and six and two in the Southern Conference. That’s a solid season. Grubb described Chattanooga as a well-coached team with one of the top defenses at the FCS level. So this isn’t a team that’s just going to roll over. The Mocs are led by senior quarterback Matthew Clemmer. FCS teams often treat a trip to Tuscaloosa like the biggest game of their lives, and Alabama has to be ready for Chattanooga’s best shot. Alabama has to come out focused, and that’s especially true for a team that has struggled with slow starts.

Historically, though, this series has been all Alabama. This will be the sixteenth all-time meeting between these two programs, and Alabama has won all fifteen of the previous games. The first meeting was way back in 1908, a twenty-three to six Alabama win. And the most recent meeting was in 2023, when Alabama won sixty-six to ten. And at home, DeBoer has been very good. He’s seventeen and two at Bryant-Denny Stadium as Alabama’s head coach.

Here are the game details. Kickoff is at one p.m. Central time, two p.m. Eastern, on Saturday at Bryant-Denny Stadium. The game will be on SEC Network Plus, and you can stream it on fuboTV. Clay Matvick will have the play-by-play, Rocky Boiman will be the analyst, and Laura Stickells will report from the sideline. And if you’re heading to the stadium, the forecast calls for clouds with a chance of rain, mostly in the morning, and temperatures in the sixties.

Now let’s talk about the injury report, which ties right back to what DeBoer said about health being the key to this final run. Alabama has listed seven players as out for Saturday: linebacker Cayden Jones, defensive back Nick Sherman, defensive lineman Jeremiah Beaman, linebacker Desmond Umeozulu, defensive lineman Mhari Johnson, tight end Josh Ford and offensive lineman Casey Poe. We wish all of those guys a speedy recovery. And especially Josh Ford, who is out for the year but continues to lead this team from the sideline.

But here’s the good news. DeBoer said after the Vanderbilt game that he believes Alabama will “continue to get guys back here these last couple weeks.” And one of the best ways to get healthy is a game like this one, where the starters can do their jobs, build a lead, and then get some rest while the younger players get valuable experience.

So, what does Alabama need to see on Saturday? Let me give you my keys to the game.

Key number one: a fast start. After that slow first half at Vanderbilt, Alabama needs to come out and set the tone early. Score on the first couple of drives and take control.

Key number two: protect the quarterback. Whether it’s Russell or Mack, Alabama can’t let the quarterback take unnecessary hits. That means clean pass protection and getting the ball out quickly.

Key number three: get the run game going. Daniel Hill had his best yards-per-carry game of the season at Vanderbilt, with eighty-nine yards on fourteen carries. And here’s a fun fact: Saturday is his twenty-first birthday. A big day for Hill, and for the rest of the running backs, would give this offense a lot of confidence heading into the Iron Bowl. Breaking that streak of four games under one hundred rushing yards would be huge.

Key number four: force some takeaways. DeBoer was frustrated that Alabama didn’t get any against Vanderbilt. This defense has been great, but adding turnovers to the mix would make it even better.

And key number five: stay healthy and get the backups some reps. If Alabama takes care of business early, the young players and the backups get valuable experience, and the starters are fresher for Auburn. Games like this are where the future of the program gets a chance to show what it can do. A young receiver, a backup linebacker or a freshman on the offensive line could get real snaps in front of a big crowd, and those reps can pay off not only in the postseason but in seasons to come.

And then, of course, the big one. One week from Saturday, on November twenty-eighth, Alabama hosts Auburn in the Iron Bowl at Bryant-Denny Stadium. That’s the game everybody is already thinking about. And everything Alabama does this week, from how it handles Russell’s ankle to how it manages the snaps, is about being as ready as possible for that game.

So let me sum it up. The College Football Playoff committee kept Alabama at number three, even after its ranked wins lost. Keelon Russell is still managing his ankle, but DeBoer and Grubb are both signaling that he’ll play if he’s healthy enough. The most likely scenario is that he starts, Alabama builds an early lead, and he gets an early rest. Austin Mack could get some meaningful snaps. Alabama will honor twenty seniors on Saturday, including Bray Hubbard and Caleb Woodson, who will still have eligibility remaining. And the Crimson Tide face a solid Chattanooga team in what should be the perfect tune-up for the Iron Bowl.

Put it all together, Tide Nation, and what you see is a coaching staff that knows exactly what’s at stake, managing its quarterback carefully and making sure this team is ready for the biggest game of the regular season.
