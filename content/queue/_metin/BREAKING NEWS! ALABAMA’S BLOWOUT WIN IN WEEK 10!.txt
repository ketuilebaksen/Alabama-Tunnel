And four times, Alabama has come back and won. Tide Nation, the thirty-one-to-thirteen blowout at LSU wasn’t a fluke. It was a pattern. This team is built to finish, and today I’m going to show you exactly why Alabama might be the best second-half team in college football. Let’s go.

Alabama walked into Tiger Stadium, fell behind, and then completely took over, scoring twenty-one unanswered points to beat number fourteen LSU thirty-one to thirteen. Let’s start with the stat that jumped out at me the most this weekend.

According to one national broadcast, the LSU game was Alabama’s fourth come-from-behind win in the second half this season, the most of any Power Four team. Think about that. Four times, Alabama has been behind after halftime, and four times it has found a way to win. Some teams fold when they fall behind. This team gets better.

So let’s go back and look at all four, because when you put them side by side, you see exactly what kind of team Kalen DeBoer has built.

Comeback number one: Kentucky, in Lexington. Alabama went into halftime trailing seventeen to thirteen on the road. A lot of people were nervous. And then the Tide came out of the locker room and scored thirty-two unanswered points in the second half to win forty-five to seventeen. That’s not a comeback. That’s a takeover. And Kane Wommack’s defense was so dominant in that second half that fans were going crazy on social media about him. That win over Kentucky looks even bigger now, because Kentucky came into last weekend ranked sixth in the country, and Alabama holds that head-to-head win.

Comeback number two: Florida State. This one was wild. Lightning delayed the kickoff. Lightning interrupted the second quarter. The teams even agreed to skip halftime because of the delays. And at one point, Alabama was down twenty-one to six. But the offense caught fire. Ryan Coleman-Williams caught a forty-six-yard touchdown. Keelon Russell hit Kaleb Edwards for a sixteen-yard score with twenty-two seconds left before the break to take the lead. Florida State fought back and retook the lead in the third quarter. And then Alabama answered again. EJ Crowell ran it in from four yards out, and Russell broke loose on a fifty-seven-yard touchdown scramble. Trae’shawn Brown iced it with a twenty-yard touchdown run with two minutes and fifteen seconds left. Final score, fifty to thirty-six. Alabama scored touchdowns on five straight possessions during that comeback and piled up six hundred fifteen total yards. Russell went twenty-three of thirty for three hundred twenty yards, three touchdown passes, a rushing touchdown and no interceptions.

Comeback number three: Texas A&M. This one came after back-to-back losses to Georgia and Tennessee, when the outside noise was at its loudest. Alabama went into the fourth quarter trailing twenty to seventeen. The season felt like it was on the line. And Alabama found a way, outscoring the Aggies fourteen to nothing in the fourth quarter to win thirty-one to twenty. Russell threw two touchdown passes, and Rico Scott had his breakout game. It wasn’t pretty, but it showed this team’s heart.

And comeback number four: LSU, in Death Valley. LSU took a thirteen-to-ten lead with two minutes and forty-four seconds left in the third quarter. Russell had just come back from the medical tent with a taped-up ankle. One hundred two thousand fans were going nuts. And Alabama responded with twenty-one straight points. A fifty-seven-yard catch-and-run by Rico Scott. A touchdown to Josh Ford. A thirty-two-yard punt return by Coleman-Williams. A touchdown to freshman Cederian Morgan. And a touchdown to Kaleb Edwards. Ballgame.

Four comebacks. Four different ways. Against four very different opponents. That’s not luck. That’s identity.

One came on the road in a hostile SEC stadium. One came in a lightning-delayed shootout. One came with the season on the line after two straight losses. And one came in Death Valley with the starting quarterback limping. If you were trying to design a test of a team’s toughness, you couldn’t do much better than those four games. And Alabama passed every single one.

And here’s something else that stood out. All three of Russell’s touchdown passes against LSU came in the second half. That’s a quarterback who gets better as the game goes on. And in the fourth quarter of the LSU game, Alabama’s defense didn’t allow a single point. In the fourth quarter of the A&M game, the defense didn’t allow a single point. In the second half at Kentucky, the defense didn’t allow a single point. Do you see the pattern? When it matters most, this defense shuts the door.

So why does this keep happening? What’s the secret?

I think DeBoer explained it perfectly after the LSU game. He said, “There’s a lot of football left,” and “it’s all about just being in the moment and staying focused on the next play.” He said, “We’ve had a number of situations now in the second half where you just stay the course.” And then he explained what happens next. He said, “You go back to the training you have, the toughness, the resiliency, and as long as your mind can stay in the right spot and you stay focused on the right things, there’s a physicality that I think our team still has, and a presence that we get in these SEC games, that takes over.”

That’s it. That’s the formula. Stay calm. Stay the course. And let the physicality take over in the second half. And it’s not just talk. You can see it on the field. Alabama’s defensive line wears teams down. The pass rush gets stronger as the game goes on. And the offense gets more efficient after halftime as the coaches make adjustments. Ryan Grubb and Kane Wommack deserve a ton of credit for that.

Now, let’s talk about what the national experts are saying about the LSU win, because the reaction has been really interesting.

One analyst pointed out that “numbers in this game were misleading.” LSU outgained Alabama. LSU was better on third down. According to the broadcast, LSU had twice as many first downs and dominated time of possession. “Yet,” he said, “it’s the Tide who absolutely dominated this game.”

So how? Another analyst said the story of the game was “Alabama in the trenches,” especially up front on defense. He said, “I thought Alabama’s defense really came to play in this game.” And he made a really interesting point about motivation. He said the whole Alabama building “probably heard a lot of what people on desks like this were saying about them being ranked number five” in the College Football Playoff rankings, and that they “didn’t deserve it.” And then, he said, “they went out and put out a tremendous performance.” And I love this line. He said, “This Bama team sometimes a little bit up and down this season. Well, if this is what up looks like, we’ll take it.”

Another analyst praised Alabama’s complementary football. He said the coaches “didn’t put too much on the shoulders of a young Keelon Russell.” He said the message was basically, “You don’t have to be Superman for us. We’re going to play great on the defensive side.” And he made a great point: “The scoreboard doesn’t reward yards. They reward getting into the end zone.” He said Alabama “did a fantastic job getting pressure, disguising certain blitzes and throwing different things” at Sam Leavitt, rattling him at key moments. And he said when Alabama needed to make plays on defense, it did.

And then another analyst nailed the biggest difference in the game: situational football. He said, “There’s a reason coaches emphasize situational football like they do.” He pointed out the red zone numbers. Alabama had three trips inside the LSU twenty and came away with twenty-one points. LSU got down there four times and came away with just ten points. And on top of that, there was Luke Metz’s interception near the red zone fringe late in the first half, taking away what could have been more LSU points. He summed it up: “Those situational football really was the difference between winning and losing today.”

I couldn’t agree more. Let’s look at those situational numbers more closely, because they’re incredible.

Red zone: Alabama three for three, all touchdowns. LSU four trips, one touchdown, ten points.

Fourth down: LSU zero for four. Alabama’s defense stopped every single attempt, including a fourth and four at the Alabama nine-yard line in the second quarter.

Third down: LSU went five for fourteen. So even though LSU moved the ball between the twenties, Alabama got off the field when it had to.

Turnovers: Alabama didn’t turn the ball over once. And that’s a huge deal, because in its previous three road games, Alabama had been averaging three turnovers per game. This was the first time all season Alabama played a turnover-free road game.

Pass rush: Alabama sacked Leavitt four times. Devan Thompkins had two sacks, Yhonzae Pierre had one, and Terrance Green had one in his first game back from injury.

Protection: LSU came into the game ranked second in the country in sacks. It sacked Keelon Russell only twice, with just three tackles for loss in the entire game.

That’s how you win a game when you get outgained. You win every situation that matters.

And Russell understood the assignment. He said, “We can’t take sacks, we can’t have negative plays, and we can’t turn the ball over going into a hostile environment.” That was the plan. And he executed it perfectly. Seventeen of twenty-four for two hundred six yards, three touchdowns, no interceptions and twenty-five yards rushing. On a bad ankle. That’s a sophomore quarterback playing with the poise of a veteran.

And think about how far Russell has come since that Florida State game back in September. That night, he put up huge numbers and led a wild comeback, but after the game he was honest about what still needed work. He said, “We got too many mental mistakes right now, but we’re going to fix it.” Fast forward to November, and look at him now. No interceptions in Death Valley. Throwing the ball away instead of forcing it. Taking care of the football against the second-best pass rush in the country. Managing the game, and then delivering three touchdown passes when the game was on the line. That’s growth. That’s a young quarterback who listened to his coaches, learned from every rep, and got better week after week. DeBoer said after the LSU win, “I thought he just did a great job of staying focused and locked in.” And he added that Russell “obviously got the tools to go make the plays.” The scary thing for the rest of college football? Russell is only a sophomore, and he’s still improving. Every comeback this season has made him more comfortable in big moments. By the time the playoff comes around, he could be one of the most dangerous quarterbacks in the field when his team is trailing.

Speaking of that ankle, here’s the latest. Russell said after the game, “And through the grace of God, it wasn’t a big injury.” He said, “I was able to step back on the field and just able to give it my all.” DeBoer said, “He was not getting pulled off the football field,” and called him tough, saying, “I’ve seen it before with him.” Right now, there’s no sign that Russell will miss time, but we’ll keep an eye on it this week. Get well soon, Keelon.
