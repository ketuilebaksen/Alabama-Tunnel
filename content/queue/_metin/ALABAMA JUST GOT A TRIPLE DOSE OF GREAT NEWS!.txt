Great news number one. The College Football Playoff rankings.

On Tuesday night, the committee released its first rankings of the 2026 season, and here’s the top twelve. Number one, Miami, eight and oh. Number two, Georgia, seven and one. Number three, Utah, eight and oh. Number four, Indiana, eight and one. And number five, Alabama, six and two. Then Kentucky at six, Texas at seven, UCLA at eight, BYU at nine, Ole Miss at ten, Notre Dame at eleven and Ohio State at twelve. Tennessee came in at thirteen, and LSU, Alabama’s opponent this Saturday, is fourteenth.

Let that sink in for a second. Alabama is ranked ahead of Texas, ahead of Ohio State, ahead of Notre Dame, ahead of Ole Miss, and ahead of every other two-loss team in the country. In both the AP poll and the Coaches poll, Alabama is ninth. But the committee, the people who actually decide who makes the playoff, put the Tide four spots higher.

And here’s the part that really matters. In the projected twelve-team bracket based on these rankings, Alabama would host Boise State in the first round. A home playoff game at Bryant-Denny Stadium in December. That’s what’s on the table right now. And let me explain why that matters so much. In the twelve-team playoff, the four highest-ranked teams get a first-round bye, and the teams seeded five through eight host first-round games on their own campuses. So right now, Alabama is sitting in that first hosting spot, just one place outside of a bye. If Alabama keeps winning and a team or two ahead of it slips, the Tide could even work its way into the top four. That’s how much is still on the line over the next four weeks. And just think about Bryant-Denny in December for a playoff game. That would be an unbelievable atmosphere.

So why did the committee like Alabama so much? It’s the résumé. Look at who Alabama has beaten. Remember that road win in Lexington in week two? Alabama beat Kentucky forty-five to seventeen. Well, Kentucky is now ranked sixth in the country at seven and one. That’s a top-ten road win by twenty-eight points. Alabama also beat South Carolina forty-nine to eighteen, and the Gamecocks are ranked twenty-first. Add a fifty-to-thirty-six comeback win over Florida State, a thirty-three-point road win at Mississippi State, and the fourth-quarter comeback against Texas A&M, and you can see why the committee was impressed. And think about how that Kentucky win looks now. At the time, some people shrugged it off. Kentucky? Big deal. Well, Kentucky is now seven and one, and one of those wins came against LSU in overtime. The Wildcats have turned into one of the best teams in the SEC, and Alabama went into their building and beat them by four touchdowns. So when the committee sat down and compared résumés, that game in Lexington suddenly looked like one of the best wins anybody in the country has. That’s why you never take a road win in the SEC for granted. Games that seem ordinary in September can look huge by November.

And the losses? Alabama lost to Georgia, the number two team in the country, by three points. And it lost on the road to a Tennessee team that’s ranked thirteenth. Those are quality losses. The committee clearly looked at the full body of work, and not just the last few weeks. And look at the two-loss teams Alabama is ranked ahead of. Texas at seven. Ole Miss at ten. Ohio State at twelve. Those are all programs with big-name wins and plenty of talent. The committee still put Alabama above all of them. And the four teams ahead of Alabama? Miami and Utah are undefeated. Georgia and Indiana each have one loss. So Alabama is the best of the rest, and it’s right there knocking on the door.

Win at LSU, and Alabama will likely be in an even stronger position when the next rankings come out.

Win on Saturday, take care of business against Vanderbilt and Chattanooga, win the Iron Bowl, and a ten-and-two Alabama team with wins over Kentucky, LSU and South Carolina is going to be in great shape.

Great news number two. The injured players are coming back.

We talked about this on Monday when DeBoer said that Ryan Coleman-Williams, Racin Delgatty, Zabien Brown, EJ Crowell and Terrance Green were all on the field working out. But on Tuesday, when the players spoke to the media, we got even more encouraging signs.

Let’s start with Terrance Green. Yhonzae Pierre was asked about the defense, and he said, “We got TG coming back, Terrence Green, so we’ve been working on schemes for the defense and for them.” And he added, “It’s going to be amazing to see what we do Saturday.” That’s a big deal. Green is the starting nose tackle. He’s been dealing with a lower-body injury for several weeks and missed the last two games after getting hurt against Georgia. ESPN described him as a transfer who has been key against the run. And against an LSU offense that wants to keep defenses off balance, having your starting nose tackle back in the middle is huge. A nose tackle doesn’t always show up on the stat sheet, but he’s the guy who eats up double teams, keeps blockers off the linebackers and makes it hard to run up the middle. When Green was out, Alabama had to shuffle the interior of the defensive line, and guys like Justin Hill, who DeBoer said is also back now, become even more valuable. Getting both of them healthy gives Freddie Roach a real rotation up front again. It frees up the linebackers. It keeps the interior strong. And it lets Pierre, Fatutoa Henry and Devan Thompkins attack Sam Leavitt with more freedom.

Now, Ryan Coleman-Williams. Rico Scott was asked about his teammate, and he said, “He’s done a good job of being vocal, even when he wasn’t playing, just letting his presence be felt.” And here’s the detail a lot of people noticed. In Tuesday’s practice video, Coleman-Williams was seen without a knee brace. Now, I want to be careful here. A practice video is not an official announcement. It hasn’t been confirmed that he’ll play on Saturday. But after arthroscopic knee surgery and two missed games, seeing him out there moving without a brace is an encouraging sign.

And remember who we’re talking about. Coleman-Williams is a former All-American. Before he got hurt against Georgia, he led this team in receptions and receiving touchdowns. He had twenty-three catches for four hundred fifteen yards and four touchdowns. That’s eighteen yards per catch. And back on October twenty-sixth, DeBoer said, “I’m hopeful that he’ll be ready for the LSU game.” Every sign right now is pointing in the right direction.

And then there’s the rest of the group. Zabien Brown, a preseason All-American cornerback, has been working his way back from a lower-body injury he suffered against Tennessee. Racin Delgatty, the starting center, has missed two games with a knee injury, while Mal Waldrep has filled in. And EJ Crowell, the former five-star running back, has been working back from a lower-body injury too. Crowell has four rushing touchdowns this season and he’s averaging six point eight yards per carry. That’s explosive. DeBoer said some of these guys are taking team reps and some are taking non-contact reps, and that a lot of it depends on “how their bodies respond after they’re out there being pushed a little bit more.”

Before we talk about when we’ll know for sure, let’s talk about why Delgatty and Crowell matter so much for this offense. In the first six games, Alabama averaged more than one hundred eighty rushing yards a game. In the last two, with Delgatty out at center, the Tide ran for just fifty-four yards at Tennessee and sixty-eight against A&M. And Crowell missed the A&M game too. Ryan Grubb has said he wants Crowell touching the ball at least ten times a game, and with six point eight yards per carry, you can see why. Get those two back, and suddenly the run game has its leader up front and its most explosive back in the backfield. That changes everything for Russell, because LSU can’t sit back in coverage and dare Alabama to run.

Against an offense like LSU’s, that communication is everything. One missed assignment, one blown coverage, and Jackson Harris is running free down the sideline. If Alabama’s secondary can disguise its coverages, Leavitt is going to have to make tough reads all afternoon. And that’s where turnovers happen.

Great news number three. DeBoer’s message to Keelon Russell.

This one matters a lot, because the quarterback is always going to be the story in a game like this. Russell is a sophomore, and Saturday will be one of the toughest environments he’s ever played in. So what did DeBoer tell him?

DeBoer’s message was about experience. He said, “I think it’s just learning from what you’ve been through.” He added, “It’s not just a road environment that we’ve been in. It’s all the experiences that you’ve been through to this point in the season.” And then came the line that should give every Alabama fan confidence: “That will come to him because he’s that type of player.” Earlier this season, DeBoer also talked about how a young quarterback needs a clock in his head that’s connected to his feet. Knowing when to take the easy completion. Knowing when to work through his progressions. Knowing when to use his legs to move the chains. That’s the next step for Russell, and DeBoer believes he’s ready to take it.

That’s a head coach who believes in his quarterback. Not just hoping. Believing. And according to reports from Tuscaloosa, DeBoer’s approach with Russell this week has been about reading down and distance, adjusting to how each game shifts, staying steady, and getting the ball out quickly. It’s about finding the balance between not forcing too much and still making the big plays when they’re there.

And think about everything Russell has already been through this season. In week two at Kentucky, he turned the ball over on each of his first two possessions, including a pick-six on the very first drive. That could have rattled a young quarterback. Instead, Alabama outscored Kentucky thirty-two to nothing after halftime and won by twenty-eight, and Kentucky is now a top-ten team. Against Florida State, Alabama fell behind twenty-one to six, and Russell led a comeback, throwing for three hundred twenty yards and three touchdowns and running for a fifty-seven-yard score. Then came the tough stretch. A three-point loss to Georgia, the number two team in the country. A tough day at Tennessee. Russell struggled in those games, and he’d be the first to tell you that. But then he bounced back against Texas A&M. Three hundred eleven passing yards. Three touchdown passes. And a perfect five for five for sixty-eight yards and two touchdowns in the fourth quarter, when the season was on the line.

That’s what DeBoer means by experience. Russell has seen the highs and the lows. He’s faced adversity on the road, adversity at home, and adversity in the fourth quarter. And every one of those experiences prepares him for Saturday. He’s thrown at least fifteen touchdown passes through eight games. He’s one of the most productive young quarterbacks in the country. And now he gets to show what he’s learned in front of a hundred thousand screaming fans in Death Valley.

Now, I’ll be honest about the challenge. LSU’s pass defense is very good. It ranks third in the SEC, and according to reports, it has allowed just four passing touchdowns all season while intercepting seven passes. Four touchdowns allowed. That’s elite. So Russell is not going to be able to force things into tight windows. He’ll need to be patient, take what the defense gives him, and protect the football. That’s exactly why DeBoer’s message about staying steady and getting the ball out is so important. Reading down and distance is a big part of that. On first down, take the easy completion and stay ahead of the chains. On third and long, don’t force a throw into double coverage. Check it down, punt if you have to, and trust your defense. Some of the best quarterback performances on the road are the ones where the quarterback doesn’t do anything flashy, he just doesn’t make the mistake that flips the game. If Russell plays that kind of game and hits one or two big shots when they’re there, Alabama is going to be in great shape.

And this is where great news number two connects to great news number three. If Coleman-Williams is back, Russell has his best weapon to throw to. So what are my keys?

Key number one. Protect the football. LSU’s defense has seven interceptions. Russell needs to be smart, take the checkdowns, and live to fight another down. And on the other side, Alabama’s defense needs to take the ball away, just like it did with three takeaways against A&M.

Key number two. Disguise and pressure. Bray Hubbard said it best. Don’t let Kiffin read your coverage from the alignment. Make Leavitt think. And with Terrance Green back in the middle, Pierre, Henry and Thompkins can get after him.

Key number three. Win the line of scrimmage on offense. Without Sanders, the offensive line will be patched together again. But if Delgatty is back at center, that’s a massive boost. Alabama needs to be able to run the ball enough to keep LSU honest. If Crowell is healthy, give him the ball.

Key number four. Start fast and handle the crowd. Rico Scott said the fans are “a little nasty.” The best way to quiet them is an early touchdown. If Alabama scores first, the pressure shifts to LSU.

Key number five. Finish in the fourth quarter. Alabama outgained Texas A&M one hundred two yards to fifteen in the fourth quarter. That’s the identity this team has to bring to Baton Rouge. Close games are won by the team that’s tougher and more disciplined late.
