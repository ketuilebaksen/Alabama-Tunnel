Keelon Russell is getting healthier. Alabama is still sitting at number three in the College Football Playoff rankings. And one of the top running back targets in the country is coming to Tuscaloosa for the Iron Bowl, with a commitment date set just two days later. Tide Nation, that’s a triple dose of good news for the Crimson Tide. But there was also a surprise this week. Kalen DeBoer confirmed that running back AK Dear is no longer with the program. So what does it all mean for Alabama’s backfield, now and in the future? Let’s break it down.

What’s up, everybody, James here, and welcome back to Alabama Tunnel. Alabama is eight and two, six and two in the SEC, and it’s a big week in Tuscaloosa. On Saturday, the Crimson Tide host Chattanooga on Senior Day. The Iron Bowl is one week later. And there’s been a lot of news this week, some of it great, and some of it surprising. Today we’re going to go through all of it: the three pieces of good news, the AK Dear update, the state of the running back room, and the recruiting plan that could reshape Alabama’s backfield for years to come. If you’re new here, hit that subscribe button and turn on notifications, because we bring you every Alabama update right here on Alabama Tunnel. Let’s go.

Let’s start with the good news, because there’s plenty of it.

Good news number one: Keelon Russell continues to improve.

This is the one every Alabama fan wanted to hear. Russell has been playing through a left ankle injury since the second half of the win at LSU. He barely practiced before the Vanderbilt game, was a game-time decision ninety minutes before kickoff, and then started and threw three touchdown passes in a twenty-one to six win.

On his radio show Wednesday night, DeBoer gave the latest update. He said, “He continues to improve.” He said, “There was no setbacks or anything on Saturday.” He was honest that “there’s still work to be done to continue to get him to one hundred percent.” But he praised the way Russell has handled it, saying, “He’s done a great job, just being a pro just like the rest of our guys do,” and that Russell is “working in the training room every single day.”

No setbacks. Steady improvement. A quarterback who’s doing everything right in the training room. That’s exactly the trend Alabama wants heading into Chattanooga and the Iron Bowl. And earlier this week, offensive coordinator Ryan Grubb said Russell is “doing really good right now.” The arrow is pointing up.

Good news number two: Alabama stayed at number three in the College Football Playoff rankings.

There was a real reason to worry heading into Tuesday night. Last Saturday, all three of Alabama’s wins over teams that had been ranked in the previous CFP release, Kentucky, South Carolina and LSU, lost. A lot of people thought that would drag down Alabama’s résumé. But the committee didn’t move the Crimson Tide at all. Georgia is number one, Utah is number two, Alabama is number three and UCLA is number four. Texas sits at number five.

That top-four spot matters a lot. The top four teams get a first-round bye, which means an extra week of rest and one fewer game to win on the road to a national title. And for a team that’s trying to get healthy, an extra week could be huge. The committee clearly respects what Alabama has done this season, and if the Tide take care of business against Chattanooga and Auburn, they’ll be in a great position.

Good news number three: one of Alabama’s top running back targets is coming to the Iron Bowl, and he’s set a commitment date.

His name is Tranard Roberts. He’s a running back in the 2027 class from The Villages High School in Florida. He’s five-foot-eleven and about one hundred ninety-five pounds. According to reports this week, Roberts is set to visit Alabama for the Iron Bowl, and he’s planning to announce his commitment on November thirtieth, just two days after the game. His finalists are Alabama, Arizona State, Georgia, Miami, Ohio State and North Carolina.

Think about that, Tide Nation. A top running back target is going to be inside Bryant-Denny Stadium for the biggest game of the regular season, with one hundred thousand fans going crazy, and then he makes his decision two days later. You can’t ask for a better recruiting setting than that. A big Iron Bowl win could make a huge impression.

And here’s why Roberts would be such a great fit. Alabama’s offense under Ryan Grubb loves running backs who can do more than just run between the tackles. They need to catch passes out of the backfield, pick up blitzes and create big plays in space. That’s the kind of versatile back the staff has been targeting in this class. And in a program where every running back gets real opportunities in the rotation, a talented freshman can earn snaps right away. Just look at Trae’shawn Brown and EJ Crowell this year. Both of them are true freshmen, and both of them have already found the end zone multiple times. That’s a powerful recruiting pitch: come to Alabama, and you’ll get a chance to play early.

And Alabama already has two running backs committed in the 2027 class. The first was four-star Nigel Newkirk from Gainesville, Georgia. And the second is Tai Phillips from Irmo High School in Columbia, South Carolina. Phillips committed back in June, choosing Alabama over offers from programs like Ohio State, Florida State, Missouri and North Carolina. He’s been very productive in high school. As a sophomore in 2024, he ran for eight hundred eighty yards and eleven touchdowns on just eighty-six carries. That’s more than ten yards per carry. And as a junior in 2025, he ran for nine hundred nineteen yards and twelve touchdowns on one hundred sixteen carries. He’s a big-play threat every time he touches the ball. Alabama offered him in June of last year, he made an unofficial visit to Tuscaloosa last November, and then he committed in June. That’s a recruitment where Alabama stayed consistent and built a real relationship, and it paid off. So the future of this backfield is already taking shape, and Roberts could be the piece that ties it all together.

Now, let’s talk about the surprise of the week: AK Dear.

During the SEC coaches’ teleconference on Wednesday, DeBoer was asked about Dear, and his answer was short and direct. He said, “AK is a great kid, great player. He’s just not with our program anymore.”

That’s all he said, and I think that’s the right way to handle it. DeBoer didn’t go into any details, and we’re not going to speculate here either. When a player leaves a program, there can be a lot of personal reasons behind it, and those reasons belong to the player and his family. What matters is that DeBoer spoke well of him. “A great kid, great player.” That says a lot.

Let’s remember who AK Dear is, because he came to Alabama with a lot of promise. He’s from Quitman, Mississippi, and he was one of the top running backs in the 2025 recruiting class, ranked as the number two running back in the country. He had been committed to Ole Miss for several months before choosing Alabama.

As a true freshman last year, Dear played in five games and ran for one hundred forty yards and three touchdowns on just nineteen carries. That’s more than seven yards per carry. His best game came against Eastern Illinois, when he scored two touchdowns. He also caught three passes.

This season, though, things didn’t go the way he hoped. He suffered an injury during a fall scrimmage in mid-August and missed the start of the season. And when he got healthy, he had a hard time getting on the field in a crowded running back room. He played only a few snaps this season, with two carries for six yards. He was ruled out for the Vanderbilt game. And now, he’s no longer with the team.

Tide Nation, let’s wish AK Dear the very best, wherever his journey takes him next. He’s a talented young player, and he’s got a lot of football ahead of him. We hope he finds a great situation and has a lot of success.

Now, let’s talk about what this means for Alabama’s running back room right now, heading into Chattanooga and the Iron Bowl.

First things first. On the field, this doesn’t change much in the short term. Dear had played only a handful of snaps this season, and he wasn’t part of the regular rotation. So Alabama’s game plan for the final two regular-season games shouldn’t change at all.

Here’s who Alabama has right now.

Daniel Hill leads the team in rushing with ninety carries for four hundred thirty-six yards and four touchdowns. And he’s coming off his best yards-per-carry game of the season, with eighty-nine yards on fourteen carries at Vanderbilt, six point four yards per carry. DeBoer said after that game, “Thought Daniel ran really hard, did a nice job there. That was important for us.” Hill is the leader of this group, the most experienced back in the room, and a strong pass protector. And on Saturday, he turns twenty-one years old.

Then there’s freshman Trae’shawn Brown. Brown was one of the big stories of the early season. He scored a touchdown in each of Alabama’s first three games. He had sixty-six yards and two touchdowns against Mississippi State. And he ran for ninety yards on nine carries against Georgia. His production has slowed down over the last few weeks, like the rest of the run game, but he’s already shown what he can do as a true freshman, and he’s tied for the team lead in rushing touchdowns.

Then there’s freshman EJ Crowell. He came to Alabama as one of the top running back recruits in the country, and when he’s been healthy, he’s been explosive. He’s dealt with lingering injuries going back to the spring, but he’s still scored four touchdowns this season, tied with Hill. If Crowell can get healthy for the final stretch, he gives this offense a different kind of speed.

And Kevin Riley has also seen some opportunities this season and remains part of the group.

And here’s a fun stat. Trae’shawn Brown shares the team lead in rushing touchdowns with none other than Keelon Russell. That tells you two things. First, Russell has been a real threat with his legs this season, which makes it even more important to protect that ankle right now. And second, Alabama spreads the ball around. Three different running backs have scored rushing touchdowns this season, and that’s by design.

DeBoer and Grubb have used a rotation at running back all year. Nobody is asked to carry the ball twenty-five times every Saturday. Instead, different backs bring different strengths. Hill brings power, experience and pass protection. Brown brings vision and a nose for the end zone. Crowell brings speed and explosiveness. A rotation like that keeps legs fresh late in the season, and it keeps defenses guessing. Of course, the downside is that it can be hard for any one back to get into a rhythm, and that’s part of what Alabama has been trying to solve. But in November and December, having fresh legs can make a big difference.

So Alabama still has real talent in this backfield. A veteran leader in Hill, two talented freshmen in Brown and Crowell, and depth behind them.

But let’s be honest about the challenge too, because that’s what we do here on Alabama Tunnel. The run game has been the biggest question mark on this team. Alabama ranks eighty-eighth in the country at four point one yards per carry as a team, and that number includes sack yardage, which counts against rushing totals in college football. The running backs themselves are averaging four point nine yards per carry. And the team has been held under one hundred rushing yards in four straight games. So depth matters, and with Dear gone and Crowell banged up, Alabama has to stay healthy at running back. Any injury in the next couple of weeks would make things tight.

And this connects directly to what DeBoer said after the Vanderbilt game. He said, “Get guys healthier is going to be the key as we make this final run.” He talked about “health and rhythm,” and how, when guys aren’t getting reps in practice because of injuries, the team isn’t always “synced up and executing at the highest level.” But he also said, “Fortunately, I think we’re going to continue to get guys back here these last couple weeks.” For the running back room, the biggest piece of that puzzle is EJ Crowell. A healthy Crowell, alongside Hill and Brown, would give Alabama three backs with very different skill sets for the Iron Bowl and the postseason. That’s the best-case scenario, and it’s very much within reach.

That’s why I think the next two games are so important for this group. Against Chattanooga, Alabama has a great opportunity to get the running game going. Hill can build on his Vanderbilt performance, Brown can get his rhythm back, and if Crowell is healthy, he could get some valuable carries. A big day on the ground would give this offense a lot of confidence heading into the Iron Bowl.

And remember why the run game matters so much right now. Keelon Russell is playing through an ankle injury. Every time Alabama can run the ball effectively, it takes pressure off its young quarterback. He doesn’t have to drop back as often. He doesn’t have to take as many hits. Grubb has already said he wants Russell to “get the football out of his hand.” A strong running game is the best protection a quarterback can have.

And let’s not forget the standard at Alabama. This is a program with one of the greatest running back traditions in college football history. Mark Ingram won the Heisman Trophy here. Derrick Henry won the Heisman Trophy here. Names like Trent Richardson, Eddie Lacy, T.J. Yeldon, Damien Harris, Josh Jacobs, Najee Harris, Brian Robinson Jr. and Jahmyr Gibbs all came through Tuscaloosa. When you’re a running back at Alabama, the expectations are sky-high. And that’s exactly why the staff is working so hard to make sure the next great Alabama backfield is already on its way.

Now, let’s look at the bigger picture for the future of this backfield.

After the season, every college program goes through a period of change. Players can enter the transfer portal, and players who are eligible can declare for the NFL Draft. That’s just how college football works now. So it’s natural that Alabama is planning ahead.

The good news is that Alabama’s two freshmen, Brown and Crowell, have a lot of eligibility ahead of them, and they’ve already shown they can contribute in the SEC. Hill is a veteran who has been through a lot with this program, and like every veteran, he’ll have his own decision to make about the future. But no matter what happens, there’s a strong foundation to build on.

And then there’s the recruiting class. Newkirk and Phillips are already committed. And if Alabama can land Tranard Roberts on November thirtieth, the 2027 running back class could be one of the best in the country. Think about that combination: experienced veterans, talented young players who will have a full year of experience, and a strong group of incoming freshmen. That’s how you build a backfield that can compete for championships year after year.

So my take on the AK Dear news is this. It’s a surprise, and it’s never fun to see a talented young player leave. But it’s not a crisis. In the short term, Alabama’s rotation stays the same. And in the long term, the program has a lot of talent coming in and a lot of young talent already on campus.

And I also think the way DeBoer handled it says something about his leadership. He was short and direct. He spoke well of the player. And he moved on to focus on the task at hand: Chattanooga, Senior Day and then the Iron Bowl. That’s how you keep a locker room focused in late November.

Now let’s quickly look ahead to Saturday, because it’s a big day in Tuscaloosa.

Alabama hosts Chattanooga at one p.m. Central, two p.m. Eastern, at Bryant-Denny Stadium, on SEC Network Plus. Chattanooga is seven and four, and Grubb described the Mocs as a well-coached team with one of the better defenses in the FCS. So Alabama has to come out focused and start fast.

And honestly, that’s a good thing for the running game. Facing a solid FCS defense gives Alabama’s offensive line a real test, and a chance to work on the run blocking that has struggled in recent weeks. If the line can create running lanes against a good defensive front on Saturday, it’ll build confidence and timing heading into Auburn. Every rep matters this time of year.

It’s also Senior Day, and Alabama will honor twenty players before the game, including Bray Hubbard, Caleb Woodson, Fatutoa Henry, Devan Thompkins, Keon Sabb and Danny Lewis Jr. Alabama is holding the ceremony a week early, and DeBoer made it clear that Chattanooga “is not our last home game,” because the Iron Bowl is at Bryant-Denny on November twenty-eighth. So the seniors will get one more home game after their big day.

And here’s what I want to see from the running backs on Saturday. I want to see Hill keep running hard and get a big birthday. I want to see Brown break a couple of long runs and get his confidence back. I want to see Crowell get healthy and get some touches. And I want to see Alabama finally break that streak of four games under one hundred rushing yards. If that happens, this offense heads into the Iron Bowl with momentum and balance.

On the quarterback side, the plan seems clear: if Russell is healthy enough, he’ll play. DeBoer said after the Vanderbilt game, “If he can go, I’d expect him to go.” The ideal scenario is that Alabama builds an early lead, Russell gets some reps and rhythm, and then Austin Mack finishes the game. That keeps Russell sharp and protects his ankle for Auburn.

Then comes the Iron Bowl. The game everyone has circled. Alabama at home, a top-three ranking on the line, and a big recruiting weekend with Tranard Roberts in the building. If Alabama wins that game in front of one hundred thousand fans, it sends a message to the committee, to the SEC and to every recruit watching.

And imagine this for a second. Tranard Roberts in the stands, watching Daniel Hill run hard in the fourth quarter, watching a freshman like Trae’shawn Brown or EJ Crowell break a big run, and watching Bryant-Denny shake after an Iron Bowl win. Then, two days later, he makes his decision. That’s the kind of moment that can swing a recruitment. The best thing Alabama can do for its recruiting is simple: win the football game.

So let me sum it all up. The triple dose of good news: Keelon Russell continues to improve with no setbacks. Alabama stayed at number three in the College Football Playoff rankings. And top running back target Tranard Roberts will be at the Iron Bowl, with his decision coming two days later. On top of that, Alabama already has two running backs committed in the 2027 class, Nigel Newkirk and Tai Phillips.

The surprise of the week is AK Dear, who is no longer with the program. DeBoer called him “a great kid, great player,” and we wish him nothing but the best. In the short term, it doesn’t change Alabama’s rotation. Daniel Hill, Trae’shawn Brown and EJ Crowell will carry the load. And in the long term, Alabama’s backfield has a bright future.
