Two months ago, nobody would have believed this. A two-loss Alabama team ranked third in the College Football Playoff rankings, ahead of undefeated UCLA and one-loss Notre Dame. A nineteen-year-old quarterback named a semifinalist for the Maxwell Award, given to the best offensive player in college football. A second-year linebacker named a semifinalist for the Bednarik Award, given to the best defensive player in the country. And a defense that leads the SEC in takeaways. Tide Nation, Alabama is doing things right now that almost no one saw coming. But this week also delivered a big blow. Let’s talk about all of it.

Let’s start with the big blow, because it’s the news nobody wanted to hear.

Tight end Josh Ford is expected to miss the rest of the season after suffering a knee injury in Saturday’s win at LSU. CBS Sports’ Matt Zenitz first reported it on Monday, and multiple outlets have reported that Ford is set to undergo surgery. It’s a tough loss. Ford is the six-foot-six, two-hundred-sixty-five-pound Oklahoma State transfer who broke his arm against Mississippi State, had surgery that Sunday, and then played against Georgia without missing a game. His words: “I told them I was going to play no matter what.” And in his final game of the season, he caught the go-ahead seven-yard touchdown pass that gave Alabama a seventeen-to-thirteen lead at LSU and kicked off the twenty-one-to-nothing run that won the game.

Kalen DeBoer called Ford a “uniter,” and said the team has to “find ways to make up the gap.” He also said Ford “is gonna have a fast recovery.” Kaleb Edwards called him “a great vocal leader” who “encourages us every day.” Now Edwards, Marshall Pritchett, Danny Lewis Jr., Jay Lindsey and former four-star freshman Mack Sutter will all be asked to do more. Get well soon, Josh. The whole Tide Nation is behind you.

Now let’s get to the things nobody saw coming, starting with the College Football Playoff rankings.

On Tuesday night, the committee released its second rankings of the season, and Alabama jumped from fifth to third. Here’s the top five: Georgia at one, Utah at two, Alabama at three, UCLA at four and Notre Dame at five. Alabama is the highest-ranked two-loss team in the country. And in this twelve-team format, the top four teams get a first-round bye. If the season ended today, Alabama would skip the first round and go straight to the quarterfinals.

Think about where this team was just a few weeks ago. After the loss at Tennessee, people were talking about the hot seat. After the first rankings came out with Alabama at number five, a lot of national analysts said the Tide didn’t deserve to be that high. And now? Alabama is ranked even higher. Committee chair Hunter Yurachek said the move up was not “an admission of them being too low.” It was about what Alabama did at LSU, and about the teams around it losing. Kentucky fell to eleventh after losing at Tennessee. LSU fell to twenty-first. Miami lost to Notre Dame and dropped to seventh.

And here’s a stat that explains why the committee likes Alabama so much. In the new rankings, Alabama owns three wins over teams in the College Football Playoff top twenty-five: Kentucky at eleven, South Carolina at seventeen and LSU at twenty-one. Two of those three came on the road. And both of Alabama’s losses came to top-ten teams, number one Georgia and number eight Tennessee. That’s a résumé built on who you play and who you beat.

Now, the second thing nobody saw coming: national award recognition for two young stars.

On Tuesday, the Maxwell Football Club named its semifinalists, and Alabama landed two of them. Keelon Russell is one of eighteen semifinalists for the Maxwell Award, which goes to the best offensive player in college football. And Luke Metz is one of eighteen semifinalists for the Bednarik Award, which goes to the best defensive player in the country.

Let’s start with Russell. Remember, he didn’t even win the starting job until late in fall camp. He was named the starter on August twenty-second, after a battle with Austin Mack. And look at what he’s done since. This season, Russell has completed one hundred sixty-five of two hundred forty-five passes for two thousand two hundred sixty-three yards, with eighteen touchdowns and just five interceptions. He’s also run for two hundred seventy-six yards and five touchdowns, tied for the team lead. He ranks in the top twenty-five nationally in passing yards and passing touchdowns. And in the SEC, he ranks in the top four in passing yards, touchdowns, points responsible for and total offense, averaging more than two hundred eighty-two yards of total offense per game.

And he’s only nineteen years old. That’s incredible. On Monday, offensive coordinator Ryan Grubb said, “His best football’s ahead of him. He’s one of, if not the best, quarterbacks in the country.” And the Maxwell Football Club clearly agrees that he belongs in that conversation.

Just to put it in perspective, look at the Alabama players who have won the Maxwell Award before. AJ McCarron in 2013. Derrick Henry in 2015. Tua Tagovailoa in 2018. DeVonta Smith in 2020. And Bryce Young in 2021. That’s legendary company. Russell has a long way to go to join that list, and the field is loaded, but just being named a semifinalist as a nineteen-year-old in his first season as a starter is a huge statement.

And the numbers that stand out most to me aren’t the yards. They’re the decision-making numbers. Eighteen touchdown passes and only five interceptions. And in the biggest road game of the season, at LSU, zero interceptions and three touchdowns. Think about how much he’s grown. Back in September, after Alabama’s wild comeback win over Florida State, Russell admitted, “We got too many mental mistakes right now, but we’re going to fix it.” He’s fixing them. He’s throwing the ball away instead of forcing it. He’s protecting the football in hostile environments. And he’s making the big throws in the second half, when games are decided. All three of his touchdown passes at LSU came after halftime, after he came back from the medical tent with a taped-up ankle. That’s what the Maxwell voters are noticing. Not just the stats, but the poise.

And he’s doing it as a dual threat. Two hundred seventy-six rushing yards and five rushing touchdowns, tied for the team lead. Remember that fifty-seven-yard scramble against Florida State that helped put the game away? When a quarterback can hurt you with his arm and his legs, defenses can never fully relax. And with the run game still finding its rhythm, Russell’s ability to make plays with his legs has been a huge part of keeping this offense moving.

As for his health, DeBoer said Monday that Russell is “day to day” after the ankle injury he played through at LSU. He said, “He finished out the game. That’s a good sign right there.” And Grubb said, “They seem pretty confident Keelon is going to be alright.” We’ll keep an eye on the availability reports this week.

Now, Luke Metz. Metz is a second-year linebacker who earned the starting job this season, and he’s been one of the best stories on the team. He ranks second on Alabama with sixty-eight tackles. He has five quarterback hurries, second on the defense. And here’s the big one: he leads the team with four takeaways. Two interceptions and two fumble recoveries. One of those interceptions was a pick-six at Kentucky back on September twelfth. And the other was the huge interception right before halftime at LSU, when the Tigers were already in field goal range. He had eleven tackles in that game too.

And look at the Alabama players who have won the Bednarik Award. Jonathan Allen in 2016. Minkah Fitzpatrick in 2017. And Will Anderson Jr. in 2022. Again, legendary company. The finalists for both awards will be named on November twenty-fourth, and the winners will be announced on December eleventh on the ESPN college football awards show.

Now, the third thing nobody saw coming: this Alabama defense has turned into a turnover machine.

Here’s the stat that blew me away. Alabama’s defense leads the SEC and ranks third in the entire country with twenty-one forced turnovers. Thirteen of those are interceptions. And five of those interceptions have been returned for touchdowns. Five pick-sixes. In nine games.

Think about what that means. When you force twenty-one turnovers, you’re giving your offense extra possessions and short fields all season long. And when you score five defensive touchdowns, you’re adding roughly thirty-five points to the scoreboard without your offense even being on the field. That’s a game-changing defense.

And this is exactly what DeBoer talked about after the LSU game. He said, “Against a team like this, it’s hard to go down the field each and every play and stack those plays, and so you got to have explosive plays generated somehow, whether it’s takeaways, whether it’s special teams.” Kane Wommack’s defense lives by that philosophy. Before the LSU game, Wommack said the key was to “create enough doubt in their minds and then capitalize.” And that’s what this defense has done all season.

Let’s go back to the LSU game, because it showed everything this defense does well. LSU moved the ball between the twenties. Sam Leavitt threw for three hundred eighty yards, and the Tigers outgained Alabama by more than one hundred seventy yards. But every time LSU got close, the defense dug in. LSU got into the red zone four times and came away with just ten points. It went zero for four on fourth down. Metz intercepted Leavitt with LSU already in field goal range before halftime. And in the fourth quarter, LSU scored zero points.

The pass rush was huge too. Devan Thompkins had two sacks and was named the SEC Defensive Lineman of the Week. His first sack came right before Metz’s interception. His second came late in the fourth quarter and forced a turnover on downs at the LSU eighteen-yard line, setting up the touchdown that sealed the game. Yhonzae Pierre and Terrance Green each added a sack.

And the entire secondary was named Alabama’s defensive player of the week, because even with Leavitt throwing forty-seven times, LSU didn’t score a single passing touchdown. Caleb Woodson had eight tackles, a pass breakup, a quarterback hurry and a forced fumble. Bray Hubbard and Red Morgan each had nine tackles.

So when people say LSU had all the yards, they’re right. But this defense isn’t built to win the yardage battle. It’s built to win the moments that decide the game. Takeaways. Red zone stops. Fourth-down stops. Big sacks at the right time. Twenty-one turnovers and five pick-sixes later, I’d say it’s working.

And there’s a philosophy behind it. DeBoer talked after the LSU game about the “swarm” mentality. He said, “It’s all about the swarm, you know, and pursuing and not assuming that a guy’s going to be on the ground. You create your breaks, you create opportunities by hustling and giving great effort.” That’s where turnovers come from. They don’t just happen. They happen because eleven guys are flying to the football, the second and third defenders are arriving to rip at the ball, and the defensive backs are reading the quarterback’s eyes. Thirteen interceptions in nine games means opposing quarterbacks are throwing into tight windows all season long, and Alabama’s secondary is making them pay.

And think about how much that helps a young quarterback. When your defense gives you extra possessions and short fields every week, the offense doesn’t have to be perfect. Russell doesn’t have to go ninety yards every drive. That’s complementary football, and it’s exactly how DeBoer and his staff have built this team. The defense creates chances. The special teams flip the field. And the offense finishes. At LSU, that formula produced twenty-one unanswered points.

Wommack summed it up on Monday. He said, “I thought our guys played really physical. We hit really hard.” And on those four fourth-down stops, he gave the credit to his players. He said, “They brought those calls to life and executed them.”

Now, the fourth thing: Alabama’s path to the SEC Championship Game.

Here’s where things stand. Georgia is six and one in SEC play, with two conference games left, against Missouri and South Carolina. Alabama is five and two, with two conference games left, against Vanderbilt and Auburn. And Texas, Tennessee and Kentucky are all sitting with two conference losses too.

So what does Alabama need? First, it has to win out. Beat Vanderbilt and Auburn, and Alabama finishes seven and two in the SEC. Then it gets complicated, because of tiebreakers. Let me walk you through the scenarios, based on an unofficial tiebreaker simulator cited by 247Sports, assuming Georgia also wins out and clinches one spot.

If Alabama and Kentucky are the only two teams tied at seven and two, Alabama gets in, because it beat Kentucky head-to-head forty-five to seventeen in Lexington.

If Alabama, Tennessee, Texas and Kentucky all finish tied at seven and two, it’s not settled yet, but Alabama is the most likely team to advance, because the tiebreaker would go to conference opponents’ winning percentage, and Alabama currently has the edge.

Same thing if it’s a three-way tie between Alabama, Tennessee and Texas. Alabama is currently the most likely to advance based on conference opponents’ winning percentage.

But there are some scenarios that don’t work for Alabama. If it’s just Alabama and Tennessee tied, Tennessee goes, because it beat Alabama head-to-head. If it’s just Alabama and Texas, Texas goes, based on its record against common opponents. And in a three-way tie with Texas and Kentucky, or with Tennessee and Kentucky, the other team gets the nod.

And here’s the wildcard. If Georgia loses a second conference game, to Missouri or South Carolina, the whole picture changes, and the tiebreakers have to be completely reworked. It’s even possible for Alabama to reach the SEC title game at six and three in conference play, but that would take a lot of help.

So the message is simple. Alabama has to take care of its own business, and then it needs a little help. But here’s the good news: even if Alabama doesn’t make the SEC Championship Game, the College Football Playoff committee has made it very clear how it views the Crimson Tide. Number three in the country, with two losses. A ten-and-two Alabama team with this résumé would be in a great spot no matter what happens in Atlanta.

The next College Football Playoff rankings come out on Tuesday, November seventeenth, and there are three more rankings shows before the final bracket is set on December sixth. A lot can still change. But right now, Alabama controls the most important thing: its own results.

Now, let’s talk about Vanderbilt, because that’s the only game that matters right now.

The game is this Saturday, November fourteenth, at FirstBank Stadium in Nashville. Kickoff is at eleven forty-five a.m. Central time on the SEC Network. Vanderbilt is four and five, and it just got its first SEC win of the season, twenty-five to twenty-three at Mississippi State, thanks to kicker Brock Taylor, who made all six of his field goals, including a fifty-nine-yarder. The Commodores need two more wins to become bowl eligible, so they’ll be fired up.

And of course, everybody remembers 2024. Alabama beat Georgia in a classic, and then lost forty to thirty-five at Vanderbilt the very next week. DeBoer brought that up in the locker room right after the LSU win. He said, “A lot of these guys don’t have the memory from a couple years ago. Big win, then going up to Vanderbilt.”

But here’s an interesting twist. When the players talked to the media on Tuesday, they said the 2024 game isn’t really part of the conversation. Caleb Woodson said, “It’s not really talked about much,” and pointed out, “I wasn’t here.” Kaleb Edwards said the players who went through that loss aren’t really discussing it, and that there are a lot of new guys on this roster. Racin Delgatty and Dijon Lee Jr. said the same thing.

And honestly, I like both sides of that. DeBoer made sure the message was delivered once, right after LSU. And now the players are moving forward. They’re not haunted by something most of them didn’t even experience. They’re focused on this week. Woodson put it perfectly. He said, “We’re just focused on going out and dominating the next game.” That’s the right mindset.

And it makes sense. This roster has changed a lot since 2024, with new transfers, new starters and a whole new group of leaders. Guys like Woodson, Edwards and Delgatty weren’t in that locker room in Nashville two years ago. For them, Vanderbilt is just the next team standing between Alabama and its goals. That’s a healthy way to approach it.

So what’s the key against Vanderbilt? Start fast. Alabama has had to come from behind in the second half four times this season. Against Vanderbilt, on the road, with an early kickoff, I’d love to see the Tide jump on the Commodores early and never let them believe. Keep forcing turnovers. Keep winning the red zone. And keep the penalties down. Alabama was flagged six times at LSU, and some of those came at tough moments. Clean that up, and this team is really tough to beat.

And here’s something else to watch: the run game without Josh Ford. Ford was one of Alabama’s best blocking tight ends, and the run game was already a work in progress. At LSU, Trae’shawn Brown led the team with just thirty-five rushing yards. With Ford out, it’ll be interesting to see how Grubb adjusts his personnel and blocking schemes. Kaleb Edwards is now the main “Y” tight end, and he’s coming off a three-catch, fifty-yard, one-touchdown game at LSU.

Let’s also be honest about what still needs to improve, because that’s what we do here on Alabama Tunnel.

The run game has to get better. The penalties have to come down. The defense has given up some big yardage totals, especially against LSU. And this team has to start faster in big games. These are all real areas to work on. But here’s the thing. Alabama is number three in the country while still having room to grow. That’s what makes this team so dangerous in November. It hasn’t played its best game yet, and it’s already in position for a first-round bye.

So let’s sum it all up. The big blow: Josh Ford is out for the season, and the tight end room has to step up. But look at everything else nobody saw coming. A two-loss Alabama team ranked third in the College Football Playoff rankings, in position for a bye. Keelon Russell, a nineteen-year-old first-year starter, named a Maxwell Award semifinalist. Luke Metz named a Bednarik Award semifinalist. A defense that leads the SEC with twenty-one takeaways and five pick-sixes. And a real path to the SEC Championship Game if things break right.

Here on Alabama Tunnel, we’ve been saying all season that this team was better than the national narrative. And now, the rankings, the awards and the results are all starting to back that up. The job isn’t finished, but this team is clearly peaking at the right time.
