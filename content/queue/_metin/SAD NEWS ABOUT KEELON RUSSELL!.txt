All week long, Alabama fans have been holding their breath about one question. Is Keelon Russell going to play at Vanderbilt? He went to the medical tent at LSU with an ankle injury. His head coach called him day to day. And there’s been chatter about backup quarterback Austin Mack getting ready just in case. Well, Tide Nation, the first official availability report is out, and I’ve got the answer for you. Plus, there’s a lot more good news on that report than you might expect. Let’s go.

What’s up, everybody, James here, and welcome back to Alabama Tunnel. Today we’re focused on the most important story of Vanderbilt week: the health of Alabama’s starting quarterback. We’ll go through what the SEC availability report says about Russell, what DeBoer and Ryan Grubb have said about his ankle, what the Austin Mack situation really means, and which other key players are trending toward a return this weekend. Then we’ll look at three players whose stock is rising after LSU, and what all of this means for Saturday in Nashville. If you’re pulling for Keelon this week, smash that like button, subscribe and turn on notifications. Let’s get into it.

Let’s start with the news everyone wants to hear.

On Wednesday night, Alabama released its first SEC availability report for the Vanderbilt game, and Keelon Russell is listed as probable. Probable. That’s the best possible designation for a player dealing with an injury, short of not being on the report at all. Reports say he’s been practicing this week, and the ankle isn’t expected to be a problem. So barring a setback, Alabama’s nineteen-year-old starting quarterback is expected to be under center on Saturday.

That’s a huge relief. Remember what happened at LSU. In the third quarter, Russell went down and headed to the medical tent. A lot of Alabama fans feared the worst. Russell himself said that when he went into that tent, “a lot of heads went down on the team.” But then he came back out with his ankle taped up, and he led Alabama to twenty-one unanswered points. He threw all three of his touchdown passes in the second half. He finished seventeen of twenty-four for two hundred six yards, three touchdowns and no interceptions, and he even ran for twenty-five yards on eight carries. After the game, he said, “And through the grace of God, it wasn’t a big injury.”

So the question all week was how the ankle would respond once the adrenaline wore off. And the answer, so far, looks like a good one.

Let’s go back to what the coaches said on Monday, because it tells you exactly how Alabama was handling this.

Kalen DeBoer said, “He’s day to day and will continue.” He pointed to the fact that Russell finished the LSU game as a positive sign: “He finished out the game. That’s a good sign right there.” He also said he’d expect Russell to “do everything he can to get out on the football field.”

But here’s the most important thing DeBoer said, and every Alabama fan should appreciate it. He made it clear the staff would never push Russell into a situation where he could get hurt worse. In his words, “We never want to hurt him, and we will never do that.” That’s a coach putting his player’s health first. That’s exactly what you want to hear about a nineteen-year-old with a bright future.

Offensive coordinator Ryan Grubb explained the plan in more detail. He said the forty-eight to seventy-two hours after a game are “the most critical part” of figuring out an injury like this, and that Russell’s condition on Tuesday and Wednesday would be the deciding factor. He said the staff would follow athletic trainer Jeff Allen and the medical team on how much Russell could handle in practice. And Grubb sounded confident from the start. He said, “They seem pretty confident Keelon is going to be alright.” He also pointed out that Russell bounced back really quickly from a foot injury he suffered in the Texas A&M game, which tells you he takes his rehab seriously.

And now Wednesday has come and gone, and the report says probable. That lines up exactly with what Grubb was hoping for.

Now, let’s talk about Austin Mack, because I know some people have been worried.

There’s been some chatter this week about Mack taking more reps in practice, and some people have jumped to the conclusion that Alabama might be preparing for a quarterback change. Let me be clear about this. There has been no official announcement of any kind of quarterback change. Russell is the starter, and he’s listed as probable.

But should Mack be getting extra reps this week? Absolutely. That’s just smart coaching. When your starting quarterback is managing an ankle injury, you’d be foolish not to have your backup fully prepared. DeBoer said on Monday that the team has “a lot of confidence if we had to move to Austin at some point.” And it makes sense for Alabama to limit Russell’s workload early in the week, give his ankle time to recover, and let Mack handle more of the practice snaps. That doesn’t mean Mack is replacing Russell. It means Alabama is being careful with Russell and prepared for anything.

And honestly, Alabama is lucky to have Mack. He’s a senior who competed with Russell for the starting job all summer, right up until Russell won it in late fall camp on August twenty-second. He knows the offense. He knows the receivers. And he was ready to step in at LSU when Russell went to the medical tent. If Alabama ever needs him, he would be making his first career start, and I think the staff would trust him to run the offense. But based on everything we know right now, that won’t be necessary this Saturday.

Now let’s look at the rest of the availability report, because there’s a lot more good news on it.

Along with Russell, four other Alabama players are listed as probable: defensive back Zabien Brown, offensive lineman William Sanders, running back EJ Crowell and defensive back Chuck McDonald.

That’s big. Let’s start with Zabien Brown. He’s one of the most talented players in Alabama’s secondary, and DeBoer sounded optimistic about him this week. He said, “So, getting him back this week is something I would expect.” Getting Brown back would be a huge boost to a pass defense that’s already among the best in the country at taking the ball away. Remember, Alabama’s defense leads the SEC and ranks third nationally with twenty-one forced turnovers, including thirteen interceptions and five pick-sixes.

William Sanders is also listed as probable. Sanders had surgery earlier this season, and getting him back on the offensive line would help the run game, which has struggled. DeBoer was a little more cautious about him, though. He said, “We need some improvements this week to get to where he can go out there.” So we’ll see how the rest of the week goes for him. But probable is a good sign.

EJ Crowell is probable too. Crowell is a talented running back who has dealt with injuries, and Grubb said, “We just gotta get him healthy.” With the run game still looking for consistency, a healthy Crowell would give Alabama another weapon in the backfield. Remember, he scored a key touchdown in the Florida State comeback back in September.

And Chuck McDonald, another defensive back, is probable as well. More depth in the secondary heading into November is always welcome.

Now for the players listed as out. Josh Ford, the tight end who suffered a season-ending knee injury at LSU, is out, as expected. Linebackers Desmond Umeozulu and Cayden Jones are out. Defensive back Nick Sherman is out. Defensive linemen Mhari Johnson and Jeremiah Beaman are out. And offensive lineman Casey Poe is out. We wish all of those guys a full and fast recovery.

Losing several linebackers and defensive linemen to injury means the depth on defense will be tested again. But this is where the next-man-up mentality DeBoer always talks about comes into play. Young players have stepped up all season long for this defense, and I expect that to continue in Nashville.

On the Vanderbilt side, the Commodores listed eight players as out, including quarterback Blaze Berlowitz, edge rusher Edwin Kolenge, linebackers Josiah Broxton and Daniel Vollmer Jr., running back Evan Hampton, offensive linemen Gunner Givens and Ted Gregoire, and defensive lineman Aaron Bryant. We wish them all well too.

Remember, there’s still a Thursday availability report and a final report on Friday, and then one more game-day update before kickoff. So designations can still change. But as of now, the news for Alabama is about as good as you could hope for.

And think about how far this roster has come in just two weeks. Before the LSU game, Alabama was missing several key starters. Then Ryan Coleman-Williams came back from knee surgery and a head injury and scored a touchdown in his first game back. Racin Delgatty returned at center and helped hold LSU’s elite pass rush to two sacks. Terrance Green came back on the defensive line and picked up a sack. And now, Zabien Brown, William Sanders and EJ Crowell are all trending toward a return. Yes, losing Josh Ford hurts. But overall, this team is getting healthier at exactly the right time of the year. DeBoer said after the LSU game, “When we get our complete team together, and we’re still not there yet, and maybe you never will be, there’s a confidence that grows, especially when they’re your leaders, when they’re your playmakers.” That confidence is growing every week.

Now, let’s take a look at three players whose stock is rising after the LSU win, because Russell is one of them.

Player number one: Keelon Russell. We’ve talked about the numbers. Seventeen of twenty-four. Two hundred six yards. Three touchdowns. Zero interceptions. All three touchdowns in the second half. But what makes his performance even more impressive is the context. Tiger Stadium is one of the hardest places in the country for a visiting quarterback, especially one dealing with an ankle injury. LSU came into the game ranked second in the nation in sacks, and Russell was sacked only twice. He stayed calm, he protected the football, and he made the throws that won the game.

And his season numbers are excellent. Russell has thrown for two thousand two hundred sixty-three yards, with eighteen touchdowns and just five interceptions. He’s also run for two hundred seventy-six yards and five touchdowns. On Tuesday, he was named one of eighteen semifinalists for the Maxwell Award, given to the best offensive player in college football. And on Monday, Grubb said, “His best football’s ahead of him. He’s one of, if not the best, quarterbacks in the country.” He also called the LSU performance “one of the guttier performances I’ve ever been around for a kid that age.”

Russell has had tough moments this season, including the road loss at Tennessee. But those experiences are clearly helping him grow. Back in September, after the Florida State game, he admitted, “We got too many mental mistakes right now, but we’re going to fix it.” He’s fixing it, week by week.

Player number two: Devan Thompkins. His stat line at LSU might look modest at first glance. Two tackles. But both of those tackles were sacks, for a combined loss of sixteen yards. According to Roll Tide Wire’s stock report, those two sacks matched his season high, which he set against Kentucky, and they were his first sacks since the Florida State game.

And both sacks came at huge moments. The first came late in the first half and set up Luke Metz’s interception on the very next play. The second came late in the fourth quarter and forced a turnover on downs at the LSU eighteen-yard line, which led to Alabama’s final touchdown. That’s why Thompkins was named the SEC Defensive Lineman of the Week. He didn’t need a lot of tackles to change the game. He made his opportunities count. And for Kane Wommack, having a fifth-year senior who can generate that kind of pressure gives the defense a lot of flexibility.

Player number three: Luke Metz. The second-year linebacker led Alabama with eleven tackles at LSU, including six solo stops. According to that same stock report, both of those numbers were his second-highest of the season. But his biggest play came late in the second quarter. LSU was moving the ball and already in field goal range, and Metz intercepted Sam Leavitt and returned it twenty-eight yards. Instead of LSU cutting into Alabama’s lead before halftime, Alabama went into the locker room up ten to three.

Metz has been one of the best stories of the season. He leads Alabama with four takeaways, two interceptions and two fumble recoveries, including a pick-six at Kentucky. And on Tuesday, he was named one of eighteen semifinalists for the Bednarik Award, given to the best defensive player in the country.

So when you put those three together, you see a team getting big contributions from key positions on both sides of the ball. A quarterback who delivers under pressure. A pass rusher who makes game-changing plays. And a linebacker who brings consistency and playmaking to the middle of the defense. That’s a good foundation for a team heading into the final stretch.

And there’s one more stat about that LSU win that’s worth mentioning. It was Alabama’s fourth straight victory over LSU. Forty-two to twenty-eight in 2023, in Nick Saban’s final season. Forty-two to thirteen in Baton Rouge in 2024. Twenty to nine in Tuscaloosa in 2025. And now thirty-one to thirteen in Death Valley. DeBoer is three and oh against the Tigers, with a combined score of ninety-three to thirty-five. That’s dominance in one of the SEC’s biggest rivalries.

Now, let’s talk about what this all means for Saturday.

Alabama heads to Nashville to face Vanderbilt at FirstBank Stadium. Kickoff is at eleven forty-five a.m. Central time on the SEC Network. Alabama is seven and two and ranked third in the College Football Playoff rankings, which would mean a first-round bye if the season ended today. Vanderbilt is four and five and coming off its first SEC win of the season, a twenty-five-to-twenty-three win at Mississippi State, where kicker Brock Taylor made all six of his field goal attempts, including a fifty-nine-yarder.

With Russell probable, Alabama’s offense should look like itself. But here’s what I’ll be watching. How mobile is Russell? His ability to escape pressure and extend plays has been a big part of his game this season. If the ankle limits his movement at all, Grubb might lean more on quick throws and the run game. Remember, Russell isn’t just a pocket passer. He has two hundred seventy-six rushing yards and five rushing touchdowns this season, tied for the team lead. He broke off a fifty-seven-yard scramble in the Florida State comeback. When he can move, defenses have to account for him on every snap. So if Vanderbilt sees Russell running freely early in the game, that’s a great sign, not just for Saturday, but for the Iron Bowl and the playoff run. And if he’s a little limited, I trust Grubb to build a game plan that protects him, with more quick-game concepts, play-action and designed throws that get the ball out fast. Either way, having Russell on the field, even at less than one hundred percent, gives Alabama its best chance to win. And speaking of the run game, getting Crowell and possibly Sanders back could really help an offense that’s been searching for consistency on the ground, especially now that Josh Ford is out for the season.

And on defense, getting Zabien Brown back would add even more talent to a secondary that’s already playing at a high level. Remember, the entire secondary was named Alabama’s defensive player of the week after holding LSU without a touchdown pass.

And of course, there’s the Vanderbilt history. In 2024, Alabama lost forty to thirty-five in Nashville the week after a huge win over Georgia. DeBoer brought that up in the locker room right after the LSU game. But the players say they’re focused on this week. Caleb Woodson said, “We’re just focused on going out and dominating the next game.” That’s the right mindset.

So here are my three keys for Saturday. Key number one: start fast. Alabama has come from behind in the second half to win four times this season, against Kentucky, Florida State, Texas A&M and LSU. That’s impressive, but on the road against a Vanderbilt team looking for an upset, I’d love to see the Tide take control early and keep the crowd quiet. Key number two: protect Russell. Whether that means quick throws, a balanced run game or extra help in pass protection, the offensive line and the tight ends need to keep their quarterback clean. And key number three: keep taking the ball away. This defense has forced twenty-one turnovers in nine games. Against a Vanderbilt team dealing with injuries of its own, an early takeaway or two could put this game away before it ever gets close. Do those three things, and Alabama should walk out of Nashville at eight and two, one step closer to a first-round bye.

Let’s also be honest about a few things, because that’s what we do here on Alabama Tunnel.

One great game doesn’t solve every problem. The run game still needs to get going. The offense was flagged too often at LSU. And Russell, even when he’s healthy, is still a young quarterback who’s going to have ups and downs. Alabama needs him and the rest of these rising players to keep performing at this level against tougher competition, especially in the Iron Bowl and the playoff. But the trend is clearly going in the right direction.

And here’s the bigger picture. A week ago, a lot of people wondered whether Alabama could win in Death Valley. Now, the Tide is number three in the country, its quarterback is a Maxwell Award semifinalist, its linebacker is a Bednarik Award semifinalist, its defensive lineman is the SEC Defensive Lineman of the Week, and its injury report is looking better than it has in weeks. That’s a lot of momentum heading into the final three games.

And the stakes are only getting higher. Alabama is number three in the College Football Playoff rankings, the highest-ranked two-loss team in the country. In this twelve-team format, the top four get first-round byes. The next rankings come out on Tuesday, November seventeenth. So every game from here matters, not just for the win-loss record, but for seeding. A bye would mean more rest, more time to heal and one fewer game to win on the road to a national title. That’s another reason Alabama is being so careful with Russell’s ankle. The goal isn’t just to beat Vanderbilt. It’s to have a healthy, confident quarterback in December and January.

And the path is clear. Vanderbilt on the road this Saturday. Chattanooga at home on November twenty-first. And the Iron Bowl against Auburn on November twenty-eighth. Win all three, and Alabama finishes ten and two with three wins over teams currently ranked in the College Football Playoff top twenty-five: Kentucky, South Carolina and LSU. That’s a résumé the committee has already shown it respects.

So let’s sum it all up. Despite the scary headlines, the latest news on Keelon Russell is actually good. He’s listed as probable for Vanderbilt, he’s been practicing, and the ankle isn’t expected to be a problem. DeBoer has made it clear Alabama will never put him in harm’s way. Austin Mack is ready if needed, but there’s no quarterback change. Zabien Brown, William Sanders, EJ Crowell and Chuck McDonald are all listed as probable too. And Alabama’s rising stars, Russell, Thompkins and Metz, are carrying real momentum into Saturday.
