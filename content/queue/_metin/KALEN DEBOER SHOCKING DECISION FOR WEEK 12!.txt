Let’s start with the decision that matters most: Keelon Russell.

All week, the signs have pointed in the right direction. On Monday, DeBoer said Russell “got through relatively clean” against Vanderbilt, although he admitted the ankle injury “wasn’t gone completely.” He said, “It’s just a matter of managing it, and kind of see as the week goes on where we’re at.”

Then offensive coordinator Ryan Grubb gave a more confident update. He said Russell is “doing really good right now,” and described his status as “status quo, good to go.”

And on his radio show Wednesday night, DeBoer added, “He continues to improve.” He said there were “no setbacks or anything” after Vanderbilt, but that there’s “still work to be done to continue to get him to one hundred percent.” Reports later in the week indicated that Russell is good to go for today’s game.

So the big question isn’t really whether Russell plays. It’s how long he plays.

And think about the workload Russell carried last week. At Vanderbilt, he played every offensive snap except the final victory formation, when Austin Mack came in to take a knee. That’s a full game on a sore ankle, after a week of very limited practice. He completed twenty-two of thirty-three passes for two hundred seventeen yards and three touchdowns. He also took three sacks and several other hits.

Now, DeBoer has a history with this kind of game. In 2024, he started Jalen Milroe against Mercer and got him out early. In 2025, he did something similar with Ty Simpson against Eastern Illinois. Start your quarterback, build a lead, and then get him to the sideline in a baseball cap. That’s the blueprint.

But there’s a balance to strike. Russell needs reps. DeBoer said last week that it took Russell until the middle of the second quarter to find his groove at Vanderbilt after missing practice time. Grubb said the offense “didn’t have its fastball” early in that game. And DeBoer said that when you’ve got “guys in and out” during the week, including the quarterback, it affects the rhythm of the whole offense. So getting Russell some quality snaps today, especially early, could be really valuable for his timing heading into the Iron Bowl.

At the same time, nobody wants to see Russell take unnecessary hits a week before Auburn. So here’s what I’d like to see. Russell starts. Alabama scores on its first few drives. The offense builds a comfortable lead. And then, once the game is under control, Russell heads to the sideline and Austin Mack takes over.

And for Mack, that would be a well-deserved moment. Last week, he prepared all week as if he would start, then went right back to supporting his teammate on game day. DeBoer said, “I can’t give him enough credit.” Grubb said Mack handled the situation “like a champ.” Getting him meaningful snaps in front of the home crowd would reward that preparation and make Alabama better prepared for any situation down the road.

If you’re watching from home, the game is on SEC Network Plus, and you can stream it on fuboTV. Clay Matvick has the play-by-play, Rocky Boiman is the analyst and Laura Stickells will report from the sideline. And if you’re heading to Bryant-Denny, the forecast called for clouds with a chance of rain, mostly earlier in the day, and temperatures in the sixties. So bring a jacket and get there early for the Senior Day ceremony.

Here’s a tip for you if you’re watching today: pay attention to pregame warmups and the first series. Last week, the first big clue came when Russell took the first-team reps during warmups. Today, the big clue will be how long he stays in the game.

Now, let’s talk about the national debate, because it’s been loud all week.

On Tuesday night, the College Football Playoff committee kept Alabama at number three, behind Georgia and Utah and ahead of UCLA and Texas. And since then, a lot of national analysts have pushed back. One of the most interesting voices in that conversation is a name every Alabama fan knows: Greg McElroy.

McElroy, the former Alabama quarterback who led the Crimson Tide to the 2009 national championship and now works as an analyst at ESPN, went on the Dan Patrick Show this week and said Texas should be ranked ahead of Alabama. He said, “Texas should be ahead of Alabama right now.” He said, “Texas has better moments.” And he said, “To me, a loss is a loss.” He added, “I’d have no problem with Texas being ahead of Alabama.”

Now, some fans were frustrated to hear that from a former Alabama national champion. But I actually respect it. McElroy has been consistent about this since the first rankings came out. He’s doing his job as an analyst, giving his honest opinion, and that’s exactly what you want from someone in that role. You can respect a person and still disagree with his take.

And here’s the case for Texas, to be fair. Texas and Alabama are both eight and two. Both beat LSU. But Texas won at Tennessee, where Alabama lost by sixteen. And Texas has wins over Ohio State and Ole Miss, which a lot of people view as stronger than some of Alabama’s wins. Kentucky, which was a ranked win for Alabama, has now lost two straight games, and that has hurt the value of that win.

So that’s the argument. And it’s not a crazy one. ESPN’s strength of schedule metric has Texas second in the country and Alabama sixth. So when people say Texas has played a tougher schedule, there’s data behind that.

Now here’s the other side, straight from the committee.

The committee chair, Hunter Yurachek, explained on Tuesday that Alabama is “in the top five in their schedule strength and record strength metrics.” He said, “The committee feels that they are the best team of the group that immediately follows Georgia and Utah.” He pointed to Alabama’s margin of victory in SEC games, roughly twenty-two to twenty-three points per win, second only to Georgia. And when he was asked specifically about Texas, he said the separator was “the consistent play of Alabama throughout the season.” He also called Texas “a really good football team.”

And here’s something else to remember. Alabama’s two losses came against Georgia, the number one team in the country, by three points, and at Tennessee, a top-ten team. Alabama doesn’t have a bad loss. And in SEC play, when Alabama has won, it has usually won big. That’s the consistency Yurachek was talking about.

So both sides have real points. And honestly, that’s what makes this debate so interesting. But here’s what matters for Alabama: the committee rewards consistency and margin. And that’s why today matters, even against an FCS opponent. A clean, dominant performance against a good Chattanooga team keeps reinforcing exactly what the committee said it likes about Alabama. A sloppy performance would give the critics more ammunition.

And then, of course, there’s the Iron Bowl. If Alabama beats Auburn next week, it’s very hard to see the committee dropping the Tide out of the top four. If Alabama loses, the Texas argument gets a lot louder. So the debate will be settled the way it always is: on the field.

Now let’s talk about the emotional part of today: Senior Day.

Alabama will honor its seniors before kickoff. DeBoer said this week that Alabama is recognizing nineteen players, “nine scholarship and ten walk-ons.” He said, “Proud of these guys and what they have all done for the program. Want to make it a special day.” And he explained that the staff chose this date earlier in the year, even though “it’s not our last home game.” The Iron Bowl is at Bryant-Denny next Saturday, so these seniors will get one more home game after today.

I want to highlight one name in particular: Jah-Marien Latham. Latham came to Alabama in 2020, and he was part of the national championship team that season, the last title team of the Nick Saban era. He is the last remaining link on this roster to that 2020 championship team. Think about that. He’s played for Alabama through two head coaches, multiple defensive coordinators and several position changes. He started as a defensive lineman, then moved to linebacker in Kane Wommack’s defense, and he’s done whatever this team has asked him to do. That kind of loyalty and versatility is rare in today’s college football. Tide Nation, when Latham’s name is called today, let’s make sure Bryant-Denny shakes.

And there are so many other seniors to celebrate. Bray Hubbard, the veteran safety who has been a cornerstone of the secondary. Caleb Woodson, the team’s leading tackler and the heartbeat of this defense since arriving from Virginia Tech. Fatutoa Henry and Devan Thompkins on the defensive line. Keon Sabb in the secondary. And Danny Lewis Jr., who has stepped into a bigger role at tight end with Josh Ford out for the year.

And here’s an interesting note about Hubbard and Woodson. Both are being honored today, but both will actually have eligibility remaining for 2027 under the NCAA’s new eligibility rules. So today isn’t necessarily goodbye for them. It’s a chance for Alabama to recognize what they’ve already meant to this program, whatever they decide next.

And don’t forget the walk-ons. DeBoer made a point of mentioning that ten of the players being honored are walk-ons. These are players who came to Alabama without a scholarship, who work every day on the scout team, and who make the starters better week after week. They don’t get the headlines, but they’re a huge part of what makes this program special. Some of them might get a chance to play today in front of their families. Those are the moments that make Senior Day so special. A walk-on running onto the field at Bryant-Denny with his family in the stands is one of the best scenes in all of college football.

Now, a quick word on history, because today is the end of an era. Starting next season, the SEC will play conference games on the second-to-last weekend of the regular season. That means today is the final November FCS game in SEC history. For Alabama, this tradition goes back to 2009, with Chattanooga, Western Carolina, Georgia Southern, Mercer and Eastern Illinois among the opponents. Alabama has won all sixteen of those late-season FCS games since 2009. And Chattanooga has been a familiar visitor, coming to Tuscaloosa in 2009, 2013, 2016 and 2023.

And history shows these games aren’t always easy. Back in 2011, Georgia Southern ran for over three hundred yards against Alabama. And in 2018, The Citadel was tied with the Tide ten to ten at halftime. Alabama won both of those games, but they’re reminders that you can’t just show up and expect it to be easy. You still have to play with focus for four quarters.

DeBoer’s take was simple: “Wherever the game’s at, for us, it’s just about right now, about improving and focusing on us and our standard.” And he made it clear Alabama respects what these games mean for the other side. He said, “These are big moments and cool opportunities to go in and kind of make a statement and do your thing.”

Which brings us to the opponent. And Chattanooga is a good football team.

The Mocs are seven and four overall and six and two in the Southern Conference. They closed out their conference schedule with a seventeen to ten win over Furman. They have the best scoring defense in the entire FCS, allowing just twelve point seven points per game. And they’re hoping their record is good enough to earn a spot in the FCS playoffs. So they have a lot to play for today.

Head coach Rusty Wright described this game as “climbing Everest without a sherpa.” That’s a great line. But don’t let the humor fool you. His team is physical. Kane Wommack said Chattanooga has “an identity of physicality.” And DeBoer said Coach Wright “has a football team that’s got some playoff hopes” and has “had a really good season.”

And on the other side of the ball, Alabama’s defense has been playing at an elite level. Over its last eight quarters, it has allowed just one touchdown. At Vanderbilt, it held the Commodores to six points, two hundred thirty-five total yards and twelve rushing yards. It recorded five sacks and nine tackles for loss. QB Reese had a season-high eight tackles. Justin Hill got back into the pass rush after dealing with a nagging injury. And DeBoer said the defense is “really not playing hesitant at all in those big moments.” Today is a chance for this unit to keep building that momentum, and maybe add the takeaways that were missing last week.

So Alabama has to come out ready to play. And that starts on special teams, which DeBoer has been praising all week. He highlighted Dijon Lee Jr.’s eighty-three-yard kickoff return at Vanderbilt and a punt return from Ryan Coleman-Williams. He praised the coverage units, saying they “continue to be consistent,” and even gave a shout-out to wide receiver Lotzeir Brooks for “hunting guys down” on coverage. Kicker Conor Talty has been reliable too, twelve of fourteen on field goals this season. Against a defense as good as Chattanooga’s, special teams could make a big difference in field position.

So what do I want to see today? Here are my keys to the game.

Key number one: start fast. Alabama has had too many slow starts lately. At Vanderbilt, the offense went nearly twenty-nine minutes without scoring before Russell found Marshall Pritchett right before halftime. Against the best scoring defense in the FCS, an early touchdown or two would set the tone and fire up the home crowd.

Key number two: early-down efficiency. DeBoer said it himself this week. Alabama went six for fifteen on third down at Vanderbilt, but he said the real issue was “early efficiency on early downs, and execution.” Win on first and second down, and the whole offense opens up.

Key number three: run the football. And let’s talk about Daniel Hill for a second, because today means a lot for him. Hill grew up in Meridian, Mississippi, and broke his leg twice in high school before becoming a four-star recruit. He dropped weight this offseason, worked hard on his pass protection, and made Bruce Feldman’s Freaks List. He ran for one hundred four yards at Kentucky in the SEC opener, then went through a tough stretch with limited carries, before bouncing back with that big game at Vanderbilt. DeBoer said afterward, “Thought Daniel ran really hard, did a nice job there. That was important for us.” A big day on his birthday, at home, on Senior Day, would be the perfect way to keep that momentum going.

And for the whole run game, the goal is clear. Alabama has been held under one hundred rushing yards in four straight games. Daniel Hill had eighty-nine yards on fourteen carries at Vanderbilt, his best yards-per-carry game of the season. And today is his twenty-first birthday. Let’s see him, Trae’shawn Brown and the rest of the backs break that streak against a strong defensive front.

Key number four: protect the quarterback. Quick throws, clean protection and smart play calls. Grubb has said he wants Russell to “get the football out of his hand.” The goal is to give Russell rhythm without exposing him to unnecessary hits.

Key number five: force takeaways. DeBoer was frustrated that the defense didn’t force a single turnover against Vanderbilt. He said, “We lost the turnover battle, and that’s a big deal for us.” Getting a couple of takeaways today would be a great confidence boost before the Iron Bowl.

And key number six: get the young players involved. If Alabama takes control early, the backups and freshmen get valuable game experience, and the starters get extra rest for Auburn.

If Alabama checks most of those boxes, it heads into Iron Bowl week healthy, confident and with momentum.

And one more player to watch: Lotzeir Brooks. He caught five passes for sixty-two yards and two touchdowns at Vanderbilt, including a thirty-nine-yard score to open the second half. And as DeBoer pointed out, he’s also flying down the field on kickoff coverage. With Josh Ford out for the year, Russell needs reliable targets, and Brooks has become one of them. Another strong day from him would give this passing game even more confidence heading into Auburn.

Now let’s look at the bigger picture.

After today, there’s one regular-season game left: Auburn, at Bryant-Denny, on November twenty-eighth. Alabama is second in the SEC standings at six and two, and a win in the Iron Bowl could send the Tide back to the SEC Championship Game, depending on other results. And in the College Football Playoff, the top four teams in the final rankings earn a first-round bye. That means an extra week of rest and one fewer game to win. For a team that’s still getting its quarterback back to one hundred percent, that bye could be priceless.

And next Saturday won’t just be a huge game on the field. It’s also a big recruiting weekend. One of Alabama’s top running back targets in the 2027 class, Tranard Roberts, is expected to visit for the Iron Bowl, and he’s planning to announce his decision on November thirtieth, two days after the game. So a strong finish over these next two weeks could pay off for this program in more ways than one.

And remember, this team has come a long way. Alabama started the season ranked thirteenth in the AP poll and was picked sixth in the SEC in the preseason. It replaced ten players who were taken in the NFL Draft, including two first-round picks. It handed the offense to a nineteen-year-old sophomore quarterback. And here it is, in late November, ranked third in the country and with its destiny in its own hands.
