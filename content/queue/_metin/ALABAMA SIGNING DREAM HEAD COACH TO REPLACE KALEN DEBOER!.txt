First, let’s get the facts straight, because there’s a lot of clickbait out there right now.

Is Alabama signing a new head coach? No. There is no search. There is no replacement. There is no “dream coach” coming to Tuscaloosa. Kalen DeBoer is the head coach of the Alabama Crimson Tide, and he’s under contract for a long, long time. Back in April, Alabama announced a seven-year contract extension for DeBoer that runs through 2033. It pays him twelve and a half million dollars a year, which puts him among the top five highest-paid coaches in college football, tied with Ohio State’s Ryan Day. Athletic director Greg Byrne said at the time, “We are pleased to extend Coach DeBoer and are proud to have him leading the Crimson Tide football program.”

That’s not a program looking for the exit. That’s a program that made a commitment. And remember why that extension happened. Michigan showed interest in DeBoer after the Sherrone Moore situation, and Alabama stepped up to keep its coach. When other programs try to take your head coach, and your school pays to keep him, that tells you exactly how Alabama’s leadership feels about him.

So where did all these “replace DeBoer” stories come from? Let’s be honest about it, because that’s what we do here on Alabama Tunnel.

The talk started back in the offseason. Alabama’s 2025 season ended with a thirty-eight-to-three loss to Indiana in the Rose Bowl, in the College Football Playoff quarterfinal. Indiana went on to win the national title, but for Alabama fans, that game stung. And when you’re the coach who replaced Nick Saban, every loss gets magnified. One ESPN personality went on a podcast in February and said if Alabama ever moved on, Oregon’s Dan Lanning was “the only answer, because Kirby ain’t coming.” Another analyst said that if Alabama missed the playoff again, DeBoer might want to escape the pressure cooker. Over the summer, preseason articles predicted a hot seat. Stephen A. Smith questioned whether Alabama picked the right guy. And when Alabama lost to Georgia twenty to seventeen and then lost at Tennessee twenty-six to ten, the noise got louder. Paul Finebaum said “the whispering campaign about DeBoer has accelerated,” and he said the LSU game was do or die. His words: “If they drop that game, they’re done. They’re not getting in the playoff.” There were even rumors linking DeBoer to USC, because he worked with USC’s athletic director Jennifer Cohen back at Washington, where he went twenty-five and three in two seasons.

And look, I get it. At Alabama, the standard is the standard. Fans want championships. Media members need storylines. And “is the Alabama coach on the hot seat?” gets clicks. But here’s what all that noise was missing. The facts.

Let’s look at DeBoer’s actual record at Alabama. Year one, 2024: nine and four, with a lot of roster turnover after Saban retired. Year two, 2025: eleven and four, seven and one in the SEC, and a trip to the College Football Playoff, including a comeback road win at Oklahoma in the first round. Year three, right now: seven and two, five and two in the SEC, ranked fifth in the first College Football Playoff rankings. Add it up and DeBoer is twenty-seven and ten at Alabama, with a playoff appearance already on his résumé and a real chance at another one. And remember, before he got here, he went twenty-five and three in two seasons at Washington and took the Huskies to the national championship game.

Is that the résumé of a coach who’s about to be replaced? Not even close. That’s the résumé of a coach who’s building something.

LSU was six and two and ranked fourteenth. So here’s where Alabama stands now. Seven and two. Three games left. Vanderbilt, Chattanooga, Auburn. Win all three, and that’s ten wins. That’s the magic number. And Alabama now controls its own path to get there.

Alabama’s defense held LSU to zero for four on fourth down. Keelon Russell threw three touchdown passes and no interceptions on an injured ankle. So now let’s talk about the most important part. How the LSU win answers the hot seat questions.

Think about everything people said about DeBoer. That he couldn’t win the big road game. That his teams fold when things go wrong. That the program had lost its edge. Saturday night answered every single one of those.

Big road game? Alabama went into one of the toughest environments in college football, against a ranked opponent, with the season on the line, and won by eighteen. That’s DeBoer’s third straight win over LSU, by a combined ninety-three to thirty-five. He’s now two for two in Death Valley.

Lost its edge? Alabama’s defense stopped LSU on all four fourth-down attempts. It sacked Sam Leavitt four times. Devan Thompkins had two sacks. Luke Metz had eleven tackles and an interception.
