And before we dive into the injuries, let’s quickly remember where this team stands, because context matters. Alabama opened the season with a forty-eight to ten win over East Carolina. Then went on the road and beat Kentucky forty-five to seventeen after falling behind early. Then came back from fifteen down to beat Florida State fifty to thirty-six. Then crushed South Carolina forty-nine to eighteen. Then went into Starkville and handled an undefeated, sixteenth-ranked Mississippi State team fifty-six to twenty-three. And then, last Saturday, a twenty to seventeen loss to Georgia, the team that’s about to be ranked number one in the country. Alabama led that game at halftime and was tied going into the fourth quarter.

That’s five wins, an average of nearly fifty points in those wins, and one three-point loss to the best team in the country. This is still a very, very good football team. Injuries don’t change that. They just change who has to step up.

Let’s start with the biggest name on the list. Ryan Coleman-Williams.

DeBoer was very direct about this one. He said Coleman-Williams has a couple of things going on. There’s a knee injury that he said has been, in his words, kind of going on, so this is something Ryan has been playing through for a while. And then there’s the head injury from the Georgia game. He took a helmet-to-helmet hit in the fourth quarter, one that wasn’t even reviewed for targeting, and that’s what finally knocked him out. DeBoer also mentioned a lower extremity issue that they’re addressing this week.

His exact words on Saturday’s game: there’s a pretty good chance Alabama won’t have him this weekend. And then the SEC availability report on Wednesday listed Coleman-Williams as out. After Tennessee, DeBoer said he’ll probably be week-to-week.

Now, we’ve talked a lot on this channel about how much Coleman-Williams means to this offense. He leads the team in receiving yards with four hundred fifteen, leads the team in touchdown catches with four, and is tied for the team lead with twenty-three catches. He’s the guy who flips games, like that forty-six-yard screen touchdown against Florida State right after the lightning delay. Losing him is a gut punch, no question.

But let me say this clearly, because it’s the most important point: with a head injury involved, the only thing that matters is Ryan’s long-term health. The staff is doing exactly the right thing by not rushing him back. If sitting out Tennessee means getting a fully healthy Ryan Coleman-Williams for the November stretch, with Texas A&M, LSU and the Iron Bowl still ahead, that’s a trade every Alabama fan should make in a heartbeat. Get well soon, Ryan.

And think about everything Ryan has already been through in his young career. He started his first college game at seventeen years old. As a true freshman in 2024, he caught forty-eight passes for eight hundred sixty-five yards and eight touchdowns, including the seventy-five-yard game-winner against Georgia that Alabama fans will never forget. Then 2025 was tougher, with drops and a dip in production. He changed his name to Coleman-Williams, tried playing without gloves, put the gloves back on, and turned himself back into one of the most dangerous receivers in the SEC this season. If anyone knows how to bounce back, it’s him. And against Georgia, even with that knee issue, he landed awkwardly in the second quarter, went toward the locker room, and still came back out to fight. That’s toughness. That’s a teammate this whole locker room is going to want to win one for on Saturday.

Now, injury number two. And honestly, from a football standpoint, this one might matter even more on Saturday. Starting center Racin Delgatty.

Delgatty left the Georgia game late in the fourth quarter with a lower leg injury. DeBoer said they’ll continue to evaluate him in the days ahead, and that he’d be pretty sore and limited early in the week. On the SEC report, he’s listed as questionable. So right now, this looks like a true game-time decision.

And here’s why this matters so much. Delgatty has started all six games at center for Alabama this season after transferring in from Cal Poly. Think about that. A guy who came from the FCS level, stepped into the most important communication spot on a brand-new offensive line, and has been there every single snap of this turnaround season.

Let me give you a little football one-oh-one here, because a lot of fans don’t realize how big the center position is. The center isn’t just the guy who snaps the ball. He’s the quarterback of the offensive line. Before every snap, he’s reading the defense, identifying the middle linebacker, recognizing where a blitz might come from, and setting the protection for everybody else. If the center gets that call wrong, the whole line can be wrong, and that’s when you see a free rusher in the backfield. Remember, DeBoer talked earlier this season about communication issues against Florida State’s blitzes. That’s exactly the kind of thing a veteran center helps prevent.

So who’s next up if Delgatty can’t go? Offensive coordinator Ryan Grubb already told us. Mal Waldrep slides over to center. And here’s some good news there. Waldrep isn’t some untested kid. He made a road start at guard back in Lexington against Kentucky, a game Alabama won by twenty-eight. He stepped in for Will Sanders against South Carolina and helped close out a thirty-one-point win. Some people who cover this team closely believe Waldrep might actually be the better pure blocker of the two, while Delgatty’s biggest strength is his awareness and his command at the line. And DeBoer said Casey Poe is getting extra reps in practice too, just in case.

And Grubb sounded optimistic about Delgatty. The sense out of Tuscaloosa is that Alabama is hopeful he can play by Saturday. So this one isn’t a doom-and-gloom situation. It’s a wait-and-see.

And let’s remember how far this offensive line has come, because that’s the real reason I’m not worried about one injury at center. Last season, Alabama ran for just ninety-two yards a game, one hundred twenty-ninth out of one hundred thirty-six teams in the country. Coming into the year, Michael Carroll was the only returning starter. Then Adrian Klemm arrived, rebuilt the room with transfers and young players, and turned it into one of the best rushing offenses in the country through the first month of the season. Klemm’s whole philosophy is about teaching every player in the room, not just the starters. That’s why, when Jayvin James and Will Sanders went down against South Carolina, guys like Tyrell Miller and Mal Waldrep stepped in and the offense didn’t miss a beat. DeBoer called Miller “more than capable” that night. That’s the culture Klemm has built. Next man up isn’t just a slogan in that room. It’s how they practice every day.

Now, injury number three. Terrance Green.

Green is one of the guys who does the dirty work on the interior of Alabama’s defensive front. He got hurt earlier and re-aggravated it in the first half against Georgia, and he didn’t come back. DeBoer said he’d be evaluated, and that he’s been limited in practice. And on Wednesday’s SEC availability report, Green was listed as out for Tennessee.

And this creates a little bit of a domino effect on defense, because Green isn’t the only front-seven player on the shelf. Desmond Umeozulu, the edge rusher who’s been dealing with turf toe, is out again. Cayden Jones is out for the season. Nick Sherman and Jeremiah Beaman are also listed as out.

So who steps up? Devan Thompkins is going to see a lot more snaps, and that’s a great thing, because Thompkins has been one of the best stories of this season. He led the team in sacks early on and has been a force against the run. Fatutoa Henry is also getting more reps this week. And then there’s the rest of that interior rotation, guys like Kedrick Bingley-Jones, who will have to hold the point of attack. And on the edge, there’s a little good news. Justin Hill, who has been out since the Florida State game, is listed as probable. Getting a veteran pass rusher back for this game would be a big lift.

Here’s why the defensive line matters so much this week. Georgia ran for two hundred six yards on Alabama. Tennessee’s running back DeSean Bishop is coming off back-to-back hundred-yard games, including one hundred thirty-eight yards against Arkansas. You know Josh Heupel watched the Georgia film and thought, we can run on this team. The guys up front have to prove him wrong. Stay in your gaps, set the edge, and make Tennessee one-dimensional.

So that’s Alabama’s side. Three important pieces: one out, one questionable, one out on the defensive line. Now let’s flip to the part of this story that I think is getting overlooked. Tennessee’s injury report.

On Wednesday, the Vols listed their starting quarterback, Faizon Brandon, as questionable. Brandon hurt his right shoulder back on September twenty-sixth in Tennessee’s loss to Texas. He sat out the win at Arkansas last weekend, which is why sophomore George MacIntyre made his first career start. There were reports that Brandon threw in practice on Tuesday for the first time since the injury. And when Heupel was asked about it, he said there was no update yet and that they’ll find out through the week.

So this is a real question mark. Does Alabama see Brandon, coming back from a shoulder injury and maybe not at full strength? Or does it see MacIntyre in just his second career start, in the biggest game of his life? Either way, Alabama’s defense is facing a quarterback situation with some uncertainty, and that’s an opportunity.

And let’s be fair to MacIntyre, because he played well in his first start. Against Arkansas, he went eighteen of twenty-nine for two hundred forty-seven yards and a touchdown, all career highs, and he helped lead a fourth-quarter comeback after Tennessee trailed fourteen to ten. That’s a confident young player. But going on the road to Fayetteville is one thing. Facing Kane Wommack’s defense, with all the disguises and pressure packages this staff can throw at you, is a completely different challenge. And if it’s Brandon, he’ll be playing his first game in weeks with a shoulder that was injured less than a month ago. Either way, this is a spot where Alabama’s defense can take control.

And it doesn’t stop there. Tennessee also ruled out offensive lineman Wendell Moe Jr., safety Dylan Lewis, cornerback Jadais Richard, linebacker Jaedon Harmon, tight end Ethan Davis, safety Edrees Farooq and receiver Joakim Dodson. That’s seven players, and most of them are out for the season. Two safeties and a cornerback out in the secondary. Tide Nation, that’s something to keep an eye on, even without Coleman-Williams.

So yes, Alabama is banged up. But so is Tennessee. This isn’t a fully healthy team hosting a wounded one. This is two injured teams going to war in the Third Saturday in October, and the team with the better depth is going to have the advantage. And I believe Alabama built its roster for exactly this kind of week.

And this is the Third Saturday in October, the one hundred eighth meeting between these two programs. More than a hundred thousand fans in Neyland Stadium. The tradition of the winning team lighting up victory cigars in the locker room. Generations of families who circle this date every single year. In a game like this, emotion and toughness matter just as much as the depth chart. And this Alabama team has a lot of both.

Let’s talk about that depth, starting at wide receiver.

Ryan Grubb said he feels great about the receivers behind Coleman-Williams, and he named three guys specifically: Lotzeir Brooks, Rico Scott and Derek Meadows.

Brooks is the obvious number one now. He had eight catches for one hundred one yards against Florida State, a seventy-five-yard touchdown four snaps into the South Carolina game, and that clutch thirty-nine-yard catch on third and nineteen in Starkville. He’s been reliable all year, and Russell trusts him.

Rico Scott was the target on the biggest deep shot of the Georgia game, the one where a lot of people felt there should’ve been a pass interference flag. The staff clearly believes in him in big moments.

Derek Meadows is a name a lot of casual fans might not know yet, but Grubb mentioned him by name, and that tells you he’s earned the staff’s trust in practice. This could be his chance to make a name for himself on a big stage.

Add Noah Rogers, the NC State transfer who’s been getting healthier and more comfortable every week, and true freshman Cederian Morgan, a six-foot-four target who already has a touchdown this year. Then add the tight end room, Marshall Pritchett, Kaleb Edwards and Josh Ford, which has been one of the best surprises of the entire season. Pritchett caught a twenty-two-yard touchdown against Georgia. Ford made that one-handed touchdown grab against South Carolina.

That’s a lot of weapons. No, none of them is Ryan Coleman-Williams. But together, they can make Tennessee cover every inch of the field.

Here on Alabama Tunnel, we’ve been saying since the start of the season that this offense was built differently than in years past. It isn’t built around one superstar. It’s built around options. We saw it against South Carolina, when ten different players made an impact. We saw it in Starkville, when Russell spread the ball around and Alabama scored fourteen points in every single quarter. This is exactly the kind of week where that philosophy pays off. When you have options, one injury doesn’t break you. It just changes who gets the ball.

And here’s another storyline from Grubb this week that I love. He said he wants EJ Crowell to touch the ball at least ten times a game. Against Georgia, Crowell only got six or seven touches. Crowell has averaged under six touches per game this season, and the staff clearly wants to get the five-star freshman more involved. That’s huge without Coleman-Williams. Crowell can carry the ball, catch it out of the backfield, and be a weapon in the red zone, where he’s been one of Alabama’s best finishers. Pair him with Trae’shawn Brown, who just ran for ninety yards on nine carries against Georgia, and Daniel Hill, and Alabama has a backfield that can carry the offense for a week.

So if I’m Ryan Grubb, my plan on Saturday is simple. Run the ball with Brown and Crowell to stay ahead of the chains and take pressure off a possible backup center. Get Crowell those ten touches. Spread the ball around in the passing game to Brooks, Scott, Meadows, Rogers and the tight ends. And let Keelon Russell do what he does best: protect the football and make smart decisions.

Speaking of Russell, he’s the reason I’m not panicking. Through six starts, he’s completed about sixty-eight percent of his passes for almost sixteen hundred yards, with twelve touchdown passes and only two interceptions, and he hasn’t thrown a pick since week two. DeBoer praised his ball security, his clock management and his two-minute drill this week, and said he likes the direction Russell continues to go. A quarterback who doesn’t turn the ball over is exactly what you need when you’re missing your top receiver on the road.

Now let’s talk about a defensive storyline that came out of this week, because it ties right back to the Georgia game.

Kane Wommack talked about defensive back Carmelo O’Neal, and he said, “Melo’s just steadily getting better and better every single week.” O’Neal has been playing somewhere between sixteen and twenty-five snaps a game. And with Djion Lee coming off a tough night against Georgia, including the late personal foul and some struggles in coverage, there’s a real chance O’Neal sees more snaps on Saturday.

And honestly, that’s how a healthy program works. Competition. When a player has a rough night, the guy behind him gets an opportunity. It’s not about punishing anyone. It’s about putting the best eleven on the field. And if Lee responds the right way, that competition makes both of them better. Remember, this is a secondary with Bray Hubbard, Zabien Brown and Keon Sabb. Red Morgan has developed into a real playmaker. There’s talent everywhere in that room.

Let’s also quickly touch on the Georgia game, because there’s been a lot of frustration with the officiating, and that frustration is part of why this week feels so emotional. There was the deep ball to Rico Scott with KJ Bolden all over him and no flag. Some people who have watched it back think the throw was a little underthrown, so they understand the no-call. But the hit on Coleman-Williams is a different story. A lot of people believe he was hit while still in the air, defenseless, and that it should have been a penalty. Instead, there was no review, and now Alabama’s best receiver is out.

But you know what DeBoer said. You can’t put the game in a position where the officials come into play. You control what you can control. And the best answer to all of that frustration is to go into Neyland and take care of business.

And here’s the other big message DeBoer gave his team this week. He said you can’t let Georgia beat you twice. In other words, the loss is done. If you carry it into Tennessee week, you’re letting it hurt you again. He also told them, don’t let the highs be too high, and don’t let the lows be too low. That’s a coach who knows exactly what this team needs right now.

So let’s put it all together. What does this mean for Saturday?

Kickoff is at three-thirty Eastern on ABC, in Neyland Stadium. Tennessee is five and one, two and one in the SEC, and ranked in the top fifteen. Alabama is five and one, three and one in the SEC, and still ranked sixth in the country despite the Georgia loss. And some people who cover this team are even arguing Alabama deserves to be ranked higher than that, ahead of Texas, and that both Alabama and Georgia could end up as top-four seeds in the playoff. That’s how much respect this team still has nationally.

The series is about as even as it gets lately. The teams have split the last four meetings. Alabama won last year thirty-seven to twenty in Tuscaloosa. Under Heupel, nobody has won two in a row in this rivalry. Alabama has the chance to be the first.

And think about what’s at stake beyond this one game. After Tennessee, Alabama comes home to face Texas A&M, then goes to Baton Rouge to face LSU, then Vanderbilt, Chattanooga and the Iron Bowl against Auburn. In the expanded playoff, a one-loss Alabama team is right in the middle of the national championship hunt. And if Alabama keeps winning, there’s even a real chance of a rematch with Georgia in Atlanta for the SEC title. Every week from here on out is a playoff game. And that’s exactly why managing these injuries the right way, and winning while some key players heal, is so important.

Here are my three keys for Saturday.

Key number one. Win the line of scrimmage on defense. With Terrance Green out, everyone up front has to step up. Thompkins, Henry, Bingley-Jones, and Justin Hill if he’s ready. Stop DeSean Bishop. If Tennessee can’t run, whoever plays quarterback for the Vols, Brandon with a sore shoulder or MacIntyre in his second start, has to beat Alabama through the air. That’s a matchup I like.

Key number two. Protect the middle. Whether it’s Delgatty or Waldrep at center, communication has to be perfect. Tennessee’s Xavier Gilliam already has five sacks this season. Identify him, account for him, and give Russell clean pockets. Use the tight ends and backs to help. Get the ball out quickly.

Key number three. Let the young weapons eat. EJ Crowell gets his ten touches. Lotzeir Brooks becomes the go-to guy. Rico Scott and Derek Meadows make the most of their opportunities. When one star is out, you don’t need one player to replace him. You need everybody to do a little more.

And one more thing to keep in mind: in a tight road game, special teams can swing everything. Conor Talty has been reliable all season. He was named SEC Special Teams Player of the Week after the Florida State game, and he hit a big thirty-nine-yard field goal to tie the Georgia game in the third quarter. In a rivalry game that could come down to one possession, having a kicker you trust is a huge advantage. And the linebackers, Luke Metz and Caleb Woodson, have to be ready to fill every gap against Bishop. Metz had thirteen tackles against South Carolina. Expect him to be all over the field again on Saturday.

Here’s why I believe in this team.

One, Alabama has already proven its depth this season. When two starting offensive linemen went down against South Carolina, the backups helped finish a thirty-one-point win, and then the line helped put up fifty-six points in Starkville the next week.

Two, Keelon Russell doesn’t beat himself. Four straight games without an interception. That’s the kind of quarterback who wins on the road.

Three, Tennessee is dealing with real injuries too, including a question mark at quarterback and multiple players out in the secondary.

And four, motivation. This team is coming off a three-point loss. Its best receiver was knocked out on a hit that wasn’t reviewed. And the players have heard all week that they’re banged up and vulnerable. There’s no better fuel than that.

So here’s my prediction. Alabama twenty-eight, Tennessee twenty. I think the defensive front responds after the Georgia game, I think Crowell and Brown carry the load on the ground, and I think somebody we don’t expect, maybe Derek Meadows, maybe Rico Scott, has a breakout moment. And I think this is the game where this team proves it’s more than any one player. Remember how this team responded every time it got punched this season. After the Kentucky turnovers, it answered. After falling fifteen points behind Florida State, it answered. After Wommack called out the pass defense, it answered. This week, it gets punched again, and I fully expect it to answer again.
