Update number one. The availability report.

Here’s the complete list from Wednesday night. Questionable: running back AK Dear, wide receiver Ryan Coleman-Williams, cornerback Zabien Brown, running back EJ Crowell, center Racin Delgatty, guard William Sanders and defensive lineman Terrance Green. Doubtful: freshman quarterback Jett Thomalla. Out: Desmond Umeozulu, Cayden Jones, Nick Sherman and Jeremiah Beaman.

Now, let’s break down what that actually means, because a lot of fans see the word questionable and immediately panic. Don’t. In the SEC availability report system, questionable simply means a player has a chance to play and the staff hasn’t made a final decision. And remember, this was the first report of the week. There’s another one Thursday night, another Friday night, and then a final report about ninety minutes before kickoff on Saturday. Statuses change. Players get upgraded. That’s exactly why there are four reports and not one.

And here’s the really important part. Five of these seven players are starters. Delgatty, Coleman-Williams, Sanders, Brown and Green. All five are still alive for Saturday. None of them were ruled out. Two weeks ago, every one of those guys was either out or about to be. Now they’re all in the conversation to play in Death Valley. That’s a massive shift.

Let’s go through them one by one, quickly.

Ryan Coleman-Williams, questionable with a knee injury. He’s missed the last two games after arthroscopic knee surgery and a head injury against Georgia. But DeBoer said back on October twenty-sixth, “I’m hopeful that he’ll be ready for the LSU game.” He’s been working out on the field this week, and earlier this week he was seen in practice video without a knee brace. Everything is trending the right way. Before he got hurt, he led Alabama in receptions and receiving touchdowns, with twenty-three catches for four hundred fifteen yards and four scores.

Racin Delgatty, questionable with a knee injury. He’s been out since getting hurt against Georgia, and he missed Tennessee and Texas A&M. As we’ll talk about in a minute, he might be the most important name on this entire list.

Zabien Brown, questionable with a lower leg injury. The preseason All-American corner got hurt in the first half against Tennessee and sat out the A&M game. Getting him back against an LSU passing game this dangerous would be huge.

Terrance Green, questionable with an ankle injury. He’s missed the last two games. But on Tuesday, Yhonzae Pierre said, “We got TG coming back, Terrence Green, so we’ve been working on schemes for the defense and for them.” That tells you the defense is preparing to have him out there.

EJ Crowell, questionable with a lower leg injury, and AK Dear, questionable with an ankle injury. Two running backs who would add depth to a backfield that needs fresh legs. Crowell, the former five-star, has four rushing touchdowns and is averaging six point eight yards a carry this season.

And then the surprise. William Sanders. The starting left guard was carted off against Texas A&M and had what’s called a TightRope procedure on his right ankle. That’s a procedure that’s often used to stabilize an ankle and help players get back on the field faster than a more traditional recovery would allow. When DeBoer talked about him after the A&M game, he said it might not be just a week or two, it might be a couple more than that. So a lot of people assumed he’d be out for LSU. But on Wednesday night, he was listed as questionable. Now, I want to be realistic. That doesn’t mean he’s going to play. He might still be a long shot. But the fact that he’s even on the questionable list, instead of out, is a positive sign. It tells you his recovery is going about as well as anyone could hope.

As for the rest of the list, Jett Thomalla is a freshman backup quarterback, so his doubtful status doesn’t affect the starting lineup. And Umeozulu, Jones, Sherman and Beaman being out was expected. DeBoer already told us Umeozulu, who’s been dealing with turf toe, won’t be back until toward the very end of the regular season. Get well soon, all of you.

So here’s my takeaway from update number one. Two weeks ago, Alabama was missing five starters. Today, all five of them have a chance to play on Saturday, plus a sixth starter in Sanders that nobody expected to be on the list. That’s a wave of good news.

So what could Saturday actually look like? Let’s think through three scenarios.

The best case: Coleman-Williams, Delgatty, Brown and Green all play, and Crowell is available in the backfield. That would be close to the version of Alabama we saw in September, when this team was running for over one hundred eighty yards a game and the defense had its full rotation. That’s a team that can beat anybody in the country.

The middle case: two or three of them play, maybe on limited snaps, while the others need another week. That’s probably the most realistic outcome. Even then, Alabama would be in much better shape than it was against Tennessee or Texas A&M. Remember, the Tide beat A&M without any of these guys.

And the worst case: most of them are held out to protect them for the stretch run. Even then, this team has already proven it can win. Rico Scott, Fatutoa Henry, Caleb Woodson, Djion Lee Jr. and Chuck McDonald III all stepped up two weeks ago. Next man up has been the identity of this team all season long.

So no matter which scenario plays out, Alabama is going into Baton Rouge with a real chance. And that’s the most important thing.

Update number two. DeBoer responds to Kiffin.

You probably heard about this. Lane Kiffin wasn’t happy that the Alabama game was scheduled for two-thirty in the afternoon instead of at night in Tiger Stadium. Kiffin joked that anyone who thinks the SEC wants LSU to win this year “must have just been on Mars the last four months,” and he suggested the kickoff time wasn’t doing LSU any favors.

So what did DeBoer say when he was asked about it? He kept it simple, and honestly, it was perfect. He said, “We’re on the road, so, you know, I’d rather play at home if we want to have all these advantages.” And then he added, “It is what it is. We just got to show up and play.”

I love that answer. No drama. No back-and-forth. Just a reminder that Alabama is the team going into a hostile road environment, and that the only thing that matters is what happens on the field. That’s a coach who’s focused on his team, not on the noise.

And let’s be honest about the night game thing, because the history is pretty funny. LSU is just one and six in its last seven night home games against Alabama. Alabama has gone into Tiger Stadium under the lights and won six of those seven. So the idea that a night game would automatically have been a big advantage for LSU? The record book doesn’t really back that up. And remember, the last time Alabama played in Baton Rouge, in 2024, the Tide won forty-two to thirteen. Day, night, whenever. This program knows how to win in that building. And since DeBoer arrived in Tuscaloosa, Alabama is two and oh against LSU, with a combined score of sixty-two to twenty-two. Last season, the Tide won twenty to nine at home, with Yhonzae Pierre racking up two sacks and the strip-sack that sealed the game. DeBoer has had LSU’s number so far. Now he gets to do it against a familiar face. Kiffin was Nick Saban’s offensive coordinator in Tuscaloosa from 2014 to 2016 and was part of the 2015 national championship team. He knows Alabama better than almost any opposing coach. That’s what makes this matchup so fascinating.

Update number three. LSU’s injury report.

Alabama isn’t the only team dealing with injuries. On LSU’s side, the big name is linebacker Whit Weeks. He’s a senior and a team captain, and he’s listed as doubtful. Weeks broke his fibula against Texas A&M back on September twenty-sixth, had surgery, and has missed the last four games. He’s returned to limited practice, so there’s a chance he gets upgraded later in the week, but right now, he’s doubtful. His younger brother, redshirt freshman linebacker Zach Weeks, is listed as questionable. And freshman defensive tackle Richard Anderson is out.

Now, we never celebrate an injury to anybody. We wish Whit Weeks and every injured player on both sides a full recovery. Football is a brutal game, and every one of these guys has worked incredibly hard to get back on the field. But from a football standpoint, a captain and veteran linebacker potentially missing this game matters. Linebackers are the communicators of a defense. They make the calls, they fit the run, and they’re responsible for a lot of the checks against motion and play-action. If LSU is without its most experienced linebacker, that could create some opportunities for Ryan Grubb’s offense, especially in the run game and with the tight ends over the middle. And LSU has a tough stretch ahead too. After Alabama, the Tigers host Texas and then go to Tennessee. So this game is huge for them as well. LSU is coming off its own bye week after beating Auburn thirty-eight to seventeen on October twenty-fourth. Both teams are rested. Both teams have had two weeks to prepare. And both teams know a third loss could end their playoff hopes. That’s why this one feels like an elimination game.

Update number four. The battle in the trenches.

And this is where the game might be decided. Because here’s the number that jumps off the page. LSU’s defense has thirty-four sacks this season. That’s the second most in the entire country. Edge rushers Princewill Umanmielen and Jordan Ross have combined for thirteen and a half sacks, and Umanmielen alone has nine, which is among the best totals in the nation. This LSU front can get after the quarterback.

So now you understand why the offensive line injuries matter so much. And why Racin Delgatty might be the most important player on Alabama’s availability report.

Think about it. Through the first six games, with Delgatty starting at center, Alabama averaged around one hundred eighty-two rushing yards per game. In the two games since he got hurt, Tennessee and Texas A&M, Alabama averaged about sixty-one. That’s a massive drop. Now, it’s not all on one player. The opponents were tough, and other injuries piled up too. But the center is the brain of the offensive line. He makes the protection calls. He identifies the blitzes. He sets the slides. Against a pass rush with thirty-four sacks, in a stadium where communication is going to be incredibly hard, having your starting center back would be enormous.

And if Sanders somehow makes it back at left guard, that’s even better. Mal Waldrep has done a solid job filling in at center, and he has experience at guard too, so the staff has some flexibility. But the more of the original starting five Alabama can put on the field, the better its chances of keeping Keelon Russell clean.

Left tackle Jackson Lloyd spoke to the media this week, and he summed up the mindset perfectly. He said, “We know what we have to do.” And then he added, “Backs against the wall, everyone knows what we got to do.” That’s a lineman who understands the stakes. The offensive line has heard all the criticism after the last two games. Now it gets the chance to answer it against one of the best defensive fronts in the country. And this is where Adrian Klemm’s work matters. Remember, this offensive line was rebuilt almost from scratch this offseason, and in September it was one of the most improved run-blocking units in the country. Klemm has had two weeks to get his group back on the same page, figure out the best combination with the healthy bodies he has, and prepare for LSU’s edge rushers. A good offensive line coach can make a huge difference in a week like this.

And this connects directly to DeBoer’s message to Russell this week. DeBoer said, “I think it’s just learning from what you’ve been through,” and he added, “That will come to him because he’s that type of player.” According to reports from Tuscaloosa, the focus for Russell this week has been on reading down and distance, staying steady, and getting the ball out quickly. That’s exactly how you beat an elite pass rush. You don’t hold the ball and wait for the big play. You take the quick throws, you use the tight ends and the backs, and you make the pass rushers run a long way for nothing. Marshall Pritchett, Kaleb Edwards and Josh Ford have been excellent all season. Rico Scott just had six catches for one hundred fifty-six yards against A&M. Russell has options. He just has to use them. And remember, Russell has already shown he can respond to pressure this season. At Kentucky in week two, he turned the ball over on each of his first two possessions, including a pick-six. Alabama outscored the Wildcats thirty-two to nothing after halftime. Against Florida State, the Tide fell behind by fifteen, and Russell led the comeback with three hundred twenty passing yards and three touchdowns. And against Texas A&M, he went five for five for sixty-eight yards and two touchdowns in the fourth quarter. When the moment gets big, this young quarterback has a way of rising to it.

And if Coleman-Williams is back? Then LSU has a real problem. Because you can’t send everybody after the quarterback when Alabama has a former All-American receiver who can beat you deep. The return of Coleman-Williams doesn’t just help the passing game. It helps the protection too, because it forces LSU to keep more players in coverage.

Update number five. Alabama’s plan for Sam Leavitt.

Let’s talk about the other side of the ball, because Sam Leavitt is playing really good football right now. In his last two games, he’s thrown seven touchdown passes and zero interceptions. Against Auburn on October twenty-fourth, he threw for three hundred seventy-eight yards and three touchdowns in a thirty-eight-to-seventeen win. As of late October, he had over twenty-one hundred passing yards and twenty-five total touchdowns, one of the highest totals in the country. And he’s dangerous with his legs too. He’s run for three hundred twenty-four yards and eight touchdowns this season, and five of those rushing touchdowns came at Tiger Stadium.

So this isn’t just a pocket passer. Remember, Leavitt arrived in Baton Rouge from Arizona State as the top quarterback in the transfer portal, and he’s lived up to that billing. He has weapons around him too, like Jackson Harris, who had one hundred sixty-three yards and two touchdowns against Texas A&M earlier this season, plus Trey’Dez Green and Winston Watkins Jr. This is a quarterback who can hurt you with his arm, extend plays with his legs, and run the read-option and RPOs. And that’s exactly what Kane Wommack has been studying.

Wommack spoke this week about how Alabama has handled RPO-heavy offenses this season. He said, “So far, we’ve found ways to capitalize and create takeaways off RPOs.” And he explained the approach: “You’ve got to create enough doubt in their minds and then capitalize.” That’s the whole philosophy. Make Leavitt hesitate. Make him hold the ball an extra half-second. Disguise the coverage so he can’t pre-determine where he’s going. And when he makes a mistake, take it away. And Alabama’s pass rush can do some damage of its own. Fatutoa Henry had two sacks against A&M and was named one of Alabama’s players of the week. Yhonzae Pierre has a history of big plays against LSU, with two sacks and a game-sealing strip-sack in last year’s win. Devan Thompkins has been a steady presence inside. If Terrance Green is back to take on double teams in the middle, those guys get more one-on-one chances. LSU’s pass rush gets a lot of attention, but Alabama’s front can make Leavitt uncomfortable too.

And Wommack has some personal knowledge here too. He talked about his ties to the Kiffin family, saying, “Chris Kiffin and I worked together for a while.” He also said he studies live TV broadcasts of opponents to get a feel for how they call games and set their tempo. That’s the kind of detail work that wins big games. And it lines up with what Bray Hubbard said earlier in the week about Kiffin’s offense trying to read the defense before the snap. Hubbard said, “We got to do a great job disguising and making sure they can’t just tip our coverages off of just our alignment.”

So the plan is clear. Disguise. Create doubt. Pressure. Take the ball away. And if Terrance Green is back to anchor the middle and Zabien Brown is back on the outside, Wommack has a lot more tools to make that plan work.

And look at the evidence from two weeks ago. Against Texas A&M, Alabama’s defense forced three turnovers, including a pick-six by Djion Lee Jr., and held the Aggies to just fifteen total yards in the fourth quarter. Fatutoa Henry had two sacks. Caleb Woodson had ten tackles. Chuck McDonald III got his first career interception. This defense knows how to make game-changing plays. Now it has to do it against an even more dangerous quarterback, in an even tougher environment.

Update number six. The playoff picture.

We covered this in detail in our last video, but it’s worth repeating because it raises the stakes for Saturday. In the first College Football Playoff rankings, the committee put Alabama at number five, the highest-ranked two-loss team in the country. In the projected bracket, Alabama would host a first-round playoff game at Bryant-Denny Stadium. And a big reason for that ranking is the road win at Kentucky, which is now ranked sixth.

LSU is fourteenth. So a win in Baton Rouge would give Alabama another ranked road win, and with Vanderbilt, Chattanooga and Auburn left on the schedule, Alabama would be in a great position to stay in that hosting spot or even climb higher. A loss would make things much more complicated. That’s why this game feels like a playoff game in November. And yes, not everyone agreed with the committee. CBS Sports analyst Danny Kanell criticized the ranking, pointing out that Tennessee beat Alabama and is ranked thirteenth. That’s a fair debate, and Tennessee deserves credit for that win. But the best way to end the debate is simple. Go win in Death Valley. Do that, and nobody is going to be talking about whether Alabama deserves to be in the top five.

So let’s put the whole wave together. Seven Alabama players listed as questionable, including all five injured starters plus a surprise appearance from Will Sanders. DeBoer with a calm, confident answer to Kiffin’s complaints. LSU’s captain linebacker listed as doubtful. A massive trench battle against the country’s second-ranked pass rush. And a clear defensive plan to make Sam Leavitt uncomfortable. That’s a lot of news, and most of it points in Alabama’s direction.

Now let’s talk about how Alabama wins this game.

Key number one. Protect Keelon Russell. Against a defense with thirty-four sacks, Alabama has to win up front, use quick throws, and keep Russell clean. If Delgatty is back, that’s step one. If the tight ends and backs chip the edge rushers, that’s step two. And if Russell gets the ball out on time, LSU’s pass rush loses its biggest weapon.

Key number two. Run the ball well enough to stay balanced. Alabama doesn’t need two hundred rushing yards to win this game. But it needs to be better than sixty. If Crowell is healthy, give him his touches. If LSU is without Whit Weeks, test the middle of that defense early. A successful run game slows down the pass rush and sets up play-action.

Key number three. Contain Leavitt. He has eight rushing touchdowns, five of them in this stadium. Alabama’s edge defenders have to stay disciplined, keep him in the pocket, and make him throw under pressure. And when he does, the secondary has to be ready to jump the routes.

Key number four. Win the turnover battle. Leavitt hasn’t thrown an interception in his last two games. Wommack’s defense has created takeaways against RPO offenses all season. Something has to give. If Alabama forces even one or two turnovers, that could swing the entire game.

Key number five. Handle the environment. Rico Scott said this week, “It’s a crazy atmosphere. Fans are a little nasty, but I feel like ours are too.” That’s the right attitude. Alabama has already won in Lexington and Starkville this season, and it won by twenty-nine in this same stadium two years ago. Start fast, stay composed, and take the crowd out of it.

And one bonus key. Finish drives with points. Conor Talty had a rough day against A&M, missing two of his three field goals after making fifteen in a row before that game. Every kicker has an off day, and I fully expect Talty to bounce back. But against a top-fifteen team on the road, Alabama can’t afford to leave points on the field. Get touchdowns in the red zone, and when Talty gets his chance, I trust him to deliver.

My prediction hasn’t changed. Alabama twenty-seven, LSU twenty-four. I think it’s going to be a physical, back-and-forth game, the kind of game this rivalry always seems to produce. LSU’s pass rush will make some plays. Leavitt will make some plays. But I think Alabama’s returning players make the difference. Coleman-Williams makes a big catch. Delgatty helps stabilize the line. And Alabama’s defense makes the key stop late, just like it did against A&M.
