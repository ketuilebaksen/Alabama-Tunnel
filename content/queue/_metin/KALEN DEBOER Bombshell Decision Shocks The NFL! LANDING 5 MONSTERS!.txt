DeBoer didn’t guarantee anything, and some of them are still in non-contact work. But every single one of them has a chance. And when you’ve been missing this many key players for this long, even getting two or three of them back would completely change this team. And the timing of the bye week couldn’t have been better. Keelon Russell said going into the off week that it was about correcting mistakes on both sides of the ball, understanding assignments and preparing as a team. That’s exactly what you want from your quarterback. And DeBoer has talked about Russell’s development, saying the key for a young quarterback is having a clock in his head that’s connected to his feet, knowing when to take the easy completion, when to work through his progressions, and when to use his legs. Two weeks of practice without a game gave Russell and Ryan Grubb time to sharpen all of that, and gave the trainers time to get these five monsters back on the field.

So let’s go through the five monsters, one at a time.

Monster number one. Ryan Coleman-Williams.

This is the big one. Coleman-Williams is Alabama’s number one receiver, and he’s missed the last two games, Tennessee and Texas A&M. He took a hit to the head against Georgia, and he’s been dealing with a knee issue that required arthroscopic surgery. Before he got hurt, he had twenty-three catches for four hundred fifteen yards and four touchdowns. That’s eighteen yards a catch. He’s the guy who stretches the field. He’s the guy defensive coordinators game plan around.

And DeBoer has been sounding optimistic about him for over a week now. Back on October twenty-sixth, at the Birmingham Monday Morning Quarterback Club, DeBoer said, “I’m hopeful that he’ll be ready for the LSU game.” And he added, “Ryan, I hope by next week he’ll be able to give us quite a bit.” Well, now it’s next week. And Coleman-Williams is on the field working out.

Think about what his return would mean. While he was out, Rico Scott exploded for six catches and one hundred fifty-six yards against A&M. Lotzeir Brooks has been steady in the slot. The tight ends, Marshall Pritchett, Kaleb Edwards and Josh Ford, have become red-zone weapons. Now add Coleman-Williams back into that group. LSU’s secondary would have to pick its poison. Roll the coverage to Ryan, and Rico gets one-on-one. Focus on Rico, and Ryan beats you deep. Drop the safeties, and the tight ends eat underneath. That’s the kind of passing attack that can quiet a loud stadium in a hurry.

And it would be huge for Keelon Russell. Remember, Russell went five for five for sixty-eight yards and two touchdowns in the fourth quarter against A&M, and he did it without his top target. He’s thrown at least fifteen touchdown passes through eight games. Give him his number one receiver back, and this offense has a completely different ceiling.

Monster number two. Racin Delgatty.

If Coleman-Williams is the flashiest name on this list, Delgatty might be the most important. He’s Alabama’s starting center, a transfer from Cal Poly who started the first six games of the season before a knee injury knocked him out of the last two. And look at what’s happened to the run game without him. Over the first six games, Alabama averaged over one hundred eighty rushing yards a game. In the two games without Delgatty, Alabama ran for fifty-four yards at Tennessee and sixty-eight against A&M. That’s around sixty-one yards a game.

Now, it’s not all on one player. Football never is. But the center is the quarterback of the offensive line. He makes the protection calls, he identifies the fronts, and he’s involved in almost every double team in the run game. When you lose him, everything gets a little harder. Mal Waldrep did an admirable job stepping in, and he deserves credit for battling in a tough spot. But getting Delgatty back would give this line its leader again. DeBoer said after the A&M game that the run game had too many negative plays and too many guys unblocked at the point of attack, and that the team had to get back to the basics. A healthy Delgatty is one of the fastest ways to fix that. And there’s another layer to this. With William Sanders out at left guard, which we’ll get to in a minute, the staff has to figure out the best five up front. Waldrep has experience at guard too. He started at guard on the road at Kentucky earlier this season, and he filled in for Sanders against South Carolina. So if Delgatty can go at center, it gives the coaches more flexibility to put the right combination together. That’s the kind of chess match Adrian Klemm and this staff have to solve this week, and the more healthy bodies they have, the easier it gets.

Monster number three. Zabien Brown.

Alabama’s All-American cornerback missed the Texas A&M game with a lower-body injury, and the defense still played great without him. Now imagine it with him. Brown is the kind of corner who can take away one side of the field. And this week, that matters more than ever, because LSU has a very dangerous passing attack.

LSU quarterback Sam Leavitt came to Baton Rouge as the top quarterback in the transfer portal after his time at Arizona State. He threw for three hundred thirty-five yards in the season opener against Clemson and three hundred twelve yards and two touchdowns against Texas A&M. And he has weapons. Jackson Harris had one hundred sixty-three yards and two touchdowns against A&M. Trey’Dez Green and Winston Watkins Jr. are dangerous too. If Brown is back, Kane Wommack can be more aggressive with his calls, play more man coverage and send more pressure. And with the secondary a little thinner after the departure of Dre Kirkpatrick Jr., getting your best corner back is massive.

And here’s something I love about this secondary. While Brown was out, other guys stepped up. Djion Lee Jr. took a pick-six thirty-nine yards to the house against A&M. Chuck McDonald III got his first career interception. Carmelo O’Neal has been getting better every single week, according to Wommack. So if Brown comes back, he joins a group that’s playing with a ton of confidence. And if he’s not quite ready, the good news is that this secondary has already proven it can survive without him. That’s what depth looks like. But make no mistake, Brown on the field changes how LSU attacks you. Quarterbacks look the other way when an All-American is lined up on their top receiver.

Monster number four. EJ Crowell.

Here’s one a lot of fans might not have realized. EJ Crowell also missed the Texas A&M game with a lower-body injury. And that’s another reason the run game struggled so much in that one. Crowell was one of the top running back recruits in the country when he signed with Alabama, and Ryan Grubb has made it clear he wants Crowell touching the ball at least ten times a game. He’s a physical runner who can grind out tough yards and also break the big one.

Trae’shawn Brown, Daniel Hill and AK Dear have carried the load, and they’ve had some nice moments. Remember, Brown had eighty-six yards and a touchdown against Florida State, including that twenty-yard touchdown run to finish the game. But a healthy Crowell gives Grubb another option, fresh legs, and a different style. And against an LSU defense coordinated by Blake Baker, Alabama is going to need to run the ball well enough to keep the Tigers honest. If Crowell and Delgatty both come back, the run game could look like a different unit than the one we saw the last two weeks.

Monster number five. Terrance Green.

And finally, the big man up front. Terrance Green has missed the last two games with an ankle injury. Green is in his first season at Alabama, and before he got hurt, he was a key part of a defensive line that was rebuilt this offseason. When he went down, guys had to step up, and Fatutoa Henry did exactly that. Henry had two sacks against A&M and was named one of Alabama’s players of the week.

Now imagine Green back next to Henry, Devan Thompkins and Yhonzae Pierre. That’s a pass rush that can make Sam Leavitt very uncomfortable. And there’s more good news on the defensive line. DeBoer also said Justin Hill is back, which gives the front another body at a position that’s been short-handed. In the SEC, you need a deep rotation up front to keep everybody fresh in the fourth quarter. And that’s exactly when the A&M game was won.

Here’s a storyline that should fire up every Alabama fan. LSU opened as a four-point favorite in this game. Alabama going into Death Valley as the underdog? I love it. This team has played its best football this season when people doubted it. The A&M game is proof of that.

And here’s another number to remember. Since DeBoer took over in Tuscaloosa, Alabama is two and oh against LSU, and the Tide has outscored the Tigers sixty-two to twenty-two in those two games. Last season, Alabama won twenty to nine at home, with Yhonzae Pierre racking up two sacks and a strip-sack that sealed the game. So DeBoer knows how to beat LSU. This staff has done it twice. And think about the last time Alabama went to Baton Rouge. In 2024, in DeBoer’s first season, the Tide walked into Tiger Stadium and won forty-two to thirteen. That’s not just a win. That’s a statement in one of the toughest environments in college football. Some of the players on this roster were part of that night, and they know they can win there.

Of course, this year has a big twist. Lane Kiffin is on the other sideline. Kiffin was Nick Saban’s offensive coordinator in Tuscaloosa from 2014 to 2016, and he was part of the 2015 national championship team. He knows Alabama inside and out. And after leaving Ole Miss, he’s in his first season at LSU with Charlie Weis Jr. running the offense and Blake Baker running the defense. So expect some creative play calls and probably a few trick plays. Kiffin loves to keep defenses guessing. And remember, Kiffin has been a head coach at Florida Atlantic and at Ole Miss, and he’s beaten plenty of good teams with tempo and creativity. Alabama’s defense has to stay disciplined, communicate on every snap, and not get caught looking at the sideline when LSU goes fast. That’s where two weeks of preparation pays off. Kane Wommack and his staff have had extra time to study every wrinkle Kiffin likes to use.

Let’s look at LSU’s season. The Tigers beat Clemson fifty-one to ten in the opener, crushed Texas A&M thirty-five to six, and shut out Mississippi State thirty-one to nothing. Their two losses came on the road, thirty-two to twenty-four at Ole Miss and thirty-five to thirty-four in overtime at Kentucky. Remember, Alabama beat Kentucky by twenty-eight in Lexington. And Alabama just beat that same A&M team by eleven. So on paper, these teams are very evenly matched. This one is going to come down to execution. And this rivalry has a long history of coming down to the wire. Alabama and LSU have played some of the most memorable games in SEC history, and more often than not, they’ve been decided by a couple of plays in the fourth quarter. So don’t expect a blowout either way. Expect a fistfight. And that’s exactly the kind of game this Alabama team showed it can win when it outgained Texas A&M one hundred two yards to fifteen in the fourth quarter two weeks ago. When the game was on the line, Alabama’s offense found its rhythm and the defense shut the door completely. That’s the formula in Baton Rouge too.

So what are my keys for Alabama in Baton Rouge?

Key number one. Win the turnover battle. Against Tennessee, Alabama turned it over four times and lost. Against A&M, Alabama forced three turnovers, including a pick-six, and won. It’s that simple. In a road environment like Tiger Stadium, turnovers are everything. Protect the football, take it away, and keep the crowd out of it.

Key number two. Win in the trenches. That’s where the returning players matter most. If Delgatty is back at center and Crowell is back in the backfield, Alabama needs to establish the run early. Don’t let LSU pin its ears back and come after Russell. A balanced offense is a dangerous offense, and that starts up front, even without Sanders.

Key number three. Make Sam Leavitt uncomfortable. Henry, Pierre, Thompkins, and hopefully Terrance Green have to get pressure. And if Zabien Brown is back on the outside, Wommack can send more heat without worrying as much about the coverage behind it. When Leavitt has time, LSU’s receivers can hurt you. When he’s under pressure, mistakes happen.

Key number four. Finish drives. Conor Talty had a rough day against A&M, missing two of three field goals after making fifteen in a row. On the road against a top-fifteen team, every point matters. Get touchdowns in the red zone, and if it comes down to a kick, I trust Talty to bounce back. His track record says he will.

Key number five. Start fast. Tiger Stadium feeds off momentum. If Alabama can score early and take the crowd out of it, the pressure flips to LSU. An early touchdown drive with Coleman-Williams making a big catch? That would be the perfect way to start.

That’s why this is the biggest game of the season so far. Win on Saturday, and Alabama goes into the final stretch knowing that if it keeps winning, it should be in the field.
