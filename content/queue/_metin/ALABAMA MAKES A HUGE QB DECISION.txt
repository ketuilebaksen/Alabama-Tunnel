On Friday night, ESPN reported that Keelon Russell would sit and Austin Mack would make the first start of his career. Ninety minutes before kickoff, Alabama’s final availability report still listed Russell as a game-time call. And then, when the Alabama offense ran out for warmups in Nashville, it was Russell taking the first-team snaps. Alabama made its quarterback decision at the last possible moment, Russell played every snap, threw three touchdown passes, and the Crimson Tide beat Vanderbilt twenty-one to six. Tide Nation, revenge for 2024 is complete. Let’s break it all down.

What’s up, everybody, James here, and welcome back to Alabama Tunnel. Alabama is eight and two, six and two in the SEC, and it got out of Nashville with a win, something it couldn’t do two years ago. It wasn’t pretty. It was sloppy at times. But the defense was dominant, Russell gutted it out on that ankle, and some new faces stepped up in big ways. Today we’re going to talk about the huge quarterback decision and how it played out, the key moments of the game, the defense’s incredible day, the honest areas that need work, and what’s next with Chattanooga and the Iron Bowl. Let’s start with the decision that had everybody talking.

All week long, the big question was whether Keelon Russell’s ankle would be ready after he hurt it at LSU. He was listed as probable on Wednesday and Thursday. Then on Friday, ESPN reported that Alabama would sit Russell and start Austin Mack. That report made sense to a lot of people. Alabama was a big favorite. Chattanooga is next. Why not give Russell’s ankle more rest and have him fully healthy for the Iron Bowl?

Before the game, one analyst laid out the debate really well. He described Mack as “the steady drum beat,” a quarterback who would give you “seven out of ten to seven and a half out of ten” quarterback play every week. And he described Russell as the guy with the higher ceiling, capable of “nine out of ten” play. He said that against Vanderbilt, that steady, reliable Mack-level play “should be good enough for Alabama to walk away victoriously.” And he made the case for resting Russell: “You don’t want him reinjuring his ankle.” He said Alabama “absolutely” wants him back for Auburn.

So that’s where things stood going into Saturday. But the decision wasn’t actually final. DeBoer explained afterward that the staff was handling Russell’s ankle “day by day,” and that they didn’t know the final direction until close to kickoff. The final availability report, which came out ninety minutes before the game, listed Russell as a game-time call. And then, in warmups, Russell was with the first team.

So why did Alabama change course? Because Russell got better, fast. DeBoer said Russell had limited reps in practice, but improved noticeably in the final twenty-four to forty-eight hours before the game. He said, “Every day he was getting better.” And after the game, DeBoer said, “I thought Keelon found ways to impact the game, even on just minimal opportunities or reps.”

And Russell? He made it clear he wanted to be out there. After the game he said, “We can battle through anything.” He described his mindset as “bruised, bumps, whatever we’ve got to do.” And when he was asked about the ankle, he said he “was very healthy” and was “feeling phenomenal right now.”

Now, I want to give a huge amount of respect to Austin Mack here. He spent the entire week preparing to make the first start of his career. He took a lot of first-team reps. He got himself ready for the biggest moment of his college career. And then, at the last minute, he went back to the backup role. DeBoer called it a difficult position to be in and praised how Mack prepared. And Russell said Mack is “always ready.” That’s a great teammate. Remember, Mack followed DeBoer from Washington. He came back to Alabama this season to compete for the job, lost a close battle in camp, and stayed ready all year. That kind of player is a huge part of a championship culture. Shoutout to Austin Mack.

And the pregame analyst made a good point about the bigger picture. He said that DeBoer had told everyone over the offseason that both quarterbacks were close, and that Alabama could trust Mack “to still play good football.” That’s why Alabama was comfortable planning for Mack to start. It wasn’t a panic move. It was a team with two quarterbacks it believes in, making the best call for that specific day.

And honestly, this was the best of both worlds. Alabama got its starting quarterback back. And it also learned that its backup is ready to step in at any time. If something happens in December, Alabama knows Mack can handle it.

Now let’s go through the game itself, because it was a slow start and then a strong finish.

The first quarter was a punting contest. Alabama punted twice, Vanderbilt punted twice, and nobody could get anything going. Then in the second quarter, Vanderbilt struck first. Brock Taylor, the kicker who made six field goals the week before at Mississippi State, hit a fifty-two-yarder to give the Commodores a three-to-nothing lead. And for a little while, you could feel the 2024 memories creeping in. Early kickoff. Road game. Alabama trailing in Nashville.

But this Alabama team responded the way it has all season. Late in the first half, Alabama put together a nine-play, seventy-five-yard drive in the final four minutes. And with just six seconds left before halftime, Russell found tight end Marshall Pritchett for a nine-yard touchdown. Seven to three, Alabama, at the break.

And that touchdown means a lot. With Josh Ford out for the season, the tight ends had to step up, and Pritchett delivered the biggest play of the first half. Ryan Grubb said this week that Kaleb Edwards would take on more, and that Danny Lewis Jr. and Jay Lindsey would have expanded roles. But on Saturday, it was Pritchett who found the end zone. That’s a deep tight end room doing its job.

Then came what one writer called the “middle eight,” the last four minutes of the first half and the first four minutes of the second half. On Alabama’s opening drive of the second half, the Tide went seventy-five yards in six plays, and Russell hit Lotzeir Brooks for a thirty-nine-yard touchdown. Fourteen to three. In the span of about eight minutes of game time, Alabama went from trailing three to nothing to leading by eleven. That’s how you take control of a road game.

Vanderbilt answered with a fifty-seven-yard field goal from Brock Taylor. That’s an incredible kick. Fourteen to six. And the Commodores had some life again.

And then came the play of the game. Dijon Lee Jr. took the ensuing kickoff and returned it eighty-three yards, all the way down to the Vanderbilt twelve-yard line. Four plays later, Russell found Lotzeir Brooks again, this time for a nine-yard touchdown. Twenty-one to six. That kick return completely flipped the momentum. Right after Vanderbilt had gotten back within one score, Lee made sure the Commodores never got any closer.

Think about Lotzeir Brooks’ day. Two touchdown catches, including a thirty-nine-yarder. On a day when the offense was searching for big plays, Brooks delivered twice. And Dijon Lee Jr., a defensive back, became a game-changer on special teams. Remember, Lee was one of the players who said this week that the 2024 loss wasn’t really part of the conversation, because so many of these guys weren’t there. Well, he made sure this group wrote its own story in Nashville.

From there, it was all about the defense. And what a performance it was.

Let’s talk about Kane Wommack’s defense, because it was the star of the show.

Vanderbilt did not score a touchdown. Not one. The Commodores finished with just two hundred thirty-five yards of total offense, their lowest total of the season. They went two for fourteen on third down. Two for fourteen. And they went one for four on fourth down. Alabama’s defense has now allowed just one touchdown in its last eight quarters, going back to the LSU game.

And the pass rush was relentless. Alabama sacked Vanderbilt quarterbacks five times, with five different players getting a sack, including Yhonzae Pierre, Justin Hill and Fatutoa Henry. Early in the game, Vanderbilt tried to stay ahead of the chains with runs and quick passes. But once Alabama took the lead, the Commodores had to throw more, and that’s when the pass rush took over.

Alabama also forced three turnovers. Dontae Carter intercepted a pass. Miles Capers forced a fumble. And the defense got another stop on downs. That keeps building on what’s been an incredible season for this unit. Coming into the game, Alabama’s defense already led the SEC with twenty-one forced turnovers. Now it’s added even more.

And here’s the part I love most. One writer pointed out that the 2024 loss in Nashville came down in large part to third-down struggles. Vanderbilt kept the ball, kept converting and kept the Alabama offense on the sideline. This time, Vanderbilt went two for fourteen on third down. That’s the difference between the 2024 team and the 2026 team. DeBoer said this week that Vanderbilt wanted to “keep the ball,” “use the time of possession” and “limit your possessions.” Wommack’s defense said no.

Vanderbilt also committed fourteen penalties for ninety-two yards, so the Commodores didn’t help themselves. And Clark Lea was honest afterward, saying his offense felt like “a mess.” Credit to Vanderbilt for fighting, and especially to Brock Taylor, who drilled field goals from fifty-two and fifty-seven yards. But on this day, Alabama’s defense was just too much.

Now, let’s go back to Russell’s numbers, because they were really impressive for a guy playing on an injured ankle. He completed twenty-two of thirty-three passes for two hundred seventeen yards and three touchdowns. That’s his second straight three-touchdown game. And he did it after very limited practice reps all week.

And look at his season totals now. With Saturday’s game, Russell is up to roughly two thousand four hundred eighty passing yards, twenty-one touchdown passes and six interceptions on the season, plus two hundred seventy-six rushing yards and five rushing touchdowns coming into the day. He’s a Maxwell Award semifinalist, he’s nineteen years old, and he just played two straight road games on an injured ankle and threw six touchdown passes. That’s toughness, and that’s production.

And here’s what impresses me most. In the first half, when the offense was stuck and Vanderbilt led three to nothing, Russell didn’t panic. He kept taking what the defense gave him, and when the two-minute drill came, he led a seventy-five-yard drive and threw a touchdown with six seconds left. Then he came out of halftime and went seventy-five yards again. Ryan Grubb said this week that “the bigger moments are fewer and further between” for Russell, meaning the big mistakes are disappearing. Saturday wasn’t perfect, but in the biggest moments of the game, the two-minute drill and the opening drive of the second half, he was at his best.

Now, let’s be honest about the things that need work, because that’s what we do here on Alabama Tunnel. And there were a few.

First, the turnovers. After a turnover-free game at LSU, Alabama gave the ball away twice on Saturday. Russell threw an interception on a tipped ball, which was a bit unlucky. And he lost a fumble on a strip sack in the fourth quarter. Remember Ryan Grubb’s ritual this week, asking Russell every day how many turnovers he was going to have? The answer was supposed to be zero. Saturday was a reminder of why that ritual matters.

Second, the sacks. Russell was sacked three times. Grubb talked this week about wanting Russell to protect himself and get the ball out of his hands instead of taking negative plays, especially with that ankle. The protection and the decision-making will both need to be sharper.

Third, and most important, the run game. Daniel Hill had a really nice day, with eighty-nine yards on fourteen carries. That’s encouraging. But Alabama finished with just ninety-two rushing yards as a team. That’s the fourth straight game Alabama has been held under one hundred rushing yards. DeBoer and Grubb both said this week that improving the run game is the number one priority. It still needs work, especially with Josh Ford out.

And fourth, the slow start. The first quarter was all punts, and Alabama trailed three to nothing in the second quarter. That’s become a pattern this season. The national reaction reflected that too. The Associated Press called it a “sloppy” win, and some writers said it did little to help Alabama’s College Football Playoff case.

DeBoer knows it. After the game, he said, “We know we got to continue to move on, continue to improve.” He also stressed that Alabama needs to do a better job finishing off opponents. That’s the right message. You celebrate the win, and then you get back to work.

But here’s the other side, and it’s important. Great teams win ugly games. This was a road game, with an early kickoff, against a team that beat Alabama in this same stadium two years ago, a week after an emotional win in Death Valley, with the quarterback playing on a bad ankle. That’s exactly the kind of game that trips up good teams. And Alabama won it by fifteen points without allowing a touchdown. That’s a win.

And remember 2024. That year, Alabama came into Nashville after a huge win over Georgia and lost forty to thirty-five. DeBoer brought that up in the locker room right after LSU, saying, “A lot of these guys don’t have the memory from a couple years ago. Big win, then going up to Vanderbilt.” This team made sure it didn’t happen again. That’s growth. That’s a program learning from its past.

And with this win, DeBoer is now twenty-eight and ten at Alabama. More importantly, he’s now beaten Vanderbilt in Nashville, a place that gave him one of the toughest days of his first season. That matters. Every coach has a game that stays with him, and DeBoer clearly wanted this one. He didn’t hide from the memory. He addressed it head-on, his players responded, and now that chapter is closed.

So who’s my player of the game? There are a lot of good choices. Keelon Russell, with three touchdown passes on a bad ankle. Lotzeir Brooks, with two touchdown catches. Dijon Lee Jr., whose eighty-three-yard kickoff return killed Vanderbilt’s momentum right when the Commodores were getting back into it. But for me, it’s the entire Alabama defense. Holding an SEC opponent without a touchdown, to two hundred thirty-five yards, two for fourteen on third down, with five sacks from five different players and three takeaways, is the kind of performance that wins championships. When the offense was sputtering in the first half, the defense kept Alabama in control. Honorable mention to Lee, because that return might have been the single most important play of the game.

Now let’s look at the rest of the SEC, because Saturday had a big impact on the race.

Georgia beat Missouri forty-eight to twenty-one to stay in front of the SEC race. Tennessee beat Texas A&M twenty-two to seven. And Texas went into Baton Rouge and beat LSU twenty-two to twenty-one in a thriller. So Alabama, Tennessee and Texas all keep winning, and the tiebreaker picture for the second spot in Atlanta is still very much alive. Alabama is six and two in conference play with one SEC game left: the Iron Bowl against Auburn.

And remember, DeBoer said this week that he wants to be in the SEC Championship Game and win it. He said, “I’ve not had a chance to win one.” With the College Football Playoff expanding to twenty-four teams next season, and reports saying conference title games are expected to end as we know them, this year’s SEC Championship Game could be the last one in its current form. So there’s a lot riding on the next two weeks.

As for the playoff, Alabama was third in the last College Football Playoff rankings. The next rankings come out on Tuesday night. A win is a win, and Alabama is still eight and two, with three wins over teams that were ranked in the last College Football Playoff rankings: Kentucky, South Carolina and LSU. We’ll see how the committee views this one.

Some people will say a fifteen-point win over a four-and-five team isn’t impressive enough. And it’s fair to say Alabama didn’t play its best offensive game. But think about what the committee actually looks at. Who did you beat, where did you beat them, and how did your defense perform? Alabama won on the road in the SEC, didn’t allow a touchdown, and held an opponent to its lowest yardage total of the season. Meanwhile, several teams around Alabama have their own tough tests. Holding a top-four spot and that first-round bye will come down to the final two weeks: handle Chattanooga, and then win the Iron Bowl. If Alabama does that, it’ll be ten and two with one of the strongest résumés in the country. Style points matter a lot less than wins in November.

Now, let’s talk about what’s next.

Alabama comes home to Bryant-Denny Stadium to host Chattanooga on Saturday, November twenty-first. And the big question will be the same one we’ve been asking all week. What does Alabama do with Keelon Russell? DeBoer said after the game that Alabama will keep weighing “what we think is best for Keelon and for our football team moving forward.” And he said, “If he can go, we’ll expect him to go.”

So this is going to be another week of watching the availability reports. It wouldn’t surprise me if Alabama is careful with Russell against an FCS opponent, and maybe gives Austin Mack the start he prepared for this week. But based on what DeBoer said, if Russell is healthy, he’ll play. Either way, the goal is clear: have Russell at full strength for the Iron Bowl against Auburn on November twenty-eighth.

And against Chattanooga, I want to see the run game take a step forward. Daniel Hill showed what he can do. EJ Crowell is getting healthier. This is a great opportunity to build some rhythm on the ground before the Iron Bowl.

It’s also a chance to clean up the details. Fewer penalties. Fewer negative plays. Better finishing in the red zone and better closing out of games, which DeBoer specifically mentioned after Saturday. And it’s a chance to get some younger players valuable snaps. Remember, this roster has been hit hard by injuries, with players like Josh Ford, AK Dear, Desmond Umeozulu, Cayden Jones and others out. Every healthy rep for the depth players is an investment in December. A clean, dominant performance against Chattanooga would be the perfect way to build momentum for the Iron Bowl.

So let me sum it all up. Alabama made a huge quarterback decision at the very last minute. ESPN reported Austin Mack would start, but Keelon Russell improved so much in the final days that he ended up starting and playing every snap. He threw for two hundred seventeen yards and three touchdowns on a bad ankle. Marshall Pritchett scored right before halftime. Lotzeir Brooks caught two touchdowns. Dijon Lee Jr. changed the game with an eighty-three-yard kick return. And Kane Wommack’s defense held Vanderbilt without a touchdown, to just two hundred thirty-five yards, two for fourteen on third down, with five sacks and three takeaways. Austin Mack prepared like a starter and stayed ready like a pro. And Alabama got its revenge in Nashville. The offense still has work to do, especially in the run game, but Alabama is eight and two and still in control of its own path.
