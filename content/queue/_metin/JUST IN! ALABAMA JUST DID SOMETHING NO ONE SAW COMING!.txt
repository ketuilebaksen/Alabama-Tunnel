On Saturday, Alabama will do something it has never had to think twice about. It will play an FCS opponent the week before the Iron Bowl. But Tide Nation, this time is different. This is the last time it will ever happen. After this season, the SEC is ending the November “cupcake week” for good. A tradition that has been part of Alabama football for almost two decades comes to a close on Saturday against Chattanooga. And on top of that, Kalen DeBoer opened up this week about special teams, the offense’s rhythm, Keelon Russell’s ankle, and how Alabama builds its roster behind the scenes. Let’s get into all of it.

What’s up, everybody, James here, and welcome back to Alabama Tunnel. Alabama is eight and two, six and two in the SEC, and number three in the College Football Playoff rankings. On Saturday, the Crimson Tide host Chattanooga at Bryant-Denny Stadium at one p.m. Central on SEC Network Plus. It’s Senior Day. It’s Daniel Hill’s twenty-first birthday. And it’s the end of an era. Today we’re going to break down the history behind this final cupcake week, everything DeBoer said in his Monday press conference that we haven’t covered yet, and what it all means heading into the Iron Bowl. If you’re new here, hit that subscribe button and turn on notifications so you never miss an update here on Alabama Tunnel. Let’s go.

Let’s start with the news that a lot of people didn’t see coming: the end of cupcake week.

Back in May, SEC athletic directors voted to start playing conference games on the second-to-last weekend of the regular season. That’s the weekend right before Thanksgiving, the same weekend when SEC teams have traditionally scheduled an FCS opponent before their big rivalry game. Starting next season, that weekend will be filled with SEC matchups instead. So Saturday’s game against Chattanooga will be the final time SEC teams face FCS opponents in November.

SEC commissioner Greg Sankey even had a little fun with it, joking about the nickname: “We never got that one sponsored.”

For Alabama, this is a big change. Let’s go back through the history. In 2006, an NCAA rule change expanded the regular season to twelve games and allowed teams to count a win over an FCS opponent toward bowl eligibility every year. That’s when the trend of scheduling an FCS team before Thanksgiving really took off across the SEC.

And for Alabama, it became a tradition. In 2009, 2010, all the way through 2019, and again in 2022, 2023 and 2025, Alabama played an FCS team the week before the Iron Bowl. Chattanooga has been one of the most familiar opponents, coming to Tuscaloosa in 2009, 2013, 2016 and 2023. Western Carolina came three times. Georgia Southern came in 2011. Mercer came in 2017. And Eastern Illinois came last season.

There were a few exceptions. In 2020, the season was SEC-only because of the pandemic. In 2021, Alabama played Mercer in September instead. And in 2024, Alabama played Mercer two weeks before the Iron Bowl, then went on the road the following week and lost at Oklahoma. Some fans still point to that season as a reminder of how much the timing of these games can matter. Having a lighter game right before your rivalry game gives your team a chance to rest key players and get healthy. Without it, the grind of SEC football never lets up.

And the results? Alabama has been dominant in these games. It has won all sixteen of its FCS matchups in that late-season slot since 2009, by an average of about forty-five points. Its last loss in a cupcake-week game came all the way back in 2007, against Louisiana-Monroe, in Nick Saban’s first season. Since then, the Tide have won eighteen straight games on cupcake week.

But not every one of those games was easy. Alabama fans remember the 2011 Georgia Southern game, when the Eagles ran for over three hundred yards against the Tide. And in 2018, The Citadel was tied with Alabama ten to ten at halftime before the Tide pulled away. So even in these games, you can never just show up and expect to win. You still have to play.

And now the tradition moves to the beginning of the season. Alabama’s future FCS games are already lined up for early in the year: Mercer in September of 2027, UT Martin in September of 2028, and Gardner-Webb in October of 2029.

So what did DeBoer think about it? He was asked about it on Monday, and his answer was classic DeBoer. He said, “Wherever the game’s at, for us, it’s just about right now, about improving and focusing on us and our standard.”

And here’s something a lot of people don’t realize: DeBoer has coached at the lower levels of college football himself. He knows exactly what these games mean for the other team. He said, “Having been in those, part of those programs, these are big moments, and cool opportunities to go in and kind of make a statement and do your thing.” He talked about how much momentum a smaller program can build when it competes with, or even beats, a big-time program. He understands that Chattanooga will treat Saturday like the biggest game of its season.

DeBoer also talked about how the future schedule will look. He said, “There’s going to be twelve games, and we got to play the ten Power Four games, nine in our schedule and at least one outside.” So nine SEC games plus at least one more Power Four opponent outside the conference every year. That’s a tough schedule, and it’s one of the reasons the SEC made this change.

Defensive coordinator Kane Wommack, who has also coached at the FCS level, added some perspective. He said changes in college football have created “a greater margin in between teams at the FBS level and the FCS level.” But he made it clear Alabama won’t take Chattanooga lightly. He said, “Obviously, you look at this team in Chattanooga, good football team, has an identity of physicality.” And he said, “It always comes down to being about us and executing at a high level regardless of opponent.”

So Tide Nation, enjoy this one on Saturday. It’s the last of its kind. For almost twenty years, this weekend has been a chance for young players to get their first snaps at Bryant-Denny, for backups to shine and for fans to celebrate before rivalry week. After Saturday, that chapter of Alabama football closes for good.

Now, let’s talk about the opponent, because Chattanooga is not your typical cupcake.

The Mocs are seven and four overall and six and two in the Southern Conference. They closed out their conference schedule with a seventeen to ten win over Furman. And here’s the number that jumps off the page: Chattanooga has the best scoring defense in the entire FCS, allowing just twelve point seven points per game. That’s a real defense.

Head coach Rusty Wright has a great sense of humor about this matchup. He described playing Alabama as “climbing Everest without a sherpa.” But his team has a lot to play for. Chattanooga is hoping its record is good enough to earn a spot in the FCS playoffs. DeBoer acknowledged that on Monday. He said Coach Wright “has a football team that’s got some playoff hopes,” and that with their record, they’ve “had a really good season.”

So Alabama needs to respect this opponent. A team with the best scoring defense in the FCS and playoff hopes on the line is going to come out swinging. And remember, Alabama has struggled with slow starts lately. This is a chance to fix that.

Historically, Chattanooga has never beaten Alabama. The Tide have won all of the previous meetings, and the four games this century have been lopsided, including a sixty-six to ten Alabama win in 2023. But history doesn’t play the game on Saturday. Execution does.

Now let’s go back to the beginning of DeBoer’s Monday press conference, because he opened with something that a lot of people missed: special teams.

DeBoer started by talking about the importance of the win at Vanderbilt. He said every SEC win is important, “especially the ones on the road,” and noted it was Alabama’s “fifth road SEC game.” He said he’s proud of the way his players “continue to stack the days and stack games” and keep moving forward.

And then he went straight to special teams. He said, “One thing to continue to highlight that I’m proud of also is just the special teams, and the two returns in particular.” The big one, of course, was Dijon Lee Jr.’s eighty-three-yard kickoff return that set up Alabama’s third touchdown. But DeBoer also mentioned a punt return from Ryan Coleman-Williams. He said it wasn’t as impactful as Lee’s, but he loved the “good individual efforts” and “smart decisions” being made. And remember, Coleman-Williams also had a big thirty-two-yard punt return in the fourth quarter at LSU the week before.

DeBoer pointed out that return units can be a dangerous place for penalties. He said, “A lot of penalties can happen in those moments, a lot of space to cover.” But Alabama has been clean. And he gave credit to experience. He said there are still “a few first-year players out there, but a lot of second-year guys, and even some third-year guys” executing on special teams.

He also praised the coverage units. He said, “I think our coverage units continue to be consistent.” A lot of Alabama’s kickoffs go through the back of the end zone. But when the ball is returned, the coverage has been excellent. And DeBoer gave a shout-out to an unexpected name: wide receiver Lotzeir Brooks. He said that “a lot of times, you would never expect him to be the first guy to make that play, and he’s hunting guys down.” Think about that. Brooks caught two touchdown passes against Vanderbilt, and he’s also flying down the field on coverage. That’s a team-first player.

DeBoer finished that part by saying punt coverage “continues to be done at a high level because of effort. And as long as we have that, we’ll continue to have that success.” Kicker Conor Talty has also been reliable, twelve of fourteen on field goals this season. Special teams have quietly become a real strength for this team, and in November, that matters a lot.

Then DeBoer turned to Chattanooga and Senior Day. He said Alabama will recognize nineteen players, “nine scholarship and ten walk-ons.” And he said, “Proud of these guys and what they have all done for the program. Want to make it a special day.” He also explained the timing. He said, “I know it’s not our last home game, but thought it was a good choice to pick this one earlier in the year when we were kind of deciding on these dates.” The Iron Bowl is at home on November twenty-eighth, so the seniors will get one more game at Bryant-Denny after their big day.

And I love that DeBoer made a point to mention the walk-ons. Ten of the players being recognized are walk-ons. Those are guys who came to Alabama without a scholarship, who show up every day in practice, who give everything on the scout team, and who make the starters better. They don’t get the headlines, but they’re a huge part of this program. Tide Nation, let’s give them a big ovation on Saturday.

DeBoer also talked about mindset. He said, “One-and-oh mindset. Nothing carries over from week to week, the good, bad or the ugly. It’s all about resetting and our focus moving forward.” He said there are always “new obstacles” and “new opportunities,” and that he thinks the team has “a good mindset moving forward” to build off what it’s done the last few weeks.

Now let’s get to the offense, because that was the first question DeBoer got from reporters. He was asked what needs to happen for the offense to find a better rhythm in games.

DeBoer was direct. He said, “People are going to look at third downs, and we were six for fifteen.” But he said the real issue is “early efficiency on early downs, and execution.” In other words, if you’re better on first and second down, you’re not facing third and long as often.

He said Alabama’s play calls are sound. He said, “We’re picking concepts that are ones that we should be able to roll with.” And then he said something really interesting: “A lot of it has to just do with one guy.” What he means is that on any given play, if just one player misses an assignment, the whole play can break down. He added, “It’s never as big as you think, never as small as you think.”

And he explained that execution goes beyond the play itself. He said, “Execution isn’t just about the play itself. It’s the pre-snap, it’s the communication, it’s all those types of things.” That’s a coach who knows exactly where the problems are. It’s not about a lack of talent or bad schemes. It’s about details. And DeBoer said, “It’s amazing what can happen” when everyone focuses on doing their small details right.

He also pointed out that continuity in practice would help, which ties right into the next topic.

A reporter asked DeBoer about Ryan Grubb’s comment that the offense didn’t have a good week of preparation heading into Vanderbilt. And DeBoer explained why. He said, “You got guys in and out, right? I can think of at least three that were significantly…” and then he mentioned that the quarterback was one of them. He said there were guys at other positions too who “went on the football field and tried to give it a go.”

But DeBoer made sure to defend his team’s effort. He said, “It’s not like the effort’s not there. Our team gives great effort every single day.” He said the coaching staff is pushing the players, and that when the team falls short of what it wants, “there’s usually other reasons for that.” He also said that ten weeks into the season, familiarity with the system is good, “but you still got to match that up against the opponent,” and that Vanderbilt does some things that make it hard to just “walk out on the football field and go get it done.”

So the message is clear. The offense’s struggles at Vanderbilt were more about a disrupted week of practice, with injuries to key players, than about effort or ability. And that’s why health is so important right now.

Which brings us to Keelon Russell. A reporter asked DeBoer about Russell’s status and what the offense can do to limit his exposure to more injuries.

DeBoer was honest. He said, “Anytime you step on the football field, you got that risk, right? And so, that is what it is.” He said Russell “got hit a couple times, but I think he got through relatively clean.” And he said the injury “wasn’t gone completely, so it’s just a matter of managing it, and kind of see as the week goes on where we’re at.”

And since then, the updates have been positive. On his radio show Wednesday, DeBoer said, “He continues to improve,” and that there were “no setbacks or anything on Saturday,” while adding there’s “still work to be done” to get Russell to one hundred percent. Ryan Grubb described Russell’s status as “status quo, good to go.” And according to Field Level Media, Russell has been listed as probable for Saturday. So all signs point to Russell being ready to play against Chattanooga.

The final topic DeBoer covered was one we don’t hear about very often: how Alabama builds its roster.

A reporter asked about the transfer class and how the staff works with Courtney Morgan, Alabama’s general manager, to put it together. And DeBoer gave a really interesting look behind the curtain. He said, “It’s a year-round process.” He explained that it starts with evaluating Alabama’s own roster first, “all the way through the spring, the summer,” and that it changes as young players develop and show more.

Then he said the staff is always looking ahead to January, when the transfer portal opens. He said, “Your head’s always on a swivel. You’re first taking care of your own roster, but you’re also mindful of what’s going to happen and trying to be ahead of the game when January comes around.”

He said last year’s transfer class was built to fit Alabama’s needs, and that some positions needed more help than others. He said the offensive line “was probably the area we needed the most help from a number standpoint, not just quality.” So the staff wasn’t just looking for starters. It was looking for depth, “the ones, but also the twos and maybe even some threes.”

And DeBoer said that work never stops. The staff has constant recruiting meetings, both as a full staff and with the coordinators on each side of the ball. He said, “It’s a continual and never-ending kind of journey.” And right now, the staff is managing two things at once: finishing the season strong and preparing for the high school signing class that’s coming up soon, along with figuring out “what the roster shakeup’s going to be” with guys moving on and graduating.

That’s a great reminder of how much work goes on behind the scenes in modern college football. While fans are focused on Saturday, the staff is also planning for next season, every single day.

So what do I want to see from Alabama on Saturday? Here are my keys.

Key number one: start fast. Alabama has had slow starts in recent weeks, including nearly twenty-nine minutes without a point to start the Vanderbilt game. Against the best scoring defense in the FCS, an early touchdown or two would set the tone and take the crowd’s energy to another level.

Key number two: win on early downs. DeBoer said it himself. Better efficiency on first and second down means fewer third and longs. If Alabama can stay ahead of the chains, the offense will find its rhythm.

Key number three: get the run game going. Alabama has been held under one hundred rushing yards in four straight games. Daniel Hill had eighty-nine yards on fourteen carries at Vanderbilt, and Saturday is his twenty-first birthday. Let’s see him, Trae’shawn Brown and the rest of the backs break that streak.

Key number four: protect Keelon Russell. Quick throws, clean protection, and if the game is under control, an early exit for Russell so Austin Mack can get some meaningful snaps.

And key number five: force takeaways. DeBoer was frustrated that the defense didn’t get any at Vanderbilt. Chattanooga will want to play a physical, ball-control game, so a turnover or two could break this game open.

If Alabama checks those boxes, it’ll head into Iron Bowl week healthy, confident and rolling.

So let’s put it all together.

Saturday is the end of an era. Alabama’s final November cupcake game, after almost two decades of tradition. But Chattanooga isn’t a pushover. It has the best scoring defense in the FCS and is playing for an FCS playoff spot. Alabama has to come out sharp and start fast.

DeBoer wants his team focused on a “one-and-oh mindset.” He’s proud of the special teams, which have made huge plays the last two weeks. He knows the offense needs to be more efficient on early downs and cleaner in its execution. He explained that the tough week of practice before Vanderbilt was mostly about injuries, not effort. And he’s managing Russell’s ankle carefully, with the latest signs pointing to Russell being ready to go.

And then comes the big one. One week later, Auburn comes to Bryant-Denny for the Iron Bowl. And with a win in that game, Alabama could clinch a second straight trip to the SEC Championship Game, depending on other results. Add in the number three ranking in the College Football Playoff, and there’s a lot on the line in the next two weeks. Alabama is second in the SEC standings at six and two, and the path to Atlanta runs straight through Auburn. And the top four teams in the final CFP rankings earn a first-round bye. So every snap over the next two Saturdays matters.
