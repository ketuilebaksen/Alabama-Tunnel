Let’s start with thing number one you can count on today: Alabama should win this football game.

Now, let me be clear. That doesn’t mean Chattanooga is a bad team. Far from it. The Mocs are seven and four overall and six and two in the Southern Conference. They’ve won six of their last eight games, and one of those losses came in overtime. They closed out conference play with a seventeen to ten win over Furman. And they have the best scoring defense in the entire FCS, giving up just twelve point seven points per game. They’re also hoping to earn a spot in the FCS playoffs, so they have plenty to play for.

Head coach Rusty Wright summed up the challenge with a great line, calling this game “climbing Everest without a sherpa.” Kane Wommack said Chattanooga is a “good football team” with “an identity of physicality.” And DeBoer said Coach Wright “has a football team that’s got some playoff hopes” and has “had a really good season.”

So Chattanooga deserves respect. But Alabama has more depth, more size and more speed on both sides of the ball. The Tide have never lost to Chattanooga. The last meeting, in 2023, was a sixty-six to ten Alabama win. And Alabama has won all sixteen of its late-season FCS games since 2009. If Alabama plays its brand of football, it should take care of business today.

Now, thing number two you can count on: Alabama is going to make running the football a priority.

This one is almost guaranteed. DeBoer and offensive coordinator Ryan Grubb know the run game has to show more life. Alabama has been held under one hundred rushing yards in four straight games. And against a team like Chattanooga, the coaching staff will want to establish the ground game early, build confidence up front and give the running backs a chance to find a rhythm before the Iron Bowl.

And there are good reasons to expect a strong day. Daniel Hill is coming off his best yards-per-carry game of the season, with eighty-nine yards on fourteen carries at Vanderbilt. DeBoer said afterward, “Thought Daniel ran really hard, did a nice job there. That was important for us.” And today is Hill’s twenty-first birthday, at home, on Senior Day. Trae’shawn Brown, the freshman who scored touchdowns in each of Alabama’s first three games, will want to get his rhythm back too. And if EJ Crowell is healthy enough to contribute, he gives this offense a different kind of explosiveness.

Plus, running the ball well is the best way to protect Keelon Russell, who’s still managing his ankle injury. Every successful run is one less drop-back, one less hit, one less chance for the ankle to get aggravated. So expect the staff to lean on the ground game, and expect the box score to look good.

And that brings us to thing number three, which is the most exciting one: today can be the start of something. Not the finish line, but the launching pad. The real tests are still ahead in the Iron Bowl and the postseason, and this is the perfect week to build toward them.

Let me explain with some numbers, and I want you to look at the trend, because the trend is the good part. According to Bama Hammer, Alabama is averaging three point five nine rushing yards per play in SEC games this season. Yes, that’s lower than Alabama fans are used to. But look at where it came from.

Here’s the important context. This isn’t a brand-new problem, and it didn’t start with DeBoer or Grubb. In 2023, Nick Saban’s final season, Alabama averaged three point eight five rushing yards per play in SEC games, even with Jalen Milroe’s legs. That ranked eighty-eighth in the FBS. And in 2025, Alabama averaged just two point eight seven rushing yards per play against SEC opponents. Only six FBS teams were worse.

So the run game has been a challenge across college football’s best programs for a few years now, including at Alabama. And here’s the encouraging part: the 2026 numbers are a clear improvement over last season. Alabama has gone from two point eight seven yards per rushing play in SEC games to three point five nine. That’s real progress in one year. The trend is moving in the right direction, and there’s still time for it to keep climbing.

And remember how this season started. In the opener against East Carolina, Alabama ran for two hundred twenty-seven yards and five touchdowns in a forty-eight to ten win. Daniel Hill scored twice. Russell, Crowell and Brown each scored once. And Russell led the team with eighty-six rushing yards in his first career start. Then in the SEC opener at Kentucky, Hill ran for one hundred four yards on eighteen carries. It looked like the run game was turning a corner.

Then the schedule got tougher in October and November, and the production cooled off for a few weeks. That happens to almost every team in the SEC. But it also tells us something positive: when this offense is healthy and in rhythm, it has already shown it can run the ball at a high level. Two hundred twenty-seven yards and five touchdowns in the opener. A hundred-yard day for Hill at Kentucky. That ability hasn’t gone anywhere. It just needs to be unlocked again.

Now, let’s talk about the good news, because there’s a lot of it.

Alabama’s offense is still one of the most explosive in the country. According to Bama Hammer, Alabama and Georgia lead the SEC in passing plays of twenty, thirty and forty-plus yards. And Alabama is tied for sixth in the SEC in rushing plays of twenty yards or more. So the big plays are there, both through the air and on the ground. This offense scored forty-five or more points in each of its first five games. It can explode at any moment.

And the committee chair said it himself on Tuesday: Alabama is in the top five nationally in relative scoring offense. Its margin of victory in SEC wins is second only to Georgia. So this is not a broken offense. Far from it. It’s a dangerous offense that’s looking for more consistency.

The issue isn’t explosiveness. It’s consistency. It’s the ability to get two or three yards when everybody in the stadium knows you’re going to run. Third and one. Fourth and one. Short yardage at the goal line. Those are the moments where a strong running game wins championships.

And here’s the thing: we’ve already seen signs of progress in exactly those moments. At Vanderbilt, Daniel Hill converted a third and one with a three-yard run. And in the fourth quarter, on third and two, he broke off a twelve-yard run to keep a drive alive and help Alabama run the clock. Those are the plays that matter most. If Alabama can keep doing that against Auburn and in the postseason, this offense becomes very hard to stop.

So why should Alabama fans feel confident that the run game can break out? Let me give you five reasons.

Reason number one: Daniel Hill has momentum. Eighty-nine yards on fourteen carries at Vanderbilt, six point four yards per carry, with clutch conversions on third down. He reshaped his body this offseason, made Bruce Feldman’s Freaks List and worked hard on his pass protection. He’s playing with confidence right now, and confident running backs are dangerous.

Reason number two: Trae’shawn Brown has already proven he can do it against the best. Remember, this freshman ran for ninety yards on just nine carries against Georgia, the number one team in the country. He had sixty-six yards and two touchdowns at Mississippi State. He scored a touchdown in each of Alabama’s first three games, and he shares the team lead in rushing touchdowns. That kind of talent doesn’t disappear. It’s waiting for its moment.

Reason number three: EJ Crowell brings a different gear. He came to Alabama as one of the top running back recruits in the country, and when he’s been healthy, he’s been explosive, with four touchdowns this season. If he’s available down the stretch, he gives Alabama a home-run threat out of the backfield.

Reason number four: Keelon Russell can run. In his very first career start against East Carolina, Russell led the team with eighty-six rushing yards and scored a touchdown. He shares the team lead in rushing touchdowns. Defenses have to account for him, and that opens lanes for the running backs. As his ankle keeps improving, that dual-threat ability becomes a weapon again.

And reason number five: the offensive line is getting healthier and deeper. DeBoer said Alabama is going to “continue to get guys back here these last couple weeks.” The staff built this line with depth in mind, and more continuity in practice means better timing on game day. Run blocking is all about five guys moving as one, and that only gets better with health and reps.

Put those five things together, and you can see why the ceiling for this run game is still very high. This is a backfield with a veteran leader, two talented freshmen and a quarterback who can make defenses pay with his legs. That’s a group with real upside, and the best part is that its best football might still be ahead of it.

Now let’s connect the run game to Keelon Russell, because the two are closely linked.

Russell has been terrific this season. He’s thrown for nearly two thousand five hundred yards and twenty-one touchdowns as a nineteen-year-old sophomore. He went into Death Valley and helped Alabama win by eighteen. He went into Nashville on a bad ankle, after barely practicing, and threw three touchdown passes. That’s toughness and talent.

And here’s why a stronger run game could make him even better. When the run game carries more of the load, the quarterback doesn’t have to do everything himself. He gets more favorable down-and-distance situations. Play-action opens up. Defenses can’t sit back and wait for the pass. At Vanderbilt, two of the plays that went wrong were a tipped interception and a strip sack, the kind of plays that happen less often when an offense is balanced.

That’s not a knock on Russell. It’s the nature of quarterback play. Every quarterback in the country is better when he has a reliable running game. And that’s why fixing the run game isn’t just about the running backs. It’s about protecting and supporting the young quarterback who has carried this offense all season.

Grubb has already talked about wanting Russell to “get the football out of his hand.” And DeBoer said this week that Russell’s ankle “wasn’t gone completely,” and that it’s “just a matter of managing it.” So the more Alabama can run the ball effectively, the better Russell’s chances of staying healthy and sharp for December.

So what’s the good news about fixing the run game? DeBoer gave us a lot of clues this week.

When he was asked about the offense’s rhythm, he said the issue is “early efficiency on early downs, and execution.” He said Alabama is “picking concepts that are ones that we should be able to roll with.” And then he said something that really stuck with me: “A lot of it has to just do with one guy.” In other words, one missed block, one wrong step, one miscommunication can blow up a whole play. He said, “It’s never as big as you think, never as small as you think.” And he said execution “isn’t just about the play itself. It’s the pre-snap, it’s the communication.”

That’s encouraging, because those are fixable problems. They’re not about talent. They’re about details and repetition.

DeBoer also talked about continuity. He said that before the Vanderbilt game, there were “at least three” key players who were “in and out” of practice, including the quarterback. When players are missing reps during the week, the timing of the whole offense suffers. And that includes the run game, which depends on all five offensive linemen, the tight ends and the running back being perfectly in sync.

But DeBoer also delivered good news on that front after the Vanderbilt game. He said, “Fortunately, I think we’re going to continue to get guys back here these last couple weeks.” And he said getting healthier “is going to be the key as we make this final run.” So if Alabama can get closer to full strength, the run game could take a real step forward at exactly the right time.

There’s also the roster-building side. DeBoer explained this week that the offensive line was the area where Alabama “needed the most help from a number standpoint, not just quality,” when it built last year’s transfer class. So the staff has been working on depth up front all year. As those players get more experience together, the run blocking should continue to improve.

And let’s give some love to the big guys up front on Senior Day. Several offensive linemen are being honored today, including Baker Hickman, Davis Peterson, Billy Roby and Mac Smith. These are players who have put in years of work in the weight room and on the practice field, often away from the spotlight. Offensive linemen rarely get the headlines, but they’re the heart of any great running game. Wouldn’t it be perfect if the run game had its best day of the month on the afternoon those linemen are honored? Tide Nation, when those guys are introduced, make some noise.

So what should you actually watch for today? Here’s my guide to evaluating the run game against Chattanooga, beyond just the final rushing total.

Number one: watch the first quarter. That’s when Alabama’s starting offensive line and top running backs will be on the field together. How does the run game look against Chattanooga’s starting defense, which is the best scoring defense in the FCS? Are the running backs getting to the second level? Is the line creating push? That will tell you a lot more than the yards that come late in the game against backups.

Number two: watch short-yardage situations. Third and one, fourth and one, goal-line runs. Can Alabama get the yards it needs when Chattanooga knows the run is coming? That’s the exact situation that will matter in the Iron Bowl and the postseason.

Number three: watch the early downs. DeBoer said early-down efficiency is the key. If Alabama is gaining four or five yards on first-down runs, the offense stays ahead of the chains, and the whole playbook opens up.

Number four: watch the running back rotation. Does Daniel Hill get the bulk of the early carries? How does Trae’shawn Brown look? Does EJ Crowell get involved? The staff will want to see who’s running with confidence heading into Auburn.

And number five: watch how Russell is used. If Alabama can run the ball effectively, Russell might not need to play the whole game. An early exit for the starting quarterback would be a great sign that the offense, including the run game, did its job.

If Alabama checks those boxes, then the rushing numbers today will mean something, even if they come against an FCS opponent.

And here’s something else working in Alabama’s favor: the rest of the team makes the run game’s job easier. The defense has allowed just one touchdown in its last eight quarters, which means the offense keeps getting the ball back with good field position. Special teams have been outstanding, with Dijon Lee Jr.’s eighty-three-yard kickoff return at Vanderbilt and Ryan Coleman-Williams making plays in the return game. Conor Talty is twelve of fourteen on field goals. When you’re starting drives on a short field, the run game doesn’t have to be perfect. It just has to keep the chains moving.
