Great news number one. The health picture.

Let’s start with Thursday night’s availability report. For Alabama, it looked exactly like Wednesday’s. Center Racin Delgatty, questionable with a knee injury. Wide receiver Ryan Coleman-Williams, questionable with a knee injury. Guard William Sanders, questionable with an ankle injury. Cornerback Zabien Brown, questionable with a lower leg injury. Defensive lineman Terrance Green, questionable with an ankle injury. And running backs EJ Crowell and AK Dear, both questionable as well.

Now, some people might look at that and say, “Nothing changed. Why is that good news?” Here’s why. When players are coming back from injuries, Thursday is a big test. By Thursday, the team has had several days of practice, and the staff has seen how each player’s body responds to being pushed harder. If a player has a setback, that’s usually when you see him downgraded to doubtful or out. And that didn’t happen to a single Alabama starter. All five are still in play for Saturday. Nobody went backwards. That’s exactly what you want to see the day before the team travels to Baton Rouge. And it’s worth remembering how the system works. Teams have to submit these reports on Wednesday, Thursday and Friday nights, and then again about ninety minutes before kickoff on game day. Players are listed as probable, questionable, doubtful or out. Probable means the player is very likely to play. Questionable means it’s a real possibility and the staff will decide later. Doubtful means it’s unlikely. And out means out. So Alabama has kept all five starters in that questionable category through Thursday, which tells you the staff still believes each one has a legitimate chance to go. The Friday report and the final pregame report will tell the rest of the story. And honestly, sometimes coaches keep players listed as questionable until game day for strategic reasons too. It forces the opponent to prepare for both possibilities. LSU has to game plan for Delgatty at center and for Waldrep. For Coleman-Williams on the field and off it. That uncertainty is a small advantage for Alabama.

And remember what DeBoer said on Monday. He said, “They’re not all in the exact same spot, but they’re all out there, going through workouts.” He added that the progress by all of them is heading in a good direction, and that a lot depends on how their bodies respond after they’re pushed a little more. Well, they’ve now been pushed through two more days of practice, and the reports haven’t changed. That’s encouraging.

Now let’s talk about Ryan Coleman-Williams, because his story is a special one. After the loss to Georgia on October tenth, he had arthroscopic surgery on a meniscus injury in his knee, and he’s missed the last two games. And now, according to Tiger Rag, Lane Kiffin himself said, “I bet Ryan will be back.” When the opposing head coach is preparing for your receiver to play, that tells you something.

And here’s why this matters so much, not just for the game, but for Ryan himself. Some of you might still know him as Ryan Williams. Before this season, he changed his name to Ryan Coleman-Williams to honor his mother, and he switched from number two to number one, the number he wore at Saraland High School. He called it “an opportunity to have a hard reset.” He said, “I have a legacy that I want to write.” And DeBoer said, “It’s great to see his leadership capabilities.”

And he was writing that legacy before he got hurt. Through the early part of this season, he had twenty-three catches for four hundred fifteen yards and four touchdowns. That’s eighteen yards per catch. Remember, as a freshman, he had forty-five catches for eight hundred fifty-seven yards and eight touchdowns, and he became one of the most famous young receivers in the country. Last season was a tougher year for him, and he’d be the first to admit it. But this season, he came back with a new name, a new number and a new mindset, and he was playing like one of the best receivers in the SEC. Then the injury hit. Now, he has a chance to come back on one of the biggest stages of the year. If he plays on Saturday, that’s a storybook moment. Think about the timeline. He got hurt in the Georgia game on October tenth. He had surgery. He missed Tennessee on October seventeenth and Texas A&M on October twenty-fourth. Then came the bye week, which gave him an extra seven days of rehab. On October twenty-sixth, DeBoer said, “I’m hopeful that he’ll be ready for the LSU game.” And on Monday of this week, he was back on the field working out with his teammates. Every step of that timeline has gone the right way. Now it’s about how he feels on Saturday morning. And even when he wasn’t playing, Rico Scott said, “He’s done a good job of being vocal, even when he wasn’t playing, just letting his presence be felt.” That’s leadership.

Now, imagine Alabama’s offense with Coleman-Williams back. Rico Scott, who just had six catches for one hundred fifty-six yards against Texas A&M, on the other side. Lotzeir Brooks in the slot. Marshall Pritchett, Kaleb Edwards and Josh Ford at tight end. LSU’s defense has thirty-four sacks this season, the second most in the country. The best way to slow down a pass rush like that is to have weapons that force defenses to keep extra players in coverage. Coleman-Williams is exactly that kind of weapon.

And it’s not just him. If Delgatty is back at center, Alabama’s offensive line gets its leader back. Through the first six games, with Delgatty starting, the Tide averaged around one hundred eighty-two rushing yards per game. In the two games without him, that number fell to about sixty-one. If Terrance Green is back, Alabama’s defense gets its starting nose tackle back. Yhonzae Pierre already said this week, “We got TG coming back, Terrence Green.” If Zabien Brown is back, Alabama gets a preseason All-American corner back on the outside. And if Crowell is back, Alabama gets a former five-star back who’s averaging six point eight yards per carry. Every single one of those returns would make this team better. And let’s give some credit to the guys who filled in. Mal Waldrep stepped in at center in two of the toughest environments a lineman could face, Knoxville and a must-win home game against A&M. Rico Scott stepped up as the go-to receiver and had the best game of his career. The defensive line rotated bodies and still sacked Marcel Reed when it mattered. That’s the kind of depth that wins championships. And now, if the starters come back, those backups become a deeper rotation. That’s how you stay fresh for the fourth quarter in a game like this.

Now let’s look at the other side of the injury report. LSU’s star linebacker and team captain Whit Weeks was listed as doubtful again on Thursday, and CBS Sports reporter Matt Zenitz reported that Weeks is “expected to be out on Saturday.” Zenitz added, “it sounds like he’s not ready to return yet.”

I want to be really clear about this. We never celebrate an injury. Whit Weeks is a tough, talented player, and we hope he gets back on the field healthy as soon as possible. He broke his fibula against Texas A&M on September twenty-sixth, had surgery, and has been working hard to get back. That’s not easy. But from a pure football standpoint, his absence matters. Weeks had twenty-three tackles in four games before he got hurt. And the last time he faced Alabama, in 2024, he had fifteen tackles. Fifteen! That was in the game Alabama won forty-two to thirteen in Baton Rouge, but he was everywhere that night. Without him, LSU has had Dahvon Keys step in at the second linebacker spot.

And according to Heavy, LSU has also ruled out safety Dashawn Spears, wide receiver Phillip Wright III, linebacker Charles Ross II and defensive tackle Richard Anderson. Linebacker Zach Weeks, Whit’s younger brother, is listed as questionable, and backup defensive tackle Stephiylan Green, a transfer from Clemson, was added to the report as probable. So LSU is dealing with injuries too. After Weeks went down earlier this season, Kiffin said, “I’ve not been around this many significant injuries early in the year like this.” Both teams are battling through it. That’s SEC football in November.

But here’s the bottom line on great news number one. Alabama is getting healthier at exactly the right time. LSU is still missing one of its most important defensive leaders. And for a game that’s expected to be this close, those details could make the difference.

Great news number two. The committee’s respect for Alabama, and the debate it’s created.

All week long, the national conversation has been about one thing: the College Football Playoff committee ranking Alabama fifth in the country. That’s the highest-ranked two-loss team in America. And as you can imagine, not everybody is happy about it.

On one national TV show, an analyst said, “To see Alabama sitting at number five right now, to see them above Texas. No, maybe I can, I don’t have a bigger problem with that. How are they eight spots ahead of Tennessee? That’s the shock.” Another analyst made the case for Texas, pointing out that the Longhorns have wins over Ole Miss, Ohio State and Tennessee on the road in Knoxville. And CBS Sports analyst Danny Kanell called this committee “the worst, most non-sensical one yet.”

Now, here on Alabama Tunnel, we always respect the other side of the argument. Tennessee did beat Alabama twenty-six to ten. That’s real, and the Vols deserve credit for it. Texas does have some strong wins. Those are fair points, and the Texas case is a real one. But even the analysts making that case acknowledged that Texas has two losses to unranked teams. Alabama’s two losses came against number two Georgia and number thirteen Tennessee. When the committee compares résumés, the quality of your losses matters just as much as the quality of your wins. That’s a big reason Alabama is at five and Texas is at seven. But here’s what the critics are missing, and here’s why I think the committee got this right.

First, look at the Kentucky win. When Alabama went to Lexington in week two and won forty-five to seventeen, some people shrugged. Now Kentucky is seven and one and ranked sixth in the country. As one analyst pointed out, Kentucky’s only loss is to Alabama, and some of the Wildcats’ metrics are “just exceptional in terms of strength of record and strength of schedule.” According to that same panel, Kentucky’s résumé includes close wins over LSU and South Carolina, two teams that are both currently in the committee’s top twenty-five. So when Alabama beat Kentucky by twenty-eight on the road, it beat one of the best teams in the country. That’s a top-ten road win. Not many teams have one of those.

Second, look at South Carolina. Alabama beat the Gamecocks forty-nine to eighteen. South Carolina is now ranked twenty-first. That’s another win over a ranked team.

Third, look at the losses. Alabama lost to Georgia by three points. Georgia is number two in the country. One analyst on the same panel called that Georgia game the hardest-hitting game in college football this season, and suggested the committee is taking into account that both teams came out of that game beat up. Alabama’s other loss came on the road to a ranked Tennessee team. Those aren’t bad losses. Those are losses to good teams.

And one analyst said something I really agree with. He said, “I think the selection committee is going to like what they see on film from the Tide.” That’s the key. The committee doesn’t just look at the scoreboard. It watches the games. It sees the fourth-quarter comeback against Texas A&M, when Alabama outgained the Aggies one hundred two yards to fifteen in the final quarter. It sees Keelon Russell going five for five for sixty-eight yards and two touchdowns when the season was on the line. It sees a defense that forced three turnovers, including a Djion Lee Jr. pick-six. That’s what a top-five team looks like on film. And some critics have said Alabama hasn’t been dominant this season. Really? Alabama beat East Carolina forty-eight to ten. It beat Kentucky, now a top-ten team, by twenty-eight on the road. It beat South Carolina, now ranked twenty-first, by thirty-one. It went on the road and beat Mississippi State by thirty-three. Those are dominant wins. Yes, there were two tough weeks against Georgia and Tennessee, but over a full season, this team has been one of the most impressive in the SEC.

Now, what about the people who say Alabama has no margin for error? On the SEC Network, Paul Finebaum said this game against LSU is basically a playoff game, especially for Alabama. He said, “If Alabama loses this game, forget it.” And honestly? He’s not wrong about how big this game is. A third loss would make the playoff path very difficult. But that’s the beauty of it. Alabama doesn’t need anybody’s help. It just needs to win. Finebaum also pointed out that Alabama’s non-conference schedule isn’t as strong as some other contenders, with Florida State as the biggest name, and the Seminoles haven’t had the season many expected. That’s fair too. But Alabama can’t control how Florida State plays in November. What Alabama can control is beating ranked SEC teams. It already beat Kentucky and South Carolina. LSU is the next one. And remember, last season, Alabama made the playoff with three losses after reaching the SEC Championship Game. The committee has shown it respects the strength of an SEC schedule. Win on Saturday, and Alabama will have one of the best résumés in the country.

And here’s the other side of that coin. If Alabama does win in Baton Rouge, the path opens up wide. Another ranked road win. Then Vanderbilt on the road, Chattanooga at home, and Auburn in the Iron Bowl. None of those three teams are in the committee’s top twenty-five right now. Win out, and a ten-and-two Alabama team with wins over Kentucky, LSU and South Carolina is going to be in great shape for a home playoff game, and maybe even more.

One analyst described teams ranked eleven through fourteen, Notre Dame, Ohio State, Tennessee and LSU, as being “on the chopping block,” meaning a loss this weekend could knock them out of the picture. That’s exactly why this game is so big for LSU too. The Tigers are fighting for their playoff lives. They have Texas next and then a trip to Tennessee. So expect LSU’s very best effort. But Alabama has the higher ranking, the better résumé, and, if these injury reports hold up, a much healthier roster than it had two weeks ago.

So that’s the double dose. One: Alabama is getting healthy, with all five starters still alive and even Kiffin expecting Coleman-Williams to play. Two: the committee has made it clear it respects this team, and the national debate is all about how good Alabama’s résumé really is. Now all that’s left is to go prove it on the field.

Let’s talk about the game.

LSU is six and two, with wins over Clemson, Texas A&M, Mississippi State and Auburn. Its two losses came on the road, at Ole Miss and in overtime at Kentucky. Those were close games, thirty-two to twenty-four at Ole Miss and thirty-five to thirty-four in overtime at Kentucky. And the wins have been impressive, including fifty-one to ten over Clemson in the opener, thirty-five to six over Texas A&M and a thirty-one-to-nothing shutout of Mississippi State. Remember, Alabama beat that same Texas A&M team by eleven and that same Kentucky team by twenty-eight. So on paper, these two teams are about as even as it gets. The Tigers are led by first-year head coach Lane Kiffin, Alabama’s former offensive coordinator, and quarterback Sam Leavitt, who arrived as the top quarterback in the transfer portal. And Leavitt is hot. In his last two games, he’s thrown seven touchdown passes with no interceptions, including three hundred seventy-eight yards and three touchdowns in a thirty-eight-to-seventeen win at Auburn. He’s also run for eight touchdowns this season, five of them at Tiger Stadium. And he has weapons. Jackson Harris had one hundred sixty-three yards and two touchdowns against Texas A&M earlier this season. Trey’Dez Green and Winston Watkins Jr. are dangerous too. This is a balanced, explosive offense, and Kiffin knows how to use every piece of it. Kane Wommack has said he studies live TV broadcasts of opponents to get a feel for how they call games and set their tempo, and he mentioned his ties to the Kiffin family, saying, “Chris Kiffin and I worked together for a while.” Expect Wommack to have a detailed plan for whatever Kiffin dials up.

On the other side, Alabama is two and oh against LSU under DeBoer, with a combined score of sixty-two to twenty-two. That includes that forty-two-to-thirteen win in Baton Rouge in 2024 and a twenty-to-nine win in Tuscaloosa last season, when Yhonzae Pierre had two sacks and the strip-sack that sealed it.

And don’t forget the kickoff time storyline. Kiffin wasn’t thrilled about a two-thirty kickoff instead of a night game. DeBoer’s answer was perfect: “We’re on the road, so, you know, I’d rather play at home if we want to have all these advantages.” And he added, “It is what it is. We just got to show up and play.” And for what it’s worth, LSU is just one and six in its last seven night home games against Alabama. So maybe the afternoon isn’t the disadvantage Kiffin thinks it is.

Here are my final keys for Saturday.

Key number one. Get the ball out quickly. LSU’s defense has thirty-four sacks, with Princewill Umanmielen and Jordan Ross combining for thirteen and a half. DeBoer’s message to Russell this week was about learning from everything he’s been through. He said, “That will come to him because he’s that type of player.” Russell has to take the quick throws, use his tight ends and backs, and not hold the ball waiting for the big play. If Coleman-Williams is out there, the big plays will come. And Russell has already shown he can handle adversity on the road. At Kentucky, he turned it over on each of his first two possessions, including a pick-six, and Alabama outscored the Wildcats thirty-two to nothing after halftime. Against Florida State, the Tide fell behind by fifteen, and Russell led a comeback with three hundred twenty passing yards and three touchdowns. And against Texas A&M, he went five for five for sixty-eight yards and two touchdowns in the fourth quarter. When the moment gets big, this sophomore has a way of rising to it.

Key number two. Attack the middle of LSU’s defense. With Whit Weeks expected to be out, Alabama should test LSU’s linebackers early. Run the ball between the tackles. Use play-action to the tight ends over the middle. Make the young linebackers make tough decisions.

Key number three. Contain Leavitt. Kane Wommack said this week, “You’ve got to create enough doubt in their minds and then capitalize.” And he added, “So far, we’ve found ways to capitalize and create takeaways off RPOs.” Make Leavitt hesitate. Disguise the coverages, just like Bray Hubbard talked about. Keep him in the pocket, and when he makes a mistake, take the ball away.

Key number four. Win the turnover battle. Against Tennessee, Alabama turned it over four times and lost. Against A&M, Alabama forced three turnovers and won. That’s the game in a nutshell. And Alabama’s defense has playmakers everywhere. Fatutoa Henry had two sacks against A&M. Caleb Woodson had ten tackles. Djion Lee Jr. had the pick-six. Chuck McDonald III got his first career interception. Yhonzae Pierre has a history of making huge plays against LSU. If that group brings the same energy to Baton Rouge, Leavitt is going to have a long afternoon.

Key number five. Handle the noise. Rico Scott said, “It’s a crazy atmosphere. Fans are a little nasty, but I feel like ours are too.” He’s ready for it. Start fast, stay composed, and take the crowd out of the game. Left tackle Jackson Lloyd summed it up this week: “Backs against the wall, everyone knows what we got to do.”

And a bonus key. Finish drives. Conor Talty had a tough day against A&M, missing two of his three field goals after making fifteen in a row before that game. Every kicker has an off day, and I fully expect him to bounce back. But in a game this close, Alabama needs touchdowns in the red zone, and points every time it gets in range.

My final prediction. Alabama twenty-seven, LSU twenty-four. I’ve had that score all week, and I’m sticking with it. I think this is going to be a tight, physical, back-and-forth game. LSU’s pass rush will make some plays. Leavitt will make some plays. But I think the returning players give Alabama just enough. A big catch from Coleman-Williams. A healthier offensive line. And a defense that makes the key stop late, just like it did against A&M. And when it’s over, nobody will be asking why Alabama is ranked fifth anymore.
