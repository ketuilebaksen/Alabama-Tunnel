DeBoer admitted that, twenty-four hours before kickoff, the staff truly didn’t know which direction they would go at quarterback. He said one word will be the key to this final run. And he made it clear what he wants to see fixed before the Iron Bowl. Dijon Lee Jr.’s game-changing kickoff return. Life without Josh Ford at tight end. And whether this team is really playing like the number three team in the country.

Let’s start with how DeBoer opened the press conference, because it tells you a lot about his mindset. The very first thing he talked about wasn’t the win itself. It was what Alabama didn’t do. He said, “Got the win, and you know you’d love to separate yourselves more when we had opportunity at the end of the third, fourth quarter.”

That’s a coach who isn’t satisfied. Alabama had chances late in the game to turn a two-score lead into something bigger, and it didn’t happen. And DeBoer knows that in the games ahead, against better opponents, those missed chances can come back to bite you. He even said that separating from opponents is “the area that you want to see better.”

But he also pointed to what went right. He said he “liked what we did at the end of the half.” And that makes sense. Remember, Alabama trailed three to nothing late in the second quarter, and then put together that nine-play, seventy-five-yard drive that ended with Keelon Russell’s touchdown pass to Marshall Pritchett with just six seconds left before halftime. DeBoer said the team “got in a little bit of groove” there, and that it “extended into the third quarter.” And it did. Alabama opened the second half with another long touchdown drive, capped by Lotzeir Brooks’ thirty-nine-yard touchdown catch, and then Dijon Lee Jr.’s eighty-three-yard return set up Brooks’ second touchdown. That stretch, from the end of the second quarter through the third quarter, is where Alabama won this game.

Then DeBoer gave out some praise, and the first name he mentioned was the one we just talked about in our last video. He said, “Thought Daniel ran really hard, did a nice job there. That was important for us.” Daniel Hill finished with eighty-nine yards on fourteen carries, six point four yards per carry, and he converted some huge third downs late in the game. And the head coach saying “that was important for us” tells you the staff noticed. If you missed our Daniel Hill breakdown, go check it out, because his role could keep growing down the stretch.

And then DeBoer did something I really respect. He made a point to thank the fans. He said, “Last thing I just want to do is thank our fans.” He talked about seeing the Alabama fan base in the stands in Nashville and said, “We know we travel, we were kind of expecting that, but you still got to do it, and the fans showed up. So, we appreciate you guys.” Tide Nation, that’s for you. A road game, an early kickoff, and Alabama fans showed up and made their presence felt. The head coach noticed, and he wanted you to know it.

And he finished his opening statement with the line that I think is the key to this entire press conference. He said Alabama has to “continue to move on, continue to improve,” and then he said, “get guys healthier is going to be the key as we make this final run.” Health. That’s the word. And it comes up again and again in this press conference, so keep it in mind.

Now let’s go to the defense, because the first question DeBoer got from reporters was about Kane Wommack’s unit. And he was clearly proud of it.

He said, “It’s been really good to see here the last few weeks.” And he described exactly what he’s seeing. He said the defense is “attacking,” and “really not playing hesitant at all in those big moments.” He specifically mentioned third down, fourth down, and “holding to field goals,” which, he said, continued to be the case again on Saturday.

And the numbers back that up completely. Vanderbilt went two for fifteen on third down and one for four on fourth down. The Commodores only scored on two field goals. No touchdowns. Just two hundred thirty-five total yards, their lowest of the season. Twelve rushing yards. And five sacks of freshman quarterback Jared Curtis. Alabama’s defense has now given up just one touchdown in its last eight quarters.

DeBoer also talked about how the defense is playing as a group. He said, “The way they’re flying around, getting pressure at the right times, that helps when the personnel up front is doing what they’re doing.” And he finished by saying they’re “playing good team football on that side of the ball for sure.” That’s the theme with this defense right now. It’s not one superstar carrying everyone. It’s eleven guys flying to the football, the pass rush getting home at the right moments, and everybody doing their job.

Later in the press conference, DeBoer was asked a follow-up question about the defense and where it’s heading. And this answer gave us some great insight into the game plan. He said he “challenged them” not to let Vanderbilt put together those long, clock-eating drives. He said, “these eight, nine minute drives, and even a ten-minute drive over the last few weeks that Vanderbilt has had.” Vanderbilt had been controlling games by holding onto the football for long stretches, keeping opposing offenses on the sideline. And Alabama’s defense shut that down, especially early.

DeBoer explained how. He said “getting off the field on the first third down is the key,” and “having the attacking mindset” for the players. That’s exactly what happened. Vanderbilt couldn’t sustain drives, because it couldn’t convert on third down.

But DeBoer was also honest about one thing that bothered him. He said, “We didn’t get the takeaways. I know they’re going to be really frustrated about that.” And he added, “We lost the turnover battle, and that’s a big deal for us.” Alabama gave up two turnovers, the tipped interception and the strip sack fumble, and didn’t force any of its own. DeBoer said those turnovers are “never at a good time,” and that the team will “go back to work as a team to make sure that is the emphasis and that we win that battle.”

And honestly, Tide Nation, that’s what makes this defensive performance so impressive. Alabama lost the turnover battle two to zero on the road in the SEC, and still won by fifteen and held Vanderbilt out of the end zone. Imagine what this defense looks like when the takeaways start coming again.

And here’s a classy moment from DeBoer. He praised Vanderbilt’s young quarterback. He said, “I think their quarterback is going to be a heck of a player here in the years ahead.” He talked about “the throws he can make down the sideline,” and said Curtis has made a throw like the one he made against Alabama “almost every game that he’s been a part of.” That’s respect for the opponent, and it’s well deserved. Jared Curtis is a talented freshman, and Alabama’s defense still found a way to sack him five times and keep him out of the end zone.

Now let’s get to the question everybody wanted answered: Keelon Russell.

A reporter asked how much Russell had to fight through to be able to play, and how close he was to not playing. And DeBoer gave a really detailed answer. He said, “Every day he was getting better, and so, the last twenty-four to forty-eight hours, a lot of improvement.”

But DeBoer was also honest about how Russell looked early. He said, “Probably just a few things early in the game, I felt he wasn’t probably as sharp as to be expected.” And he took some responsibility for that as a coaching staff, saying, “I think other ways we could have helped him out a little bit more.” That’s a coach protecting his player and putting some of it on himself.

And then DeBoer described how Russell got better as the game went on. He said, “As the game went on there, in the middle of the second, I felt like he was starting to kind of find his groove,” and that it carried “into the third quarter for sure.” And that matches what we saw. Early on, Alabama’s offense was stuck. Then Russell found his rhythm late in the second quarter, and he led three straight touchdown drives.

DeBoer also praised Russell’s mental preparation. He said Russell “was ready mentally for whatever he had to do.” Think about that. Russell barely practiced during the week. He didn’t know if he was going to play. And he still showed up mentally prepared for everything Vanderbilt was going to throw at him. For a nineteen-year-old sophomore, that’s impressive.

He finished twenty-two of thirty-three for two hundred seventeen yards and three touchdowns. Not bad for a guy who was a game-time decision on a sore ankle.

Then came the question about Austin Mack, and this was one of my favorite parts of the press conference. A reporter asked what DeBoer’s message to Mack was during the week, while the staff monitored what Russell could and couldn’t do.

DeBoer said, “Pretty much all of us were approaching it that he had to be out there and be ready to go, and he was.” And then he said, “That’s a hard spot for him to be in, and just, I can’t give him enough credit.”

I can’t give him enough credit. Think about the week Austin Mack had. He prepared like a starter. There were reports that he would start. He took the reps. And then, on game day, Russell was able to go. And Mack went right back to being the ultimate teammate. DeBoer said, “I know he was working extremely hard to get out there and help our team win, and he was prepared to do that.” And then he said, “I think our guys saw that, and that’s what you appreciate so much about him.”

That’s the kind of player every team needs. A backup quarterback who prepares like a starter, never complains, and earns the respect of the entire locker room. Mack came in at the end to take the final knee, but his real contribution was the way he prepared all week. Tide Nation, give Austin Mack some love in the comments, because he deserves it.

Next, DeBoer was asked about Dijon Lee Jr.’s kickoff return, and he didn’t hold back. He said, “Huge.”

He explained that Alabama’s special teams “has made a couple big plays now, setting us up the last couple weeks.” And he gave an interesting reason why. He said it’s about experience. He said you’re seeing guys that are “not just year one, but year two and year three” on those units, and the younger guys “continue to gain more experience.” Special teams can sometimes be overlooked, but this is a unit that’s improving as the season goes on, and it’s making game-changing plays.

DeBoer also revealed that the staff saw an opportunity coming into the game. He said, “We felt like there was an opportunity, if we got one of those kicks, that we could get some good field position.” So this wasn’t luck. The coaches saw something on film. And Dijon Lee Jr. made it happen. DeBoer said Lee “went above and beyond,” and added, “It was great to see him hit it the way he did. Seems like when he’s got those opportunities, he’s done a really good job.”

But DeBoer made sure to spread the credit. He said, “All the guys got to block hard,” and he specifically praised the team for “staying away from the penalties on those returns.” That’s a big deal. How many times have we seen a great return get wiped out by a flag? Not this time. DeBoer said the players are “using the technique that they need to,” they’re “giving great effort,” and then “those playmakers like Dijon, like Ryan, are doing the rest when they get those chances.” He’s talking about the return game as a whole, with Dijon Lee Jr. and Ryan Coleman-Williams both able to change a game when the ball is in their hands.

So that’s a big positive for Alabama heading into the final stretch. When your offense is still finding consistency, a special teams unit that can flip field position is a huge weapon.

Now let’s get to the question about what’s next, because this is where the “decision” part of this story comes in.

A reporter asked DeBoer about the plan for Russell going forward. Would the staff split reps? Would Russell rest during the week? And DeBoer was completely honest. He said, “I honestly don’t know.”

And then he revealed just how uncertain the situation was during the week. He said, “We were just literally playing it, I want to say hour by hour, but we were definitely playing it day by day here throughout this week, and especially the end of the week.” And then came the big one. He said, “We truly didn’t really know the last twenty-four hours what direction we’d go.”

Think about that, Tide Nation. Twenty-four hours before kickoff, the Alabama coaching staff didn’t know who would start at quarterback. That explains why there were so many different reports on Friday. It wasn’t that anybody was hiding anything. The staff truly didn’t know yet.

And then DeBoer explained how they’ll approach this week. He said, “We’ll continue to process what we think is best for Keelon, for our football team, moving forward going into next week.” And he finished with the line that made the headlines: “If he can go, I’d expect him to go.”

So that’s the decision. If Keelon Russell is healthy enough to play against Chattanooga, he’s going to play. But the staff will take it day by day again, and they’ll do what’s best for Russell and for the team. And with the Iron Bowl one week later, that’s the smart approach. You want Russell getting reps and staying in rhythm, but you also want him as close to one hundred percent as possible for Auburn and beyond.

Next, DeBoer got one of the toughest questions of the press conference. A reporter asked, “Do you think Alabama is playing like the number three team in the country?”

And DeBoer gave an honest answer. He said, “I think there’s times when we really show it and we flex and do a nice job.” But then he added, “We just got to take advantage of having some consistency for four quarters. And I think that’s always hard to do.”

That’s the challenge for this team right now. When Alabama is clicking, like it was from the end of the second quarter through the third quarter on Saturday, it looks like one of the best teams in the country. But it hasn’t been able to sustain that for a full sixty minutes as often as it wants to.

DeBoer also gave Vanderbilt credit. He talked about how many teams have struggled against Vanderbilt in the first half, and he said Vanderbilt’s games have often gone in that direction. He said, “Vandy’s got a team that’s going to come and they’re going to fight,” and he credited Clark Lea, saying, “Coach Lea does exactly that.” He said a lot of the plan Vanderbilt had “was out there again today,” much like Alabama saw two years ago and last year. That’s respect for a program that has given Alabama some real battles recently.

And then DeBoer came back to the fourth quarter. He said, “At the end of the game, just had some opportunities where we could just do some simple things that we’re capable of, and stretch the lead, and make it a little bit easier there at the end.” Simple things. That’s what DeBoer wants. Not big changes, just executing the basics well enough to put games away.

And then he said something that I think is the most important part of the whole press conference. He said, “I think we have the makings. A lot of it’s just health and rhythm as we go through our week of practice.”

There it is again. Health. DeBoer explained that when guys aren’t getting reps during the week because of injuries, it shows up on Saturday. He said, “There’s a lot of times where we aren’t synced up and executing at the highest level.” But then he delivered the good news: “Fortunately, I think we’re going to continue to get guys back here these last couple weeks.”

Tide Nation, that’s huge. Alabama has been dealing with injuries all season. Russell’s ankle. Josh Ford. EJ Crowell. AK Dear. Several others on the availability report. If DeBoer is right, and guys start coming back over the next couple of weeks, this team could be at its healthiest heading into the Iron Bowl and the postseason. And a healthier team means more reps in practice, which means better rhythm, which means more consistency for four quarters. That’s exactly what DeBoer said this team needs.

The final question of the press conference was about the tight ends, and this one matters a lot for the rest of the season. Josh Ford is a big loss for Alabama. He’s been one of the most important players on this offense, both as a blocker and as a receiver. And a reporter mentioned that Ford is going to continue to lead from the sideline, which says a lot about the kind of teammate he is.

So how did Danny Lewis Jr. and the other tight ends do with Ford out? DeBoer said he told Lewis earlier in the week, “This is his opportunity now.” And he pointed out that Lewis hasn’t been on the field at this time of year very much in previous seasons, so this is “a big opportunity for him.” The reporter noted that Lewis made a couple of catches and did a nice job blocking.

DeBoer also explained what the offense is still working on with this new tight end group. He said, “I think there’s just a rhythm with those guys and our substitutions, and just the positioning.” He said the staff could see that “guys were flinching a little bit with some of our checks and some of our movements,” and that it was good to get a lot of those reps on Saturday. He said Alabama will “continue to work him into the mix.”

And then he finished with this: “We miss Josh and just the physical presence that he brings. But other guys are definitely more than capable.”

That’s a strong vote of confidence for Danny Lewis Jr., Marshall Pritchett and the rest of the tight end room. And remember, Pritchett caught that huge touchdown right before halftime. So even without Josh Ford, Alabama’s tight ends made a real impact on Saturday. And Tide Nation, let’s keep sending Josh Ford all the support in the world. He’s still leading this team from the sideline, and that tells you everything about who he is.

So let’s put it all together. What did we learn from this press conference?

Number one: the quarterback situation. Russell will play against Chattanooga if he’s healthy enough, but the staff will take it day by day. DeBoer said, “If he can go, I’d expect him to go.”

Number two: the defense is playing with an attacking mindset, getting off the field on third down and holding opponents to field goals. The only thing missing is takeaways, and DeBoer made it clear that will be an emphasis.

Number three: special teams are becoming a weapon, thanks to more experience and better technique.

Number four: DeBoer wants more consistency for four quarters, and he wants his team to put games away in the fourth quarter by doing simple things well.

Number five, and this is the big one: health. DeBoer said, “Get guys healthier is going to be the key as we make this final run,” and he believes Alabama will get more players back in these last couple of weeks.

And number six: the tight end room is stepping up without Josh Ford, with Danny Lewis Jr. getting his opportunity.
