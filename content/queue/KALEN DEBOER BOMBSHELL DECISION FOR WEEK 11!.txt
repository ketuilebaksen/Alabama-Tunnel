And his answer was one word: “No.” Alabama is number three in the College Football Playoff rankings. It just went into Death Valley and won by eighteen. And the head coach is saying his team still has to get better. That’s not all. DeBoer also gave a surprisingly honest answer about whether it’s actually better to skip the SEC Championship Game, and that comes in a week when the College Football Playoff announced it will expand to twenty-four teams, a change that, according to reports, could make this season’s conference title games the last ones as we know them. What’s up, everybody, James here, and welcome back to Alabama Tunnel. It’s game week in Nashville, and today we’re going through Kalen DeBoer’s full Vanderbilt-week press conference, piece by piece. Some of it you’ve heard. A lot of it you probably haven’t. We’ll talk about the big-picture decision on the SEC title game, what he said about Keelon Russell’s health, the updates on Zabien Brown and William Sanders, the run game fix he’s demanding, his emotional words about Josh Ford, and the deep respect he has for Vanderbilt’s Clark Lea. If you want the full inside look at where this team’s head is right now, smash that like button, subscribe and turn on notifications. Let’s go.

Let’s start with how DeBoer opened the press conference, because it set the tone for everything.

He looked back at the LSU win and said, “One of the more, if not the most complete game we’ve played. I thought it was a great team win for us.” He said offense, defense and special teams were “really each playing their role.” He pointed to “no turnovers,” and said the plan was “making an opponent have to drive the field,” giving the defense a chance to make LSU “earn it.” And he said “getting those stops obviously in the red zone on fourth down were key.”

He also gave a shoutout to special teams: “coverage on punt and obviously the punt return by Ryan.” That’s Ryan Coleman-Williams’ thirty-two-yard return in the fourth quarter that set up Cederian Morgan’s touchdown. DeBoer said the staff “really felt like there was ways where we could make a play that could be a difference maker,” and that’s exactly what happened.

And then DeBoer did what he always does. He gave the credit to his staff and his players. He said, “I credit our staff for a lot of things,” starting “with the plan and getting the guys to really be honed in and detailed on the plan.” He said there was “great energy by the staff that carried over to our players.” And he said the players’ “belief and confidence that they had as the week went on” led into Saturday.

Then he made a point that sums up this whole season. He said, “Second half success, I think fourth quarter in particular, continues to show up.” He called it “a credit to the guys and again how bad they want it, their determination.” He said there’s “a depth piece to it,” and “a physical conditioning and a mindset and mentality that you got to have.” And then this: “Didn’t feel like we ever flinched in any moment. Got behind there in the third quarter and responded really quickly.”

That’s this team in one sentence. It doesn’t flinch. Alabama has now won four games this season after trailing in the second half. And according to one national broadcast, that’s the most of any Power Four team.

Now, let’s get to the big-picture stuff, because this is where the press conference got really interesting.

Here’s the context. This week, the College Football Playoff officially announced it will expand from twelve teams to twenty-four, starting next season. And according to reports, including the Chicago Sun-Times, that change is expected to end conference championship games as we know them. In the new format, the top eight seeds get first-round byes, the first two rounds are played on campus, and five automatic bids go to four Power Four conference champions and the top Group of Six team. The first final under the new format is set for January twenty-fourth, 2028, in New Orleans.

So think about what that means. If those reports hold, the 2026 SEC Championship Game in Atlanta could be the last one ever, at least in its current form.

A reporter asked DeBoer about the expansion, and his answer was honest. He said, “Not a lot. I mean, we’re just so focused, you know, as you’d expect on just what we’re doing right now.” But then he added something really personal. He said, “I know the SEC championship game is a big deal, you know, and one that we were a part of last year and I want to be a part of it again this year.” And then: “I’ve not had a chance to win one, and you always want those opportunities. And that’s a little bit selfish, but want our guys to experience that too.”

I love that answer. DeBoer has won a lot of games. He took Washington to the national championship game. He took Alabama to the playoff last season. But he’s never won an SEC title. And he’s not shy about saying he wants one. And now, with the possibility that this could be the last SEC Championship Game, there’s even more on the line.

As for the expansion itself, DeBoer said, “The expansion of that will, I’m sure, affect teams and their scheduling.” He added, “Hopefully that has a role in playing competitive games more across college football. But right now we’re focused on this year.”

And then came what might be the most surprising moment of the press conference. Another reporter asked him a really interesting question. With the current twelve-team format, is it actually better to not be in the SEC Championship Game and get that extra week off before the playoff?

And DeBoer’s answer started with one word: “Probably.”

That’s a head coach being honest. He explained, “I totally understand where you’re coming from,” and he said he experienced it last year. He said, “If you win it, you get do get a longer break. If you don’t, I know last year at least that’s how it was for us. That quick turnaround is a little tougher.” And he added, “You’re going to risk losing some guys the more snaps you play.”

But then he made it crystal clear where Alabama stands. He said, “We as competitive people, you know, and here at Alabama, you know, we want to win that game. We want to be in that game first of all, and we want to win that game. That’s always going to be a part of it.” He said, “We’re going to play as hard as we can right now and want to be in that game because that’s what the schedule and what the goals are.” But he admitted, “Obviously you’re setting yourself up for the bigger picture, too.”

So that’s the decision. Alabama is going for the SEC title. No resting, no strategizing, no hoping to skip Atlanta. DeBoer understands the tradeoff, he admits it might “probably” be easier to skip it, but he and his team want that championship. That’s the Alabama standard.

And let’s look at where things stand in that race. Georgia is six and one in the SEC with games left against Missouri and South Carolina. Alabama is five and two, with Vanderbilt and Auburn left on the conference schedule. Texas, Tennessee and Kentucky all have two conference losses too. According to the scenarios laid out by 247Sports, if Alabama wins out and ends up tied with Kentucky, Alabama goes, thanks to its forty-five-to-seventeen head-to-head win. In some bigger multi-team ties, Alabama is currently the most likely team to advance. But if it’s just Alabama and Tennessee, or just Alabama and Texas, the other team would get in. So Alabama needs to win, and it needs a little help.

And here’s the safety net. Alabama is already number three in the College Football Playoff rankings. In the twelve-team format, the top four get first-round byes. So even if Alabama doesn’t reach Atlanta, a ten-and-two Tide team would still be in a great spot.

Now let’s talk about Keelon Russell, because DeBoer gave some really thoughtful answers about him.

A reporter asked about the balance between keeping Russell progressing and keeping him healthy. DeBoer said, “We got to keep moving and winning and playing our best football. And so you’re weighing how he feels, what the medical staff says.” And then the line that every Alabama fan should appreciate: “We never want to hurt him and we will never do that.”

He added, “He’s got to go out there and obviously be able to play at the level that can help us win, and obviously we got a lot of confidence if we had to move to Austin at some point.” That’s backup Austin Mack. But he said Russell is “day-to-day,” and he pointed out that Russell finished the LSU game: “That’s a good sign right there.”

And then DeBoer said something that tells you a lot about this kid. He said, “This kid’s a really tough kid, and he’s had other things that have popped up in games where he’s played through it, and you guys have seen probably that, too, when you’re really paying close attention. And he stays out on the football field and keeps doing his thing.” He added, “For him to play at the level he played at, while getting dinged up a little bit, was impressive in itself.”

And since then, the news has only gotten better. On Wednesday, Russell was listed as probable on the SEC availability report for Vanderbilt. Reports say he’s been practicing, and the ankle isn’t expected to be a problem. On top of that, he was named a semifinalist for the Maxwell Award this week. Seventeen of twenty-four for two hundred six yards, three touchdowns and no interceptions at LSU, all three touchdowns in the second half, on a bad ankle. Get well soon, Keelon, and we’ll see you in Nashville.

Next, the updates on two other key players: Zabien Brown and William Sanders.

On Brown, DeBoer explained that he’s dealing with a “lower extremity injury,” and that he was limited at LSU. He said, “Got some snaps. I think any snaps that we could get out of him on the weekend was going to be a positive.” He said just having Brown on the field “is impactful,” because of “his presence,” and because his snaps took some of the load off other players. He said Brown is “day to day,” that “he played last week and really didn’t feel like there was a setback,” and that “he pushed it as far as we felt we could push him.” And then the good news: “Getting him back this week is something I would expect.”

On Sanders, DeBoer said, “We feel good about his progress. It’s been a quick turnaround for him.” He revealed that Sanders went through warm-ups at LSU, and the staff knew he wouldn’t start, but they were trying to figure out whether he could handle an emergency role. And he said, “We need some improvements this week to get to where he can go out there and be competitive at the level we need him.”

Both Brown and Sanders were listed as probable on Wednesday’s availability report, along with Russell, running back EJ Crowell and defensive back Chuck McDonald. That’s a lot of good news for one week.

Now, the run game, because this is clearly a priority.

A reporter mentioned that offensive coordinator Ryan Grubb said his first point with the offensive staff this week was improving the run game. DeBoer agreed, and he gave a really specific example from the LSU game. He said, “First play of the game, right? It was a nice run off the left side there. And I think those yards all matter.” But then he pointed out a missed opportunity: “Unfortunately, down at the end of that drive, we had a nice big play and it was a ten-yard penalty, but it’s a bigger penalty than that because of how many yards we gained and puts us inside the ten.”

Think about that. A holding penalty didn’t just cost Alabama ten yards. It wiped out a big play that would have set the offense up inside the LSU ten-yard line. DeBoer said, “There’s just missed opportunities that we have. We executed pretty clean, but you got to keep your hands inside and can’t hold.” He called them “those technical things, those details.”

And he explained another big reason the run game numbers have looked low: Alabama hasn’t been running as many plays. He said, “The number of plays we’re running overall is limiting who gets touches, and obviously that limits the number of run plays we can have.” So the fix is simple. “Stay on the football field.” Convert third downs, extend drives, and the run game will get more chances.

And that matters even more against Vanderbilt. DeBoer said Vanderbilt is “going to be a team that’s going to try to keep the ball,” to “use the time of possession” and “limit your possessions as well.” He said, “We got to flip the script and make sure our end we’re executing, start to finish.”

Let’s be honest, the run game has been a weakness. At LSU, Trae’shawn Brown led Alabama with thirty-five rushing yards. And now Josh Ford, one of the team’s best blocking tight ends, is out. But DeBoer’s answer shows he knows exactly what needs to be fixed: penalties, details and staying on the field. Getting EJ Crowell back healthy, and maybe William Sanders up front, could help a lot too.

Speaking of Josh Ford, DeBoer’s words about him were some of the most emotional of the press conference.

DeBoer said, “Unfortunately Josh suffered an injury and it can be season ending.” And then he said this: “The guy is so tough. I mean, I know he’s probably right now trying to figure out how he’s still going to play this year.” That’s who Ford is. Remember, he broke his arm against Mississippi State, had surgery that Sunday, and then played against Georgia anyway.

DeBoer went on: “His impact has been really key for our football team in a lot of ways. Just a guy that’s a uniter.” And then he explained what he meant: “You got uniters and dividers, I think, and he’s a uniter of our football team. He’s a leader.” He said that going back to spring ball, the staff “talked about the physicality of what he brought and how other guys fed off of that.”

And then he said, “We’re gonna have to find ways to make up the gap that he leaves with him not being with us. I know he’ll be with us but not on the football field.” He added, “Our team just loves him to death. I know I do as well.” And he finished with, “I know he’s going to have a fast recovery. He’ll do everything he can to get back as soon as possible.”

You can tell how much Ford means to this program. Get well soon, Josh. Kaleb Edwards, Marshall Pritchett and the rest of the tight ends will carry that physicality forward.

Now, let’s talk about Vanderbilt, and DeBoer’s respect for Clark Lea.

DeBoer opened by saying, “Clark Lea just I think is an amazing, really great head football coach, and he and his staff just do such a good job.” He said that’s how he’s felt “all three years” he’s been at Alabama. He said, “I think just the toughness that they have starts with Coach Lea.”

And it turns out these two go way back. DeBoer said, “I’ve known Coach Lea for a long time, going back to days in South Dakota.” He said he’s “always had a lot of respect” for him.

A reporter asked DeBoer about the 2024 loss in Nashville, and how Lea was able to beat Alabama that day. DeBoer was honest. He said Vanderbilt plays “team football” with “calculated risks,” and “they just out-executed us, stayed on the football field, finished drives and put touchdowns on the board.” He said they “kept taking advantage of the momentum that they had.”

And he made a really important point about this year’s Vanderbilt team. He said there’s “momentum, continuity” there, “with players” and “with staff.” He said they have “a lot of returners on the football field,” including their top running back and receiver, “some of the same guys that we’ve seen now going back to 2024.” And he said defensively, “a lot of guys that are the same faces that we saw last season as well.”

DeBoer also said Vanderbilt has “an offense that’s going to try to run the football, but a quarterback that is extremely talented.” That’s five-star freshman Jared Curtis, the highest-rated recruit in Vanderbilt program history, who took over as the starter in September. Clark Lea said earlier this season, “There’s no limit on what this kid is capable of.”

And when asked how Alabama uses the 2024 loss, DeBoer gave a great answer. He said, “There’s coaches, there’s players that were here. There’s a lot of coaches and players that weren’t here.” He said, “My job as a head coach is to take those experiences, the good, the bad, the ugly, to always apply them and make sure that we’re better because of it.” But he also said, “This is a different team than we had last year, which is different than the year before too. So we got to take our experiences from what we’re going through right now and apply them as well.”

And he summed up the mindset this week perfectly: “Reset, do it again.” He said, “None of the plays, none of the stops, none of the points carry over. You got to go do it again here in the SEC.”

Now, back to that question I opened with. “Have we actually seen the best version of Alabama football yet?”

DeBoer said, “No, we have to get better. We have to continue to improve.” He talked about how getting guys back “really helped us.” He singled out Terrance Green, saying he “does a great job providing that physicality that we need up front that’s been helpful in big games, and it makes other guys around them better, too.” He explained that with Green back, the defensive linemen’s “snap counts come down, which means we got more fresh legs,” and “you saw guys feeding off each other.”

He said, “Ryan comes back, the receiving corps just as a whole, I think, becomes more complete.” He mentioned Racin Delgatty “setting the direction of the offense there each and every snap,” which he called “critical.” And he admitted, “Losing Josh is going to hurt.”

But then he said, “It’s more about we got to focus on improving, and having optimal performance is what the key is with who you have.” He said, “That’s our job as coaches to continue to put guys in spots as things change to be able to be our best.” And he finished with, “Keep plugging away, keep improving. That’s what’s going to be the difference in these teams that have success the latter part of the season. There’s a lot of football still to be played.”

And then, in the last question of the press conference, a reporter asked DeBoer how he would characterize his team right now. He said, “I think there’s a toughness about our guys. I think there’s a piece where our guys just really care about each other. They’re going to stay out there and fight.” And he said the players understand that “the fight on Saturday is one thing,” but they also have to prepare to “get to that spot where they can execute,” and that “they’ve refined their process.”

Toughness. Caring about each other. Fighting. That’s how the head coach describes this team. And after watching them in Death Valley, I think that’s exactly right.

Let’s be honest about the big takeaways, because that’s what we do here on Alabama Tunnel.

DeBoer isn’t satisfied, and that’s a good thing. The run game has to improve. The penalties have to get cleaned up. The offense has to stay on the field longer. And the team has to replace Josh Ford’s physicality. Those are real issues. But the fact that the head coach of the number three team in the country is openly saying, “We have to get better,” tells you everything about the culture.

So here’s the summary. DeBoer called LSU one of the most complete games of the season. He made the decision clear: Alabama wants to be in the SEC Championship Game and win it, even though he admitted it might “probably” be easier to skip it with the current playoff format. And with the playoff expanding to twenty-four teams next season, this could be the last SEC title game in its current form, the one DeBoer has never won. He said the staff would “never” put Keelon Russell in harm’s way, and Russell is now listed as probable. Zabien Brown and William Sanders are trending in the right direction. The run game is the number one priority. Josh Ford is a “uniter” the team loves “to death.” And Vanderbilt and Clark Lea have DeBoer’s full respect.
