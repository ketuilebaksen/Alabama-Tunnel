It’s game day in Nashville, and Alabama just made a big decision. According to ESPN, Keelon Russell is not expected to start against Vanderbilt. Instead, Austin Mack, the quarterback who followed Kalen DeBoer from Washington and has waited three seasons for this moment, is expected to make the first start of his college career. And at the same time, Ryan Grubb just gave us the most detailed breakdown yet of what this offense did right and wrong at LSU, and how it plans to replace Josh Ford. Tide Nation, there’s a lot going on this morning, so let’s get into it.

What’s up, everybody, James here, and welcome back to Alabama Tunnel. Kickoff against Vanderbilt is at eleven forty-five a.m. Central time on the SEC Network, and the biggest story of the day just broke late Friday. So today, we’re going to talk about the Keelon Russell decision, who Austin Mack is and why the staff trusts him, everything offensive coordinator Ryan Grubb said this week about the LSU game, how Alabama plans to fill the hole Josh Ford left at tight end, and what to watch for in Nashville. If you’re ready for game day, smash that like button, subscribe and turn on notifications. Let’s go.

Let’s start with the big news.

On Friday evening, ESPN’s Pete Thamel reported that Alabama is expected to sit Keelon Russell against Vanderbilt to give his ankle more time to heal. According to the report, Russell will be available if needed, but the plan is to keep him out of this game. Austin Mack is expected to start.

Now, this caught a lot of people by surprise, because Russell had been listed as probable on the SEC availability reports earlier in the week. And reports said he had been practicing. But this is exactly the kind of decision that tells you the staff is thinking about the big picture. DeBoer said on Monday, “We never want to hurt him and we will never do that.” And this decision is that promise in action.

Think about where Alabama is right now. Seven and two. Number three in the College Football Playoff rankings, which would mean a first-round bye if the season ended today. And Russell, the nineteen-year-old Maxwell Award semifinalist, played through that ankle at LSU, came out of the medical tent, and threw three touchdown passes in the second half. He’s been the heart of this offense. Alabama needs him healthy for the Iron Bowl, a possible SEC Championship Game, and the College Football Playoff. If giving him one more week, or even two, means he’s at full speed in December, that’s a smart trade.

And look at the schedule. After Vanderbilt, Alabama hosts Chattanooga, an FCS opponent, on November twenty-first. As CBS Sports pointed out, that’s another game where Alabama could choose to be careful with Russell. Then comes the Iron Bowl against Auburn on November twenty-eighth. So in theory, the staff has a path to give Russell real recovery time and still have him fresh for the biggest game of the regular season. That’s the kind of planning that wins championships. You don’t burn your most important player in November if you don’t have to.

And here’s the thing. Alabama can make this decision because it trusts its backup.

So let’s talk about Austin Mack.

Mack followed DeBoer from Washington, where he signed in 2023, and he’s been in Tuscaloosa ever since. This summer, he was in a real competition for the starting job. It ran from spring practice all the way into late fall camp, and Russell narrowly won it in late August. Over the last three seasons, Mack has completed twenty-nine of thirty-nine passes for two hundred ninety yards and three touchdowns in nine games. This season, he’s three of four for twenty-three yards. So this will be the biggest moment of his college career.

And he’s not new to big moments. Last season, he saw spot duty and threw for two hundred twenty-eight yards and two touchdowns. He’s been on the sideline for huge SEC games, he’s been through the playoff run, and he’s watched how Russell handled the pressure all season. That kind of experience matters. Coming into a game cold is hard. Preparing all week as the starter, with the first-team offense, is completely different. And that’s what Mack has been able to do.

But don’t think of him as just a backup. DeBoer has made that very clear. He said, “Definitely should not be thinking of him as a guy who’s just going to be a backup, because that’s not how we think of him.” And on Monday, DeBoer said the team has “a lot of confidence if we had to move to Austin at some point.” Well, that point has arrived.

And Mack knows this offense as well as anyone. He’s been in Ryan Grubb’s system since his Washington days. He knows the terminology. He knows the receivers. He knows the protections. That’s a huge advantage for a quarterback making his first start on the road. And honestly, I love this story. A guy who has waited, worked and stayed ready for three seasons finally gets his shot. And he gets it with the team ranked third in the country. Whatever happens today, Tide Nation should be behind him all the way.

And think about what this says about the culture DeBoer has built. A quarterback could have transferred after losing a close battle in camp. Mack stayed, kept working, and kept preparing like a starter. Now the team needs him, and he’s ready. That’s what a great program looks like.

Now, there’s one more change on the availability report. Running back AK Dear was ruled out on Friday. Also out: linebackers Desmond Umeozulu and Cayden Jones, defensive linemen Mhari Johnson and Jeremiah Beaman, defensive back Nick Sherman, offensive lineman Casey Poe and tight end Josh Ford. Russell, defensive back Zabien Brown, running back EJ Crowell, defensive back Chuck McDonald and offensive lineman William Sanders were listed as probable. So Crowell and Trae’shawn Brown will be important in the backfield, and the run game will matter more than ever with Mack under center.

Now let’s get to Ryan Grubb, because his press conference this week was full of honest, detailed insight about the LSU game. And a lot of it explains why Alabama feels good about where its offense is going, even with Mack at quarterback.

Grubb opened by talking about the emotion of that win. He said, “Obviously, great win. Guys were incredibly excited, and that’s probably one of the best parts.” He talked about “the moments after the football game” and “all the work they put into getting a win like that against a really good football team.” And he said the players’ “confidence they had before the football game I thought was really indicative of how they played” in “a hard-fought game against a really, really good defense.”

Then he went through the good things. First, the red zone. He said Alabama was “really three for three in the red zone,” noting that Cederian Morgan’s touchdown came from just outside of it. And he said, “In games like that, we talk quite a bit about the opportunities in the red zone. That can be the whole game.” He said “converting touchdowns in those situations” was “really, really important for us.”

Second, turnovers. And this is one of my favorite things anybody said this week. Grubb shared a little ritual he has with Russell. He said, “Every day when we started the meeting, I’d ask Keelon, ‘How many turnovers you’re going to have?’ And he’d say zero. And I said, that’s the right answer.” And then Russell went out and played a turnover-free game in Death Valley. That’s the first time all season Alabama played a turnover-free game on the road.

Grubb also had the same question about sacks. He said, “We started every meeting, how many sacks are we going to take? Zero. How many turnovers we’re going to have? Zero.” And he said, “We failed on one of those two things.” But even there, he gave Russell a lot of credit. Russell was sacked twice by an LSU defense that came into the game ranked second in the country in sacks. And Grubb said he’d only put one of those on Russell. The other was a protection issue up front, and he said, “I don’t think there’s anything he could have done.”

On the one he put on Russell, Grubb explained something really important. He said Russell got out of the pocket, “had an outlet in front of him” and “should have got the football out of his hand.” And he said that’s about protecting himself. “It seems innocent to take a two-yard sack, but the guy’s rolling up on his legs, and it’s just a play he can take off of himself.” That’s a coordinator who cares about his quarterback’s health, not just his stats. And with Russell’s ankle in mind, it’s even more meaningful now.

Then Grubb talked about Russell’s growth. He said, “He had three throwaways that I thought were ones I hadn’t seen all year.” Think about that. Three times, Russell threw the ball away instead of forcing it or taking a sack, and Grubb said those were the kinds of plays he hadn’t seen from Russell all season. He said, “Some of those mature moments from him continue happening.” And he explained, “There’s still some of these small moments that he’s learning in the course of football games, but the bigger moments are fewer and further between, which is great.”

And he called Russell’s performance “one of the guttier performances I’ve ever been around from a kid that age.” He said, “Just how tough he was dealing with an injury,” against “a really, really good defensive front,” and “how he responded to the moment,” with “nothing” being “too big or too tough.” Then he said this: “Really honestly, I think it was the first game I can truly say the ball really was never in jeopardy.”

That’s huge. The first game all season where the offensive coordinator felt the ball was never at risk. And Grubb repeated what he’s said before: “I know his best football is ahead of him. And I think he’s one of the best quarterbacks, if not the best quarterback in the country.” He added, “He’s going to keep proving that.”

So even if Russell sits today, the progress he’s shown is going to be there when he comes back.

Now, let’s talk about Cederian Morgan’s touchdown, because Grubb revealed some fascinating details about that play.

A reporter asked if Morgan was Russell’s first read on that twenty-five-yard touchdown, and whether Grubb was going for a knockout punch right after Ryan Coleman-Williams’ punt return. Grubb said, “You just hit it all on the head.” He explained that the staff had “some stuff dialed up in the red zone that we worked really hard at manufacturing, even from the personnel groupings that we were in, to get the look that we needed.” He said having Cederian Morgan and Derek Meadows in the game “was very intentional,” and “that call was ready to go in that area.”

And then he said, “We didn’t think we were going to get the ball there, and then obviously of course Ryan makes an incredible return and gets us in that position.” He said, “Had to stay aggressive in the game, just knew the team we were playing.” He talked about “some max aggression plays that we had to make,” and said that “was maybe one of the biggest ones.” And on Russell’s throw, he said it was “about as good a throw as humanly possible in that spot.”

That’s great coaching. A play designed specifically for that area of the field, with specific personnel, called at the perfect moment. And a freshman receiver and a nineteen-year-old quarterback executing it perfectly.

But Grubb was honest about the shortcomings too, and that’s what makes him so good.

First, the end of the first half. He said, “I just felt like we had such a great opportunity right before half. We get the football, and we’ve been so great in the two-minute drill.” But an offensive pass interference call set Alabama back. Grubb said, “We’re in points right there, and it was super frustrating in that moment.” He said, “I thought that could have been a pivotal moment in the football game.”

Second, pass protection. Grubb said “a couple protections broke down at times” when Alabama had receivers open. He said Ryan Coleman-Williams was “running wide open down the sideline twice,” and another receiver was open across the middle. He said, “Against that defense, there’s only so many times you’re going to get those opportunities.” But he added that being able “to convert on a couple big hits after that was critical.”

Third, the penalties. Grubb said they were “really disappointing.” He explained that the staff had “worked our tail off” and put “a lot of resources” into deciding what kind of snap count to use in the noise of Tiger Stadium. Should they go on a clap? Should they go on a silent count? He said the guys did a great job all week in practice, but there were still a few false starts in the game. And he admitted, “You’re going to have a little bit of friction on a game like that, whether you go on silent or on clap.” But he also said, “I thought we operated better than we did in Tennessee, which was a step in the right direction, but still not good enough.”

And there was one delay-of-game penalty that Grubb actually defended. He said, “That was the right move. That’s what he’s coached to do.” He explained that Alabama was already facing third and long, in the second half of a tight game, and “you don’t want to blow a timeout in that situation.” He said, “He made the right decision in that spot.”

And he wrapped up his opening statement by saying, “Huge win for the program and for the state. Couldn’t be more excited, proud of the guys that put ourselves in a really good position for the rest of the year.”

Now, let’s talk about the tight ends, because that’s the other big storyline this week.

Josh Ford is out for the season with a knee injury he suffered at LSU. DeBoer called him “a uniter,” and said the team “just loves him to death.” So who fills that role?

Grubb said, “I think Danny Lewis, it kind of starts with him. He’s your next pure inline Y, or blocker.” So Danny Lewis Jr. is the guy who’ll step into Ford’s blocking role. Then Grubb talked about Kaleb Edwards, and this was some of the highest praise he gave anyone. He said, “Kaleb is obviously our guy. He’s our starting Y.” He said, “I can’t say enough good about him,” and “he played a whale of a football game” at LSU.

And then Grubb said something that shows how highly he thinks of Edwards. He said, “I think he’s one of the five or six tight ends in the country that are that six-five, two-sixty-pound true fifty-fifty player that can block and catch, and you don’t give any tells away when he’s on the field because he can do both.” That’s a huge compliment. When a tight end can block and catch at a high level, a defense can’t tell what’s coming based on who’s on the field. Grubb said, “Think the world of Kaleb,” and he’s “probably going to put a little bit more on his plate.”

And he said Jay Lindsey “will have probably more of an expanded role as well.” So the plan is clear: Edwards as the starter, Lewis stepping into the blocking role, and Lindsey getting more snaps. Marshall Pritchett and freshman Mack Sutter could get chances too.

Now, let’s talk about what all of this means for today’s game.

With Mack at quarterback, I expect Grubb to lean on what Alabama does well. A balanced run game with Trae’shawn Brown and EJ Crowell. Quick, rhythm throws to get Mack comfortable early. And Kaleb Edwards, the “true fifty-fifty” tight end, could be a big part of the game plan as a safety valve for a first-time starter. And the defense can make Mack’s life a lot easier. Alabama’s defense leads the SEC and ranks third in the nation with twenty-one forced turnovers, including thirteen interceptions and five pick-sixes. Short fields and defensive touchdowns are the best friend a first-time starting quarterback can have.

On the other side, Vanderbilt is four and five, and one and five in the SEC. Its only conference win came last week, twenty-five to twenty-three at Mississippi State, when kicker Brock Taylor made all six of his field goals, including a fifty-nine-yarder. Vanderbilt is led by five-star freshman quarterback Jared Curtis, the highest-rated recruit in program history. DeBoer called him “extremely talented.” And the Commodores need two more wins in their last three games to become bowl eligible. So they’ll be playing with a lot of urgency.

So here are my three keys for Mack’s first start. Key number one: start fast and simple. Get him a couple of easy completions early, let him feel the rhythm of the game, and let the run game carry some of the load. Key number two: lean on Kaleb Edwards. A big, reliable tight end who can block and catch is a first-time starter’s best friend, especially on third down and in the red zone. And key number three: let the defense do its thing. If Kane Wommack’s group keeps forcing turnovers the way it has all season, Mack won’t have to drive the length of the field every time. Do those three things, and Alabama should leave Nashville at eight and two.

And remember what DeBoer said Vanderbilt wants to do. It’s “going to try to keep the ball,” to “use the time of possession” and “limit your possessions.” So Alabama has to stay on the field on offense, win the time-of-possession battle where it can, and make the most of every drive. And it has to keep Brock Taylor out of field goal range, because he can hit from just about anywhere.

And of course, there’s the 2024 memory. Alabama lost forty to thirty-five in Nashville the week after beating Georgia. DeBoer brought it up in the locker room right after LSU. But the message all week has been simple: “Reset, do it again.” And as DeBoer said, “None of the plays, none of the stops, none of the points carry over.”

Let’s be honest about the challenge, because that’s what we do here on Alabama Tunnel.

Starting a quarterback who has never started a college game, on the road, in an early kickoff, against a team that beat you the last time you were there, is not easy. There may be some growing pains. The offense might not look as explosive as it does with Russell, who can create plays with his legs. And the penalties and protection issues Grubb talked about have to get cleaned up, no matter who’s playing quarterback.

But here’s why I’m confident. This is a deep, well-coached team with an elite defense, a staff that knows exactly what it wants to do, and a backup quarterback who has been preparing for this moment for three years. And the long-term thinking is the right call. Alabama isn’t trying to just win today. It’s trying to win in December and January. Getting Russell healthy for that stretch is the priority.

So let’s sum it up. The big decision: Alabama is expected to rest Keelon Russell and start Austin Mack at Vanderbilt, per ESPN. Russell will reportedly be available if needed, but the goal is to let the ankle heal. Mack, a DeBoer guy since Washington, finally gets his first career start. Grubb called Russell’s LSU performance one of the guttiest he’s ever seen, said the ball was “never in jeopardy,” and praised his three mature throwaways. He revealed the “zero turnovers” ritual and the carefully designed play behind Cederian Morgan’s touchdown. He was honest about the penalties, the protection breakdowns and the missed chance before halftime. And at tight end, Kaleb Edwards takes on a bigger role as one of the best “true fifty-fifty” tight ends in the country, with Danny Lewis Jr. and Jay Lindsey stepping up after Josh Ford’s injury.

And one more thing about Josh Ford. He’s expected to undergo surgery on that knee, and his season is over, but his presence isn’t. DeBoer said, “I know he’ll be with us but not on the football field.” So when Kaleb Edwards, Danny Lewis Jr. and Jay Lindsey take the field today, they’ll be carrying the physical, tough-minded identity Ford helped build in that tight end room. Get well soon, Josh. This one’s for you too.
