Final total yards: LSU four hundred fifty-seven, Alabama two hundred eighty-five. Sam Leavitt threw for three hundred eighty yards. LSU had the ball more and moved it up and down the field. So how does that happen? It comes down to the moments that actually decide football games. Not the yards between the twenties. The plays inside the red zone, on fourth down, in the turnover battle and on special teams. And in every single one of those areas, Alabama won.

Let’s start with the red zone. According to the postgame numbers, Alabama went into the red zone three times on Saturday. It scored a touchdown all three times. Three for three. Perfect. LSU got into the red zone four times and came away with points only twice. That’s a huge gap. Think about it like this. Every time Alabama got close, it finished. Every time LSU got close, Alabama’s defense dug in.
