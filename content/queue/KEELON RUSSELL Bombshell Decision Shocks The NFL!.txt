“He continues to improve.” That’s the latest from Kalen DeBoer on Keelon Russell, just days before Alabama hosts Chattanooga. No setbacks. Steady progress. But the head coach also admitted there’s “still work to be done” to get his quarterback to one hundred percent. So should Alabama keep rolling with its nineteen-year-old starter, or give Austin Mack a bigger role to protect Russell for the Iron Bowl? And while everyone is focused on the quarterback, three other players just saw their stock rise in a big way. Tide Nation, let’s talk about all of it.

What’s up, everybody, James here, and welcome back to Alabama Tunnel. Alabama is eight and two, six and two in the SEC, and still number three in the College Football Playoff rankings. On Saturday, the Crimson Tide host Chattanooga at Bryant-Denny Stadium on Senior Day. One week later, it’s the Iron Bowl. And right now, the biggest storyline in Tuscaloosa is the health of the starting quarterback.

Today, we’re going to do two things. First, we’ll break down the three players whose stock is rising after the win at Vanderbilt: one on offense and two on defense. Then we’ll get into the latest on Keelon Russell, what DeBoer said on his radio show this week, and the big debate about how Alabama should handle the quarterback position on Saturday. If you love this team, hit that like button, subscribe, and turn on notifications so you never miss an update here on Alabama Tunnel. Let’s get into it.

Let’s start with the bigger picture from the Vanderbilt game. Alabama won twenty-one to six, and it wasn’t the prettiest offensive performance. But it told us something important about this team. The defense is playing at an elite level. Kane Wommack’s unit held Vanderbilt to just two hundred thirty-five total yards. It held the Commodores to three conversions on nineteen combined third and fourth down attempts. And it piled up five sacks and nine tackles for loss. Freshman quarterback Jared Curtis completed twenty-five of forty passes for two hundred twenty-three yards, but he never found a rhythm, and Vanderbilt never found the end zone.

That’s the kind of defense that wins in November. And inside that performance, a few individual players really stood out.

Player number one whose stock is rising: running back Daniel Hill.

We talked about Hill in depth earlier this week, so I’ll keep this short, but he deserves to be on this list. After a long stretch of limited production in SEC play, Hill ran for eighty-nine yards on fourteen carries against Vanderbilt. That’s six point four yards per carry, his best average of the season. And the timing of his runs was huge. He converted short-yardage situations and helped Alabama run the clock in the fourth quarter.

Why does that matter so much right now? Because Alabama’s running game has been held under one hundred yards for four straight games, and that has put enormous pressure on Russell’s arm and, now, on his ankle. When the run game works, the quarterback doesn’t have to drop back forty times a game. He doesn’t have to scramble away from pressure on every third down. A productive Daniel Hill doesn’t just help the offense. He helps protect the quarterback.

DeBoer noticed, too. After the game, he said, “Thought Daniel ran really hard, did a nice job there. That was important for us.” And here’s a fun bonus: Saturday, the day Alabama plays Chattanooga, is Daniel Hill’s twenty-first birthday. A big day at home would be the perfect way to build on Nashville and give Ryan Grubb another reliable option heading into the Iron Bowl.

Of course, one game doesn’t solve all of Alabama’s run game problems. But it gives the coaching staff something real to build on. And in late November, that matters.

Player number two whose stock is rising: linebacker QB Reese.

If you haven’t been paying close attention to Reese, now is the time to start. Against Vanderbilt, he led Alabama with eight tackles, a season high. He also picked up a sack and a tackle for loss. And he did it in a game where Alabama’s defense was flying around and attacking from the very first snap.

What makes Reese’s performance so valuable is what it says about Alabama’s depth. Championship teams don’t just need great starters. They need reliable players throughout the rotation who can step in and keep the same level of intensity. Injuries happen. Bodies wear down. And in a long SEC season, the teams that win are the teams that can plug in the next man and keep rolling.

Reese showed he can be that guy. He was physical, he was in the right spots, and he got after the quarterback when Alabama needed pressure. DeBoer talked after the game about how the defense is “attacking” and “really not playing hesitant at all in those big moments.” Reese was a perfect example of that. He played fast and he played confident.

And think about the timing. Alabama’s injury report for Chattanooga lists several defenders as out, including linebackers Cayden Jones and Desmond Umeozulu. When you’re dealing with injuries like that, having a linebacker like Reese emerge is a huge boost. Performances like this one can earn a young player more responsibility, and it wouldn’t surprise me to see him play an even bigger role in the final weeks of the season.

Player number three whose stock is rising: defensive back Dijon Lee Jr.

Lee had one of the most complete games of his season against Vanderbilt. On defense, he finished with seven tackles, five of them solo, a season high in total tackles. He was physical in run support and tight in coverage, and he was a big part of a secondary that kept Vanderbilt from creating big plays downfield.

But his biggest contribution came on special teams. In the second half, Lee took a kickoff back eighty-three yards, setting up Alabama deep in Vanderbilt territory. A few plays later, Russell found Lotzeir Brooks for a nine-yard touchdown, and Alabama took a twenty-one to six lead. That play changed the game.

And here’s what I think deserves more attention. When your offense isn’t consistently moving the football, a special teams play like that is worth its weight in gold. It flips the field. It gives your offense a short field. And it takes the life out of the other team’s sideline. DeBoer called it “huge,” and he said the staff felt there was “an opportunity, if we got one of those kicks, that we could get some good field position.” Lee made it happen. DeBoer added, “Seems like when he’s got those opportunities, he’s done a really good job.”

Lee is the definition of complementary football. Defense, special teams, and the ability to change a game in more than one way. That makes him one of the most valuable players on this roster right now.

Before we move on, a couple of honorable mentions, because these guys deserve some love too. On offense, Lotzeir Brooks caught five passes for sixty-two yards and two touchdowns, including that thirty-nine-yard score that opened the second half. Brooks has become a playmaker Russell trusts in big moments, and with Josh Ford out for the year, Alabama needs those kinds of reliable targets. And tight end Marshall Pritchett caught a nine-yard touchdown with just six seconds left in the first half, a play that completely changed the momentum of the game.

On defense, the pass rush deserves credit as a group. Five sacks, nine tackles for loss, constant pressure on a talented freshman quarterback. Wolf linebacker Justin Hill got back into the mix after dealing with a nagging injury and picked up a sack and one and a half tackles for loss. When this defensive front is healthy and rotating fresh bodies, it’s one of the most disruptive groups in the SEC. DeBoer said it best: “The way they’re flying around, getting pressure at the right times, that helps when the personnel up front is doing what they’re doing.”

So put those three together. Hill is showing he can be a productive lead back. Reese is strengthening an already impressive defensive rotation. And Lee is proving he can impact games in multiple ways. That’s exactly the kind of depth and development you want to see late in the season, when injuries and the physical grind of SEC football test every team in the country.

Now let’s get to the biggest question facing Alabama right now: the quarterback.

Let’s recap. Keelon Russell injured his left ankle in the second half of the win at LSU on November seventh. He went to the medical tent, came back and finished the game. The week before Vanderbilt was chaos. Russell had very limited practice reps. Austin Mack took a lot of first-team work. ESPN reported on Friday that Mack was expected to start. Ninety minutes before kickoff, Russell was still listed as a game-time decision. And then Russell started, and he threw for two hundred seventeen yards and three touchdowns.

AL.com also noted this week that Russell has been dealing with a foot issue from earlier in the season on top of the ankle. So this is a young quarterback who has been playing through some real pain.

On Monday, DeBoer said, “The injury wasn’t gone completely, so it’s just matter of managing it, and we’ll see as the week goes on where we’re at.” He said Russell “got through relatively clean” at Vanderbilt, even after taking a couple of hits. And offensive coordinator Ryan Grubb said, “He’s doing really good right now,” adding, “I think status quo.”

And then came the latest update, from DeBoer’s weekly radio show on Wednesday night. Here’s what he said, and this is the most recent information we have. DeBoer said, “He continues to improve.” He said, “There was no setbacks or anything on Saturday.” But he also added, “There’s still work to be done to continue to get him to one hundred percent.”

DeBoer also praised the way Russell has handled all of this. He said, “He’s done a great job, just being a pro just like the rest of our guys do.” He said Russell is “working in the training room every single day.” And he finished with this: “Just will continue to be steady and take it day by day.”

And one more detail here. Normally, Alabama releases an availability report on Wednesday night. This week, the radio show update served as the midweek update on Russell instead. So the next big clues will come from the official availability reports later in the week, and from pregame warmups on Saturday. Keep an eye on who takes the first snaps with the starters during warmups. Last week, that’s how we found out Russell was playing.

So here’s what we know. No setbacks. Steady improvement. Not yet one hundred percent. And the staff is taking it day by day. That’s encouraging news, but it doesn’t fully answer the question of how much Russell will play on Saturday.

And that brings us to the big debate.

On one side, there’s the case for giving Austin Mack a bigger role against Chattanooga. And it’s a strong case. First, it would reduce Russell’s exposure to more hits while his ankle continues to heal. Every snap carries some risk, and the Iron Bowl is just one week later. Second, it would give the coaching staff a chance to see Mack in real game situations instead of waiting until an emergency forces him into action. Remember, Mack prepared all last week as if he would start. Grubb said he handled that situation “like a champ.” And DeBoer said, “I can’t give him enough credit.” Giving Mack meaningful snaps in front of the home crowd would reward that preparation and make Alabama better prepared for any situation in the postseason.

And we’ve seen this season how much quarterback depth can matter. Look at Ohio State. The Buckeyes lost their starting quarterback, Julian Sayin, to an arm injury, and then they dropped back-to-back games to USC and Oregon with freshman Luke Fahey under center. Now, to be fair, Ohio State’s problems weren’t only at quarterback. There were issues on defense and on special teams, too. And we wish Sayin a full recovery. But it shows how quickly a season can change when the most important position on the field is disrupted. That’s exactly the kind of situation Alabama wants to avoid.

On the other side, there’s the case for keeping Russell on the field. Russell is still a young quarterback who is developing. Every rep with the starting offense has value, especially for a unit that’s still searching for consistency. Remember, Grubb said the offense “didn’t have its fastball” early against Vanderbilt, and DeBoer said it took Russell until the middle of the second quarter to find his groove after a week of limited practice. If Russell sits out another full game, he could go into the Iron Bowl without the rhythm he needs. And this offense has averaged twenty-two points over its last five SEC games after averaging nearly fifty points during an earlier five-game stretch. Alabama needs reps, and it needs to get its timing back.

And don’t take Chattanooga lightly. The Mocs are seven and four, with a well-coached team and, according to Grubb, one of the best defenses in the FCS. That’s not a team you can just show up and beat without your best effort.

So what’s the right answer? I think it’s somewhere in the middle. Here’s my plan. Russell starts, if the staff clears him. Alabama comes out fast, scores early and builds a comfortable lead. And as soon as the game is under control, Russell heads to the sideline and Austin Mack takes over. That gives Russell live reps and rhythm, it protects his ankle from unnecessary hits, and it gives Mack valuable game experience. DeBoer has done something similar before. He got Jalen Milroe out early against Mercer in 2024, and he did the same with Ty Simpson against Eastern Illinois in 2025. Now, the coaching staff hasn’t announced any plan like that for Saturday. But it seems like the most balanced approach.

And this is also where Ryan Grubb’s play calling becomes really important. If Russell is on the field, Alabama has to be smart. That means not asking him to hold the football for a long time behind the line, and not exposing him to unnecessary contact with designed runs or long-developing plays. Grubb has already said he wants Russell to “get the football out of his hand.” Quick throws, a strong run game and clean protection can keep Russell safe without taking him out of the lineup. Protecting the quarterback doesn’t always mean benching him. It means recognizing the situation and adjusting.

And Tide Nation, let’s be clear about something. This is not a quarterback controversy. Keelon Russell is Alabama’s starting quarterback. He’s led this team to an eight and two record and the number three ranking in the country, and he’s been tough as nails playing through pain. The conversation about Austin Mack is about making sure Alabama has its best possible options when the games matter most. That’s smart roster management, not a controversy.

And think about what Russell has done this season while playing through pain. He’s a nineteen-year-old sophomore. He went into Death Valley and helped Alabama win by eighteen. A week later, after barely practicing, he went into Nashville and threw three touchdown passes. He’s taken hits, he’s dealt with a foot issue and an ankle issue, and he keeps showing up for his teammates. That’s toughness. That’s leadership. And it’s a big reason this team is in the position it’s in.

There’s also no indication right now that Russell will miss significant time. DeBoer’s update was positive. “He continues to improve.” No setbacks. That’s what Alabama fans want to hear. The goal now is simple: get him as close to one hundred percent as possible by the time Auburn comes to Bryant-Denny on November twenty-eighth.

So let’s put it all together and look at the bigger picture.

The Vanderbilt win gave Alabama several encouraging signs. Daniel Hill is giving this offense a running game it desperately needs. QB Reese is showing the depth of this defense. Dijon Lee Jr. is making plays on defense and special teams. And Kane Wommack’s unit continues to perform at an elite level, having allowed just one touchdown over its last eight quarters.

But the offense still needs more consistency, and the health of the quarterback is the biggest factor of all. Alabama has the talent to compete for an SEC title and make a deep run in the College Football Playoff. But to get there, it needs a balanced offense, continued development from its young players and, most importantly, a healthy Keelon Russell.

That’s why the decisions DeBoer and Grubb make over the next couple of weeks could be just as important as the results on the scoreboard.

And let’s not forget where Alabama stands nationally. In Tuesday night’s College Football Playoff rankings, the committee kept Alabama at number three, even though all three of its wins over previously ranked teams, Kentucky, South Carolina and LSU, lost last weekend. Georgia is number one, Utah is number two, Alabama is number three and UCLA is number four. Texas is right behind at number five. The top four teams get a first-round bye, which means an extra week of rest and one fewer game to win. For a team trying to get its quarterback back to one hundred percent, that extra week could be priceless. So every decision this week, including how much Russell plays, connects directly to that bigger goal.

Here’s the schedule. Saturday, November twenty-first, Alabama hosts Chattanooga at Bryant-Denny Stadium. Kickoff is at one p.m. Central, two p.m. Eastern, on SEC Network Plus. It’s Senior Day, with twenty players being honored, including Bray Hubbard, Caleb Woodson, Fatutoa Henry, Devan Thompkins and Danny Lewis Jr. And then, one week later, it’s the Iron Bowl, at home, against Auburn.

So what would a perfect Saturday look like for Alabama? Let me give you my checklist.

Number one: a fast start. Score on the first two or three drives. After the slow first half at Vanderbilt, the offense needs to come out sharp and set the tone right away.

Number two: a clean, healthy day for the quarterback. Whether that’s Russell for a quarter or two, or Mack for most of the game, nobody takes unnecessary hits.

Number three: a real run game. Break that streak of four straight games under one hundred rushing yards. Give Daniel Hill a big birthday, and get the rest of the running backs into a rhythm too.

Number four: takeaways. DeBoer was frustrated that the defense didn’t force any against Vanderbilt. Getting a couple of interceptions or fumble recoveries would be a great confidence boost before Auburn.

Number five: snaps for the young players. If Alabama takes control early, the backups and freshmen get valuable experience, and the starters get extra rest for the Iron Bowl.

And number six: a special day for the seniors. Twenty players being honored, a full stadium and a big win. That’s the way to send this group into its final home game against Auburn.

If Alabama checks most of those boxes, it’ll head into Iron Bowl week healthy, confident and rolling.

Here on Alabama Tunnel, we’re feeling good about where this team is heading. A top-three ranking, a defense that’s playing as well as any in the country, young players stepping up, and a quarterback who keeps getting better every day. If Alabama handles this week the right way, it could be playing its best football exactly when it matters most.
