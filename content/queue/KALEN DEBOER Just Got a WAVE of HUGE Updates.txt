Update number one: the AP poll.

The new AP Top 25 came out on Sunday, and Alabama is now ranked number seven in the country. Here’s the top ten: Georgia is back at number one. Utah is two. Notre Dame is three. UCLA is four. Miami is five. Indiana is six. Then Alabama at seven, Texas at eight, Tennessee at nine and Texas Tech at ten.

And look at what happened to the teams Alabama has played. Kentucky dropped to thirteenth after losing at Tennessee. And LSU fell all the way to twenty-fourth after Saturday’s loss. That’s what an eighteen-point road win does. Alabama stays in the top ten, and the team it beat tumbles down the poll.

Now, the AP poll is nice, but what really matters is the College Football Playoff rankings. And that brings me to update number two.

Update number two: the College Football Playoff rankings drop tonight.

Remember last week? The committee put Alabama at number five in its first rankings, ahead of a seven-and-one Kentucky team, an undefeated UCLA team and a seven-and-one BYU team. And a lot of people were upset. National analysts said a two-loss Alabama team didn’t deserve to be that high. Some said the Tide had done nothing to earn it.

Well, Alabama went into Death Valley and answered on the field. It beat a ranked LSU team by eighteen points on the road. And the weekend broke in Alabama’s favor too. Kentucky, which was sixth, lost thirty-four to six at Tennessee. And Alabama already owns a forty-five-to-seventeen road win over the Wildcats. Ohio State, which was twelfth in the first CFP rankings, lost at home to Oregon. So when the committee meets again, Alabama’s résumé looks even stronger than it did a week ago. Road wins at Kentucky and LSU. Wins over South Carolina and Florida State. And two losses to Georgia and Tennessee, both of which are ranked in the top ten of the AP poll right now. I’d expect Alabama to be right in the mix near the top again. We’ll have a full breakdown as soon as the rankings come out.

And let’s not forget what was being said before the LSU game. Paul Finebaum said the LSU game was do or die, and his words were: “If they drop that game, they’re done. They’re not getting in the playoff.” Well, Alabama didn’t drop that game. It won it by eighteen. So by that same logic, Alabama is very much alive, and very much in control. There was a lot of talk in the studios and on social media about whether Alabama belonged at number five. And look, it’s fair for people to debate rankings. That’s part of college football. But the best answer is always the one you give on the field, and on Saturday, Alabama gave a loud one. The red zone numbers, the fourth-down stops and the turnover margin told the real story of that game, and those are exactly the kinds of things a committee notices when it looks closely at a résumé.

Update number three: Devan Thompkins is the SEC Defensive Lineman of the Week.

On Monday, the SEC named Alabama’s Devan Thompkins its Defensive Lineman of the Week after his performance at LSU. And he earned it. Thompkins had two tackles, and both were sacks, for a combined loss of sixteen yards.

But it’s when he got them that really matters. His first sack came late in the first half, when he dropped Sam Leavitt for a nine-yard loss. And on the very next play, Luke Metz intercepted Leavitt. That pressure set up the takeaway. His second sack came late in the fourth quarter and helped force a turnover on downs. That gave Alabama the ball at the LSU eighteen-yard line, and the offense turned it into the touchdown that sealed the game.

So both of Thompkins’ sacks led directly to huge momentum swings. That’s an impact player. It’s his second career SEC Defensive Lineman of the Week award, and his second multi-sack game of this season. He’s a fifth-year senior from Tracy, California, and this is the kind of veteran leadership you need on the defensive line in November. And here’s a fun stat: this is the eighth SEC weekly award an Alabama player has earned this season. That tells you how many guys on this roster are playing at a high level.

Update number four: Alabama’s players of the week.

On Monday, Alabama also named its own players of the week for the LSU win, and the list is a who’s who of Saturday’s heroes.

On offense, quarterback Keelon Russell, who went seventeen of twenty-four for two hundred six yards, three touchdowns and no interceptions, and added twenty-five rushing yards on eight carries. And tight end Kaleb Edwards, who tied for the team lead with three catches, including Alabama’s final touchdown.

On defense, linebacker Luke Metz, the sophomore from Hoschton, Georgia, who had eleven tackles and that big interception. Devan Thompkins, with his two sacks. And here’s a really cool one: the entire secondary was named a player of the week. That’s a reward for a group that held LSU without a touchdown pass, even with Leavitt throwing forty-seven times. Leavitt threw for three hundred eighty yards, but the secondary kept everything in front of them, tackled well and came up big in the red zone and on fourth down.

Think about how hard that is. Leavitt completed thirty-five passes. LSU had receivers running free underneath all game long. A lot of secondaries would have cracked and given up the big play. Alabama’s didn’t. Zero passing touchdowns allowed. One interception. Two LSU drives that got inside the Alabama ten-yard line and came away with nothing, including that fourth and four at the nine where the pass was knocked down. Caleb Woodson had eight tackles, a pass breakup, a quarterback hurry and a forced fumble. Bray Hubbard had nine tackles. That’s a group that bent but never broke, and that’s why the entire unit got recognized. Kane Wommack’s message to his defense all week was about getting off the field on third and fourth down, and the secondary delivered exactly that.

And Kaleb Edwards deserves his flowers too. He finished with three catches for fifty yards and the four-yard touchdown that made it thirty-one to thirteen. Edwards has been a reliable target all season, and he also caught a big touchdown in the Florida State comeback back in September. When Russell needs a big target in the red zone, Edwards keeps showing up.

And the scout team and special teams honors went to Ben Jackson, Steve Mboumoua, Elijah Murray and Noah Rogers. Those guys don’t get a lot of attention, but the scout team is a big reason the starters were so prepared for what LSU did on Saturday. Shoutout to all of them.

And let’s talk a little more about Luke Metz, because this kid is having a breakout season. He’s only a sophomore, and he’s already one of the leaders of this defense. Against LSU, he was everywhere. Eleven tackles, the most on the team. And that interception right before halftime, which he returned twenty-eight yards, was his second pick of the season. Think about the timing. LSU was driving, looking to cut into Alabama’s ten-to-three lead before the break. Thompkins sacked Leavitt, and then Metz took the ball away on the very next play. That’s how a defense wins a game. One guy creates the pressure, the next guy makes the play. And DeBoer has talked all season about the swarm mentality, about defenders flying to the ball and not assuming a ball carrier is going down. Metz is the perfect example of that. He plays every snap like it’s the last one.

Update number five: the latest on Keelon Russell.

This is the one everybody wants to know about. Russell went to the medical tent in the third quarter at LSU with an ankle injury, came back out, and threw three touchdown passes in the second half. On Monday, DeBoer said Russell is “day to day.” He said, “He’s day to day and will continue.” He also said, “He finished out the game. That’s a good sign right there.” And he added, “I’d expect him to do everything he can to get out on the football field.”

Offensive coordinator Ryan Grubb said the forty-eight to seventy-two hours after a game are “the most critical part” of recovery, and the staff will check on Russell on Tuesday and Wednesday, with the training staff managing his practice reps. And Grubb sounded optimistic. He said, “They seem pretty confident Keelon is going to be alright.” He also pointed out that Russell “responded really well from a foot injury in the Texas A&M game.”

If Alabama needs a backup, DeBoer said the team has “a lot of confidence” in senior Austin Mack, who competed with Russell for the starting job this summer. But the signs right now point toward Russell being ready. We’ll keep you updated when the SEC availability reports come out later this week. Get well soon, Keelon.

And Grubb had some incredible things to say about his young quarterback. He said, “His best football’s ahead of him. He’s one of, if not the best, quarterbacks in the country.” He called Russell’s LSU performance “one of the guttier performances I’ve ever been around for a kid that age.” That’s high praise from a coach who has worked with some great quarterbacks.

Remember, Grubb was the offensive coordinator at Washington when Michael Penix Jr. led the Huskies to the national championship game. He knows what elite quarterback play looks like. And he’s saying a nineteen-year-old sophomore is already among the best in the country. Russell was only named the starter on August twenty-second, after a summer battle with Austin Mack. Less than three months later, he went into Death Valley with a bad ankle, threw three second-half touchdown passes and didn’t turn the ball over once. Back in September, after the Florida State game, Russell admitted, “We got too many mental mistakes right now, but we’re going to fix it.” He’s fixing it, week by week, right in front of our eyes.

Update number six: DeBoer called LSU one of Alabama’s most complete games.

On Monday, DeBoer looked back at the LSU win and said, “Thought it was one of the more, if not the most complete game, we played.” He pointed to ball security first: “No turnovers.” And it’s true. Alabama didn’t turn the ball over once, the first time all season it played a turnover-free road game. In its previous three road games, Alabama had been averaging three turnovers per game.

DeBoer also said, “I thought the guys just played really hard.” And then he said something that sums up this whole season: “Second-half success, the fourth quarter in particular, continues to show up.” Alabama has now won four games this season after trailing in the second half: Kentucky, Florida State, Texas A&M and LSU. According to one national broadcast, that’s the most of any Power Four team in the country.

Defensive coordinator Kane Wommack was proud of his group too. He said, “I thought our guys played really physical. We hit really hard.” And on the four fourth-down stops, he gave the players all the credit. He said, “They brought those calls to life and executed them.”

And honestly, those fourth-down stops are the story of the game. LSU went for it four times, and Alabama stopped every single one. One of them came on fourth and four at the Alabama nine-yard line in the second quarter. Another came late in the fourth quarter, when Thompkins’ sack forced the turnover on downs. LSU moved the ball between the twenties, outgaining Alabama by more than one hundred seventy yards. But inside the red zone, Alabama went three for three with touchdowns, while LSU got in four times and came away with just ten points. As one analyst put it, the scoreboard doesn’t reward yards. It rewards getting into the end zone.

Update number seven: DeBoer was already thinking about Vanderbilt in the LSU locker room.

This might be my favorite update of the week. Minutes after the LSU win, before the team even left Baton Rouge, DeBoer brought up Vanderbilt. He told the Crimson Tide Sports Network, “We talked about it already in the locker room.” He explained, “A lot of these guys don’t have the memory from a couple years ago. Big win, then going up to Vanderbilt.”

He’s talking about 2024. That year, Alabama beat Georgia in a classic, then went to Nashville the next week and lost forty to thirty-five to Vanderbilt. DeBoer doesn’t want history to repeat itself. He said, “Every game is its own game, and this week means nothing when stepping on the football field next Saturday.”

And Wommack feels the same way. He said the 2024 loss at Vanderbilt “had a major impact on me.” He said there are “a lot of challenges in a number of different ways” with this Vanderbilt team. And about Clark Lea’s staff, he said, “This staff, I have tremendous respect for.” DeBoer called it “a great challenge there,” and said the message this week is simple: “Reset and do it again.”

That’s exactly the right approach. Enjoy the LSU win for one night, and then get back to work.

Update number eight: a first look at Vanderbilt.

So let’s take a look at the Commodores. Vanderbilt is four and five this season. And last Saturday, it picked up its first SEC win of the year, beating Mississippi State twenty-five to twenty-three in Starkville. In fact, Vanderbilt was the last team in the SEC to get a conference win this season.

The star of that game was kicker Brock Taylor. He made all six of his field goal attempts, including a fifty-nine-yarder, and accounted for nineteen of Vanderbilt’s twenty-five points. That’s a weapon. Alabama’s defense has to keep Vanderbilt out of field goal range, because Taylor can hit from just about anywhere. Vanderbilt also leaned on its running game, with Makhilyn Young and Sedrick Alexander combining for one hundred twenty-two yards and a touchdown on thirty-six carries. So expect Vanderbilt to try to run the ball, control the clock and keep Alabama’s offense on the sideline.

And Vanderbilt has plenty to play for. It needs two more wins to become bowl eligible. And you know Clark Lea’s team would love nothing more than to recreate what happened in 2024. So this game matters a lot to the Commodores.

And Alabama fans remember exactly how that 2024 game went. Alabama came into Nashville riding high after the Georgia win, and Vanderbilt controlled the game from the start, ran the ball, kept the clock moving and pulled off a forty-to-thirty-five upset that sent the goalposts into the river. It was one of the defining moments of DeBoer’s first season. This group of players has heard about it all week, and that’s a good thing. Nothing focuses a team quite like the memory of a game that got away.

But here’s the thing. Vanderbilt also nearly let that Mississippi State game slip away. It led sixteen to nothing in the third quarter, and then Mississippi State rallied, and Vanderbilt only survived because the Bulldogs missed a two-point conversion. So this is a team that has struggled to finish. If Alabama plays the way it did in the second half at LSU, physical, disciplined and clean with the football, it should be in great shape.

The game is this Saturday, November fourteenth, in Nashville, at eleven a.m. Central time, and it’ll be on the SEC Network. An early kickoff, on the road, after a huge emotional win. That’s the textbook trap game. But with how DeBoer and Wommack are talking about it, I don’t think this team will be caught off guard.

Update number nine: DeBoer’s take on the playoff expansion.

DeBoer was also asked about the College Football Playoff’s future expansion. He said, “Obviously the expansion of that will, I’m sure, affect teams and their scheduling.” He said he hopes it encourages more competitive games across college football. But he made it clear where his focus is: “Right now, I’m focused on this year.” That’s DeBoer. Never looking too far ahead.
