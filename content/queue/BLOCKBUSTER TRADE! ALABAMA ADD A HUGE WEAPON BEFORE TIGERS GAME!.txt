Let’s start with how DeBoer opened his press conference.

He said, “I thought that last week was a productive week for our team, for our coaches.” And then he explained the challenge of a bye week in the middle of a season like this one. He said, “Everyone’s in kind of a different spot, especially with the players. Guys coming back from injury, guys that need to get healthy that have been playing, some guys that need to continue to grow and develop.” That’s a coach who understands that every player on his roster needed something different from those two weeks. And then he summed it up: “I thought the guys had a great mindset. We got the work in and ready for a great week this week.”

Then he turned to LSU. And he gave Lane Kiffin a lot of credit. He said Kiffin has “done a great job bringing a lot of new faces together in a short amount of time.” He added, “You can see them continue to improve.” And then came the line that tells you how seriously Alabama is taking this game. DeBoer said LSU is “top ten offense, top ten defense, very explosive in all ways.” He also said, “Obviously going on the road, that’ll be a challenge as well.” And he finished with this: “One that I know our guys are excited about, looking forward to.”

So DeBoer isn’t underestimating anybody. He knows LSU is a complete team. Later in the week, he called the Tigers an “all-around complete football team” and said, “it’s not like you have a weakness.” But he also made it clear his team is excited for the challenge. And that’s exactly the attitude you want going into Death Valley. In that same Wednesday conversation, he went even deeper. He said, “I just think there’s an attacking mindset that the best teams in the country have.” He said, “I think the secondary gets overlooked because of what they do up front.” And he said, “They got creative schemes. They do a good job of disguising and making it tough on your quarterback.” So this is a defense that will try to confuse Alabama’s young quarterback. That’s another reason having a veteran receiver like Coleman-Williams back would be so valuable. Experienced receivers help a quarterback see through disguises, because they know how to adjust their routes when the coverage changes after the snap.

Now let’s get to the huge weapon. Ryan Coleman-Williams.

A reporter asked DeBoer about the wide receivers, regardless of whether Ryan returns. And DeBoer was really honest. He said, “With Ryan being gone, we didn’t want that, and I think that’s hurt a little bit of production. Just his impact, I mean, how can it not?” I love that honesty. A lot of coaches would just say, “Next man up, we’re fine.” DeBoer admitted what every Alabama fan already knew. When your best receiver isn’t on the field, it affects everything.

Let’s remember what Coleman-Williams was doing before he got hurt. Twenty-three catches. Four hundred fifteen yards. Four touchdowns. That’s eighteen yards per catch. He was Alabama’s leader in receptions and receiving touchdowns. He’s the guy who stretches the field, the guy defenses have to account for on every single snap. And then he got hurt against Georgia on October tenth. He was dealing with a knee injury that required arthroscopic surgery on his meniscus, and a head injury as well. He missed Tennessee. He missed Texas A&M. And during that stretch, Alabama’s offense didn’t look the same.

But here’s where the story turns. Back on October twenty-sixth, DeBoer said, “I’m hopeful that he’ll be ready for the LSU game.” On Monday of this week, DeBoer said that Coleman-Williams was among the players who are “all out there going through workouts.” On Wednesday and Thursday, he was listed as questionable on the SEC availability reports, not doubtful, not out. Earlier this week, he was seen in practice video without a knee brace. And according to Tiger Rag, Kiffin said, “I bet Ryan will be back.”

That’s a lot of signs pointing in the same direction. Now, I want to be careful. Nothing is official until the final availability report about ninety minutes before kickoff. And even if he plays, the staff might limit his snaps in his first game back. But the possibility of having Coleman-Williams back in this offense is the biggest addition Alabama could make for this game.

And DeBoer explained exactly why it matters. He said, “You add Ryan back into the mix, and you got again guys that just are more familiar and more comfortable with their roles, and also just even more comfortable with Keelon and just operating together.” Think about that. It’s not just about Ryan’s talent. It’s about chemistry. It’s about Keelon Russell knowing exactly where his top receiver is going to be on a third and seven with a hundred thousand people screaming. That kind of trust doesn’t get built overnight. It was built in camp, in the spring, and in the first six games of this season. And think about the specific ways his return could change this game. On third down, Russell gets a receiver he trusts to win a contested catch. In the red zone, he gets a big target on the back-shoulder fade. And on the deep shots, he gets a receiver who can stretch the field and force LSU’s safeties to back up. Even if Coleman-Williams only plays a portion of the snaps, the threat of him changes how LSU calls its defense. Coordinators can’t play the same way when a receiver like that is on the field.

And remember the bigger story here. Before this season, he changed his name from Ryan Williams to Ryan Coleman-Williams to honor his mother, and he switched to number one, the number he wore at Saraland High School. He called it “an opportunity to have a hard reset.” He said, “I have a legacy that I want to write.” He was writing that legacy before the injury. Now he might get the chance to write the next chapter in one of the toughest stadiums in college football.

Now let’s talk about the guys who held it down while he was gone. Because DeBoer had a lot of praise for them too.

DeBoer said what you really hope is that “a Rico Scott, and obviously Lotzeir’s been pretty consistent throughout the course of the year,” along with “Derek Meadows stepping on the field more,” and other guys getting more reps, all step up. And he said something really important about practice. He talked about guys “that maybe haven’t been on the football field in the games or had the production yet, but they’re getting more reps, you know, quality, really quality reps in practice. And so, you know, when those moments come, they’re even more ready than they have been.”

That’s how depth gets built. Not just in games. In practice. And it’s a credit to the receivers coach and the entire offensive staff that they kept those younger guys engaged and improving while the spotlight was on the injury reports. Derek Meadows getting more snaps is a perfect example. He’s now a player Russell has real game experience throwing to, and that’s going to matter in November. Every rep that a younger receiver took while Coleman-Williams was out was a rep that made this room better. So when Ryan comes back, Alabama isn’t just getting one player back. It’s getting one player back into a room that’s deeper and more experienced than it was a month ago.

And then there’s Rico Scott. Against Texas A&M, Rico had six catches for one hundred fifty-six yards, a career high, and he was named one of Alabama’s players of the week. His previous season high was fifty-six yards. And DeBoer talked about what a game like that does for a young receiver. He said, “I think you just kind of envision yourself when you do those type of things in a different light.” He added, “There’s a confidence factor, but there’s also just an expectation, you know, that, hey, I’m going to be ready. I’m going to be prepared.” And he said, “It’s a lot of fun when that happens.”

That’s what you want. Rico now sees himself as a guy who can take over a game. And if Coleman-Williams is back, defenses can’t double Rico. They have to pick their poison. Do you roll coverage toward Ryan and leave Rico one-on-one? Or do you focus on Rico and let Ryan beat you deep? And don’t forget Lotzeir Brooks working the slot, plus Marshall Pritchett, Kaleb Edwards and Josh Ford at tight end. That’s a lot of weapons for an LSU secondary that DeBoer said “gets overlooked because of what they do up front.”

Let’s move to the run game, because DeBoer got asked about it directly.

After the A&M game, DeBoer made it clear he wasn’t happy with the ground attack. Alabama ran for just sixty-eight yards on twenty-eight carries, one week after running for fifty-four at Tennessee. So a reporter asked how important it is to turn that around on the road at LSU. DeBoer said, “It’d be very important, and it will be, for us to execute. You know, everyone doing their job, being on the same page, and that helps Keelon out.” And then he explained exactly why: “Grabbing some momentum, staying on the football field, keeps their offense off the field as well.”

That last part is huge. LSU’s offense, with Sam Leavitt, is explosive. Leavitt has thrown seven touchdowns with no interceptions in his last two games. The best way to slow down an explosive offense is to keep it on the sideline. Long drives. First downs on the ground. Time of possession. And that all starts with the run game.

And then DeBoer said something that every Alabama fan should remember. He compared this situation to two years ago. He said, “A couple years ago, that was a big part of us going down there, learning from playing an environment at Tennessee, and guys adjusting and being better because of the hardships we had.” Think about what he’s referring to. In 2024, DeBoer’s first season, Alabama lost on the road at Tennessee. And then, a few weeks later, Alabama went into Baton Rouge and beat LSU forty-two to thirteen. DeBoer is saying the lessons from that tough day in Knoxville helped fuel that dominant night in Death Valley.

And now, look at 2026. Alabama lost at Tennessee again. And DeBoer said, “We struggled at Tennessee with some things,” and “taking those moments and learning from it and being better because of it. So that’s what we got to do, and the run game would be a very strong part of that.” That’s the blueprint. Learn from Knoxville. Win in Baton Rouge. Same as two years ago.

And the injuries matter here too. Center Racin Delgatty is questionable, and the run game averaged around one hundred eighty-two yards a game with him in the lineup, compared to about sixty-one in the two games without him. EJ Crowell, the true freshman running back averaging six point eight yards per carry, is questionable. Left guard William Sanders is questionable after a TightRope procedure on his ankle. If even a couple of those guys can go, the run game has a real chance to bounce back. And LSU is expected to be without its star linebacker, Whit Weeks, according to CBS Sports’ Matt Zenitz, which could create opportunities in the middle of the field.

Next, the bye week.

A reporter pointed out that DeBoer’s teams seem to play well coming out of bye weeks, and asked why. DeBoer gave a lot of credit to his staff. He said, “I think our staff does a really good job of laying out a plan for our players.” And then he explained the mindset. He said it’s about “taking advantage of it and not just letting it be time off.” He added, “Yeah, you got to refresh mentally, but we’re in it. Like, we’re in the season. Like, this isn’t even close to the end.”

I love that line. “This isn’t even close to the end.” That’s a coach who knows his team still has a lot of football left. LSU, Vanderbilt, Chattanooga, Auburn, and hopefully the playoff after that.

DeBoer also talked about how the staff used the extra time. He said they focused on “understanding the areas that we want to continue to improve and guys that we need to keep preparing to be ready.” And he pointed to the A&M game as an example, saying it “showed up in Texas A&M, where new faces got out there and extended roles and reps.” He said the staff has done “a really good job of being focused, making every rep that matters count, as we get these bonus practices for our next opponent, obviously in this case, LSU.” He called the approach “really methodical.” And he said the players got a day off on Saturday, but “these guys came back ready, and you can tell that their bodies are refreshed and they were excited, had good energy.”

So the bye week checked every box. Rest. Healing. Development of younger players. And extra game planning for LSU. That’s exactly what you want. And two weeks of preparation against a coach as creative as Lane Kiffin is a real advantage. Every trick play, every tempo change, every wrinkle Kiffin has shown this season, Alabama’s staff has had extra time to study it.

Now, DeBoer’s message to Keelon Russell.

A reporter asked what his message was to Russell heading into another hostile road environment. DeBoer said, “I think it’s just learning from what you’ve been through, and it’s not just a road environment that we’ve been in. It’s all the experiences that you’ve been through up to this point this season.” Then he got specific. He talked about “understanding down and distance situations,” and “understanding how each game, there’s ebbs and flows, but you just got to stay steady and give yourself a chance to see the next play.”

And then came the key part. DeBoer said, “It’s keeping yourself upright. Getting rid of the football.” He said that’s something Russell “continues to improve at.” He added, “I think the number of plays that you see continue to drop when it comes to those times where he’s trying to do too much or create.” And then he balanced it out: “But also balancing that out with the abilities that he has to go do some special things, but that will come to him because he’s that type of player.”

That’s the perfect message for this game. LSU has thirty-four sacks this season, the second most in the country. Princewill Umanmielen and Jordan Ross have combined for thirteen and a half. If Russell holds the ball too long, that pass rush will make him pay. So DeBoer wants him to stay upright, get the ball out, and trust his playmakers. And when the moment comes for a special play, Russell has shown he can make it. Remember, against Texas A&M, he went five for five for sixty-eight yards and two touchdowns in the fourth quarter. At Kentucky, he bounced back from two early turnovers and led a twenty-eight-point win. Against Florida State, he led a comeback from fifteen points down. This young quarterback has already been through a lot this season, and DeBoer believes all of it has prepared him for Saturday.

And having Coleman-Williams back would make DeBoer’s message even easier to follow. Getting the ball out quickly is a lot easier when your top receiver is creating separation.

Next, the secondary.

The last question of the press conference was about the leadership in the defensive backfield, with two captains in the secondary plus Keon Sabb. And DeBoer gave us a peek behind the curtain at how close Alabama came to running out of bodies against A&M. He said there were “a lot of different pieces with guys that had to move around even in this last game.” And then he said, “We were the what-ifs on the sideline that were happening with who we had on the football field last week against Texas A&M. And it wasn’t two steps away. It was getting to the point where we were one step away. It was getting interesting.”

Think about that. Alabama was one injury away from a real crisis in the secondary against A&M. Zabien Brown was out. Red Morgan was ejected for targeting. And yet this group held the Aggies to just fifteen total yards in the fourth quarter. Djion Lee Jr. had a pick-six. Chuck McDonald III got his first career interception. That’s a secondary that bent, adjusted and still came up huge. And now, with Zabien Brown questionable to return, the room could be a lot healthier in Baton Rouge. And the defensive line could be getting healthier too. Terrance Green, Alabama’s starting nose tackle, has been questionable with an ankle injury after missing the last two games. Yhonzae Pierre said this week, “We got TG coming back, Terrence Green, so we’ve been working on schemes for the defense and for them.” With Green anchoring the middle, Fatutoa Henry, who had two sacks against A&M, and Devan Thompkins can rotate and stay fresh. And Pierre, who had two sacks and a game-sealing strip-sack against LSU last season, can attack the edge. That’s a pass rush that can make Leavitt very uncomfortable.

And the leadership in that room is what made the difference. Bray Hubbard talked this week about the plan against Kiffin’s offense: “We got to do a great job disguising and making sure they can’t just tip our coverages off of just our alignment.” That’s the kind of veteran voice you want in the back end against a quarterback like Leavitt, who DeBoer said turns plays that are “dead in the backfield” into touchdowns. Leavitt is the real deal. In his last two games, he’s thrown seven touchdown passes and zero interceptions, including three hundred seventy-eight yards and three touchdowns in a thirty-eight-to-seventeen win at Auburn. He also has eight rushing touchdowns this season, five of them at Tiger Stadium. An anonymous defensive coordinator told Saturday Down South this summer that Leavitt is “sneaky athletic, really way more elusive than you would think,” and that “where he gets you is when he extends plays.” So Kane Wommack’s plan is clear. He said this week, “You’ve got to create enough doubt in their minds and then capitalize.” And he added, “So far, we’ve found ways to capitalize and create takeaways off RPOs.” Keep Leavitt in the pocket, disguise the coverage, and take the ball away when he makes a mistake.

A couple more quick notes from the press conference.

On Desmond Umeozulu, DeBoer said, “Desmond will still be out for a while yet.” He added that he’s hopeful later in the season, “but it would be towards the very back end of the regular season.” So Alabama will be without him in Baton Rouge. Get well soon, Desmond.

And on the kickoff time, DeBoer was asked about Kiffin’s comments that Alabama gets an advantage from the afternoon kickoff. His answer was classic DeBoer. He said, “I feel like we’re on the road, and so, you know, I’d rather play at home if we want to have all the advantages.” He added, “I don’t get to choose that, I guess. And so I don’t have any choice in that. It is what it is. We just got to show up and play.” No drama. Just football.

And there’s one more storyline worth mentioning on the LSU side. The Tigers are expected to be without star linebacker and team captain Whit Weeks, who broke his fibula against Texas A&M back in September. CBS Sports’ Matt Zenitz reported that Weeks is “expected to be out on Saturday.” We never celebrate an injury, and we wish him a full recovery. But it’s a big loss for LSU’s defense. The last time he played Alabama, in 2024, he had fifteen tackles. LSU has used Dahvon Keys at the second linebacker spot in his place. And that could be another reason for Alabama to lean on the run game and the tight ends over the middle.

So let’s put it all together.

Alabama comes out of the bye week refreshed, with a staff that used every day to prepare. Its top receiver, the huge weapon, is trending toward a return. The rest of the receiver room is deeper and more confident than it was a month ago. The head coach has a clear plan for the run game, built on lessons learned in Knoxville. The quarterback has a clear message: stay upright, get the ball out, and make the special plays when they come. And a secondary that was one step away from disaster against A&M could be getting its All-American corner back.

That’s a team that’s ready. And it’s a team with a lot on the line. The College Football Playoff committee ranked Alabama fifth this week, the highest-ranked two-loss team in the country. Not everyone agreed. Some analysts argued that Tennessee, which beat Alabama, should be higher, or that Texas deserves the spot. And Paul Finebaum said on the SEC Network that this game is basically a playoff game for Alabama, adding, “If Alabama loses this game, forget it.” Those are fair debates. But the committee clearly respects the road win at Kentucky, now ranked sixth, and the win over South Carolina, now ranked twenty-first. Win on Saturday, and Alabama will be in great shape. After LSU come Vanderbilt on the road, Chattanooga at home and Auburn in the Iron Bowl.

Here are my keys for Saturday. One, get Coleman-Williams involved early if he plays, even on a limited snap count, so LSU has to respect him. Two, run the ball with purpose and stay on the field, just like DeBoer said. Three, keep Russell upright against that pass rush with quick throws and smart protection. Four, contain Leavitt and don’t let him turn dead plays into big gains. And five, win the turnover battle. Alabama won it against A&M and won the game. It lost it at Tennessee and lost the game.

My prediction stays the same. Alabama twenty-seven, LSU twenty-four. And I’ll add this. If Ryan Coleman-Williams plays, I think he makes at least one huge catch that changes the game. Just like DeBoer said, how can his impact not matter?
