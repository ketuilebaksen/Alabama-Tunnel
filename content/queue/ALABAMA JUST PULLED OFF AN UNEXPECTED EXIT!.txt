On Tuesday night, the College Football Playoff committee rewarded that win in a big way. Let’s start with Josh Ford, because he deserves to be talked about first.

On Monday, CBS Sports’ Matt Zenitz reported that Ford is set to miss the rest of the season due to a knee injury he suffered against LSU. Multiple outlets have reported that he’s expected to undergo surgery. Ford left the game in Baton Rouge and didn’t return. And this one hurts, because Ford had just scored a touchdown in that very game. With thirty-six seconds left in the third quarter, right after Rico Scott’s fifty-seven-yard catch-and-run, Keelon Russell found Ford for a seven-yard touchdown that gave Alabama a seventeen-to-thirteen lead. It was the go-ahead score, and it was the start of the twenty-one-to-nothing run that won the game. Ford finished the night with two catches for fifteen yards and that touchdown.

So for Ford, the LSU game was both the high point and the hardest moment of his season. He scored the touchdown that flipped the game, and then he got hurt in the same night. That’s football, and it’s tough.

Now let’s talk about who Josh Ford is, because his story this season is one of the best on this team.

Ford is a six-foot-six, two-hundred-sixty-five-pound tight end who transferred to Alabama from Oklahoma State last December. In two seasons at Oklahoma State, he caught thirteen passes for one hundred thirty-seven yards and two touchdowns in sixteen games. At Alabama, he quickly became a big part of the offense, especially as a run blocker. Through nine games this season, he had five catches for forty-one yards and two touchdowns, and he played two hundred sixteen offensive snaps, the second most among Alabama’s tight ends.

But the numbers don’t tell the real story. Here’s what does. Back in the Mississippi State game, Ford broke his arm. Most players would have missed weeks. Ford had surgery the following Sunday. And then he played against Georgia, without missing a game. He got hurt during that Georgia game too, and finished it anyway. And when he was asked about it, he said something that tells you everything about him: “I told them I was going to play no matter what.”

Think about that. Surgery on a Sunday, and then lining up against one of the most physical defenses in the country less than a week later. And he’s been playing through that forearm injury ever since, all the way into Death Valley. That’s toughness. That’s the kind of player you want on your team in November.

And his teammates feel the same way. On Tuesday, fellow tight end Kaleb Edwards called Ford “a great vocal leader.” He said Ford “encourages us every day” and that he “means a ton” to the tight end room. And Kalen DeBoer called Ford a “uniter.” That word says a lot. Ford isn’t just a blocker or a pass catcher. He’s someone who brings the locker room together.

DeBoer also said the team has to “find ways to make up the gap” Ford leaves. And he shared something that every Alabama fan wants to hear. He said Ford “is gonna have a fast recovery.” Knowing how Ford handled the broken arm, I don’t doubt that one bit. Get well soon, Josh. Tide Nation is behind you all the way.

And here’s something I think people will overlook. Even though Ford won’t be on the field, his impact on this team isn’t over. When Edwards calls him a great vocal leader who encourages the room every day, that doesn’t stop because of a knee injury. Ford can still be in meetings. He can still be on the sideline. He can still help the younger tight ends like Mack Sutter, Danny Lewis Jr. and Jay Lindsey learn the little details of blocking and route running that he brought to the position. Some of the most important leaders on championship teams are the ones who help from the sideline after an injury. And from everything we’ve heard about Josh Ford, that’s exactly the kind of teammate he is. The guy who said “I told them I was going to play no matter what” isn’t going to disappear from this team just because he can’t suit up. He’s going to find another way to help.

Now let’s talk about the football side of it. What does Alabama do without Ford?

The biggest thing Ford brought was his blocking. He was one of the most physical players in the offense, and that matters a lot, because the run game is the one area this offense is still working to fix. At LSU, Alabama’s leading rusher, Trae’shawn Brown, had thirty-five yards on eleven carries. So losing a physical tight end who helped set the edge in the run game is a real challenge for Ryan Grubb and the offensive staff.

But here’s the good news. Alabama has depth at tight end. Kaleb Edwards is the obvious one. He plays the same “Y” tight end role Ford did, where you have to block and catch. And Edwards is coming off a big game. At LSU, he caught three passes for fifty yards and the four-yard touchdown that made it thirty-one to thirteen. He was also named one of Alabama’s offensive players of the week. Edwards has been reliable all season, including a big touchdown in the Florida State comeback in September. Now his role gets even bigger.

Marshall Pritchett is another veteran in that room. He’s more of an “H” tight end, used more as a receiver and moved around the formation. Danny Lewis Jr. and Jay Lindsey have also played significant snaps this season. And there’s a young name to watch: Mack Sutter, a former four-star freshman, who could see more playing time down the stretch.

So the snaps will be there for these guys. The challenge is matching Ford’s physicality at the point of attack. I expect Grubb to get creative with personnel packages and blocking schemes over the next few weeks. And honestly, this is where depth and player development really show up. Great teams don’t just survive injuries. They find the next guy and keep rolling. Remember, this team already did that once this season, when Ryan Coleman-Williams, Racin Delgatty and Terrance Green were all out. The young players stepped up, and now the whole roster is better for it.

Now, let’s go back to the LSU game, because there were some things in that game worth looking at more closely, both the great and the stuff that still needs work.

Let’s start with the coordinators, because both of them deserve real credit.

Kane Wommack’s defense was outstanding when it mattered. LSU moved the ball, outgaining Alabama by more than one hundred seventy yards. But Wommack’s group mixed up its blitz packages, pressured Sam Leavitt, forced an interception, and stopped LSU on all four of its fourth-down attempts. LSU got into the red zone four times and came away with just ten points. And in the fourth quarter, LSU scored nothing. The defense wasn’t perfect, especially against some of LSU’s underneath throws when Alabama played softer zone coverage. But when the game was on the line, this unit delivered. Wommack said on Monday, “I thought our guys played really physical. We hit really hard.” And on those fourth-down stops, he said, “They brought those calls to life and executed them.”

And Ryan Grubb deserves a lot of credit too. With the run game struggling, he found other ways to move the ball. He used formations and route combinations to get his receivers open. And the result? Alabama went three for three in the red zone, all touchdowns. Three different receivers caught touchdown passes. That’s creative play-calling against one of the best defenses in the SEC.

Let’s talk about one of those touchdowns in particular, because it was beautifully designed. In the fourth quarter, right after Ryan Coleman-Williams’ thirty-two-yard punt return put Alabama at the LSU twenty-five, Grubb dialed up what looked like a switch-release concept. That’s when two receivers cross each other right off the line to confuse the defensive backs. It created just enough space, Russell delivered an accurate throw, and freshman Cederian Morgan finished the play for a twenty-five-yard touchdown. That was only Morgan’s second catch of the season, and both of his catches have gone for touchdowns. That’s the kind of young playmaker who gets everybody in Tuscaloosa excited about the future.

And then there was Keelon Russell. Seventeen of twenty-four for two hundred six yards, three touchdowns and no interceptions, while playing through an ankle injury in one of the loudest stadiums in college football. After tough days against Georgia and Tennessee earlier this season, Russell showed real growth in his decision-making. He threw the ball away when nothing was there. He protected the football. And he made the big throws when Alabama needed them. On Monday, Grubb said, “His best football’s ahead of him. He’s one of, if not the best, quarterbacks in the country.” And he called Russell’s LSU performance “one of the guttier performances I’ve ever been around for a kid that age.”

As for Russell’s ankle, DeBoer said on Monday that he’s “day to day.” He said, “He finished out the game. That’s a good sign right there.” And Grubb added, “They seem pretty confident Keelon is going to be alright.” We’ll keep an eye on the availability reports later this week.

Ryan Coleman-Williams made an immediate impact in his first game back, scoring Alabama’s first touchdown on a one-yard trick play run and then delivering that thirty-two-yard punt return that set up Morgan’s touchdown. DeBoer said, “Anytime he touches the football, you know, it can be electric, and so the other teams are probably holding their breath anytime he does.”

On defense, Luke Metz was everywhere. Eleven tackles and an interception right before halftime, when LSU was already in field goal range. That pick took points off the board and sent Alabama into the locker room with a ten-to-three lead. Yhonzae Pierre was a problem for LSU’s offensive line too, rushing from the edge and attacking the interior on designed pressure packages. He finished with a sack. And Devan Thompkins had two sacks, including one late in the fourth quarter that forced a turnover on downs and gave Alabama the ball at the LSU eighteen. That led to the touchdown that sealed the game. Thompkins was named the SEC Defensive Lineman of the Week for that performance.

But now, let’s be honest about the areas that still need work, because that’s what we do here on Alabama Tunnel.

The first is discipline. Alabama was flagged six times at LSU, and some of those penalties came at bad times, especially in the first half. A few of those were pre-snap flags and holding calls up front that stalled drives and put the offense behind the chains. Against LSU, Alabama had enough elsewhere to overcome those mistakes. But in a playoff game against an elite opponent, you can’t count on that. Cleaning up the pre-snap penalties has to be a priority. And to be fair, playing in Tiger Stadium makes communication really hard for an offensive line. Still, it’s something the staff will want to fix.

The second, and probably the bigger one, is the run game. Despite the comfortable win, Alabama still struggled to run the ball consistently. There are real reasons for that. Racin Delgatty was just coming back from injury at center. The offensive line has dealt with a lot of personnel changes this season. And now, losing Ford makes it even more of a challenge. But if Alabama wants to make a deep playoff run, it needs a dependable rushing attack. When an offense depends too much on its quarterback, defenses can adjust, bring more pressure and make life hard in big moments. So the development of the run game, and of the tight ends who’ll replace Ford, is going to be one of the most important storylines over the final few weeks.

But here’s the thing. These are fixable problems. And they’re the kinds of problems a team can work on while it’s winning. And Alabama is winning.

Which brings us to the biggest news of the week: Alabama is number three in the College Football Playoff rankings.

On Tuesday night, the committee released its second rankings of the season, and Alabama jumped two spots, from fifth to third. Here’s the new top twelve. Georgia is number one at eight and one. Utah is two at nine and oh. Alabama is three at seven and two. UCLA is four at nine and oh. Notre Dame is five. Texas is six. Miami dropped to seven after losing to Notre Dame. Tennessee is eight. Indiana is nine. BYU is ten. Kentucky is eleven. And Texas Tech is twelve.

Think about what that means. Alabama is the highest-ranked two-loss team in the country by a wide margin. It’s ahead of four undefeated or one-loss teams. And here’s the big one: in this twelve-team playoff format, the top four teams get first-round byes. If the season ended today, Alabama would get one of those byes. That means skipping the first round and going straight to the quarterfinals. For a team that’s been banged up all season, an extra week of rest in December would be enormous.

Now, there’s an important detail to remember. In this format, the top four teams get the byes, and the five highest-ranked conference champions earn automatic bids, with the rest of the field filled by at-large teams. So for Alabama, there’s still a lot that has to happen, and the SEC race, where Georgia is currently in control, is still a big part of the picture. But the committee has made it very clear how much it respects Alabama’s résumé. Road wins at Kentucky and LSU. Wins over South Carolina and Florida State. And two losses to Georgia, the new number one team, and Tennessee, now ranked eighth.

And look at this. With the new rankings, Alabama now owns three wins over teams in the current College Football Playoff top twenty-five. Kentucky at number eleven. South Carolina at number seventeen. And LSU at number twenty-one. Two of those three came on the road. And both of Alabama’s losses came against top-ten teams, Georgia at number one and Tennessee at number eight. That’s why the committee keeps putting Alabama so high. It’s not about the logo or the history. It’s about who you’ve played and who you’ve beaten. And very few teams in the country can match that résumé.

And think about what a bye would mean compared to last season. In 2025, Alabama had to play a first-round road game at Oklahoma before it could even get to the quarterfinals. It won that game with a comeback, but it was another physical, high-stakes battle in December, and then the season ended against Indiana in the Rose Bowl. This year, if Alabama can hold a top-four spot, it skips that first round entirely. That means more rest for players like Keelon Russell and the guys who have been banged up all season. More time for injured players to get healthy. More time for the coaches to prepare. In a twelve-team playoff, that bye could be the difference between a short run and a long one.

Remember last week, when a lot of people said Alabama didn’t deserve to be ranked fifth? Well, the committee didn’t just keep Alabama there. It moved the Tide up. CFP committee chair Hunter Yurachek was asked about the movement, and he said, “I don’t think it was an admission of them being too low.” In other words, Alabama didn’t get moved up because the committee changed its mind. It moved up because of what it did on the field at LSU, and because the teams around it lost. Kentucky lost thirty-four to six at Tennessee and fell to eleventh. LSU fell to twenty-first. And Miami lost to Notre Dame and dropped to seventh. Yurachek said, “The committee thought moving those teams ahead of Miami was the right thing to do.”

And there are three more Tuesday-night rankings shows before the final bracket is set on December sixth, the day after conference championship weekend. So there’s still a long way to go. But right now, Alabama is exactly where it wants to be.

And here’s where it gets interesting. With the committee holding Alabama this high, the path is clear. Win the last three games, and Alabama finishes ten and two with one of the best résumés in the country. The first step is this Saturday in Nashville against Vanderbilt.

And here’s an important correction on the kickoff time. Alabama and Vanderbilt will kick off at eleven forty-five a.m. Central time on Saturday, November fourteenth, at FirstBank Stadium, and the game will be on the SEC Network. Vanderbilt is four and five, and it just picked up its first SEC win of the season, twenty-five to twenty-three at Mississippi State, thanks to kicker Brock Taylor going six for six on field goals, including a fifty-nine-yarder. Vanderbilt needs two more wins to become bowl eligible, so the Commodores have plenty to play for.

And you know DeBoer isn’t letting anyone overlook this one. Minutes after beating LSU, he was already talking about Vanderbilt in the locker room. He said, “A lot of these guys don’t have the memory from a couple years ago. Big win, then going up to Vanderbilt.” That’s a reference to 2024, when Alabama beat Georgia and then lost forty to thirty-five in Nashville the very next week. DeBoer said, “Every game is its own game, and this week means nothing when stepping on the football field next Saturday.”

So this week will be a test of focus. Alabama has to adjust to life without Josh Ford. It has to keep an eye on Russell’s ankle. It has to keep cleaning up the penalties and building the run game. And it has to handle a Vanderbilt team that would love nothing more than to play spoiler again. If Alabama takes care of business, it’ll be one step closer to locking up that first-round bye.

After Vanderbilt, it’s Chattanooga at home on November twenty-first, and then the Iron Bowl against Auburn on November twenty-eighth. Three games. Three chances to keep building. And a top-four seed within reach.
