He scored the seventy-five-yard game-winner against Georgia that year, one of the most iconic plays of the DeBoer era. Then 2025 was tougher. The drops crept in, the production dipped, and people started asking questions. He changed his last name to Coleman-Williams before this season. He tried playing without gloves, then put the gloves back on. And since then, he’s been one of the most productive receivers in the SEC.

Coming into this week, Coleman-Williams led Alabama with four hundred fifteen receiving yards and four touchdown catches, and he was tied for the team lead with twenty-three receptions. He had two touchdowns against South Carolina. He was the guy defensive coordinators had to circle on the whiteboard every single week.

Against South Carolina, he was the star of the night, five catches for ninety-eight yards and two touchdowns, including a forty-two-yarder, with a team-high forty-one yards after the catch. When this offense needed a spark, Ryan was usually the one lighting the match.

And remember, this is a player who started his first college game at seventeen years old, one of the youngest players in all of major college football at the time. He’s been carrying big expectations since the day he arrived in Tuscaloosa. Through the highs and the lows, he’s kept fighting. That’s why so many Alabama fans care about him so much.

That tells you everything about Ryan Coleman-Williams. He was hurting, and he still wanted to be out there with his teammates in the biggest game of the year. That’s toughness. That’s leadership. And that’s exactly why this team is going to want to go win one for him on Saturday.

On defense, the name to know is Xavier Gilliam. He already has five sacks this season, and he had a strip-sack against Arkansas. With Alabama possibly starting a backup at center, communication up front will be critical. Identify Gilliam on every snap, and help the line with tight ends and running backs when needed.

And of course, there’s Neyland Stadium. More than a hundred thousand fans, a lot of orange, and a lot of noise. DeBoer even mentioned this week what this game means to Alabama’s fan base and thanked the fans who make the trip. This is the Third Saturday in October, the one hundred eighth meeting between these two programs. The teams have split the last four. Alabama won last year thirty-seven to twenty in Tuscaloosa. Neither team has won two straight in the Heupel era. Alabama has a chance to change that on Saturday.

Kickoff is three-thirty Eastern, two-thirty Central. Get your snacks ready, because this one is going to be a battle.

So how does Alabama win without Coleman-Williams? Here are my three keys.

Key number one. Run the football with purpose. Trae’shawn Brown just averaged ten yards a carry against Georgia. EJ Crowell and Daniel Hill are great finishers near the goal line. AK Dear is healthy. Without your top receiver, the best way to take pressure off a young quarterback and a possible backup center is to run the ball, stay ahead of the chains and make Tennessee’s defense respect the ground game. That also slows down Gilliam, because a pass rusher can’t just pin his ears back when he’s worried about the run.

Key number two. Spread it around. Brooks, Rico Scott, Rogers, Morgan, Pritchett, Edwards, Ford, and the backs. Make Tennessee cover the whole field. The more names that catch passes on Saturday, the harder Alabama is to defend. And that quick, on-time passing game also helps protect Russell against the rush.

Key number three. Win the turnover battle. Russell has gone four straight games without an interception. The defense has been creating takeaways, like Hubbard’s interception against Georgia and Red Morgan’s pick-six against South Carolina. Against a quarterback in his second career start, one or two takeaways could decide this game. And DeBoer said it best after Georgia: you make your own breaks.

Here’s why I believe in this team heading into Saturday.

One, the depth is real. We saw it in the South Carolina game, when two starting offensive linemen went down and the backups helped finish a thirty-one-point win. We saw it in Starkville, when this offense scored fifty-six points. This roster was built to handle injuries.

Two, the quarterback is steady. Russell doesn’t turn the ball over, he makes plays with his legs, and his coach trusts him in the biggest moments.

Three, the defense has a chip on its shoulder. After giving up two hundred six rushing yards to Georgia, you know this front seven wants to prove a point against Tennessee’s run game.

And four, the emotional fuel. This team just lost a heartbreaker. Its best receiver is hurt after a hit that wasn’t even reviewed. There’s no better motivation than going into a rival’s stadium and winning one for a teammate.

So here’s my prediction. I still like Alabama in this one. I think the offense leans on the run, spreads the ball around, and finds a new hero at receiver. I think the defense makes a statement against the run and forces at least one big turnover from a young quarterback. Alabama twenty-eight, Tennessee twenty. And I think when we look back on this season, this will be the game that proved this team was more than one player.
