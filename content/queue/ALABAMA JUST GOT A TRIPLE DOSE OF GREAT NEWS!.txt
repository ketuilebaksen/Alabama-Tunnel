Three starters coming back on the same day, for the biggest game of the season. That’s not just good news. First, the full picture from the Friday report.

Upgraded to probable: wide receiver Ryan Coleman-Williams, center Racin Delgatty, defensive lineman Terrance Green and running back AK Dear. Still questionable: cornerback Zabien Brown, running back EJ Crowell and guard William Sanders. Ruled out: linebackers Desmond Umeozulu and Cayden Jones, defensive back Nick Sherman, offensive linemen Casey Poe and Jared Graham, and defensive lineman Jeremiah Beaman.

And here’s the bigger news. ESPN’s Alex Scarborough reported that Alabama announced through its NIL entity, Yea Alabama, that Coleman-Williams, Delgatty and Green “have been cleared to play.” So this isn’t just a status upgrade on a report. This is the program telling you these three guys are ready to go. And the timing tells you something too. Coaches usually keep their cards close to the vest the day before a big game. They don’t want to give the opponent any extra information. So when Alabama goes out of its way to announce that three starters are cleared, that’s a sign of real confidence. The staff isn’t hoping these guys can go. It knows they can.

Now, remember where this team was two weeks ago. Against Texas A&M, Alabama was missing its top receiver, its starting center, its starting nose tackle, its All-American cornerback and its explosive freshman running back. And it still won thirty-one to twenty with a fourth-quarter comeback. Now three of those five starters are back. That’s a massive swing. And it’s not a coincidence. DeBoer said the bye week was “a productive week for our team, for our coaches.” He said the staff did “a really good job of laying out a plan” and treated the open date not as time off but as “bonus practices” for LSU. He said, “We’re in the season. Like, this isn’t even close to the end.” And he noticed something when the players came back: “You can tell that their bodies are refreshed and they were excited, had good energy.” Two weeks of rest, rehab and preparation, and now three starters are back. The bye week did exactly what it was supposed to do.

Great news number one. Ryan Coleman-Williams is back.

Let’s start with the headliner. Coleman-Williams is a junior, a team captain, a former freshman All-American and Alabama’s leading receiver. This season, he has twenty-three catches for four hundred fifteen yards and four touchdowns. That’s eighteen yards per catch. He’s the guy who stretches the field. He’s the guy defensive coordinators build their game plan around. And he’s done it on big stages before. As a true freshman in 2024, he caught forty-five passes for eight hundred fifty-seven yards and eight touchdowns, and he became one of the most talked-about young receivers in the country. Now he’s a junior and a captain, the kind of leader who has seen just about everything college football can throw at a player. A hostile road crowd in Baton Rouge isn’t going to rattle him.

And think about what he’s been through. He last played against Georgia on October tenth. According to the Associated Press, he left that game twice, and he exited for good after a hit to the head early in the fourth quarter. He was also dealing with a knee injury that needed an arthroscopic procedure. He missed Tennessee. He missed Texas A&M. And then he used the bye week to get back to full strength.

Even when he wasn’t playing, he was leading. Rico Scott said this week, “He’s done a good job of being vocal, even when he wasn’t playing, just letting his presence be felt.” And DeBoer was honest about how much his absence hurt. He said, “With Ryan being gone, we didn’t want that, and I think that’s hurt a little bit of production. Just his impact, I mean, how can it not?”

And now, he’s back. Even Lane Kiffin saw it coming. Earlier this week, according to Tiger Rag, Kiffin said, “I bet Ryan will be back.” He was right.

So what does Coleman-Williams’ return mean for this offense? Everything. DeBoer said this week, “You add Ryan back into the mix, and you got again guys that just are more familiar and more comfortable with their roles, and also just even more comfortable with Keelon and just operating together.” That’s the key. It’s not just about one player. It’s about how one player makes everyone else better.

With Coleman-Williams on the field, LSU can’t double-team Rico Scott, who just had six catches for one hundred fifty-six yards against A&M. LSU can’t sit on Lotzeir Brooks in the slot. LSU can’t crowd the middle against Marshall Pritchett, Kaleb Edwards and Josh Ford. And LSU can’t stack the box against the run, because if the safeties creep up, Coleman-Williams can beat them over the top.

And that matters even more against this LSU defense. DeBoer said earlier this week that LSU has “creative schemes” and does “a good job of disguising and making it tough on your quarterback.” He also said, “I think the secondary gets overlooked because of what they do up front.” LSU’s pass defense ranks third in the SEC. So Russell needs a receiver he trusts, someone who knows how to adjust his route when the coverage changes after the snap. That’s exactly what a veteran like Coleman-Williams brings.

And there’s the personal story too. Before this season, he changed his name from Ryan Williams to Ryan Coleman-Williams to honor his mother, and he switched to number one. He called it “an opportunity to have a hard reset.” He said, “I have a legacy that I want to write.” Today, in Death Valley, he gets to write the next chapter. Ryan, welcome back. Tide Nation has been waiting for this.

Now, one note of caution. He’s coming back from surgery and a head injury, and he hasn’t played in four weeks. So don’t be surprised if the staff manages his snaps, especially early. He might not play every down. But even on a limited workload, his presence changes how LSU has to defend Alabama. And if he gets one or two shots down the field, watch out. And don’t forget what this means for Rico Scott. DeBoer said after Rico’s big game against A&M, “I think you just kind of envision yourself when you do those type of things in a different light.” He talked about the confidence and the expectation that comes with it. Now Rico gets to play alongside Coleman-Williams instead of being the guy every defense focuses on. That could be the best thing that’s happened to Rico all season.

Great news number two. Racin Delgatty is back at center.

This one might not get as many headlines as Coleman-Williams, but it could be just as important. Delgatty, the starting center and a transfer from Cal Poly, missed the last two games with a lower leg injury. Mal Waldrep filled in and did a solid job in a tough spot. But the numbers tell you how much Delgatty means to this offense.

Through the first six games, with Delgatty starting at center, Alabama averaged around one hundred eighty-two rushing yards per game. In the two games without him, Alabama averaged about sixty-one. It ran for fifty-four yards at Tennessee and sixty-eight against Texas A&M. That’s a massive drop. Now, it’s not all on one player. Football never is. But the center is the brain of the offensive line. He makes the protection calls. He identifies the fronts. He sets the slides. And against an LSU defense with thirty-four sacks, the second most in the country, those calls are everything. And think about the noise. Tiger Stadium holds more than one hundred thousand fans, and even in the afternoon, it’s going to be deafening. In that kind of environment, the offense usually relies on a silent count, with the center and quarterback communicating through hand signals and timing. That only works when the center and quarterback are completely in sync. Delgatty started the first six games with Russell. They know each other’s rhythm. That chemistry is a big deal against a defense that DeBoer says loves to disguise its pressures.

And here’s a hidden bonus, as ESPN pointed out. With Delgatty back at center, the coaches can move Mal Waldrep to guard if William Sanders isn’t able to play. Remember, Sanders had a TightRope procedure on his ankle after the A&M game and is still questionable. Back on October twenty-sixth, DeBoer said he’d be out “probably two to three weeks.” So if Sanders can’t go, Waldrep can slide over to left guard, where he has experience. That’s a huge piece of flexibility. Instead of having two backups on the interior, Alabama could have its starting center back and an experienced player at guard. And credit to offensive line coach Adrian Klemm, who has had to juggle lineups for weeks. Getting Delgatty back gives Klemm his most experienced communicator in the middle and lets him put together the strongest five he has. Left tackle Jackson Lloyd summed up the group’s mindset this week: “Backs against the wall, everyone knows what we got to do.”

And this connects directly to what DeBoer and Ryan Grubb said about the run game this week. DeBoer said fixing the run game will be “very important” in Baton Rouge. He said, “Everyone doing their job, being on the same page, and that helps Keelon out. Grabbing some momentum, staying on the football field, keeps their offense off the field as well.” And Grubb said, “You’ve got to run disciplined, and you’ve got to run physical.” He also warned the team not to get greedy and to be patient for the big play. I love that. Don’t get greedy. Be patient. Against a defense this good, you’re not going to hit a sixty-yard run on every drive. But four yards, five yards, six yards at a time, keeping the chains moving, wearing down the defensive front, that’s how you win on the road. And then, when LSU’s safeties start creeping up to stop the run, that’s when you take a shot to Coleman-Williams. The run game and the deep passing game work together. And now Alabama has both pieces back.

That’s the formula. Physical, disciplined runs. Stay ahead of the chains. Keep Sam Leavitt and that explosive LSU offense on the sideline. And with Delgatty back in the middle making the calls, Alabama has a much better chance to make it happen. And AK Dear being upgraded to probable gives the backfield another healthy option alongside Trae’shawn Brown and Daniel Hill. And if EJ Crowell, who’s averaging six point eight yards per carry, is able to go, even better.

DeBoer also connected this to a lesson from two years ago. He said, “A couple years ago, that was a big part of us going down there, learning from playing an environment at Tennessee, and guys adjusting and being better because of the hardships we had.” In 2024, Alabama lost at Tennessee, and then went into Baton Rouge and beat LSU forty-two to thirteen. This year, Alabama lost at Tennessee again. And DeBoer said, “taking those moments and learning from it and being better because of it. So that’s what we got to do, and the run game would be a very strong part of that.” Learn from Knoxville. Win in Baton Rouge. That’s the blueprint.

Great news number three. Terrance Green is back in the middle.

And this might be the most underrated return of the three. Terrance Green is Alabama’s starting nose tackle. He’s six-foot-six and three hundred thirteen pounds, a massive presence in the middle of the defensive line. He got hurt against Georgia and has been dealing with a lingering foot injury, and he missed the last two games.

And here’s what ESPN noted: without Green’s size in the middle, Alabama’s run defense struggled. Remember what happened in Knoxville? Tennessee’s DeSean Bishop ran thirty-two times for one hundred fifty-nine yards and two touchdowns. That’s what can happen when you lose the big man in the middle. A nose tackle eats up double teams. He keeps blockers off the linebackers. He makes it hard to run straight up the middle. When he’s gone, everything gets harder.

Now Green is back. And the timing is perfect. Yhonzae Pierre said on Tuesday, “We got TG coming back, Terrence Green, so we’ve been working on schemes for the defense and for them.” And he added, “It’s going to be amazing to see what we do Saturday.” Well, Saturday is here.

With Green anchoring the middle, Caleb Woodson, who had ten tackles against A&M, can flow to the ball. Fatutoa Henry, who had two sacks against A&M, can rotate and stay fresh. Devan Thompkins and Justin Hill give the line more depth. And Pierre, who had two sacks and the game-sealing strip-sack against LSU last season, can attack the edge.

And that matters a lot against Sam Leavitt. LSU’s quarterback has eight rushing touchdowns this season, five of them at Tiger Stadium. DeBoer said this week that there are “a lot of plays that are dead in the backfield” where Leavitt “somehow ends up going right and ends up in the end zone.” So Alabama’s defensive line has to collapse the pocket, keep Leavitt from escaping, and make him throw under pressure. Having Green back to push the middle of the pocket makes that a lot easier. ESPN also described Green as a transfer who has been key against the run, and that’s exactly what Alabama needs today. LSU wants to stay balanced. If the Tigers can run the ball, Leavitt gets easier throws off play-action. If Alabama shuts down the run with Green in the middle, LSU becomes one-dimensional, and that’s when Alabama’s pass rush can take over.

Kane Wommack has a lot of respect for Leavitt. He said, “I’m very impressed with his arm talent,” and praised his toughness and accuracy. But he also laid out the plan: “You’ve got to create enough doubt in their minds and then capitalize.” And he said, “So far, we’ve found ways to capitalize and create takeaways off RPOs.” With Green back, Wommack has the tools to make that plan work.

So that’s the triple dose. Coleman-Williams to stretch the field. Delgatty to lead the offensive line. Green to anchor the defense. Three starters, all cleared, all back for the biggest game of the year.

Now, what about the three players still listed as questionable?

Zabien Brown, the preseason All-American cornerback, is still questionable with a lower leg injury. If he can play, that’s a huge boost against LSU’s passing game, which features weapons like Jackson Harris, who had one hundred sixty-three yards and two touchdowns against Texas A&M, plus tight end Trey’Dez Green and Winston Watkins Jr. If Brown can’t go, Alabama will lean on Djion Lee Jr., who had a pick-six against A&M, Chuck McDonald III, who had his first career interception, and Carmelo O’Neal, who Wommack has said is getting better every single week. Bray Hubbard and Keon Sabb lead the back end. DeBoer admitted this week that against A&M, the secondary was “one step away” from running out of bodies. But it held up, and it held the Aggies to just fifteen total yards in the fourth quarter.

EJ Crowell is still questionable with a lower leg injury. If he plays, he adds explosiveness to the backfield. If not, Brown, Hill and Dear are ready.

And William Sanders is still questionable after his TightRope procedure. If he can’t play, Waldrep can move to guard, as we talked about.

The final availability report comes out about ninety minutes before kickoff. That’s when we’ll know for sure about these three. But even if all three sit, Alabama is in a much better place than it was two weeks ago.

And on LSU’s side, the Tigers are still expected to be without star linebacker and team captain Whit Weeks, who broke his fibula in September. CBS Sports’ Matt Zenitz reported that Weeks is “expected to be out on Saturday.” We never celebrate an injury, and we wish him a full recovery. But from a football standpoint, that’s a big loss for LSU’s defense. The last time he faced Alabama, in 2024, he had fifteen tackles. LSU has used Dahvon Keys at the second linebacker spot in his place. That’s a matchup to watch, especially against Alabama’s play-action passes to the tight ends. If Delgatty and the offensive line can get to the second level, Alabama’s backs could find some room.

Now let’s talk about the kickoff time controversy one more time, because it’s been a big storyline all week.

Lane Kiffin wasn’t happy about the two-thirty kickoff. He said, “It’s interesting that all of a sudden this year, for the first time ever since 2010,” Alabama is coming to LSU and not playing at night. He suggested the earlier kickoff gives Alabama an advantage. He even joked that anyone who thinks the SEC wants LSU to win this year “must have just been on Mars the last four months.”

And DeBoer’s response? Calm, cool and perfect. He said, “Well, I feel like we’re on the road, so, you know, I’d rather play at home if we want to have all the advantages.” He added, “I don’t get to choose that, I guess. And so I don’t have any choice in that. It is what it is. We just got to show up and play.”

That’s exactly the right answer. No drama. No back-and-forth. And honestly, the history of night games in Baton Rouge isn’t as scary as people think. LSU is just one and six in its last seven night home games against Alabama. And the last time Alabama played in Tiger Stadium, in 2024, the Tide won forty-two to thirteen. Day game, night game, it doesn’t matter. Alabama has to go play football, and with these starters back, Alabama is ready to do exactly that. And remember, under DeBoer, Alabama is two and oh against LSU, with a combined score of sixty-two to twenty-two. Last season’s twenty-to-nine win in Tuscaloosa was sealed by a Yhonzae Pierre strip-sack. This staff knows how to beat LSU.

But give LSU credit. This is a very good team. The Tigers are six and two, with wins over Clemson, Texas A&M, Mississippi State and Auburn. Their two losses came on the road, by eight at Ole Miss and by one in overtime at Kentucky. Kiffin has done a great job in his first season, and DeBoer even said Kiffin has “done a great job bringing a lot of new faces together in a short amount of time.” DeBoer called LSU “top ten offense, top ten defense, very explosive in all ways.” And Leavitt is red hot, with seven touchdown passes and no interceptions in his last two games, including three hundred seventy-eight yards and three touchdowns in a thirty-eight-to-seventeen win at Auburn. This is going to be a battle.

And let’s not forget what’s at stake. The College Football Playoff committee ranked Alabama fifth this week, the highest-ranked two-loss team in the country. Not everyone agreed with that ranking. But the committee clearly values the road win at Kentucky, now ranked sixth, and the win over South Carolina, now ranked twenty-first. A win today would give Alabama another ranked road win, and with Vanderbilt, Chattanooga and Auburn left, it would put the Tide in a great position for a home playoff game. A loss would make things a lot harder. Paul Finebaum said this week, “If Alabama loses this game, forget it.” This is a playoff game in November.

So here are my final keys for today.

Key number one. Get Coleman-Williams involved early. Even if his snaps are limited, get him a catch on the opening drive. Let LSU know he’s back and make them adjust.

Key number two. Run disciplined and physical, just like Grubb said. With Delgatty back, the run game should look much better than it did in the last two games. Stay patient, stay on schedule, and don’t get greedy.

Key number three. Protect Keelon Russell. LSU has thirty-four sacks. DeBoer’s message to Russell this week was clear. He said, “It’s keeping yourself upright. Getting rid of the football.” He added that Russell has improved at avoiding the plays where he’s “trying to do too much,” and that the special plays “will come to him because he’s that type of player.” Quick throws. Smart decisions. Let the playmakers make plays.

Key number four. Contain Leavitt. With Green back in the middle, collapse the pocket. Keep him from escaping to his right. Disguise the coverages, just like Bray Hubbard said: “We got to do a great job disguising and making sure they can’t just tip our coverages off of just our alignment.”

Key number five. Win the turnover battle. Alabama won it against A&M and won the game. It lost it at Tennessee and lost the game. It’s that simple.

And a bonus key: handle the crowd. Rico Scott said this week, “It’s a crazy atmosphere. Fans are a little nasty, but I feel like ours are too.” Start fast, take the crowd out of it, and make LSU play from behind.

So what would a perfect first quarter look like? Alabama receives the kickoff. Russell hits a quick pass to the tight end to settle in. The run game picks up a couple of first downs behind Delgatty. And then, on a play-action, Russell finds Coleman-Williams down the sideline for a big gain or a touchdown. The crowd goes quiet. On defense, Green and the line stuff LSU’s first run, Pierre gets pressure on third down, and LSU punts. That’s the dream start. It won’t be that easy, of course. But that’s the kind of start that would put Alabama in control of this game.

My final prediction. Alabama twenty-seven, LSU twenty-four. I’ve had that score all week. And honestly, with Coleman-Williams, Delgatty and Green all back, I feel better about it than ever. I think Coleman-Williams makes at least one huge catch. I think the run game bounces back behind Delgatty. And I think Alabama’s defense, with Green anchoring the middle, makes the key stop late, just like it did against A&M.
