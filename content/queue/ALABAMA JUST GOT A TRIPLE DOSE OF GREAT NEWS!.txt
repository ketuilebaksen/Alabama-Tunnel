Let's start with good news number one: Keelon Russell is ready to go.

A reporter asked DeBoer whether he expects Russell to be limited at all this week. And DeBoer's answer couldn't have been more encouraging. He said, "He's in a good spot." Then he explained why resting Russell against Chattanooga made such a big difference. He said, "Just him not having to go on the football field will help in just kind of working through the weekend and now into the start of the week."

DeBoer gave a really honest explanation of how injuries like this work. He said, "Any type of stress that you put on yourself, anything that's sore, it just lingers and kind of prohibits you from being able to play and practice the way you need to." In other words, every time Russell played on that ankle, it kept the soreness around. But by keeping him off the field on Saturday, Alabama gave the ankle a real chance to heal.

DeBoer said, "Because of what we were able to do on Saturday, keeping him off of it, it's going to allow him to continue to progress and move forward in a really good way." And then he said, "I'm excited about his health and the direction it's going." And finally: "I'd expect him to be full go here all week."

Full go, all week. Tide Nation, that's huge. Think about the last few weeks for Russell. Before Vanderbilt, he barely practiced, and he was a game-time decision until ninety minutes before kickoff. He still threw three touchdown passes, but DeBoer admitted it took him until the middle of the second quarter to find his groove. Now he gets a full week of practice, with all his reps, heading into the Iron Bowl. That's the best-case scenario.

And remember what Russell has done this season while playing through pain. He's thrown for nearly two thousand five hundred yards and twenty-one touchdowns as a nineteen-year-old sophomore. He went into Death Valley and helped Alabama win by eighteen. He went into Nashville on a sore ankle and led three straight touchdown drives. Now imagine that same quarterback, rested and fully prepared. That's a big problem for Auburn.

And let's not forget, the decision to rest Russell also gave us a great performance from Austin Mack, who threw for two hundred fifty-four yards and four touchdowns, and ran for another score, in his first career start. So Alabama goes into the Iron Bowl with a healthy starter and a backup who just proved he can lead this offense. That's a great position to be in. Not many teams in the country can say they have two quarterbacks who have both shown this season that they can lead the offense and put points on the board.

Now let's go to good news number two: the injury report is looking a lot better.

One reporter came prepared with a long list of names, and DeBoer went through almost every one of them. The room even laughed when the list was read. But the answers were really encouraging.

Let's start with Caleb Woodson, Alabama's leading tackler and one of the heartbeats of this defense. He was held out against Chattanooga on Senior Day. But DeBoer said, "I would expect him to be ready to go this weekend." He said Woodson is "making good progress" with an injury he picked up at Vanderbilt. That's a huge boost for the defense.

Red Morgan, the starter at the Husky position, also sat out on Saturday. DeBoer said Morgan "probably could have played on Saturday" and that he was a game-time decision. The staff simply wanted to do everything it could to keep him off the field. So he should be good to go for Auburn.

Ivan Taylor, who left the Chattanooga game in the second half, will be fine. DeBoer said it was a minor injury and that "he probably could have gone back out on the football field on Saturday."

Chuck McDonald the Third got "a little dinged up" with what DeBoer called a recurring injury. But DeBoer said, "He's definitely moved along," and "I don't think it was too much of a setback." He expects McDonald to keep moving forward once practice starts this week.

And remember, on Saturday, Alabama actually ran out of healthy players at the Husky position. Now Morgan, Taylor and McDonald are all trending in the right direction. That's great news for the secondary heading into a game against a dangerous Auburn offense.

On the offensive line, Mal Waldrep Jr. left the Chattanooga game with an upper-body injury. But DeBoer said, "I'm optimistic with him, that he'll be good and confident, ready to go." And center Luke Metz, who was held out on Saturday, is "on track with his injury to be able to play."

So Woodson, Morgan, Taylor, McDonald, Waldrep and Metz all have positive outlooks for Saturday. That's a lot of experience coming back at exactly the right time.

There was one tough update. Safety Jireh Edwards will miss the Iron Bowl because of an injury he suffered in the Vanderbilt game. DeBoer said, "Jireh will be out this weekend." We wish Jireh a speedy recovery, and I know his teammates will be playing for him on Saturday. The good news is that Alabama's depth in the secondary is getting healthier at the same time, with Morgan, Taylor and McDonald all expected to be available.

And DeBoer also gave a great update on one of the team's leaders: Ryan Coleman-Williams.

A reporter asked about Coleman-Williams' season and his leadership. DeBoer said, "Ryan's leadership has never wavered. Even the games when he could not be with us, he was leading all week long and motivating." He talked about Coleman-Williams' "passion for this place." He said he didn't want to call it "unmatched," because so many players on this team care deeply about Alabama, but it's clear how much he values it.

And then DeBoer shared some really encouraging news about Coleman-Williams' health. He said Coleman-Williams has gone through the "ups and downs" of a season, and that "the last couple weeks," he's continued "to get his body even in a better spot." And he said he's "looking forward to a big weekend here with him on the football field, helping us lead."

Tide Nation, a healthier Ryan Coleman-Williams is a big deal. He's a team captain, an Iron Bowl veteran and one of the most dynamic playmakers on this roster. He's already been making plays in the return game, with a big punt return at LSU and another at Vanderbilt. If he's closer to one hundred percent for Saturday, that gives Keelon Russell another weapon to work with, alongside Lotzeir Brooks and the rest of the receivers.

And that brings us to good news number three: EJ Crowell is targeting a return.

DeBoer gave a detailed update on the freshman running back. He said Crowell is still working through "a significant injury" that he suffered about "five, six weeks" ago. And here's the encouraging part. DeBoer said, "The timeline, I think probably most people would say, this next week would be the week that you'd really see and be shooting for to get him out there."

So this week is the target. DeBoer said Crowell has been "taken in increments" and that "the progress has been what you'd hope for." He did get some carries against Chattanooga, but the staff wanted to be careful, and Crowell "wasn't quite comfortable to continue to finish the game on Saturday." That's the smart approach. You don't push a young player too fast.

But think about what a healthy Crowell could add. He came to Alabama as one of the top running back recruits in the country. He's already scored four touchdowns this season. And he brings a level of explosiveness that can change a game with one carry. Add him to a run game that just put up two hundred sixty-three yards against Chattanooga, with Daniel Hill and Trae'shawn Brown both running with confidence, and Alabama's backfield suddenly looks very deep heading into the Iron Bowl.

So there's your triple dose of great news. Keelon Russell is full go. Key defenders and linemen are expected back, and Ryan Coleman-Williams is getting healthier. And EJ Crowell is targeting a return this week.

So let's put it all together and look at what's at stake on Saturday.

The ninety-first Iron Bowl kicks off at six thirty p.m. Central on ABC, with Chris Fowler and Kirk Herbstreit on the call. Alabama leads the all-time series fifty-two to thirty-seven to one. The Tide have won six straight in the rivalry, including a twenty-seven to twenty win at Auburn last season, and seven straight in Tuscaloosa. Auburn's last win at Bryant-Denny came back in 2010.

And the stakes are huge. A win would put Alabama in a great position to reach the SEC Championship Game, depending on other results, and to stay in the top four of the College Football Playoff rankings, which comes with a first-round bye.

And Alabama is heading into this game with so much momentum. A healthy, full-go quarterback. A defense that hasn't allowed a touchdown in nine straight quarters. A run game that just piled up two hundred sixty-three yards. Key starters returning. A team captain getting healthier. A talented freshman running back targeting a return. And a full week of football-only focus, with no classes.

So what does Alabama need to do to win this game? Here are my keys.

Key number one: take care of the football. Ryan Grubb said it himself. If Alabama protects the ball, it has "a really good shot to win the football game." Rivalry games are often decided by turnovers, and with a healthy Russell and a full week of practice, Alabama should be sharp.

Key number two: contain Byrum Brown. Auburn's quarterback ran for one hundred fifteen yards against Georgia. He's a dual-threat player, and Wommack knows it. The good news? This Alabama defense has been incredible against the run lately. Vanderbilt ran for twelve yards. Chattanooga ran for twenty-seven. If Alabama keeps Brown in the pocket and forces him to beat them with his arm, the Tide will be in great shape.

Key number three: start fast. In front of a prime-time crowd at Bryant-Denny, an early touchdown could set the tone and take the air out of Auburn's sideline. Alabama has had some slow starts this season, but a rested quarterback and a confident offense could change that.

Key number four: keep the run game rolling. Alabama just ran for two hundred sixty-three yards against Chattanooga. Daniel Hill, Trae'shawn Brown and possibly EJ Crowell give this offense a real ground game. Running the ball well opens up play-action shots for Russell and keeps Auburn's offense on the sideline.

And key number five: win on special teams. Dijon Lee Jr.'s eighty-three-yard kickoff return at Vanderbilt and Ryan Coleman-Williams' punt returns have been game-changers. In a rivalry game, one big return can swing everything.

If Alabama checks those boxes, it should be celebrating a seventh straight Iron Bowl win on Saturday night.
