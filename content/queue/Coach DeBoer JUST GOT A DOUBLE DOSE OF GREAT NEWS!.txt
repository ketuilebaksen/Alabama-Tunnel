Let’s start with good news number one: the committee chair made it very clear why Alabama is number three.

The chair of the College Football Playoff committee this year is Hunter Yurachek, the athletic director at Arkansas. And on Tuesday night, he explained the decision in detail. He said, “Regarding Alabama, evaluating their full profile over the entire season supports the number three ranking.” He said Alabama is “in the top five in their schedule strength and record strength metrics,” and that “the committee feels that they are the best team of the group that immediately follows Georgia and Utah.”

And then he said the line that stood out the most: “We really feel like they’re one of the best three and four teams in the country.”

Yurachek also gave specific reasons. He pointed to Alabama’s margin of victory in SEC games, roughly twenty-two to twenty-three points per win, second only to Georgia. He said Alabama is in the top five in relative scoring offense and in the top twenty in relative scoring defense. And when he was asked why Alabama is ahead of Texas, he said the separator was “the consistent play of Alabama throughout the season.” He called Texas “a really good football team,” but he said Alabama’s results have been more consistent from week to week.

Tide Nation, that’s a big deal. Remember, there was real worry heading into Tuesday. Last Saturday, Alabama’s wins over Kentucky, South Carolina and LSU all lost value when those three teams lost. A lot of people expected Alabama to drop. Instead, the committee looked at the full body of work and said Alabama is still one of the very best teams in the country.

Good news number two: Keelon Russell continues to improve.

This one matters even more than any ranking. Russell has been playing through a left ankle injury since the second half of the win at LSU. He barely practiced before the Vanderbilt game, was a game-time decision until ninety minutes before kickoff, and then went out and threw three touchdown passes in a twenty-one to six win.

On his radio show Wednesday night, DeBoer gave the latest update. He said, “He continues to improve.” He said, “There was no setbacks or anything on Saturday.” He was honest that “there’s still work to be done to continue to get him to one hundred percent.” But he praised Russell for “just being a pro” and for “working in the training room every single day.” And offensive coordinator Ryan Grubb said earlier in the week that Russell is “doing really good right now.”

So that’s the double dose of good news for DeBoer. The committee believes in his team. And his quarterback is trending in the right direction, with Chattanooga on Saturday and the Iron Bowl one week later.

And Tide Nation, here’s the truth. Alabama didn’t vote. Alabama didn’t sit in that room. Alabama didn’t choose its strength of schedule or decide how the committee would weigh margin of victory. Alabama just played the games on its schedule. And DeBoer’s attitude about the rankings has been the same all year: it doesn’t really matter, you have to keep playing. That’s exactly what this team has done.

And it’s worth understanding why the polls and the committee see Alabama differently. The AP poll is voted on by media members across the country, and the coaches poll by head coaches, every single week. Most voters move teams up and down based largely on wins, losses and how teams look on a given Saturday. The committee works differently. It’s a group of athletic directors, former coaches, former players and administrators who meet in Grapevine, Texas, and they dig through data and film. They look at strength of schedule, the quality of wins and losses, results against common opponents, margin of victory and how a team performs from week to week. So when the AP has Alabama at seven and the committee has Alabama at three, it doesn’t mean one of them is crazy. It means they’re using different tools. And the committee has made it clear that its tools see Alabama as one of the most complete teams in the country.

So let’s talk about the team, because whatever you think about the number three, this is a legit football team.

Let’s go back to where this season started. Alabama opened the year ranked thirteenth in the AP poll. In the preseason SEC poll, Alabama was picked to finish sixth in the conference. Ten players from last year’s roster were taken in the NFL Draft, including two first-round picks, offensive tackle Kadyn Proctor and quarterback Ty Simpson. There was a brand-new starting quarterback in Keelon Russell, a nineteen-year-old sophomore. Nobody was handing this group anything.

And today? Alabama is eight and two. It’s six and two in the SEC. Russell has thrown for nearly two thousand five hundred yards, twenty-one touchdowns and six interceptions. He’s grown up fast, and he’s done it while playing through injuries. A team that’s just riding its logo doesn’t go into Death Valley and win by eighteen. A team riding its logo doesn’t go into Nashville with a banged-up quarterback and win on the road. This team grew up in a hurry.

And think about Russell’s journey specifically. At the start of the season, there were questions about whether a nineteen-year-old could handle being the starting quarterback at Alabama, with all the pressure and expectations that come with it. Now he’s led this team through a brutal SEC schedule. He’s thrown twenty-one touchdown passes. He’s made big throws in big moments, like the late touchdown to Marshall Pritchett right before halftime in Nashville. And he’s done it while dealing with a foot issue and an ankle injury. His teammates have rallied around him, and the staff has trusted him. That’s a young quarterback who has earned respect, not just a ranking.

Now let’s talk about the losses, because that’s where every argument against Alabama starts.

Loss number one was to Georgia, twenty to seventeen, in Tuscaloosa. Georgia is the number one team in the country. That game came down to the final moments, and Alabama had a real chance to win it.

Loss number two was at Tennessee, twenty-six to ten. There’s no way to sugarcoat that one. Alabama made too many mistakes, and it was a tough day in Knoxville. But Tennessee is a top-ten team in the committee’s rankings.

So here’s the key point. Both of Alabama’s losses came against teams in the committee’s current top ten, one by three points and one on the road in a hostile environment. Alabama does not have a bad loss. Not one. And the committee has said again and again that it looks at the quality of losses, not just the number of them.

Next, the schedule. ESPN’s strength of schedule metric has Alabama sixth in the country. Alabama has played five true road games, all in SEC stadiums, and it’s won four of them. That includes a thirty-one to thirteen win at LSU and a fifty-six to twenty-three win at Mississippi State, when both of those teams were ranked. Now, you can say some of those wins have aged poorly as the season has gone on. That’s fair. But Alabama played those teams when they were ranked, on the road, and won going away. That’s all you can ask a team to do. And don’t forget the rest of the résumé. Alabama went into Lexington for the SEC opener and won forty-five to seventeen. It beat South Carolina forty-nine to eighteen. It beat Texas A&M thirty-one to twenty. It beat Florida State fifty to thirty-six. Those are not the results of a team that’s just getting by. Those are results of a team that has been dominant for long stretches of the season, and that’s exactly what the committee meant when it talked about margin of victory and consistency. When Alabama wins, it usually wins big, and it does it against SEC competition.

And then there’s the eye test, which the committee chair specifically mentioned. Alabama’s average margin of victory in SEC wins is one of the best in the country. Its offense ranks in the top five in relative scoring offense, according to the committee. And its defense has been playing at an elite level lately, allowing just one touchdown in its last eight quarters. Against Vanderbilt, Kane Wommack’s unit held the Commodores to six points, two hundred thirty-five total yards and twelve rushing yards.

And the defense deserves its own moment here. Over the last few weeks, Wommack’s unit has turned into one of the most dangerous groups in the country. It’s attacking. It’s getting off the field on third down. It’s holding teams to field goals. DeBoer said after the Vanderbilt game that the defense is “really not playing hesitant at all in those big moments.” Caleb Woodson, the team’s leading tackler, has been the heartbeat of the unit. Dijon Lee Jr. made plays on defense and broke an eighty-three-yard kickoff return. QB Reese had a season-high eight tackles. And the pass rush got to the quarterback five times. When people say Alabama is overrated, they’re usually ignoring how good this defense has become.

So when someone tells you this team is a fraud, the facts say otherwise.

But here’s where I want to be fair, because I don’t think it’s right to ignore the other side.

There are real counterarguments. Tennessee beat Alabama by sixteen, has the same record, and sits six spots lower. That’s a tough one to explain, and plenty of fans in Knoxville are upset about it. Alabama is seventh in both the AP poll and the coaches poll, so the people who vote every week see a top-ten team, not necessarily a top-three team. And the Vanderbilt game was sloppy. A twenty-one to six win over a four and six team, with two turnovers and a slow start, didn’t look like a typical number three team.

And the committee is the one that makes those calls. If you’re upset about where Tennessee is, take it up with the committee. It’s not a reason to attack a group of college players who just went eight and two against one of the toughest schedules in the country.

The real issue, if there is one, is consistency in how the committee explains its decisions from week to week. One week it’s about quality wins. The next week it’s about margin and film. That’s a fair criticism of the process. But again, that’s the committee, not Alabama.

And let’s talk about something that’s actually been really impressive: how DeBoer and his players have handled all of this. They haven’t talked trash. They haven’t campaigned for a ranking. Wommack said the defense has to “stay relentless in the details” heading into the postseason. That’s not a staff that thinks it has arrived. That’s a staff that knows there’s still work to do, and is holding its players accountable. Honestly, that’s the best response to the critics. Don’t argue. Just get better.
