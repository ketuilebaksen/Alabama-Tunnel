And before we get to the news, let’s remember how we got here. Alabama opened with a forty-eight to ten win over East Carolina. Then a forty-five to seventeen road win at Kentucky after falling behind early. Then a fifty to thirty-six comeback win over Florida State after trailing by fifteen. Then a forty-nine to eighteen blowout of South Carolina. Then a fifty-six to twenty-three road win over an undefeated, sixteenth-ranked Mississippi State team. And then the twenty to seventeen loss to Georgia, the team that’s now expected to be ranked number one in the country, in a game Alabama led at halftime and was tied going into the fourth quarter. That’s a résumé. Five wins by an average of more than twenty-five points, and one three-point loss to the best team in the sport. This is a team that has earned the benefit of the doubt.

Let’s start with the biggest news from Thursday’s report. Racin Delgatty is now doubtful.

On Wednesday’s first availability report, Delgatty was listed as questionable with the lower leg injury he suffered late in the fourth quarter against Georgia. DeBoer had said earlier in the week that he’d be pretty sore and limited early on, and offensive coordinator Ryan Grubb had sounded optimistic that he might be able to go. But on Thursday, the status moved the wrong way. Questionable became doubtful. And when a player is listed as doubtful on Thursday night, you have to plan as if he won’t play.

So let’s talk about what that means, because the center position matters more than most fans realize.

Delgatty has been the anchor of this offensive line all season. He transferred in from Cal Poly, won the starting job, and started all six games. On a line that came into the year with only one returning starter, Michael Carroll, Delgatty became the guy making the calls. The center is the quarterback of the offensive line. Before every snap, he identifies the defense, points out the middle linebacker, recognizes where pressure might be coming from, and sets the protection for everyone else. When that’s done right, the quarterback has a clean pocket. When it’s done wrong, somebody comes free.

Now here’s the good news. Alabama has known this might be coming since Monday, and the plan has been clear all week. Ryan Grubb said Mal Waldrep is next up at center. And Waldrep is not some unknown backup thrown into the fire. He made a road start at guard back in week two in Lexington, in a game Alabama won by twenty-eight. When Will Sanders left the South Carolina game, Waldrep stepped in and helped Alabama close out a thirty-one-point win. Some people who cover this team closely have even said they think Waldrep might be the better pure blocker of the two, while Delgatty’s biggest strength has been his awareness and command at the line. So the concern isn’t really about whether Waldrep can block. It’s about communication, and that’s something this group has been working on all week in practice. DeBoer also said Casey Poe has been getting extra reps, so the depth is there behind Waldrep too.

And Waldrep won’t be doing it alone. Right next to him is Michael Carroll, the only returning starter from last year’s line, a preseason All-SEC player and an Outland Trophy watch list guy. Carroll has seen just about everything an SEC defense can throw at an offensive line, and he can help with the calls and the communication. On the other side, Will Sanders, who has been one of the vocal leaders of this group all year. And behind them, a quarterback in Keelon Russell who has gotten better and better at reading defenses before the snap. Remember, after the Florida State game, DeBoer said Russell could have worked a protection a different way on one play. Since then, he’s been sharper and sharper. A smart quarterback can help a new center by identifying the pressure and adjusting the protection himself. That’s a big part of how this offense survives a week like this.

And honestly, this is where everything Adrian Klemm has built in that room pays off. Remember, his whole philosophy is to coach the entire unit, not just the starting five. Will Sanders talked about it back in September. He said Klemm is a teacher before he’s a coach, that he finds new ways to explain things until everybody gets it. That’s why when Jayvin James and Will Sanders went down against South Carolina, Tyrell Miller and Mal Waldrep stepped in and the offense didn’t miss a beat. And the very next week, that line helped Alabama score fifty-six points in Starkville. This group has been tested before, and it’s passed every time.

Right here on Alabama Tunnel, our very first video this season was about Adrian Klemm and this offensive line. Back then, we talked about how a unit that ran for just ninety-two yards a game last year, one hundred twenty-ninth in the country, had turned into one of the best rushing offenses in the nation in the first month of the season. Four new starters, one returning starter, and a new position coach who believed in teaching every single player in the room. That’s exactly why a week like this doesn’t scare me. This line was built from the ground up to handle change.

So yes, losing your starting center for a road rivalry game isn’t ideal. But this isn’t a crisis. This is next man up, and Alabama has a good man up next.

Now, the rest of Alabama’s report didn’t change on Thursday, so let’s run through it quickly.

Ryan Coleman-Williams is out. We covered this in detail earlier this week. He’s dealing with a knee issue and a head injury from the Georgia game, and DeBoer said he’ll be week-to-week after Tennessee. Get well soon, Ryan.

Defensive lineman Terrance Green is out after re-aggravating an earlier injury against Georgia. Also out: defensive lineman Jeremiah Beaman, linebacker Desmond Umeozulu, defensive back Nick Sherman and linebacker Cayden Jones.

And the good news: linebacker Justin Hill is still listed as probable, same as Wednesday. Getting him back would be a real boost for a front seven that’s missing some bodies.

The report gets one more update on Friday, and then there’s a final pregame report ninety minutes before kickoff. So keep an eye on Delgatty right up until game time, because doubtful isn’t the same as out. But Alabama will be ready either way.

And with Coleman-Williams out, the question everyone keeps asking is who catches the ball. Ryan Grubb answered it himself this week. He said he feels great about Lotzeir Brooks, Rico Scott and Derek Meadows. Brooks has been the steady, big-play guy all season, with an eight-catch, hundred-and-one-yard game against Florida State, a seventy-five-yard touchdown four snaps into the South Carolina game, and that clutch thirty-nine-yard catch on third and nineteen in Starkville. Rico Scott was the target on the biggest throw of the Georgia game, the deep ball where a lot of fans wanted a pass interference flag. Derek Meadows got a direct shout-out from his offensive coordinator, which tells you he’s been earning it in practice. Add Noah Rogers, the NC State transfer, and true freshman Cederian Morgan, plus a tight end room that has been one of the best surprises of the season, and Alabama has plenty of ways to move the ball through the air.

Now let’s flip over to Tennessee, because the Vols’ quarterback situation is the other big storyline of the week.

Faizon Brandon, Tennessee’s starting quarterback, has been dealing with a right shoulder injury he suffered back on September twenty-sixth in the loss to Texas. He missed last week’s game at Arkansas. He was listed as questionable on both Wednesday’s and Thursday’s availability reports. And on Thursday, reports out of Knoxville indicated that sophomore George MacIntyre is expected to start against Alabama.

Before the injury, Brandon had completed about sixty percent of his passes for six hundred ninety-seven yards, with seven touchdowns and one interception. So he’s been efficient and careful with the football. But if MacIntyre gets the start, Alabama’s defense is looking at a very different challenge.

MacIntyre, a Franklin, Tennessee native, made his first career start last week at Arkansas. He completed eighteen of twenty-nine passes for two hundred forty-seven yards with one touchdown, but he also threw two interceptions. Josh Heupel liked what he saw, saying, “I thought he was decisive with the football,” but he also said the team needs a cleaner overall effort. And give MacIntyre credit, because he helped Tennessee come back from a fourteen to ten deficit in the fourth quarter to win that game twenty-four to seventeen.

But here’s what I keep coming back to. Going into Fayetteville is one thing. Making your second career start against Kane Wommack’s defense, in the Third Saturday in October, with an Alabama team coming off a loss and looking for a statement, is a completely different thing. Wommack is going to throw disguised coverages, different pressure looks and things MacIntyre has never seen on film. And remember, two interceptions in his first start. Alabama’s secondary, with Bray Hubbard, Zabien Brown, Keon Sabb and Red Morgan, has playmakers who can take advantage if he makes mistakes.

And don’t forget about Tennessee’s offensive style. Josh Heupel’s offenses are known for tempo. They want to snap the ball quickly, spread you out and get explosive plays down the field. That can be dangerous, but it also puts a lot of pressure on a young quarterback to get the offense lined up, make the right read and get the ball out fast. When things go well, it looks unstoppable. When a young quarterback is under pressure and gets behind the chains, the tempo can work against you, because there’s no time to settle down. That’s exactly why Alabama’s defense wants to win on first and second down, force third and long, and make MacIntyre beat them with his arm in obvious passing situations.

And that also ties into a defensive storyline from earlier this week. Kane Wommack said defensive back Carmelo O’Neal is, in his words, steadily getting better and better every single week. After a tough night in coverage against Georgia, there’s competition in that secondary right now, and competition brings out the best in everybody. Against a young quarterback, a hungry, competitive secondary is exactly what you want.

Now let’s talk about the prediction that has everybody talking this week.

ESPN’s Bill Connelly released his SP-plus projection for the game, and it reportedly calls for Alabama to win thirty to twenty-six, with about a fifty-nine percent win probability for the Crimson Tide. That’s a four-point game. A coin flip with a slight edge.

Now compare that to the betting markets. Alabama has been favored by somewhere between seven and a half and eight and a half points this week. And here’s the interesting part: one report noted that the line opened with Alabama as just a one-and-a-half-point favorite. So the market has moved a lot in Alabama’s direction since the start of the week. And the total, the number of points people expect in the game, has dropped from about fifty-six and a half down to around fifty and a half.

So what does all of that tell us? Two things.

First, the people setting the lines clearly believe Tennessee’s quarterback situation matters. When it became more likely that Brandon might not play, the line moved toward Alabama. That’s a sign that a lot of people think the Alabama defense is going to have an advantage against a young quarterback.

Second, the total dropping tells you people expect a more physical, lower-scoring game. That makes sense. Alabama is without its top receiver and possibly its starting center. Tennessee might be without its starting quarterback. Both teams might lean on their running games and their defenses.

And another model that was published this week projected Alabama to win twenty-nine to twenty-two, giving the Tide about a seventy-five percent chance to win. So depending on who you ask, this is either a close game or a comfortable Alabama win. But almost everyone agrees on one thing: Alabama is the favorite. Even on the road. Even banged up. Even after the Georgia loss.

Now, I’m not here to tell anybody how to bet. This channel is about football. But I think the analytics and the markets give us a great way to understand the game. And what they’re telling me is that Alabama is still considered the better team, and that the biggest question is whether the injuries close the gap.

So let’s talk about why I think Alabama is the better team, starting with the quarterback.

Keelon Russell leads the SEC in passing yards. Sixteen hundred yards through six games. Twelve touchdown passes and just two interceptions. And he’s added two hundred twenty-five rushing yards and four rushing touchdowns on forty-six carries. That’s a dual-threat quarterback who doesn’t turn the ball over. He hasn’t thrown an interception since week two. Four straight games without a pick, including against Georgia’s defense.

Yes, the Georgia game wasn’t his best statistically. Sixteen of thirty for one hundred eighty-five yards and a touchdown. But DeBoer was clear afterward that Russell made a lot of great plays, kept battling, and continued to make good decisions. He drove the offense down the field at the end of the first half to score with no time left. He extended plays with his legs and picked up big third downs. And this week, DeBoer praised his ball security and his two-minute drill, saying he likes the direction Russell continues to go.

So when you compare the quarterbacks on Saturday, it’s not close. On one side, the SEC’s leading passer, a sophomore with six starts and the confidence of a veteran. On the other side, likely a sophomore making his second career start, coming off a two-interception game. That’s the biggest edge Alabama has.

But let’s be fair and look at what Tennessee does well, because this is a good team.

DeSean Bishop is one of the best running backs in the SEC. He’s reportedly second in the conference in rushing yards with six hundred seventy-eight, and he’s coming off back-to-back hundred-yard games, including one hundred thirty-eight yards and a fifty-nine-yard run against Arkansas. After Georgia ran for two hundred six yards on Alabama, you know Tennessee is going to try to run the football.

And Tennessee’s defense has been strong. The Vols reportedly rank fifth in the SEC in total defense, giving up just under three hundred yards per game. Defensive lineman Xavier Gilliam has five sacks and a strip-sack against Arkansas. That’s a front that can make life hard for a new starting center.

So here’s the matchup that I think decides this game. Alabama’s interior offensive line, with Waldrep likely at center, against Tennessee’s defensive front with Gilliam. And on the other side, Alabama’s defensive front, without Terrance Green, against DeSean Bishop. Whichever team wins those two battles in the trenches probably wins the game.

There’s one more number I want to mention, because it’s an area where Alabama can get better. One report noted that Alabama’s red-zone defense ranks near the bottom of the country this season. That’s something Wommack will have been working on all week. But here’s the positive side: we saw what happens when Alabama does make a red-zone stop. Against Georgia, Bray Hubbard broke up a pass on third and goal and forced the Bulldogs to settle for a field goal. That’s the kind of play that wins rivalry games. If Alabama can turn a couple of Tennessee red-zone trips into field goals instead of touchdowns on Saturday, that could be the difference.

And here’s something else to think about. One report pointed out that Alabama’s games have gone over the total in five of six games this season, while Tennessee’s last four have stayed under. So we’ve got a high-powered offense meeting a team that plays low-scoring, physical games. Something has to give.

So how does Alabama win on Saturday? Here are my three keys.

Key number one. Win the line of scrimmage with depth. On offense, that means Waldrep and the line communicating perfectly, picking up Gilliam and Tennessee’s pressures, and giving Russell clean pockets. Use the tight ends, Marshall Pritchett, Kaleb Edwards and Josh Ford, to help in protection. Use quick throws to get the ball out early. On defense, it means Devan Thompkins, Fatutoa Henry, Kedrick Bingley-Jones and Justin Hill stepping up without Terrance Green and staying disciplined in their gaps against Bishop.

Key number two. Pressure the young quarterback. If MacIntyre starts, make him uncomfortable from the very first snap. Show him looks he hasn’t seen. Make him hold the ball. Make him throw into tight windows. He threw two interceptions against Arkansas. Alabama’s secondary needs to be ready to make him pay for any mistake. And if Brandon plays instead, test that shoulder. Make him throw the ball deep and outside.

Key number three. Run the ball and spread the passing game. Ryan Grubb said this week he wants EJ Crowell to touch the ball at least ten times. Trae’shawn Brown just ran for ninety yards on nine carries against Georgia. Daniel Hill and AK Dear give Alabama even more depth. A strong run game helps a new center settle in, takes pressure off Russell and keeps Tennessee’s pass rush honest. And in the passing game, without Coleman-Williams, get the ball to Lotzeir Brooks, Rico Scott, Derek Meadows, Noah Rogers and the tight ends. Grubb said he feels great about those receivers. Saturday is their chance to prove him right.

Now, let’s talk about what’s at stake, because this is a huge game.

Alabama is five and one, three and one in the SEC, and ranked sixth in the AP Poll and seventh in the coaches poll. Tennessee is five and one, two and one in the SEC, and ranked fourteenth. The loser of this game takes a serious hit in the SEC race and the playoff race. For Alabama, a win keeps the Tide firmly in the national championship conversation and keeps alive the possibility of a rematch with Georgia in Atlanta. And a convincing win in Knoxville would go a long way toward restoring the confidence that took a hit after the Georgia loss.

And this is the Third Saturday in October. The one hundred eighth meeting between these two programs. The teams have split the last four games, and under Josh Heupel, nobody has won two in a row in this series. Alabama won last year thirty-seven to twenty in Tuscaloosa. A win on Saturday would give Alabama back-to-back victories over Tennessee for the first time in the Heupel era. And the winner gets to light up those victory cigars in the locker room.

And there’s another piece of history here. In this stretch, the game in Knoxville has gone Tennessee’s way. The Vols won the last two meetings at Neyland, in 2022 and in 2024. So this is also a chance for Alabama to go into a stadium that has been tough on the Tide recently and take it back. For a young team with a sophomore quarterback, a win in that environment would be a massive confidence boost heading into the second half of the season.

And let’s not forget the first quarter. Look at how Alabama played when it started fast this season. A seventy-five-yard touchdown four snaps into the South Carolina game. Twenty-one points before the second quarter was a minute old in Starkville. When this team scores early, it’s nearly impossible to beat. On the road, in a loud stadium, with a new center, an early score would calm everybody down and take the crowd out of it. That’s the first thing I’m watching on Saturday. If Alabama scores on one of its first two drives, I like its chances a lot.

Here on Alabama Tunnel, we’ve been saying all week that this game is the true test of the character question DeBoer raised after the Georgia loss. Can this team handle adversity on the road, without its top receiver, possibly without its starting center, against a ranked rival? DeBoer’s message to his players this week was simple. You can’t let Georgia beat you twice. Don’t let the highs be too high or the lows be too low. Pick up, move on and go win. I think this team has heard that message loud and clear.

And think about the road ahead if Alabama takes care of business in Knoxville. Texas A&M comes to Tuscaloosa next week. Then a trip to Baton Rouge for LSU. Then Vanderbilt, Chattanooga, and the Iron Bowl against Auburn. In the expanded playoff, a one-loss Alabama team with a sophomore quarterback leading the SEC in passing is right in the middle of the national title hunt. Some people who cover this team have even argued Alabama deserves to be ranked higher than sixth, and that Alabama and Georgia could both end up as top-four seeds. A win on Saturday keeps all of that in play. And it also buys time for Ryan Coleman-Williams, Racin Delgatty and Terrance Green to get healthy for that November stretch, which is exactly what this team will need.

One, the quarterback advantage. The SEC’s leading passer against a quarterback likely making his second career start.
